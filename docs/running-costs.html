Almost nothing, for a long time — and then it depends entirely on one line of code that nobody has had a reason to think about yet.

The short answer

For your first hundred or so users the app costs $110 a year, and none of that is servers — it is the Apple developer account and a domain name. Hosting is free and stays free. Sign-in is free with no ceiling.

The cost grows with a user's history, not with their training

Because a cold open re-reads every session document, somebody three years in costs three times what they cost in year one — for doing exactly the same amount of exercise. A committed user at year five costs 91,000 reads a month on their own. Your most loyal users become your most expensive, at a rate that never levels off.

Where the free tier ends

Firestore gives 50,000 reads a day free, and keeps giving it after you enable billing. Reads run out long before storage or bandwidth do — storage would last to about 1,900 users and bandwidth to about 320.

The one change worth making

Everything above turns on readShard() asking Firestore for every session document on every cold start. Offline persistence is already enabled — the data is sitting on the phone — but a plain fetch goes to the server anyway and is billed for every document it brings back.

Asking only for what changed since the last sync is worth roughly 20× at every scale. It is the difference between paying at a hundred users and paying at two thousand, and between $13,000 a month and $640 at a million.

It is also the fix for the thing you already noticed

The lag you reported on the nav bar in August was this same pattern — asking again for data the page already had. Some of that was fixed with an in-memory cache. The rest is this.

What could go wrong with the bill

  • There is no hard spending cap on Firestore.confirmed Google shipped real spend caps in July 2026, but Firestore, Auth and Identity Platform are explicitly not covered. Budget alerts are alerts only, and can lag by up to a few days. The only true stop is a script that deletes the billing account, which takes the whole project down with it and may not be recoverable.
  • Region doubles it, and cannot be undone.confirmed Multi-region costs exactly twice single-region on every operation. It is fixed at database creation. Worth checking which one this project is on before it matters.
  • The free tier is a cliff, not a slope.confirmed 50,000 reads a day is fine, and then it is billed at full rate. There is no ramp.
  • Don't opt into Identity Platform — and this one has your name on it.confirmed Basic Firebase Auth is unlimited and free for email, Google, Apple and anonymous sign-in. The opt-in upgrade turns it into per-user billing: free to 50,000 monthly active users, then $0.0055 each, tapering to $0.0025 above ten million. That looks harmless until you remember this app signs everybody in anonymously by default — and anonymous accounts count as billable active users unless automatic clean-up is switched on, which deletes them after 30 days. Every abandoned browser profile would be a line on the bill. Staying on basic Auth avoids the question entirely.
  • Indexes are billed as storage.confirmed A collection with several composite indexes can store more index than data. Not a problem at this size; worth knowing before adding indexes casually.

The ceiling you set

You said revenue must never exceed what it costs to run. That number is now concrete: it is $110 a year today, and it stays under about $1,700 a year all the way to ten thousand users on the current code — or under $280 with the read change.

Donations

GitHub Pages explicitly permits donation buttons and crowdfunding links; ads are the thing its terms push back on. GitHub Sponsors takes 0% from personal accounts. Ko-fi is 0% on one-time tips if you opt out of its Standard tier, plus Stripe at about 1.5% + 20p in the UK. Buy Me a Coffee is nearer 9–10% all in.

Below £1,000 a year the UK trading allowance means there is nothing to report. Above it, Self Assessment. Whether donations attached to a product count as taxable income is genuinely fact-dependent, and worth ten minutes with someone qualified rather than my reading of HMRC's manuals.

Ads are a worse fit than they look

Apple's guideline 2.5.18 forbids targeted or behavioural advertising based on health data, which is most of what this app knows about anybody. Apps "designed predominantly for the display of ads" are refused outright. Given the numbers above, ads would be solving a $110 problem by introducing a review risk and a tracking-consent prompt.

One correction to your notes

Your direction file says Sign in with Apple will be required because of guideline 4.8. That guideline has been rewritten. It is now called Login Services, it names no Apple product, and it describes a capability: if you offer a third-party login, you must also offer one that collects only name and email, lets people hide their email, and doesn't use their activity for ads. Sign in with Apple satisfies it — but so could other things, and the obligation is triggered by offering Google sign-in at all. Anonymous accounts and your own email accounts trigger nothing.

How to read the confidence. Everything tagged measured came out of this repository. Everything tagged confirmed was read off Google's, Apple's or GitHub's own documentation on 2026-09-01, not from memory.

Four figures could not be confirmed and are not load-bearing here: Firestore's storage price in a single region and in European regions, the Identity Platform per-user bands, and Apple's fee in pounds. The first would make the single-region column slightly cheaper than shown; the others do not enter the model. Engagement rates — how often people open the app, how many friends they have — are assumptions, and they are the numbers most worth arguing with.
