# Incline Curls Favour the Biceps, Preacher Curls Favour the Brachialis

**Source:** House of Hypertrophy, "Incline vs Preacher Curls for Biceps Growth (New Study)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NieCscho0UM)) · 2025-02-10 · 12 min
**Topic:** Exercise selection

## Bottom line
- Four studies now compare incline and preacher curls. His read: **incline (shoulder-extended) curls likely grow the biceps more, particularly mid-to-upper regions; preacher curls likely grow the brachialis and brachioradialis more.**
- **The new study ([Kassiano 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39809454/)):** 63 previously untrained women, randomised to incline or preacher dumbbell curl, supinated, 0–120° elbow flexion, **3 sets of 8–12 reps to momentary failure, 2×/week for 8 weeks**. Elbow flexor thickness at 50/60/70% of upper arm length: **50% favoured incline, 60% similar, 70% favoured preacher.**
- The **50% site contained only biceps** in the study's own example image, which is why he attributes that result to the biceps specifically. The 70% site contains both biceps and brachialis, so it cannot be assigned on this study alone.
- **A second study assigns it** — but it is a **conference abstract only, not peer-reviewed and with no full text**. 21 previously untrained adults, within-subject, 12 weeks. It separated the muscles: **biceps volume greater with incline** (differences at 20–40% of biceps length), **brachialis volume greater with preacher** (at 70–80% of its length), and **brachioradialis volume greater with preacher** (at 60% of its length).
- **Differences are not large.** He is emphatic that preacher curls still grow the biceps well and incline curls still produce respectable brachialis and brachioradialis development, and that all four studies trained to or close to failure — the reminder being that training hard matters more than exercise choice here.

## The two studies that disagree
**The 2023 study.** 31 women with some training experience, same three measurement regions. Growth **numerically favoured preacher at every region**, largest at 70%. But **none of the between-exercise differences reached significance, and the incline group's own gains were not significant either** — the study was severely underpowered. Its 70% result agrees with the new data; its 50% result contradicts it. He treats it as the weakest of the four for exactly the right reason.

**The blood-flow-restriction study ([Vendruscolo 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11556575/)).** 21 men averaging two years' training, within-subject, incline versus preacher with **light loads under blood flow restriction**. Elbow flexor CSA at 45/65/85% showed **no significant regional differences**. The kicker: it ran **three weeks**. He is surprised by up to 8% growth in one region over that span, and raises — without resolving — whether cuff pressure on the tissue could itself affect regional growth. He concludes it is not more reliable than the others despite using trained subjects.

## How he weighs them
The tally: brachioradialis, 1 study favouring preacher. Brachialis, 3 studies pointing to preacher. Biceps, 2 favouring incline and 2 finding no evidence of an incline advantage.

The obvious objection is that the two studies finding no biceps advantage used **trained** subjects while the two favouring incline used **untrained** — a clean training-status explanation for the split. He raises this objection himself and then rejects it, arguing he would rather weigh study quality than training status: the underpowered 2023 study and the three-week BFR study are simply weaker designs. That is a defensible call, but it is a judgement, and the training-status confound remains available as an alternative reading.

He also declines to explain the mechanism, saying it is too speculative for the video and pointing to a pinned comment instead — an unusual and welcome restraint given how much of this channel's material is mechanistic.

## Supporting and complicating evidence
- One study in trained men had one group repeat the same exercises across three weekly sessions (standing barbell curl as the only biceps isolation) and another vary them across days (incline, standing barbell, cable preacher). Elbow flexor growth at the **50% region tended to favour the varied group**; the authors speculated the incline curl caused it, which fits the regional pattern above. Indirect support at best.
- An unpublished paper compared cable curls with the **shoulder extended versus neutral** and found similar biceps growth at middle and lower regions. This cuts against the shoulder-extension explanation — though he notes both cable set-ups were hardest at the start of the curl, so the resistance profiles were not equivalent to free-weight versions.
- Open questions he names: incline versus shoulder-extended cable curls, and other variations such as neutral or pronated grips.

**Practical line:** it is a safe bet to include a shoulder-extended curl for the biceps, but if you cannot or do not want to, other movements will still grow them.

**Flagged:** the muscle-by-muscle attribution — the most useful claim in the video — rests substantially on a **conference abstract with no full text**. He labels it as such, notes he cannot rule out that preacher curls also produced greater lower-biceps growth, and says explicitly he is "not completely certain". That is honest, but the conclusion is still built on unpublished data.
