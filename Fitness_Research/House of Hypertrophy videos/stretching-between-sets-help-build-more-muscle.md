# Stretching Between Sets: One Study, Cautiously Positive

**Source:** House of Hypertrophy, "Does STRETCHING Between Sets Help Build More Muscle?" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2VgI8wClZjE)) · 2020-09-30 · 6 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- As of this video there is exactly **one published trial** on inter-set stretching and hypertrophy. Everything here rests on it.
- In 29 untrained men over 8 weeks, adding a **30-second static stretch inside the 90-second rest** produced significantly greater **vastus lateralis** growth; elbow flexors, triceps and rectus femoris trended in the same direction but were **not statistically significant**.
- Two boundary conditions matter: stretches were held at a **pain-free** intensity and for **30 seconds**, not longer. Both painful and longer stretching have evidence of harm or force loss.
- An unpublished conference abstract in **trained** men found greater gastrocnemius growth with inter-set stretching — interesting, not evidence.
- Verdict: low-cost, plausibly mildly beneficial, unproven in trained lifters.

## The Evangelista study
[00:31] 29 untrained men, randomised to stretch or no-stretch. Both groups ran the same programme — bench press, seated row, seated dip machine, biceps curl, leg extension, leg curl — **4x8–12 to failure, 2x/week for 8 weeks**, with 90 s rest between sets. The stretching group spent **30 of those 90 seconds** in a static stretch for the muscle just worked (chest and front delts after bench, lats after rows, and so on), at the **maximum pain-free range** each participant could reach.

Muscle thickness was measured at the elbow flexors, triceps brachii, rectus femoris and vastus lateralis. Only **vastus lateralis** reached statistical significance in favour of stretching. Dhim is straight about the rest: the other sites *slightly* favoured stretching without reaching significance, and he says a longer study or a larger sample might have separated them — which is a hypothesis about power, not a result.

## Why the details of the stretch matter
[03:05] Junior et al. found that a quadriceps stretch taken to a **painful** intensity before leg extensions produced **less** vastus lateralis growth than leg extensions alone. That was pre-training rather than inter-set, but it makes intensity a live variable rather than a footnote.

[03:36] Siatras et al. found that stretching the quadriceps for **60 seconds** caused a larger drop in force output than **30 seconds**. If you lose more force, the following set may deliver less stimulus. Dhim flags this chain explicitly as his own speculation needing validation.

Together these give the practical envelope: **short (≤30 s) and comfortable**, not long and aggressive.

## The unpublished trained-subject data
[04:08] A conference abstract from Silva et al.: 24 **trained** men, 4x8–12 calf raises on the leg press, with the stretching group letting the machine's load hold the gastrocnemius in dorsiflexion during rest. The stretching group gained more gastrocnemius thickness. Dhim discounts it appropriately — never published, never peer reviewed, summary only.

## Where the evidence is thin
"There would be no downside" is the one line that outruns the data: it is inferred from a single 8-week untrained study, and he says so in the same breath. If you try it, treat it as a preference, not an optimisation — and note you cannot self-measure whether it worked.
