# Shoulder-Extended vs Shoulder-Flexed Curls: What the Bayesian-vs-Preacher Trial Shows

**Source:** House of Hypertrophy, "Bigger Biceps: This NEW Study Is Interesting" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5zxj4rXg4tU)) · 2025-05-12 · 12 min
**Topic:** Exercise selection

## Bottom line
- First trial to compare a **shoulder-extended** curl (Bayesian cable curl) against a **shoulder-flexed** curl (preacher curl) with the **resistance profiles matched** — both set up to peak at ~60° of elbow flexion.
- Result: **no statistically significant difference** in any region, for either biceps or brachialis. Both exercises grew everything.
- He argues the null is likely underpowered rather than genuinely empty, and points to the numeric pattern plus four incline-vs-preacher studies as converging weak support for shoulder-extended curls.
- The regional split is the more interesting finding across the literature: shoulder-extended curls tend to favour the **upper biceps**, preacher curls tend to favour the **brachialis and brachioradialis**. That argues for combining rather than picking.
- His own closing position is that differences between curl variations are small and nowhere near the top of the list of things that drive growth.

## The new study
15 untrained men, within-subject: one arm on cable Bayesian curls (shoulder extended, biceps lengthened at the shoulder), the other on preacher curls (shoulder flexed, biceps shortened). Twice weekly for **10 weeks**, 3–5 sets of 8–12 reps to or very near failure, with load adjusted across sets and sessions to hold the rep range.

Two design points he rightly praises. The cables were set so **both exercises were hardest at roughly 60° of elbow flexion**, which removes resistance profile as a confound — no previous comparison had done this. And thickness was measured separately for **biceps** (three regions) and **brachialis** (two regions), rather than lumping them as "elbow flexors", which is what most of the older literature does.

Both arms grew at every region. No significant differences anywhere.

## Why he does not read the null as "no difference"
Numerically, the biceps at the **55%** region grew more with Bayesian curls, and the lowest brachialis region slightly favoured preacher curls. He quotes the standard corrective — "not significant does not equal no difference; in many cases it means not enough power" — and notes that n=15 leaves a study underpowered for small-to-moderate effects.

He is explicit that if this were the only evidence, he would agree nothing can be said. The argument rests on the incline-vs-preacher literature.

## The four incline-vs-preacher studies
Incline curls put the shoulder in extension, preacher curls in flexion, but unlike the cable setup above their **resistance profiles differ** — incline is hardest mid-to-late in the curl, preacher hardest at the start. He flags this as a limit on the comparison.

- **Two found no difference.** One (Vendruscolo) ran only **3 weeks** and used blood flow restriction. The other (Zabaleta-Korta) had raw numbers trending toward preacher at all regions, but subjects trained **unsupervised** and dropout was unequal between groups. Both measured only "elbow flexor" growth, which cannot separate biceps from brachialis.
- **A Japanese conference abstract (Kobayashi)** is the most informative: untrained men, within-subject, with biceps, brachialis **and** brachioradialis measured across multiple regions. Biceps volume increases were greater with incline curls, with the difference concentrated at **20–40%** of biceps length. Brachioradialis volume favoured preacher curls (difference at 60% of its length). Brachialis volume also favoured preacher curls (difference at 70–80%).
- **Kassiano 2025**, a decent sample of previously untrained women, measured elbow flexors at three sites. The 50% region favoured incline; 60% was similar; 70% favoured preacher. He notes 50% should be predominantly biceps and 70% contains more brachialis, and labels that mapping speculative.

The Kobayashi abstract matters for a specific reason: the largest biceps differences sat at 20–40%, and the new study's highest measurement site was 55%. If the effect lives higher up the muscle, the trial may simply not have looked where it is.

## The supporting analogy from the quads
Hip extension lengthens rectus femoris the same way shoulder extension lengthens the biceps. Leaning-back leg extensions grew rectus femoris more than regular leg extensions, with the difference largest at the middle-to-upper regions, while vastus lateralis — which does not cross the hip — grew similarly. A separate study in competitive bodybuilders found greater rectus femoris activity in the leaning-back version, again largest mid-to-upper.

**Flag:** these quad studies are not in the video's reference list, so the specific papers cannot be verified from the description. And the second is an activity measure, not growth — he does note it has been correlated with growth in a few papers, which is a weaker claim than it sounds.

## Bayesian vs incline, and whether to combine
Since Bayesian curls use a cable, you can move the hardest point of the curl. Some assume making it hardest at the stretched start would grow more. He does not endorse this: a study comparing free-weight preacher curls to cable preacher curls set hardest mid-to-late found similar biceps growth at the mid region — though other regions and muscles were not measured. He treats incline and Bayesian curls as effectively similar.

Two variety studies are relevant. Costa 2021 had trained men train three times weekly for 8 weeks, either with the same exercises every day (barbell curl as the only biceps isolation) or varied across days (incline, barbell, cable preacher). Elbow flexor growth at 50% tended to favour variation; the authors speculated the incline curl was responsible, which fits everything above. Hinson's preprint, a small well-trained sample, compared six sets of Bayesian curls per session against three Bayesian plus three preacher; no clear difference in elbow flexor growth, though cross-sectional area and some circumference measures leaned toward the all-Bayesian arm.

## Practical position
Include a shoulder-extended curl somewhere in the programme or rotation. The worst case if future evidence goes the other way is that you build the same amount of muscle; the best case is slightly faster growth. The regional data hint that preacher curls may serve the brachialis and brachioradialis better, so combining is defensible for complete elbow flexor development. He closes by putting the whole thing in proportion: the differences are probably small and other variables matter more.
