# All-Long-Length Curls vs a Mix: One Underpowered Study, Two Honest Readings

**Source:** House of Hypertrophy, "We Have to Talk About Biceps Growth" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lAogfm-RR54)) · 2025-02-24 · 9 min
**Topic:** Range of motion & tempo

## Bottom line
- First study to ask whether training a muscle **entirely** at long lengths beats a **mix** of long and short — rather than long vs short.
- Seven trained subjects, within-subject, with a crossover after a washout. Results tended to favour the all-long arm but did not clearly separate from measurement error on the best outcome measure.
- His read: probably a small advantage to all-long, obscured by low power. He states the opposing read fairly and reserves the right to be wrong.
- Practical conclusion is deliberately unexciting: include at least one shoulder-extended curl in your rotation; if you prefer a mix of variations, you will still grow fine.

## The design — better than n=7 suggests
Seven individuals averaging **5.5 years** of training. Each subject trained both conditions simultaneously: one arm did six sets of a lengthened (shoulder-extended) cable curl per session; the other did three sets lengthened plus three sets of a shortened cable curl. Twice per week, every set to momentary failure in the 8–12 rep range, loads adjusted to stay in range.

Two things rescue the sample size. Within-subject design removes genetics, nutrition and lifestyle as confounders, with no cross-limb transfer and dominant arms balanced across conditions. And after 8 weeks the subjects took a 6–8 week washout and repeated the whole thing with arms **re-randomised** — giving the observation count of a 14-subject study. He credits the researchers for this explicitly.

Note from the authors: most subjects failed near the *start* of the lengthened curl and near the *end* of the shortened curl.

## What was measured, and what happened
Four outcomes: elbow flexor cross-sectional area, elbow flexor thickness at 33% and 66% of muscle length, and arm circumference at 30%, 50% and 70%. "Elbow flexor" because the measurements include brachialis as well as biceps.

- **Cross-sectional area:** meaningfully increased in both conditions, trending toward the long condition — but the between-condition difference did not exceed the region of practical equivalence (set at typical measurement error), so the authors called it no difference.
- **Thickness:** increased numerically at both regions but not beyond the ROPE, so no meaningful growth was claimed, and no between-condition difference. He offers three possible reasons for the mismatch with CSA — only two regions sampled, measurement error, or the novel and unvalidated thickness method.
- **Circumference:** increased at all regions for the long condition only, with the difference exceeding the ROPE comfortably at the 70% site.

## The two interpretations
He lays them out rather than picking one silently.

1. **No difference.** CSA favoured long but stayed inside measurement error, and circumference is a far cruder proxy for growth than CSA or thickness — so the circumference result deserves a pinch of salt.
2. **Long grew more.** Both CSA and circumference trend the same way; the study is underpowered; and the two conditions differed only modestly (long vs *mixed*, not long vs short), so a detectable difference in trained lifters was a lot to ask.

He leans to the second while conceding the first is reasonable.

## Flag
Leaning on the circumference result to support "long grew more" sits awkwardly with his own warning that circumference is the least precise measure available. The two positions are held in the same breath.

## The wider biceps picture he attaches
Incline curls appear to produce greater overall biceps growth than preacher curls, and one paper suggests incline curls grow the upper biceps region more than standard barbell curls — while preacher curls may favour brachialis and brachioradialis. The mixed condition here used a shoulder-flexed cable curl, so it might have driven more brachialis growth, but the researchers did not measure those muscles separately.
