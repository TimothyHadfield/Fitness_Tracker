# Lengthened Partials, Region by Region

**Source:** House of Hypertrophy, "Faster Muscle Growth: This New Study Is Epic" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wT3SGXW-_B4)) · 2025-09-29 · 9 min
**Topic:** Range of motion & tempo

## Bottom line
- New trained-subject study on preacher curls: full ROM and lengthened partials grew the elbow flexors **identically at the 50% region**, but lengthened partials had a modest edge at the **70% (distal) region** — Bayes factor 4.87, i.e. moderate evidence, not a clear win.
- That makes **six** studies comparing full ROM to lengthened partials. All three in untrained people favour lengthened partials. All three in trained people show similar growth — but the two older trained studies only measured mid-muscle regions, so distal effects were invisible to them.
- Fair summary: lengthened partials are **at least as good** as full ROM, occasionally slightly better. Not a revolution.
- Shortened partials are worse on average but far from useless — and **extending** a shortened partial somewhat may close most of the gap.

## The new study
13 trained individuals averaging ~6 years of experience. Unilateral machine preacher curls, twice per week for 8 weeks. One arm used **full ROM, 0–140° of elbow flexion**; the other used a **lengthened partial, 0–70°**. Both arms did 5 sets of 10–15 reps to momentary failure with 2 minutes rest, loads adjusted to keep them failing in range.

Elbow flexor thickness (biceps plus brachialis) was measured at 50% and 70% of muscle length. The 50% site was a dead heat. The 70% site favoured lengthened partials with a Bayes factor of **4.87** — moderate evidence. He is careful here: this is not a large or crystal-clear effect, and he interprets it as *slightly* greater distal growth, in line with the authors.

He also addresses, and dismisses, the worry that distal-biased growth makes muscles look odd — pointing to a pinned comment rather than arguing it in the video.

## Where it fits
Six studies now compare full ROM to lengthened partials. The three on untrained subjects all found more overall growth with lengthened partials, and Pedrosa's measured multiple regions — showing a larger numerical gap at the **distal** vastus lateralis, which is exactly the pattern the new study reproduces.

The three trained-subject studies (including one with a notably large sample) tend to show similar growth. The critical caveat: the two prior ones measured only mid-muscle regions. If the effect is distal, they could not have seen it.

His conclusion is deliberately unforced: continuing with full ROM is entirely justifiable and you will still grow. If lengthened partials appeal, experiment. It's not either/or — you can use lengthened partials on some exercises, on final sets, alternating within sets, or as extended reps once full-ROM failure hits.

## Shortened partials aren't tragic
Ten studies compare shortened partials to full ROM or lengthened partials. Overall growth is generally a bit worse, but growth happens, and **mid-region** growth has sometimes matched the other conditions. One dumbbell preacher curl comparison found the lengthened partial better at 70% but equal at 50%.

The more interesting point is that **how short** the partial is matters:

- Bloomquist: squatting to 60° vs 120° knee flexion — 120° grew more across multiple regions.
- Kubo: squatting to 90° vs 140° — **similar quadriceps hypertrophy**. Extending the shortened partial from 60° to 90° apparently closes the quad gap.
- Larsen 2025: leg press at 100° vs an average 154° knee flexion — similar overall regional quad growth.

But he immediately supplies the counter-evidence to his own point: in Kubo, although quad growth matched, **glute max and adductor growth were clearly better at 140°**. Depth still buys you something, just not in the quads.

## Flag
The title ("Faster Muscle Growth… Epic") sells a regional difference of moderate Bayesian evidence in 13 people considerably harder than the video's own content does. The body of the video is measured throughout; the packaging is not.
