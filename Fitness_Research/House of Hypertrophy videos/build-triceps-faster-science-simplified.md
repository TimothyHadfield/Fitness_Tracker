# Triceps Training: Presses Build the Lateral Head, Overhead Work Builds the Long Head

**Source:** House of Hypertrophy, "Build Your Triceps Faster: The Science Simplified" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fyPZ9rGVBWc)) · 2024-04-22 · 16 min
**Topic:** Exercise selection

## Bottom line
- The triceps has three heads that do **not** grow together. Horizontal pressing grows the **lateral head** well and the **long head** barely at all; skull crushers do the reverse.
- Because the long head crosses the shoulder, it is anatomically disadvantaged in any pressing movement — this extends to incline, decline, machine presses, push-ups and dips.
- **Isolation work is required** for complete triceps development. Presses alone leave the long head — the largest head — undertrained.
- The best-supported single isolation exercise is the **overhead extension**: in the strongest study here it beat pushdowns for long-head growth *and* for combined lateral+medial growth, despite lighter absolute loads.
- Pairing overhead extensions with pushdowns may give more even growth along the length of the long head, but that regional evidence is weak (n = 9, 6 weeks, confounded order).

## Compound vs isolation: they grow different heads
[00:33] Brandão et al. (2020) randomised **43 men** into four conditions: barbell bench press only, barbell skull crusher only, bench then skull crusher, or skull crusher then bench.

- **Pecs:** the bench-press groups grew best, and doing skull crushers *first* produced slightly less pec growth than the reverse — pre-fatigued triceps presumably became the limiting factor on the bench.
- **Triceps overall:** bench-only produced the least growth. Exercise **order did not matter much** for the triceps.
- **By head** — the finding that matters: skull crushers grew the **long head** well, which the bench press failed to do. Both grew the **medial head**, skull crushers more. The bench press grew the **lateral head** well, which skull crushers failed to do.

So the bench press is not redundant. It is the exercise doing the lateral head work.

[02:34] That data used a **two-times-shoulder-width grip**. There is **no research** comparing wide to close grip bench pressing for actual triceps hypertrophy — only EMG work that inconsistently shows higher triceps activity with closer grips.

[03:05] The closest evidence is Wakahara et al. (2013): subjects performed the **neutral-grip dumbbell bench press**, which moves in the same (sagittal) plane as close-grip barbell benching. T2-weighted imaging showed **high lateral and medial head activity, low long head activity** — and after 12 weeks of training the exercise, **long head thickness did not increase**. So a closer grip likely does not rescue the long head.

## Why the long head is missed by presses
[03:35] All three heads cross the elbow, so all can extend it. Only the **long head also crosses the shoulder**, contributing to shoulder extension and adduction. Pressing requires shoulder *flexion*, so a strong long-head contraction would work against the lift — a sensible reason for the nervous system not to recruit it heavily.

[04:06] That logic applies to all horizontal pressing: machine chest presses, push-ups, incline, decline and dips. He extends it tentatively to **vertical presses** (overhead pressing also requires shoulder flexion and abduction) but flags honestly that the shoulder angles differ and there is no direct research. Lateral and medial heads presumably work reasonably well in vertical presses; how that compares to horizontal pressing is unknown.

## Choosing the isolation exercise
[05:38] **Dumbbell skull crushers** appear to behave like the barbell version: Wakahara et al. (2012) found low lateral-head activity and high long- and medial-head activity, matching the hypertrophy pattern Brandão found.

[06:09] **Maeo et al. (2022)** is the strongest exercise comparison available. **21 men**, within-subject design: one arm trained **overhead extensions**, the other **pushdowns**, both moving the elbow from 90° to 0°, with progressive overload, over **12 weeks**.

- Subjects lifted **heavier absolute loads on the pushdown** throughout, yet **long-head hypertrophy was clearly better with overhead extensions**. The likely explanation is stretch: elevating the arm lengthens the long head across the shoulder, so overhead extensions train it at longer muscle lengths.
- Unexpectedly, **combined lateral+medial head growth was also better** with overhead extensions. Shoulder position cannot lengthen those heads, so this has no clean explanation. The researchers could not separate the two heads, which is unfortunate given that skull crushers reportedly miss the lateral head.

[08:44] **Stasinaki et al. (2018)** used the same within-subject setup on **9 people for 6 weeks**, but with different elbow ranges for the two exercises, and measured only the long head. Long-head thickness at 50% and 60% of upper-arm length and CSA at the **lower** portion favoured overhead extensions; CSA at the **upper** portion favoured **pushdowns** — hinting the two exercises grow different regions.

[09:44] He handles the comparison well. Both studies used the **within-subject design**, which controls genetics, nutrition and lifestyle perfectly ("your right arm doesn't sleep more than your left"). But the Greek study had a third the subjects, half the duration, and — critically — always ran **pushdowns first each session**, so residual fatigue could have blunted the overhead-extension arm. The Japanese study **alternated which exercise went first**, which he calls the perfect approach. He therefore puts far more weight on Maeo, without discarding the regional signal.

## Practical recommendations
[11:45] **If you do one isolation exercise, do overhead extensions.** The studies used cables; free-weight versions are plausible but the only supporting data is a five-person study showing appreciable triceps hypertrophy and increased long-head pennation angle — a very thin basis.

[12:17] **Skull crushers** stretch the long head more than pushdowns but less than overhead extensions, so he suspects overhead extensions still win — while noting there is no direct comparison, and that lateral/medial growth between them is unknown. If you dislike overhead extensions, pushdowns or skull crushers will still grow your triceps respectably.

[13:21] **If you use more than one**, combining overhead extensions with pushdowns may develop the upper and lower long head more evenly (from the weak Greek data), and overhead extensions plus skull crushers is also defensible since skull crushers showed high long-head activity at the uppermost portion.

[14:21] Costa et al. (2021) offers modest support for variety: **22 people**, 3×/week for 8 weeks. The non-varied group did only pushdowns; the varied group did pushdowns Monday, cable seated (overhead) extensions Wednesday, kickbacks Friday. Triceps thickness across three regions **tended to** favour the varied group. He immediately concedes the effect could be entirely attributable to the seated extension training the long head at a longer length rather than to variation as such — and that both groups also bench pressed.

## Where the evidence is thinner than the conclusions
Several load-bearing steps here rest on **activity proxies rather than growth outcomes**: the grip discussion leans on EMG and T2 imaging, and the anatomical argument about the long head fighting shoulder flexion is mechanism, not measurement. He labels these as such. The regional-growth recommendation (overhead plus pushdown for uniform development) rests on a 9-subject, 6-week study whose own confound he identified — treat it as a hypothesis, not a prescription. The "tended to" phrasing on Costa signals a non-significant difference.
