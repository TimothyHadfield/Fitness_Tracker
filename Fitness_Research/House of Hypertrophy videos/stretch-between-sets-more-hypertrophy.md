# Inter-Set Stretching: Two New Studies Undo the Original Positive Finding

**Source:** House of Hypertrophy, "Stretch Between Sets for More Hypertrophy? (New Research)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9dlwvYq26NA)) · 2021-10-17 · 10 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- **This is an explicit self-correction.** Thirteen months earlier he made a video on the Evangelista trial finding inter-set stretching enhanced hypertrophy. Two subsequent studies found nothing, and he now says the "ever so slight majority" of evidence points against it.
- **Nakamura**: 16 untrained men, within-subject (one leg stretched, one not), flywheel squat, 5 weeks. **No difference at any of the twelve measured muscle regions.**
- **Wadhi**: 26 trained men (~3 years experience), loaded chest stretch on a cable at ~15% of working load for 30 s between bench sets, 8 weeks. **No difference at either pectoralis major region.**
- **Strength is not harmed** by stretching between sets in any of the three studies, despite static stretching acutely reducing force output. In Nakamura, isometric torque actually favoured the stretching leg.
- The three studies are **too heterogeneous to pool** — trained vs untrained, loaded vs unloaded, 30 s vs 60 s, flywheel vs conventional — so his conclusion is "not supported," not "refuted."

## What the two new studies did

**Nakamura et al.** used a within-subject design, which is the strongest feature here: 16 untrained men had one leg assigned to stretching and the other to normal rest, so between-subject variation is eliminated. They trained bilateral squats on an **inertial flywheel** (which overloads the eccentric phase) for 3 sets of 10 with 3 minutes rest, twice weekly for 5 weeks. The stretching leg did 30 s of quad stretch, 30 s rest, another 30 s stretch, then 90 s of remaining rest. Thickness was measured at upper and lower regions of vastus lateralis, vastus medialis, rectus femoris and both regions of vastus intermedius, plus hamstrings and glute max. **Every region: statistically similar.**

**Wadhi et al.** ran 26 men averaging 3 years of training, twice weekly for 8 weeks on flat and incline bench, progressing 3 sets to 5 sets per exercise. Day one was 4-6 reps at ~2 RIR, day two 8-10 reps at ~2 RIR, with the final incline set to failure both days. Both groups rested 2 minutes; the stretching group spent the first 30 s in a **loaded cable chest stretch at ~15% of the load just used**, instructed to produce no active tension and let the load stretch the pec. Pec thickness at the belly and a lateral portion: **similar between groups.**

## Why the studies might disagree

He does not simply tally the score. Three candidate explanations, each stated with its own weakness:

**Stretch duration.** Evangelista used a single 30-second bout; Nakamura used **two 30-second bouts (60 s total)**. Longer static stretching produces larger acute force decrements — [Siatras](https://journals.lww.com/nsca-jscr/fulltext/2008/01000/The_Duration_of_the_Inhibitory_Effects_with_Static.8.aspx) found 60 s of quad stretching reduced force output more than 30 s. If the stretch degrades the subsequent set, the stimulus gets worse, not better. But he immediately notes the problem with his own hypothesis: **Wadhi used 30 seconds and still found nothing.**

**Exercise modality.** Nakamura used a flywheel with eccentric overload; Evangelista used conventional free weights and machines like the leg extension. Whether that could explain the conflict is left open.

**Population and loading.** Wadhi is the only trained-subject study and the only loaded-stretch study, so it is not directly comparable to either of the others.

## The measurement argument

The most interesting speculative section: **all three studies measured muscle thickness only** — a single dimension. He asks what would have happened with cross-sectional area, volume, or **fascicle length**. His reasoning is that increased fascicle length likely reflects longer muscle fibres, stretching stimuli can induce fibre lengthening in animal work, and some (non-unanimous) human evidence shows intense static stretching protocols increase fascicle length. So inter-set stretching might be a tool for a growth outcome nobody has measured in this context.

**This is speculation and he labels it as such**, but it is worth flagging as the one place in the video where a mechanism argument runs ahead of outcome data. Nothing in the three studies supports it; it is a hypothesis for future work.

## Strength was not compromised

The intuition — static stretching lowers force, therefore stretching between sets should blunt strength adaptations — does not hold up:

- **Evangelista**: bench press and leg extension 1RM increased similarly with 30 s pain-free stretching vs passive rest.
- **Nakamura**: leg press 1RM and peak concentric and eccentric torque similar; **isometric torque increases were greater in the stretching leg.**
- **Wadhi**: bench 1RM increases similar.

He is careful to bound this: no detriment "at least within the confines of the protocols used."

## How this fits the library

Our library records a creator who called inter-set stretching bro science and then **publicly reversed in 2019** on the strength of the Evangelista trial. This video is the other half of that story from a different channel: House of Hypertrophy made his own positive video on Evangelista in September 2020, and by October 2021 the two follow-up studies had pulled the ground out from under it. Anyone holding the 2019 reversal as settled should note that **the single study that produced it is now the minority result**. He closes by flagging another weighted-stretch study due by end of 2021 and promising an update — which he delivers in March 2023, and which goes further against the method.
