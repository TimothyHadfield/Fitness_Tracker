# Drop Sets Look Good on Isolation Work and Not on Compounds

**Source:** House of Hypertrophy, "The Science of Drop Sets (Vs Normal Sets) For Hypertrophy" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Efj3kfWCHNw)) · 2021-09-09 · 12 min
**Topic:** Effort & failure

## Bottom line
- Across the **five** studies that existed at the time, drop sets split cleanly by exercise type: **promising with isolation exercises, no advantage with compounds**. He states outright that five studies is not much and that future work could overturn this.
- The new study (Varović et al.) used a **within-subject leg extension design in 16 men with under a year of training**, 3×/week for 8 weeks, 2–5 sets or drop sets per session. Vastus lateralis growth was **similar at all regions**; rectus femoris growth was **greater for drop sets at 30% and 50% of muscle length** but similar at 70%. So the win was regional, not global.
- [Fink 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28474868/) is the strongest result: a **single** triceps pushdown drop set (12RM to failure, then three consecutive 20% load reductions) twice weekly for 6 weeks beat **three** normal sets on triceps CSA in 16 men.
- [Ozaki 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28532248/) found a single dumbbell curl drop set (80% 1RM dropping through 65/50/40/30%) produced **similar** elbow flexor growth to three normal sets in nine untrained men — equal growth from far less work.
- Both compound studies were null. [Angleri 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28130627/) (16 men, 4+ years trained, unilateral leg press and extension, 12 weeks) and [Enes 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34260860/) (18 men, 2+ years trained, squat/leg press/leg extension, 8 weeks) both found **no difference** in thigh growth.

## The isolation evidence
The three isolation results are consistent, and two of them are notable for what the drop-set condition did *not* do. Fink's drop-set group performed a **single** drop set per session against three full sets and still grew more triceps; Ozaki's performed a single drop set with four load reductions and matched three sets. His reading is that both trials probably **understated** drop sets, because more sets per session generally means more growth up to a point — had those groups done three drop sets, the gap might have widened. That is a fair inference, but it is an inference: no study has tested multiple isolation drop sets against volume-matched normal sets.

The Varović study is the only one with multiple drop sets per session on an isolation exercise, and its advantage appeared only at two of six measured sites. He reads this as drop sets growing **more regions** of the quadriceps rather than growing the quadriceps more, which is the correct reading of the data.

## Why compounds may differ
Two mechanisms, both offered explicitly as speculation:

**Technique breakdown.** Compound lifts need coordination across multiple joints. Drop sets taken to failure with several load reductions are extremely fatiguing and may degrade execution, more so in less experienced lifters.

**Central fatigue.** Force depends on descending drive; central fatigue reduces that drive and therefore recruitment and force. He argues drop sets prolong contraction duration (more central fatigue) and compounds involve more active muscle mass (also more central fatigue, per [Rossman 2014](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4159732/) and [Weavil 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7430763/)). Combined, drop sets on compounds could generate enough central fatigue to blunt the stimulus.

He then undercuts his own mechanism in one sentence: *"the notion that compound exercises generate more central fatigue is not completely supported by the research itself."* This is characteristic of the channel and worth noting — the hypothesis is presented and then explicitly labelled as a hypothesis.

## Caveats worth carrying forward
- Enes' drop sets were **not to failure** — a prescribed 10 reps at 70% 1RM then 6 reps at 55%, one drop only. He flags this as a weaker drop-set stimulus than typical, which weakens the null result for compounds.
- Angleri measured vastus lateralis at a **single site (50% of thigh length)** on a large muscle. Given that Varović's drop-set advantage appeared only at specific regions, a single measurement site could plausibly have missed a regional effect.
- Sample sizes are 9–18 throughout. Training status ranges from untrained to 4+ years, so the isolation/compound split is partly confounded with training status: **both compound studies used the most trained subjects.** He does not address this, and it is an alternative explanation for the pattern he attributes to exercise type.

**Flagged:** the framing "isolation good, compound bad" rests on two nulls against three positives, with training status and drop-set intensity varying alongside exercise type. He is appropriately tentative in the conclusion, but the practical takeaway is stronger than five small trials can carry.
