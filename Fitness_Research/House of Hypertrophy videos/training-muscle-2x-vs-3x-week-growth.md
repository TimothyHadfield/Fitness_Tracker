# 2× vs 3× a Week: The Study Can't Separate Frequency From Volume

**Source:** House of Hypertrophy, "Training a Muscle 2x vs 3x a Week for Growth (New Study)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7FWwOeVn71Q)) · 2023-10-09 · 9 min
**Topic:** Volume & frequency

## Bottom line
- **21 previously untrained** subjects, split by muscle fibre type, trained one side of the body **2×/week (6–8 weekly sets per muscle)** and the other side **3×/week (9–12 weekly sets)** for the same exercises. Quads, hamstrings, biceps and triceps all **tended to grow more with 3×/week**, in both fibre-type groups.
- **The design changed frequency and volume together**, so it cannot tell you which caused the difference. Dhim says this plainly and spends the second half of the video on it.
- His verdict: the **higher set count was likely the main driver for most subjects**, with frequency possibly contributing for some. Meta-analytic data finds no frequency effect once weekly sets are equated, but does find ≥9 weekly sets beats <9.
- The effect was small: an extra **1–3% growth** for quads, hamstrings and biceps; **4–5%** for triceps. A large bulk of the available gains was already captured by the lower-volume, lower-frequency condition.
- The fibre-type hypothesis failed. Fast-twitch subjects did **not** benefit from training less — they grew better on 3×/week too.

## The design and what it found
[01:02] Fibre type was measured by biopsy in lower-body muscles (the authors note lower-body fibre type can correlate with upper-body). All subjects trained the unilateral leg extension, leg curl, dumbbell curl and skull crusher for **3–4 sets of reps to failure**. One side: twice weekly, ≥72 hours apart. Other side: three times weekly, ≥48 hours apart.

Individual responses show the large majority of subjects in both fibre-type groups grew better on the 3× side, with only a few cases favouring 2×.

[02:02] Two secondary findings he flags. The **slow-twitch group grew as well as the fast-twitch group** on average — fibre type is one input among many, so more fast-twitch fibres doesn't translate into more growth at the whole-person level. And the slow-twitch group performed **more reps** than the fast-twitch group, consistent with fatigue resistance, though he notes previous research hasn't consistently found strong fibre-type/rep-count correlations.

## The confounds he raises
[04:05] The "slow-twitch" group wasn't strongly slow-twitch dominant — some subjects were near a **50/50 split** — while the fast-twitch group averaged **61/39**. A cleaner contrast might give a different answer.

Soreness and fatigue were **similar between fibre-type groups**, so 9–12 weekly sets at 3×/week wasn't excessive for the fast-twitch subjects. But he doesn't over-read that: at meaningfully higher set counts or frequencies, the fast-twitch group might have struggled to recover.

He also notes the subjects were untrained, so the benefit of more training may grow with experience — while adding that various well-trained individuals still see reasonable growth from lower amounts.

## Frequency or volume?
[05:05] **Schoenfeld, Grgic and Krieger's 2019 frequency meta-analysis**: when total weekly sets are equated, there is **no difference** in growth between higher and lower frequencies. Graphing the frequencies does show 3×/week slightly ahead, but not significantly, and he cautions that the underlying studies differ notably. Substantial average hypertrophy is achieved across a wide range of frequencies.

[06:07] **Neves and colleagues (2022)** is the more informative design: trained men performed **nine quad sets per week**, one leg doing all nine in a single session and the other spreading them across three non-consecutive days. Some subjects grew similarly; others grew more with the 3× distribution. So frequency itself may matter for some people.

[06:37] **Schoenfeld, Ogborn and Krieger's 2017 volume meta-analysis**: **nine or more weekly sets per muscle produced more growth than fewer than nine.** Since the 3× condition crossed that line and the 2× condition didn't, this is the most parsimonious explanation for most of the difference.

## Bearing on our position
Our library flags 2× versus 3× weekly frequency as genuinely unresolved, and this video does not resolve it — it reinforces the flag. The trial that looks like a frequency study is a volume study wearing a frequency label, and the only equated-volume evidence still says frequency doesn't matter on average while allowing that it may for some individuals.

**Overreach flag:** none — the title promises a frequency answer and the video spends most of its length explaining why the study cannot give one.
