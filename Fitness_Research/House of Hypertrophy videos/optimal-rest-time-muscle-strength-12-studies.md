# Rest Intervals: 2.5-3 Minutes on Big Movements, Short Rests Fine on Isolation

**Source:** House of Hypertrophy, "The OPTIMAL Rest Time for Muscle & Strength (12 Studies)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lENxKKjaG6Q)) · 2022-10-03 · 11 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- **The optimal rest depends on how much muscle mass the exercise recruits**, not on whether you're training for size or strength. That is the organising idea of the video and it is unusual.
- **Large-muscle-mass exercises** (compounds, plus lower-body isolation): four studies agree that **2.5-3 minutes** beats 1 minute or less for hypertrophy. Whether 5 minutes beats 3 is unknown — no clean study exists.
- **Small-muscle-mass exercises** (upper-body isolation): three Japanese studies suggest short rests are fine and *possibly* better. He emphasises "potentially" twice.
- **For strength**, 3 minutes beats 1.5 minutes and 30 seconds; 3 and 5 minutes are statistically equivalent, with leg press hinting at 5.
- The **1-minute-rest penalty is recoverable by adding sets**: 4.5 sets at 1 min matched 3 sets at 3 min for quad growth, at roughly the same total session time.
- **Anabolic hormone spikes are decisively decoupled from growth here** — the 1-minute condition produced *higher* acute testosterone and *lower* myofibrillar protein synthesis.

## The framing, and where the practice came from

A 1987 US survey ([Kraemer](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3667019/)) reported bodybuilders resting **10-90 seconds** and powerlifters **120-420 seconds**. Arnold's book recommended a minute or less. The video asks whether that division — short rest for size, long rest for strength — survives testing. It does not: the size-versus-strength axis turns out to be the wrong one.

## Large muscle mass: 2.5-3 minutes wins

Four studies, consistent:

- **Trained men, compounds plus a lower-body isolation exercise**: hypertrophy superior at 3 min vs 1 min ([Schoenfeld 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26605807/)).
- **Untrained men, leg press**: quad gains superior at 3 min vs 1 min ([Longo 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35622106/)).
- **Untrained men, large-muscle-mass programme**: arm and thigh gains greater at 2.5 min vs 1 min ([Buresh 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19077743/)).
- **Untrained men, bench and squat**: triceps similar at 2.5 min vs 30 s, thigh gains tending to favour 2.5 min ([Fink 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27984843/)).

He calls this "solid evidence," and given four studies pointing the same way across training statuses, that is a fair label rather than an overstatement.

**Is 5 minutes better than 3?** No direct study exists. The nearest is a Finnish trial finding similar quad gains at 2 vs 5 minutes ([Ahtiainen 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15507691/)) — but the 2-minute condition performed **one extra set per exercise**, and he flags that the study was unsupervised and relied on training diaries, so he declines to use it in either direction. His recommendation is 2.5-3 minutes, with 5 as an optional experiment for people with time.

## The hormone result

An English study had trained men do leg press and leg extension and measured myofibrillar protein synthesis rather than long-term growth ([McKendry 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27126459/)). MyoPS was **higher with 5 minutes than 1 minute**, most clearly in the immediate post-training hours — while **acute testosterone was higher in the 1-minute condition**. Since short rests are usually defended on hormonal grounds, this is a direct refutation on the defenders' own terms: the condition with the bigger hormone spike built less contractile protein.

## Small muscle mass: short rests are viable

Three Japanese studies, all with confounders he names:

- Biceps and triceps isolation, **20RM loads to failure with 30 s rest** vs **8RM loads with 3 min rest**, sets matched: arm gains **greater in the 30-second group** ([Fink 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28032435/)). He notes the load difference is probably not an issue since 6-35 reps to failure grow muscle similarly.
- Dumbbell curl, three conditions — 80% 1RM with 3 min, 30% 1RM with 90 s, and a five-load drop set with no rest: **elbow flexor gains similar across all three** ([Ozaki 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28532248/)). The drop-set condition effectively did two more sets.
- Triceps pushdown, normal sets with 90 s rest vs drop sets with no rest: gains **tended to favour the drop sets** ([Fink 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28474868/)). Again one extra set for the drop-set condition.

He concludes only that short rests are "viable, if not potentially superior," and repeats the hedge. Given that two of the three studies gave the short-rest condition extra sets, that hedging is appropriate — the drop-set findings could just be a volume effect.

## Sets can buy back what short rest costs

The Longo study had a third arm he saves for this point: 1-minute rest with sets added until volume load matched the 3-minute condition. That worked out to an average of **4.5 sets per session against the 3-minute group's 3 sets**, and quad growth was **similar**. So short rest does not forbid growth; it lowers the yield per set. Total session time comes out roughly equal, so the choice is about preference, fatigue, and whether you want the conditioning stimulus.

## Strength

- 3 min beat 1 min for bench and squat ([Schoenfeld 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26605807/)).
- 3 min tended to beat 1.5 min and 30 s for squat ([Robinson 1995](https://journals.lww.com/nsca-jscr/abstract/1995/11000/effects_of_different_weight_training_exercise_rest.2.aspx)).
- 3 and 5 min were **not significantly different** for leg press and bench, both beating 1 min ([de Salles 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19811949/)). Leg press numerically favoured 5 min; he explicitly refuses to read a non-significant single measure as a finding.

**Practical note he adds:** if 2.5-3 minutes is unaffordable, superset unrelated muscle groups — you shorten the session while still resting long between sets of the same exercise.

**Where the evidence is thin:** the isolation-exercise recommendation rests on three studies from one country, two with unequal set counts, and it is the part of the video most likely to move.
