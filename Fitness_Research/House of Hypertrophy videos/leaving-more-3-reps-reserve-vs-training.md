# Can More Than 3 Reps in Reserve Still Build Muscle Optimally? (Four Studies, No Answer)

**Source:** House of Hypertrophy, "Leaving More Than 3 Reps in Reserve vs Training to Failure (Research Overview)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nP1-3Wb1YlA)) · 2021-12-06 · 16 min
**Topic:** Effort & failure

## Bottom line
- The companion video established that **3–0 RIR ≈ failure** for hypertrophy. This one asks whether **4, 5, 6 or more RIR** can also be optimal. By his count only **four** longitudinal studies bear on it, and they disagree.
- Two say no ([Martorelli 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28713535/), [Karsten 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31365457/)), one says yes-if-you-add-sets ([Lasevicius 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31895290/)), one says yes-in-a-confounded-way ([Carroll 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31373325/)).
- His verdict is **agnostic, not permissive**: "we unfortunately cannot conclude if leaving more than three repetitions in reserve can optimize muscle hypertrophy," so err toward **≤3 RIR**.
- Notably, he **retracts** a claim from his own earlier video. He had said velocity-based research suggested >3 RIR might be fine; after planning a 6,000-word video on it he concluded the RIR figures in those studies required so much extrapolation that they were unreliable, and dropped the whole thing. That is a rare and creditable public reversal.
- The recurring pattern in the data that *does* favour higher RIR: **extra sets compensate**. Where 5 RIR matched failure, the 5-RIR condition performed 2–4 more sets.

## Study 1 — Martorelli: extra sets did not rescue it
89 previously untrained women, barbell biceps curl, 2×/week for 10 weeks, three groups, 2 min rest, 1RM retested at week 5.

- **Failure:** 3 sets to failure at 70% 1RM.
- **Non-failure, set-equated:** 3 sets of 7 at 70% 1RM. Since subjects could manage 12–13 reps at that load, set one sat at roughly 5–6 RIR, dropping to ~4–2 RIR by sets two and three as fatigue accumulated.
- **Non-failure plus one set:** 4 sets of 7 at 70% 1RM, same ~6–2 RIR range.

Elbow flexor thickness increases appeared greatest in the failure group — **regardless of whether the non-failure group did an extra set**. This is the cleanest evidence against >3 RIR in the video.

## Study 2 — Lasevicius: 5 RIR worked, 14 RIR did not
25 previously untrained men, unilateral leg extension, 2×/week for 8 weeks, 2 min rest, 1RM retested at week 4. Four legs across two load conditions:

- **High-load failure (80% 1RM):** 3 sets to failure, averaging 12 reps/set.
- **High-load non-failure:** 60% of the failure leg's average reps (~7), with extra sets added to equate volume load — ending at 5–6 sets of 7, so ~**5 RIR** with 2–3 extra sets.
- **Low-load failure (30% 1RM):** 3 sets to failure, averaging 34 reps/set.
- **Low-load non-failure:** 5–6 sets of 20, so ~**14 RIR** with 2–3 extra sets.

Quadriceps CSA increases were similar for every leg **except** the low-load non-failure leg, which grew least. Two readings: 14 RIR cannot be rescued even by 2–3 extra sets; but 5 RIR **can** be, matching three sets to failure when done for five to six sets.

He draws the honest inference: on a **set-equated** basis, failure probably beats 5 RIR (as Martorelli suggests) — the extra sets are what close the gap.

## Study 3 — Karsten: the conflict
18 men with 2–5 years' training, a range of exercises, 2×/week for 6 weeks.

- **Failure:** 4 sets of 10 to failure, **2 min** rest. If a set fell short of 10 reps, subjects got a 30 s break to finish it.
- **Non-failure:** 8 sets of 5 at roughly **5 RIR**, load adjusted each set to hold that, **1 min** rest.

Vastus medialis and elbow flexor thickness increases tended to favour failure; anterior deltoid was similar. So despite **four extra sets**, 5 RIR underperformed — directly contradicting Lasevicius.

He offers three explanations rather than picking one:
1. **Training status.** Lasevicius used untrained men, Karsten trained men. Extra sets at 5 RIR may only compensate in novices.
2. **The 30 s rest-pause.** The failure group's 30 s top-up is effectively rest-pause training, and the researchers never reported how often it happened.
3. **Unequal rest intervals.** 2 min vs 1 min is itself a confounder — longer rest is likely better for hypertrophy, at least on compounds. The non-failure group's worse result may be a rest-interval finding, not an RIR finding.

Explanation 3 is the strongest and he treats it as such. It is a good example of him refusing to bank a result that supports the tidier conclusion.

## Study 4 — Carroll: >3 RIR won, with an asterisk
15 men averaging 7.7 years' training, 3×/week for 10 weeks, an intricate periodised plan, same exercises and roughly the same sets and reps for both groups.

- **Repetition maximum group:** used RM loads (e.g. 3×8–12 with a 10RM load), reaching failure on the last set of each exercise, likely ≤3 RIR elsewhere.
- **Relative intensity group:** used 70–90% of those RM loads — so ~2–6 RIR on two training days per week and ~4–14 RIR on the third.

Vastus lateralis anatomical CSA gains **favoured the relative intensity group**.

The confounder he raises: **both groups also did sprint training twice weekly**. Training to failure is more fatiguing, so the added sprints may have eaten into the RM group's recovery. If that is the mechanism, the practical reading is narrow — for lifters also doing sprints or frequent high-intensity conditioning, backing off from failure may help manage fatigue. He wants a cleaner study (3 RIR vs 5 RIR vs failure, all with added cardio) before believing it.

## What to do with this
Nothing changes. Four studies, three designs pulling in different directions, one of them confounded by rest intervals and one by concurrent sprint training. His closing line is that it is "probably best to err on the side of caution and aim to leave three or fewer repetitions in reserve."

**Overreach flag — in the other direction.** If anything he under-claims: he treats Carroll as evidence for higher RIR despite it being the most confounded of the four, and he treats Martorelli's untrained-women result as informative despite thickness measurements on a single small muscle group.

## Where this pair lands
Read together with the [failure video](training-failure-must-hypertrophy.md), House of Hypertrophy's 2021 position is: **1–3 RIR is the working range**, failure is optional rather than required, and anything beyond 3 RIR is unproven rather than disproven. He does **not** endorse the view that failure has near-zero independent stimulatory effect, and he does **not** accept that 4–6 RIR matches 0 RIR once reps are equated — Lasevicius is the only study here that could support that, it used untrained subjects, it needed 2–3 extra sets to get there, and Karsten found the opposite in trained men. He is also explicit that the two-study conflict is unresolved, which is the correct handling of the evidence rather than a hedge.
