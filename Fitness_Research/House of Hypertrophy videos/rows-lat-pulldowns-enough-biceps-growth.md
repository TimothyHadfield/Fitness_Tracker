# Rows and Lat Pulldowns Are Not Enough for Biceps Growth

**Source:** House of Hypertrophy, "Are Rows and Lat Pulldowns Are Enough For Biceps Growth?" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WBo7MzU6U-w)) · 2021-06-28 · 11 min
**Topic:** Exercise selection

## Bottom line
- **Rows are clearly inferior to curls** for elbow flexor growth: in a within-subject study, the curl arm gained roughly 11% thickness versus roughly 5% for the row arm.
- **Lat pulldowns matched barbell curls** for elbow flexor growth — but only at a single measurement site 10 cm from the cubital fossa, which is the whole weakness of that finding.
- Muscles do not grow uniformly, so a one-site measurement can miss real regional differences between two exercises.
- A varied-exercise study found rotating pulldown and curl variations across the week beat doing the same front pulldown and barbell curl every session, with the advantage concentrated at 50% and 60% of upper arm length.
- Conclusion is **indirect**: if variety matters for regional growth, a back day of rows and pulldowns alone is unlikely to be sufficient biceps training. No study tested that comparison directly.

## Lat pulldowns versus curls
[01:00] **Gentil et al.** assigned 29 untrained men to train either the lat pulldown or the barbell biceps curl, twice per week for 10 weeks, 3 sets of 8–12 reps to failure per session. Elbow flexor thickness (biceps plus brachialis) was measured on the right arm, **10 cm from the cubital fossa**.

Increases were similar between groups — roughly **6%** each. On its face, a compound matched an isolation exercise for biceps growth. The measurement limitation is flagged immediately and returned to later.

## Rows versus curls
[02:31] **Mannarino et al.** used a stronger design: 10 untrained men, one arm assigned to dumbbell rows and the other to dumbbell curls, twice per week for 8 weeks, 4–6 sets of 8–12 to failure. Elbow flexor thickness was measured at **25%, 50% and 75% of biceps length** and averaged.

The curl arm gained significantly more — roughly **11% versus 5%**. He adds a fair point in the study's disfavour: both exercises were performed with a supinated grip, and since supination is a biceps function, that should if anything have *helped* the row arm. Rows still lost.

## Why he refuses to pool the two studies
[03:33] The obvious cross-study reading is that dumbbell curls (11%) beat lat pulldowns, rows and barbell curls (5–6%) alike. He rejects this, and the reasoning is the most useful part of the video:

- **Modality almost certainly is not the explanation.** Machines produce similar growth to free weights, push-ups similar growth to bench press, and dumbbell and barbell curls involve near-identical elbow motion at matched proximity to failure. There is nothing special about a dumbbell.
- **Volume differed.** Mannarino used 4–6 sets per session versus Gentil's 3, and more sets produce more growth up to a point (Schoenfeld et al. dose-response).
- **Measurement differed.** One site versus three averaged sites.
- **Subjects differed, and that matters enormously.** Hubal et al. trained 243 men and 342 women with preacher and concentration curls for 12 weeks and found a very wide spread of individual change in biceps cross-sectional area. With samples of 10–30, two studies drawing subjects with comparable trainability is unlikely.

He also notes that because modality is unlikely to matter, the Mannarino result for **dumbbell** rows probably generalises to barbell rowing variations.

## The regional growth argument
[09:04] Even taking the pulldown result at face value, it rests on one measurement site, and the research indicates muscles do not grow uniformly (Zabaleta-Korta et al.). Growth at 10 cm from the cubital fossa says nothing about the rest of the muscle, and the two exercises could easily diverge elsewhere.

[09:36] **Costa et al.** provides the positive case for variety. 22 trained men, three sessions per week for nine weeks, 3 sets of 8–12 per exercise in both groups:
- **Non-varied:** front lat pulldown and barbell biceps curl, every session.
- **Varied:** day one front lat pulldown and barbell curl; day two behind-the-neck lat pulldown and preacher curl; day three narrow-grip lat pulldown and incline dumbbell curl.

Elbow flexor thickness was measured at **50%, 60% and 70% of upper arm length**. Overall increases were greater for the varied group, **particularly at 50% and 60%**.

He is careful about what this does and does not show: it is not clear whether the benefit came from varying the pulldowns, varying the curls, or both. What it establishes is that a fair amount of exercise variety helps regional growth — from which he infers, and explicitly calls indirect, that rows and pulldowns alone are not sufficient for overall biceps development.

**Overreach flag:** the conclusion is one inferential step removed from the data. Costa's groups *both* did curls; no group trained biceps with pulling compounds only. The claim "rows and pulldowns are not enough" is a reasonable reading of the regional-growth literature, not a tested result — and he says as much rather than hiding it.

**Small inconsistency to note:** he gives Costa et al. as **nine** weeks here and as **eight** weeks in his isolation-exercises video. One of the two is a slip; the study design described is otherwise identical.
