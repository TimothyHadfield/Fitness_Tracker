# Four Reasons EMG Can't Rank Exercises for Growth

**Source:** House of Hypertrophy, "Ryan Humiston Has Made a MISTAKE (EMG)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=US_xgPuxvDk)) · 2022-11-07 · 8 min
**Topic:** Evidence & method

## Bottom line
- EMG does not measure muscle activation. It measures **neuromuscular excitation** — the electrical signal arriving at the muscle — and activation happens several steps downstream, with fatigue, dynamic contractions and fluctuating neural input breaking the correspondence.
- It doesn't reliably measure excitation either. Electrically stimulating a muscle at **constant** intensity across three joint angles produced **different EMG amplitudes**, so joint angle alone changes the reading.
- EMG reads **higher with heavier loads** even when both go to failure — yet heavy and light loads taken to or near failure produce similar growth. So an exercise can read higher purely for being technically heavier.
- EMG is blind to **passive tension**, which appears to drive growth — a fatal gap when comparing exercises that load a muscle at different lengths.
- His verdict: conclusions drawn from EMG about which exercise builds most muscle have "a high probability of just being plain wrong or at least misleading."

## The four failures
**1. Wrong quantity.** Activation means the force-generating machinery inside fibres is in motion. Skin electrodes can't record that; they record the electrical signal preceding it.

**2. Unreliable even at that.** A 2017 Italian study electrically stimulated the tibialis anterior at constant intensity at three foot angles. Excitation was identical by design, but EMG amplitude differed across angles. Recruitment strategy compounds this: a muscle recruiting deep-to-superficial gives a different surface signal than one recruiting superficial-to-deep at the same excitation. (The biceps is estimated to hold roughly **172,000–418,000 fibres**.) So two exercises training a muscle at even subtly different angles can produce EMG differences that mean nothing.

**3. Load confound.** Heavier loading raises EMG whether or not it raises growth. His worked example: Humiston found dips produced higher triceps EMG than close-grip push-ups — plausibly just because dips are heavier. Run long-term with both to failure, triceps gains would likely be similar, as heavy-vs-light comparisons generally are.

**4. Passive tension is invisible to it.** Muscles generate passive force when stretched; EMG records only electrical activity. He points to a German study where intense static calf stretching produced gastrocnemius growth comparable in magnitude to what calf raise studies achieve, and to the stretch-position literature, where passive force at long lengths appears to augment active force. Second worked example: Humiston found incline curls hit the biceps long head poorly and the brachialis more — but incline curls train the long head **stretched**, exactly where EMG under-reads.

## Where EMG is still usable
He does not throw it out. Three acceptable contexts: comparing exercises with **similar relative loading and joint angles** (weighted push-ups vs bench press at matched range of motion); comparing before and after a program **within the same person**; and comparing the same exercise **across individuals**, with caveats. Otherwise, long-term growth studies remain the highest-quality data.

## Flag
The calf-stretching comparison is the weakest link: it sets a stretching protocol against a *different* body of calf-raise studies with different subjects and methods, and its time cost goes unmentioned. It establishes that passive tension matters, which is fair, but "similar magnitudes" outruns a cross-study comparison.

Worth recording against the library: this is his hardest anti-EMG position. By the 2024 chest guide he had **softened it explicitly**, arguing EMG can be informative for subtle comparisons (grip widths, bench angles, biomechanically similar presses) while staying unreliable across exercises differing in muscle length — and his upper-chest content leans on EMG counts throughout. The positions are compatible, but the change is real and he flags it himself.
