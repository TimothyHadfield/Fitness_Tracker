# What the Squat Misses

**Source:** House of Hypertrophy, "Is the Squat ENOUGH for Leg Growth?! (Research Overview)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=19Ts8Nvs6qE)) · 2020-08-31 · 8 min
**Topic:** Exercise selection

## Bottom line
- Squats build a lot of leg, but three muscle groups are systematically under-served: **rectus femoris, hamstrings, and the calves** (which get nothing at all).
- Fonseca: groups that squatted only failed to produce statistically significant growth in the rectus femoris; groups doing varied leg exercises grew all four quadriceps heads.
- Kubo: **neither** 90° nor 140° knee flexion squats grew the rectus femoris or hamstrings significantly. Depth doesn't rescue them — the structural problem does not depend on how deep you go.
- Depth does matter for glutes and adductors: 140° produced substantial glute max and adductor growth, 90° produced minimal.
- Squats also miss gluteus medius/minimus (hip abduction) and most of the adductor group other than adductor magnus.

## Quads
Fonseca and colleagues split 49 untrained men into five groups (one control, four training), training twice weekly for 12 weeks:

1. Back squat only, ~8 reps per set
2. Varied exercises — squat and leg press (wks 1–4), squat and deadlift (5–8), squat, deadlift and lunge (9–12), all ~8 reps
3. Squat only, varied rep ranges
4. Varied exercises, varied rep ranges

Cross-sectional area was measured for all four quadriceps heads. The varied-exercise groups (2 and 4) grew all four significantly. Group 1 failed to significantly grow **vastus medialis and rectus femoris**; group 3 failed on **rectus femoris**.

He flags the design limitation himself: the squat was a Smith machine squat to only 90° of knee flexion. He argues this probably doesn't matter much, pointing to Kubo showing similar quadriceps growth between 90° and 140° squats.

Kubo's 17 untrained men (2×/week, 10 weeks, 90° vs 140°) found **neither depth** significantly grew the rectus femoris. The anatomical reason is clean: rectus femoris crosses both hip and knee, and during a squat it stays at roughly constant length — knee extension shortens it while hip extension lengthens it — so it barely contracts. Leg extensions and reverse Nordic curls are documented as good rectus femoris builders.

## Hamstrings
Same problem, same mechanism. The hamstrings (bar the biceps femoris short head) cross both joints, extending the hip while flexing the knee, and hold near-constant length through a squat.

Kubo found no significant hamstring growth at either depth. Bloomquist measured hamstring cross-sectional area at multiple regions with squats to 60° vs 120° and found **minimal increases in some regions and decreases in others**. Dedicated hamstring work is not optional.

## Glutes and adductors
This is where depth pays. In Kubo, 90° produced minimal glute max growth while 140° produced substantial growth. Adductor volume followed the same pattern — likely the adductor magnus, which modelling suggests contributes meaningfully to hip extension in the squat, especially deep.

Even so: gluteus medius and minimus are hip abductors and need abduction work such as cable hip abductions; and the adductors beyond adductor magnus are hip adductors, needing something like cable hip adduction.

## Flag
His argument for wide-stance squats favouring the glutes rests on **EMG** showing high gluteal electrical activity — exactly the kind of inference he demolishes at length two years later in his Ryan Humiston EMG video. It is a single sentence here and it is the weakest claim in the video.

Note also the tension with his later squats-vs-hip-thrusts note: there he stresses that no study has directly compared parallel to deeper squats for glute growth, which makes the depth story here (90° vs 140°) the whole evidence base rather than one data point among several.
