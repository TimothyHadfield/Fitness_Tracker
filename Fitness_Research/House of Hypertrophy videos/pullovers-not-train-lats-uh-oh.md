# Do Pullovers Train the Lats? Machine > Cable > Free Weight, on Indirect Evidence

**Source:** House of Hypertrophy, "Pullovers DO NOT Train Lats? (Uh Oh)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pC54mxsg8LQ)) · 2023-12-04 · 6 min
**Topic:** Exercise selection

A clip lifted from his full Ultimate Guide to lat development ([source video](https://youtu.be/veoIr2CUEAY)).

## Bottom line
- The anti-pullover case is real: isometric studies show lat activity is **highest at low shoulder elevation angles and falls progressively as the arm goes overhead** — and free-weight pullovers are hardest precisely in the overhead position. sEMG studies find the **middle and lower pec major** out-activate the lats there.
- He does **not** accept the conclusion. Low activity plus a deep stretch may still be a good hypertrophy stimulus, and he brings an outcome-level analogy for that: deep squats stress the glute max in a lengthened, low-leverage, low-EMG position and still grow it well.
- Ranking he lands on: **machine pullover > cable pullover > free-weight pullover** for the lats, because a machine keeps tension high both at the lengthened position *and* at the lower elevation angles where lat activity is greatest.
- Regional guess: free-weight pullovers may slightly bias the **lower lat** (better leverage overhead); cable pullovers cover the upper region well and are hardest mid-range, where he argues the **middle and lower** regions have most leverage.
- **Every step of this is EMG, moment arms and analogy.** No study has measured lat hypertrophy from any pullover variation.

## The case against free-weight pullovers
Two papers had subjects perform maximal isometric shoulder extension at a range of shoulder elevation angles. Both show lat activity peaking at low elevation and declining as the arm elevates — which is mechanically expected, since lat leverage for shoulder extension deteriorates toward the overhead position. He notes the isometric design makes the sEMG here more reliable than it would be in a dynamic lift.

Since a dumbbell or barbell pullover is most challenging with the arms overhead, the exercise loads the lats at their weakest point. A few sEMG studies report middle and lower pec major showing relatively greater activity than the lats during free-weight pullovers.

## Why he still won't write them off
Lat leverage and activity at that position are **limited, not zero**, and the lat is well stretched there. His supporting case is the glute: back squats are hardest deep, where the glute max is stretched, has poor leverage and shows lower sEMG — yet a Japanese trial found deeper squats grew the glute max more effectively than 90-degree-knee-flexion squats, and another recent paper found squats to roughly parallel produced similar upper, middle and lower glute max growth to hip thrusts. Stretch plus limited leverage can still be a fine stimulus.

## Cables and machines
A cable pullover reaches a comparable overhead stretch but adds active range at lower elevation angles, where lat activity is higher — so total lat electrical activity should be greater. He supports this with a 2022 Spanish sEMG analysis comparing barbell pullovers to cable pulldowns (greater lat activity with the cable movement), while conceding the pulldown is not the same exercise, only similar in shoulder motion and resistance profile.

The machine version, if it maintains resistance through the range, gets both: challenging at long lengths *and* challenging where the lats are most active. Hence his recommendation, hedged — use the free or cable variant if the machine feels wrong or your gym lacks one.
