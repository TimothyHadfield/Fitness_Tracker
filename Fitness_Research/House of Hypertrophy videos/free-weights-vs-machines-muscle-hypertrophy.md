# Free Weights and Machines Grew Muscle Equally Over Eight Weeks

**Source:** House of Hypertrophy, "Free Weights vs Machines for Muscle Hypertrophy (New Study)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JouADY-m9Ko)) · 2020-07-29 · 8 min
**Topic:** Exercise selection

## Bottom line
- Schwanbeck and colleagues (2020) trained 36 people for 8 weeks on matched free-weight or machine programs and found **similar increases in biceps and quadriceps thickness**.
- Strength followed specificity only **half** the time: machines won the Smith bench, free weights led numerically on the barbell squat — but barbell bench and Smith squat gains were similar.
- Only **two muscles** were measured by ultrasound despite both groups training nearly the whole body, over 8 weeks.
- His mechanistic case: both modalities generate high **active tension** (any load 30–85% 1RM close to failure does it) and both allow **passive tension** (eccentrics, and long muscle lengths).
- The instability argument for free weights may hold early in a set, but recruitment converges as reps approach failure.

## The study
15 men and 21 women averaging **2 years of training experience**, randomised to a free-weight or machine group. Two days on, one off, for 8 weeks: day 1 chest, back and triceps; day 2 legs, shoulders and biceps.

Programs were matched movement for movement — barbell bench and incline, row, chin-ups, skull crushers and kickbacks against Smith bench and incline, row machine, pulldown and pressdowns; back squat, straight-leg deadlift, lunge, calf raise, dumbbell presses and curls against the machine equivalents.

Loading progressed across three blocks, every set **to failure**: weeks 1–3 at 4×8–10, 1 min rest; weeks 4–6 at 4×6–8, 90 s; weeks 7–8 at 3×4–5, 2 min, adding load whenever subjects could manage it. Measures: ultrasound thickness of quadriceps and biceps; 1RM barbell and Smith bench; 6–10RM barbell and Smith squat converted to predicted 1RMs.

## Results
**Hypertrophy:** similar biceps and quadriceps thickness increases in both groups.

**Strength:** barbell bench 1RM improved similarly. Smith bench 1RM favoured the machine group. Predicted barbell squat 1RM was statistically similar but percentage-wise clearly larger in the free-weight group. Predicted Smith squat 1RM was similar.

He reads this against specificity, which predicts each group should win on its own equipment. That held for the Smith bench and (numerically) the barbell squat, not for the barbell bench or Smith squat. His caveat: 8 weeks is short, and long term you still have to practise the lift you want to maximise.

## Why the mechanisms predict a tie
Mechanical tension is the best-characterised driver of growth, and it has two components.

**Active tension** is force from the contractile units — myosin heads pulling actin toward the M line, transmitted mostly laterally into the extracellular matrix and onto the tendon, where mechanosensors are believed to detect it and start the signalling cascade. Whatever the implement, a set close to failure at 30–85% 1RM produces enough active tension for long enough. So even if free weights recruit more fibres early in a set, that washes out by the end.

**Passive tension** comes from stretched elastic elements — titin and the extracellular matrix — and is also thought to be sensed. Titin appears to change function during eccentrics so it produces force through that phase, and every modality involves an eccentric. It also arises at long muscle lengths: the vastus sarcomeres appear stretched there, available from a barbell squat, Smith squat or hack squat alike.

## Flag
The mechanistic section is reasoning, not evidence — it explains a null rather than testing anything, and it does most of the persuasive work here, since the study is 8 weeks, ultrasound-based and measured two muscles. He acknowledges the measurement limitation but not that a null in an underpowered trial is weak grounds for "these are equivalent".
