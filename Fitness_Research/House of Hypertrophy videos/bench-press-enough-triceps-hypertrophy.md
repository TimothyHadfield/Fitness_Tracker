# The Bench Press Grows the Lateral Triceps Head and Little Else

**Source:** House of Hypertrophy, "Is the Bench Press Enough for Triceps Hypertrophy?" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jTEZEL_RLw4)) · 2020-07-01 · 5 min
**Topic:** Exercise selection

## Bottom line
- [Brandão 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32149887/): **43 untrained men**, four groups — bench press only, skullcrusher only, bench→skullcrusher, skullcrusher→bench. **80% 1RM to failure, 3 min rest, 2×/week for 10 weeks**, sets ramping 3 → 4 → 5 across the block. MRI cross-sectional area of pec major, whole triceps and each triceps head.
- **Whole triceps CSA was statistically similar across all four groups**, but the bench-only group gained about **half** the percentage of the three groups doing skullcrushers. Dhim calls the null a type II error.
- By head: **lateral head** favoured the bench groups over skullcrushers-only; **long head** significantly favoured the skullcrusher groups over bench-only; **medial head** was statistically similar but roughly **double** in the skullcrusher groups.
- **Anatomical reason:** the long head is the only head crossing the shoulder. During the bench eccentric the shoulder extends, shortening the long head, so it enters the concentric already shortened with limited force capacity.
- Practical: multi-joint pressing does contribute to triceps growth, but **elbow extension work at varying shoulder elevations is still needed** to develop all three heads.

## The dispute this settles
A 2016 review concluded single-joint exercises add nothing over multi-joint exercises for hypertrophy ([Gentil 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27677913/)). A response by Ribeiro and colleagues pointed out that only three studies existed at the time, leaving most single-joint / multi-joint pairings untested — so the conclusion was premature. Brandão is one of the missing tests, and it goes against the review.

## The pec finding, and the order effect
For pec major CSA, the three groups that benched beat the skullcrusher-only group, which is unsurprising since they were the only group not pressing. More interesting: doing skullcrushers **before** benching produced lower relative pec growth than benching first, though the difference was not significant. His speculation is that pre-fatiguing the triceps makes them the limiting factor on the bench, so the pecs get less stimulus.

## Why the head-by-head data is the useful part
The clean comparison in this study is **bench-only versus skullcrusher-only** — one exercise each, the same sets, so volume is matched. On that comparison the bench wins the lateral head, loses the long head, and the medial head goes to skullcrushers on percentages. That is a genuine exercise-selection finding rather than a volume artefact.

He extends the anatomy one step further: since holding the shoulder flexed lets the long head produce force throughout the movement, putting the shoulder into **more** flexion — overhead extensions — should stretch the long head further still and let it produce even more force. This is speculation in 2020, and it was borne out two years later by [Maeo 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819335/), which found substantially greater triceps hypertrophy from overhead versus neutral-arm elbow extension (covered in the library's note on his [myths video](popular-muscle-strength-building-myths.md)).

**Flagged:** twice he overrides a non-significant result by pointing at percentage differences and calling it a type II error. He is transparent that this is his reading — but the study's own conclusion for whole-triceps and medial-head CSA is "no difference", and 43 untrained men split four ways is about 10–11 per cell, which is exactly the situation where percentage differences are unstable in either direction. The direction of his interpretation is supported by the significant long-head result; the magnitude is not established. Also note: untrained subjects, and the two combined-exercise groups performed twice the total set volume of the single-exercise groups, so those arms are not volume-matched to the others.
