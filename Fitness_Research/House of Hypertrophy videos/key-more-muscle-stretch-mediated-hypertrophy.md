# Stretch-Mediated Hypertrophy: Which Muscles Actually Grow More Under Stretch

**Source:** House of Hypertrophy, "This is KEY for More Muscle (Stretch-Mediated Hypertrophy)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5UTsWafnM0M)) · 2022-10-31 · 12 min
**Topic:** Range of motion & tempo

## Bottom line
- Muscle-by-muscle sweep of the direct evidence as of late 2022. **Quads, glutes, adductors, hamstrings, biceps and triceps** all show more growth when trained at longer muscle lengths, and the advantage is usually largest in the **distal regions** of the muscle.
- At the time of recording there was **no direct research at all** on back, shoulders, calves, abs or chest. He says so plainly rather than extrapolating.
- The regional pattern matters for study design: two of the "stretch doesn't matter" results in the video measured growth only at the **mid-belly**, which is exactly where the effect is smallest.
- He stops short of saying stretched partials beat a full range of motion — one study found it, and he refuses to conclude from one study.
- **Library note:** this is 2022. The channel's own 2025 coverage (see `training-beyond-failure-new-study-epic.md`) reports two trials in *trained* lifters, n=25 and n=297, finding no arm or thigh difference between full ROM and lengthened partials. The optimism here is dated on that specific question.

## Quads
Four studies, and they line up. Leg extensions trained at a stretched partial beat a shortened partial for vastus lateralis growth across three regions, with the gap widest at the 50% and especially 75% sites (McMahon 2014). The same group found full ROM beat a shortened partial, again with a very large difference at 75% (McMahon 2014b). Deeper squats beat shallow squats at every measured thigh region, once more with bigger differences lower down (Bloomquist 2013).

The exception: Kubo 2019 found partial squats to 90° of knee flexion produced quad growth comparable to full squats. He reads that as 90° already achieving enough quad stretch — and immediately labels it speculation from a single paper.

Pedrosa 2021 compared full ROM, stretched partial, shortened partial and a stretched-plus-shortened combination on leg extensions. Rectus femoris and vastus lateralis growth was best with the **stretched partial**, again most clearly at the lower regions. One study, one exercise — worth experimenting with, not worth concluding from.

## Glutes and adductors
Only one clean study: Kubo 2019 again, where the deeper squat grew glute max and adductors strikingly more.

A 2020 Brazilian paper comparing squats to hip thrusts (squats loading the glutes stretched, hip thrusts shortened) reported markedly more glute growth with squats. He **discards it**: the research group behind it has had the validity of its data seriously questioned, and he says he cannot trust the results. Crediting him for this — it is a result that supports his thesis and he threw it out anyway.

## Hamstrings
All hamstring heads except biceps femoris short head cross both hip and knee, so a seated leg curl stretches them more than a lying curl at the same knee range. Maeo 2021 found exactly that: greater hypertrophy in every two-joint hamstring muscle with seated curls, while short-head growth was similar. Regional analysis of biceps femoris long head and semitendinosus favoured seated at both upper and lower sites.

His extension — that stiff-legged deadlifts may build hamstrings better than conventional because of less knee flexion — he flags as speculation. It is untested.

## Biceps
Pinto 2012 found full ROM preacher curls tended to beat a partial for biceps thickness, but not significantly. He argues the difference is likely real rather than noise, on the grounds that Sato 2021 — comparing a shortened partial to a stretched partial on the same movement — found greater regional biceps growth in the stretched condition, most strikingly at 70%. That is a defensible inference but it is still using one study to reinterpret another's null.

The contrary result is Nunes 2020: barbell vs cable preacher curls with matched range, similar biceps thickness gains, despite the barbell loading the stretched position harder. His rebuttal is the good one — cable preacher curls still place meaningful tension at long lengths, and **thickness was measured only at the halfway point of the muscle**, the region where stretch effects are consistently weakest.

## Triceps
Goto 2019 found partial-ROM skull crushers beat full ROM at one measured region. He does not dismiss it; he points out the partial still stretched the triceps substantially, so it argues against *extra* range adding benefit rather than against stretch itself. He also states outright that there is no known mechanism for that result.

For the long head — the only triceps head crossing the shoulder — overhead extensions should win if stretch matters. Stasinaki 2018 found long head thickness tended to favour overhead at two regions, non-significantly, in **nine subjects**; he calls it underpowered rather than negative. Maeo 2022 then found significantly greater long head growth with overhead extensions, and, unexpectedly, greater **medial and lateral** head growth too, despite those heads not being stretched more overhead. He defends keeping the result: no available explanation is not the same as no effect. The study was within-subject (one arm per condition), which removes diet, genetics and lifestyle as confounders.

## The chest argument is the weak one
No direct hypertrophy data existed. He instead cites Martínez-Cava, where Smith machine bench pressing at full ROM produced greater strength gains at *all three* tested ranges than training the two-thirds or one-third top range — a result that contradicts specificity. Since a Smith machine strips out most of the skill component, he suggests the full-ROM group must have gained more muscle, plausibly from greater pec stretch.

**Flag:** that is a hypertrophy conclusion built entirely on a strength study with no growth measurement, in a video that is otherwise scrupulous about outcome data. He labels it "very indirect", but it is the one section where the reasoning outruns the evidence.
