# Squats vs Hip Thrusts: A Dead Heat for the Glutes

**Source:** House of Hypertrophy, "Squats vs Hip Thrusts for Glute Growth (NEW Research)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iI_zdaXlkCM)) · 2023-07-03 · 9 min
**Topic:** Exercise selection

## Bottom line
- The first trustworthy head-to-head found **no difference** in gluteus maximus growth between barbell back squats and barbell hip thrusts — at upper, middle *and* lower regions.
- The widely-shared Barbalho result claiming squats produced ~2.5× more glute growth is set aside entirely: that author's body of work carries improbable data patterns and cannot be relied on.
- Squats additionally grew **quads and adductors**; hip thrusts did not. Neither grew hamstrings. So squats are the better all-round lower-body developer, hip thrusts the better glute isolator.
- Both exercises grew the **lower** glute region most, then middle, then upper. Gluteus medius and minimus barely moved in either group.
- Hip thrusts train the glutes in a shortened position and are likely easier to recover from — which makes them a good vehicle for *adding volume* rather than a superior stimulus per set.

## The new study
34 untrained individuals were assigned to squat-only or hip-thrust-only training, twice per week, 8–12 reps to volitional failure, sets per session rising across the study, load progressed to keep them in range. Squatters went "as deep as comfortable" — four hit ass-to-grass, most reached parallel.

Glute max growth was equivalent across all three measured regions. He offers two reasons the lower region grew best: it likely contributes most to hip extension (upper fibres also do hip abduction), and indirect evidence suggests upper regions carry proportionally more slow-twitch fibres.

Squat strength rose more in squatters, hip thrust strength more in thrusters — but **deadlift strength rose equally**, which is a neat aside.

Two limitations he states plainly: subjects were untrained, and "volitional failure" is a vague stopping criterion.

## Why didn't the deeper stretch win?
This is the interesting part. The literature says stretch is powerful — Kubo's 2019 squat-depth study found 140° knee flexion grew the glute max substantially more than 90°; Maeo's hamstring and triceps studies both favoured the stretched-position exercise. So why did parallel squats not beat hip thrusts?

His working hypothesis, offered explicitly as a hypothesis: the glute max is odd. At deep hip flexion it is stretched but has **poor leverage** for hip extension, so it may not experience high contractile force where it is longest. The adductor magnus, with better leverage, takes the load instead. Stretch plus high contractile force is the recipe; the glute may not get both simultaneously in a squat.

He also refuses to close the depth question: nobody has directly compared parallel to deep squats for glute growth, and he says so.

## The volume angle
Kassiano and colleagues had 33 untrained women train 3×/week for 10 weeks. One group did leg press and stiff-legged deadlift; the second added hip thrusts. Glute growth was greater in the second group — but he immediately notes the second group therefore did **more sets**, and higher set counts produce more growth on average. The honest reading is that hip thrusts are a fine way to add glute volume, not that they are magic.

## Where this sits against the library
Our library carries a long-running dispute here: one source ranked the walking lunge first and the barbell hip thrust only high-B in 2025, having earlier held hip thrusts superior and dismissed the squat-depth advantage as an artefact. This channel's verdict cuts across both positions — **a tie for glute max**, with squats winning on total lower-body coverage — and it explicitly does *not* treat Kubo's depth finding as an artefact, citing it as real and unexplained by the new trial.

## Flag
The regional fibre-type argument and the leverage hypothesis are both mechanism, not outcome data; he labels them as such.
