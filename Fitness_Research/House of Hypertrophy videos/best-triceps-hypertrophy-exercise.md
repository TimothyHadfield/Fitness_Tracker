# Overhead Extensions Beat Pushdowns by About 40%

**Source:** House of Hypertrophy, "The BEST Triceps Hypertrophy Exercise" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tuF40f90tCQ)) · 2022-07-25 · 9 min
**Topic:** Exercise selection

## Bottom line
- Maeo and colleagues found overhead cable extensions produced **1.4× the whole-triceps volume growth** of pushdowns — and **1.5×** for the long head — over 12 weeks in a within-subject design.
- That matches the library's existing position (Maeo 2023, roughly 40% more growth from overhead work). He reaches the same conclusion from the same study, published as it broke.
- The surprise: overhead extensions also grew the **medial and lateral heads** 1.4× more, despite those heads not being stretched any further overhead. Nobody knows why.
- His recommendation: some overhead extension is likely required to maximise triceps growth. Overhead alone is enough if you want efficiency; add a skull crusher variant if you want regional coverage; pushdowns are optional and unsupported either way.

## The study
21 untrained adults (14 men, 7 women). One arm trained overhead cable extensions, the other cable pushdowns. Both moved 0–90° of elbow flexion with a supinated grip — so **range of motion was matched**, and only shoulder position differed. Twice per week for 12 straight weeks, 5 sets of 10 reps, 2 minutes rest, alternating which arm went first each session.

Load ramped 50% → 60% → 70% of 1RM over the first three sessions, then stayed at 70%. If a subject couldn't complete 10 reps, a researcher assisted to guarantee the rep count; once they hit 5×10 unassisted, load went up 5%.

Outcomes were **muscle volume** (MRI-based, three-dimensional) for the whole triceps and the long head separately; medial and lateral heads were measured together because the researchers couldn't reliably separate them distally.

Results: whole triceps **1.4×**, long head **1.5×**, medial+lateral combined **1.4×** — all favouring overhead.

A second, smaller paper out of Greece (Stasinaki and colleagues, n=9, 6 weeks) found the same direction for the long head at 50% and 60% of upper arm length, but not statistically significant. He handles this well: he attributes the null to n=9 and 6 weeks, and states the general principle that statistical insignificance is not evidence of no difference.

## Why the long head grew more
The long head crosses both shoulder and elbow; the medial and lateral heads cross only the elbow. Overhead positioning therefore stretches the long head and nothing else. He connects this to the wider stretch literature: Maeo's own hamstring study (seated leg curls beat lying leg curls for every two-joint hamstring head, with no difference in the single-joint biceps femoris short head), isometric training at long vs short lengths, the range-of-motion meta-analyses, and Pedrosa's 2021 finding that a *lengthened partial* leg extension outgrew full range of motion.

## The unexplained result
The medial and lateral heads aren't stretched overhead, yet grew 1.4× more. Two speculations from the paper, both of which he treats sceptically:

1. Modelling suggested the long head operates on the **descending limb of its force-length curve** overhead — i.e. it can't produce much active force there — so the nervous system may lean harder on the other two heads.
2. Overhead positioning may restrict blood flow and raise **metabolic stress**. He points out immediately that metabolic stress often fails to track with hypertrophy, so this is weak.

## Is overhead alone enough?
Volume is a good measure precisely because it is three-dimensional — but it collapses **regional** differences, and regional hypertrophy is well documented. He argues different triceps exercises may develop different regions, citing Kawakami (dumbbell overhead extensions, 16 weeks → growth mainly in slightly upper regions) and Wakahara (dumbbell skull crushers, 12 weeks → growth mainly in middle regions).

## Flag
That last inference compares two **separate Japanese studies** with different subjects, durations and methods, and reads a regional division of labour out of them. It is a reasonable hypothesis, not a finding, and the "add skull crushers" recommendation rests on it. He is candid that the pushdown extension of the same argument has no data at all.
