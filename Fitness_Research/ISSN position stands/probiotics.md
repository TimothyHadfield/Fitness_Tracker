# Probiotics: Strain-Specific Effects, and Why "Probiotics" Is Not One Supplement

**Source:** ISSN position stand, *Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition*, 2019 Dec 21 · [PMID 31864419](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31864419/) · [doi](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-019-0329-0)
**Authors:** Jäger R, Mohr AE, Carpenter KC, Kerksick CM, Purpura M, Moussa A, et al (12 authors)
**Licence:** CC BY 4.0 — <https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/>
**Topic:** Supplements

## The position, in one paragraph

Probiotic effects are **strain-specific and dose-specific**, and the stand makes that its organising principle rather than a caveat. Two organisms can share a genus and species and still differ functionally — the taxonomic threshold for "same species" is only **70% DNA-DNA reassociation**, whereas a human and a lemur share **78%**. So a product labelled "*Lactobacillus acidophilus*" without a strain designation tells you essentially nothing. Where the evidence is strongest is **upper respiratory tract infection in athletes with high training loads**, and even there it is strain-by-strain and inconsistent. Performance benefits are mostly absent. Recovery benefits rest on two small trials of two specific strains — both authored by authors of this stand. Everything else in the document is flagged, correctly, as future direction.

## Bottom line

- **Read the label for the strain, not the species.** A valid probiotic label names genus, species *and* strain, plus CFU count at end of shelf life. Products giving only genus and species "should be discouraged."
- **Best-evidenced single result:** *B. animalis* subsp. *lactis* **Bl-04 at 2 × 10¹⁰ CFU/day for 150 days** cut the risk of any URTI episode by **27%** in 465 active adults ([West 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24268677/)). The two-strain arm in the same trial managed 19% — **not statistically significant**. Same trial, same design, different strains, different answer.
- **Performance: 24 studies assessed it. 17 found nothing.** Single-strain probiotics produced an aerobic benefit in exactly **one** study, in untrained men with no training intervention.
- **Immunity: 22 studies. 14 positive, 8 null.** This is the only domain where the balance clearly favours supplementation.
- **Gut health: 10 studies. The majority found no effect**; four found something (reduced zonulin, reduced endotoxin, reduced hyperpermeability, shorter symptom episodes).
- **Dose:** the IOC's 2018 consensus gives moderate support at **1 × 10¹⁰ live bacteria/day** ([Maughan 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29540367/)). Published athlete studies span **1 × 10⁹ to 4 × 10¹⁰ CFU**.
- **Start at least 14 days before a competition or training block.** Adaptation takes ~14 days, and **8 days after you stop, the strain is no longer detectable in the gut** ([Kullen 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9040550/)). Probiotics are transient passengers, not permanent residents.
- **Sex differences are real and unexplained.** *L. fermentum* PCC reduced respiratory symptom severity **in men only, with no effect in women**, at the same dose ([West 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21477383/)).
- **Recovery:** **B. coagulans GBI-30, 6086 at 1 × 10⁹ CFU with 20 g casein for 2 weeks** reduced soreness and CK after single-leg damaging exercise ([Jäger 2016b](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27547577/)); **B. breve BR03 + S. thermophilus FP4 at 5 × 10⁹ CFU each for 3 weeks** improved isometric peak torque and range of motion in acute recovery in 15 men ([Jäger 2016a](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27754427/)). Both n < 30.
- **Fermented foods are not probiotics** by the FAO/WHO definition — sauerkraut and kimchi contain live microbes but have not been characterised to strain level or tested for a defined health benefit.
- **Safety is good but under-measured:** of the studies reviewed, **only 11 recorded adverse events; 30 did not**. The one reported issue was five episodes of flatulence and stomach rumbling.

## Where the stand cannot recommend a strain — and says so

This is the more useful half of the document, and it is long. For each of the following, the stand explicitly states that the evidence is preclinical, non-athletic, or absent:

- **Muscle mass.** Increases in fat-free mass "have only been shown in animal models" — *L. plantarum* TWK10 in mice ([Chen 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27070637/)). No human athlete data.
- **Testosterone.** Aging mice on *L. reuteri* had larger testes and higher testosterone. In humans, one pilot found **no change** in testosterone, DHT, androstenedione, DHEA-S or SHBG in 31 healthy men over two months ([McMullen 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17109580/)). The one positive human study was in **infertile male patients**, not athletes.
- **Lactate clearance / buffering.** Entirely mouse data.
- **Mood and cognition.** No studies in athletes at all. The general-population trials are small (n = 25–40) and use different strains.
- **Cortisol / stress response.** Attenuated in students during exams; **no effect** in the only athlete trial — 8 endurance-trained males, high-dose *L. casei*, running in 34 °C heat ([Gill 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26479711/)).
- **Neurotransmitter synthesis / choline.** "There is currently no research in athletes or active individuals."
- **Single- versus multi-strain.** "Potential additive or even synergistic benefits would need to be validated in a control clinical study, and currently those data do not exist."
- **Optimal timing.** One *in vitro* model suggests taking it **30 min before or with a meal**, not after, and with milk rather than juice or water. That is the whole evidence base.

## Immunity: the one domain with a real case

Every positive URTI result in the table above comes with a matching null somewhere. The same group that found *L. casei* Shirota reduced URTI incidence over 16 weeks ([Gleeson 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21411836/)) found **no effect of the same strain** over 20 weeks in 243 college athletes ([Gleeson 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27294502/)), and **no effect of *L. salivarius*** over four months ([Gleeson 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21385218/)). *L. rhamnosus* GG did nothing for infection incidence in 141 marathon runners ([Kekkonen 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17962710/)), though GI symptom episodes were **57% shorter**.

What survives: the effect is plausible, appears in more trials than not, and is largest in athletes carrying heavy training loads through winter. The size is modest — roughly a quarter reduction in risk in the best-powered study, plus about a **0.8-month delay** in time to first illness. If illness is what costs you training weeks, that is worth something. It is not a performance supplement.

## Performance and body composition

The honest summary is that probiotics do not improve aerobic performance. Null results across single strains (*L. fermentum* VRI-003, *L. fermentum* PCC, *L. gasseri* OLL2809, *L. casei*, *L. helveticus* L10) and across multi-strain blends (14-strain, 6-species, triathlon blend, marathon blend, kefir). The stand's own key point: single-strain supplementation "has produced a significant aerobic performance benefit in only one study."

That one study — **1 × 10¹¹ CFU of *L. plantarum* TWK10 for 6 weeks** improving treadmill endurance ([Huang 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29962176/)) — was in **16 untrained men with no exercise intervention**, and the stand says to interpret it conservatively. Do so.

Where positives appear, the mechanism the stand proposes is **indirect**: fewer infections means more consistent training. [Strasser 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27886064/) is the cleanest version of this — the probiotic group did not perform better but *trained more hours per week*, and their exercise-induced drop in circulating tryptophan was blunted.

On body composition the two DE111 trials disagree. In 23 Division I female soccer and volleyball players taking DE111 with a post-workout drink (**45 g CHO / 20 g protein**), 10 weeks produced greater fat loss and fat-free-mass gain than placebo, with **no performance change** ([Toohey 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33105368/)). In 25 Division I baseball players on the same strain at a lower dose for 12 weeks, **nothing** on strength, performance or body composition — only lower TNF-α ([Townsend 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30049931/)). The stand's explanation is that healthy young athletes have microbiomes that are already fine and resist modification, which is plausible but also explains away any inconvenient null.

## Practical use

1. Pick a product naming **genus, species and strain**, with CFU guaranteed **at end of shelf life** — not at manufacture. Match the strain to your goal using the table above; do not substitute another strain of the same species.
2. **Start ≥14 days before** the competition or travel block. Stopping resets you within about a week.
3. Take it **30 min before or with a meal**, with milk rather than juice or water.
4. Expect mild transient flatulence and gut rumbling during colonisation. Store it properly — heat in a car boot will kill it. Trial it in the offseason, and use third-party-tested products given WADA strict liability.
5. If you have severe acute pancreatitis, IBD, liver disease or are immunocompromised, talk to a clinician first.

## Strength of the evidence

**What is solid:** the strain-specificity argument itself, the taxonomy, the transient-colonisation pharmacology, the safety profile, and the honest tallies (17 of 24 performance studies null; 8 of 22 immune studies null; most GI studies null). A consensus document that publishes its own negative count is doing something right, and this one does it repeatedly.

**What is thin:**

- **The recovery claim rests on two trials by the stand's own authors.** References [42] and [43] — the FP4/BR03 and the BC30 studies — are both **Jäger *et al*, 2016**, and R. Jäger is lead author of this position stand while M. Purpura is a co-author of both papers and of the stand. Those two studies (n = 15 and n = 29) carry the entire "administration of selected anti-inflammatory probiotic strains have been linked to improved recovery from muscle-damaging exercise" position point. Self-authorship is not misconduct, but a position of the Society should not rest on two small trials by the position's own lead author with no independent replication. It does.
- **Author overlap is extensive elsewhere too.** Gleeson, West, Pyne, Lamprecht, Townsend and Black are all listed authors and all appear as study authors in the evidence table. Roughly half the reviewed literature was produced by the people writing the review.
- **Two of the supported strains rest on non-indexed publications.** The claim that *L. delbrueckii* ssp. *bulgaricus* "can increase VO₂max and aerobic power" comes from Salehzadeh 2015, published in a journal with **no PubMed record and no DOI**. Worse, the stand lists the effective dose as **1 × 10⁵ CFU** — four to five orders of magnitude below every other entry in its own summary and below the bottom of its own *Lactobacillus* dosing table (1 × 10⁹ – 10 × 10¹⁰ CFU). That figure is the CFU/g of a yogurt drink, not a supplement dose, and reproducing it as a strain recommendation is an error. Similarly, the *L. salivarius* UCC118 gut-permeability result cited in the summary comes from a **conference abstract** with the dose "not indicated" (Brennan *et al*, ACSM 2018 — no PubMed record, no DOI) — the stand recommends a strain whose dose it cannot state.
- **Table 3 contains at least two citation errors.** The two Jäger 2016 rows carry each other's reference numbers ([42] is the BR03/FP4 paper, [43] the BC30 paper, and the table has them swapped). And the Toohey row is numbered [20], which resolves to [Estaki 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27502158/), an unrelated paper on cardiorespiratory fitness and microbial diversity; the correct reference [103] is used in the body text.
- **Regulatory context cuts against the whole field.** The stand notes plainly that **EFSA has rejected every probiotic health claim submitted to it**, and that US supplement GMP rules "do not extend to testing quality or efficacy." It then reproduces a two-page table of *Health Canada–approved* sport-performance claims, including a DOMS claim for BC30 — the strain studied by the stand's lead author. Those are marketing-permission decisions from one jurisdiction, not evidence.

**On funding and competing interests:** the declaration is substantial and is set out under *Disclosures and funding* below. Six authors are employed by or founded probiotic businesses, and the lead author declares probiotic-industry consulting — which matters because the recovery position point rests on two of his own studies involving named commercial strains.

## Disclosures and funding

**What the document declares.** The stand was commissioned by the editors of the *Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition*, and the authors received no remuneration for writing or reviewing it. The competing-interests statement is where the substance sits, and **six authors are employed by, or founded, probiotic businesses**: AEM and KCC are employed by **Isagenix**, a company selling branded probiotics; CJW by **Jamieson Labs**, likewise; MPa by **Biolab Research Srl**, performing research and development for **Probiotical SpA**, a leading probiotic supplier; JS is a co-founder of **FitBiomics**, which identifies, researches and commercialises new probiotic strains; and JAT is employed by the **International Probiotic Association** and consults within the probiotic and microbiome industries. **RJ** (Jäger, lead author) has received grants to evaluate the efficacy and safety of probiotics, serves on scientific advisory boards, and has served as an expert witness and legal and scientific consultant. **MP** (Purpura) has received probiotic grants and served as a scientific consultant. **ML** conducts industry-sponsored studies and is a scientific consultant to the Juice Plus+ Company. **DSK** works for a contract research organisation funded by the probiotic industry and sits on Dymatize's advisory board; **BIC** is also on Dymatize's board. CMK, JRT, MG and DBP report probiotic-related research funding. **JA** is CEO of the ISSN. AM, ASR, KB, LB and SDW declare none.

**Is it material? Yes.** This is a document that makes strain-specific recommendations naming commercial products, written by a panel including employees of probiotic manufacturers and of the industry's trade association. It is all declared, and the stand's willingness to publish its own null tallies suggests the panel was not simply selling. But strain-level recommendations are exactly where a manufacturer's interest concentrates, and this is the clearest case in this library of a declared interest sitting directly on top of the conclusion.

**The recovery point compounds it.** As set out above, the recovery recommendation rests on two small trials — references [42] and [43], both Jäger *et al*, 2016, n = 15 and n = 29 — authored by this stand's lead author with co-author Purpura, on named commercial strains including GanedenBC30, and both of those authors declare probiotic-industry consulting relationships. Self-authorship is not a declared conflict and is not misconduct. But a Society position resting on two unreplicated trials by its own lead author, on branded strains, with industry consulting declared alongside, is the combination that should make you wait for independent replication before acting on it.
