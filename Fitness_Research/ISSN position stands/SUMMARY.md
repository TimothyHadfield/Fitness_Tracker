# The ISSN corpus in one file

Twenty-six position stands and one comprehensive review, 2007 to 2026, distilled to what you'd
act on — and, just as usefully, to where the society's confidence outruns its own evidence.

The International Society of Sports Nutrition is the closest thing sports nutrition has to a
standards body. A position stand is a multi-author review that ends in a numbered list of
things the society holds to be true. That format is the source of both its value and its
characteristic failure mode: **the numbered position is frequently firmer than the review
that precedes it.** Reading all 27 turned up that pattern repeatedly, and this file says so
each time rather than passing the headline along.

Index of every stand: [README.md](README.md). Bibliography of all
[5,192 unique references](RESEARCH-CITATIONS.md).

## Quick reference

| Compound / variable | The ISSN position |
|---|---|
| **Creatine** | **0.3 g/kg/day for 5–7 days**, then **3–5 g/day** — or just 3–5 g/day and wait longer ([2017](creatine-2017.md)) |
| **Caffeine** | **3–6 mg/kg**, ~60 min before; 1–3 mg/kg also works and side effects scale with dose ([2021](caffeine-2021.md)) |
| **Sodium bicarbonate** | **0.3 g/kg**, 60–180 min before, for efforts of **30 s to 12 min** ([stand](sodium-bicarbonate.md)) |
| **Beta-alanine** | **4–6 g/day**, divided, for 2–4 weeks; benefits efforts of **1–4 min** ([stand](beta-alanine.md)) |
| **HMB** | ~**3 g/day**; a clinical agent for muscle preservation, not a bodybuilding supplement ([2025](hmb-2025.md)) |
| **Protein** | **1.4–2.0 g/kg/day**; **20–40 g** per meal, every 3–4 h ([2017](protein-2017.md)) |
| **Carbohydrate** | **5–12 g/kg/day** by training load ([timing 2017](nutrient-timing-2017.md)) |
| **Nutrient timing** | No protein deadline. Total intake dominates ([2017](nutrient-timing-2017.md)) |
| **In-race carbs, ultra** | **30–50 g/h** — deliberately below the general 60–90 g/h ([stand](ultra-marathon.md)) |
| **Combat-sport weight cut** | Walk-around **12–15%** above class; descend **0.5–1 kg/week** ([stand](weight-cutting-strategies.md)) |

## What the society is confident about, and has earned

**Creatine monohydrate** is the most secure recommendation in the corpus. Two stands eighteen
years apart agree on mechanism, dose and safety, and the 2017 revision is more confident than
its predecessor rather than less ([2017](creatine-2017.md), [2007](creatine-2007.md)).

**Caffeine** is nearly as secure, and the 2021 stand is the best-executed document here — it
walks back three of its own 2010 claims explicitly, adds genotype, sleep state, anxiety and
delivery form, and argues for the *low* end of its own dose range because side effects scale
linearly while performance does not ([2021](caffeine-2021.md)).

**Sodium bicarbonate** overreaches least of any stand in the corpus. Its lead authors declare
no competing interests, its dose-response is well characterised, and where its position
statement is looser than its review the gap is small and in the conservative direction
([stand](sodium-bicarbonate.md)).

**The ketogenic diet stand** is the corpus's model of good practice, and it is worth reading
for the method as much as the conclusion. It argues *against* a commercially popular diet,
lists every excluded trial with reasons, and criticises its own headline finding — noting that
no study has used a diet-controlled design ([stand](ketogenic-diets.md)).

## Where the society changed its mind

Five topics have been revised. The revisions are the most valuable reading in the folder,
because the society generally does not announce that it has changed position — the 2017
protein stand opens by declaring itself "in alignment with our previous position stand" while
retiring four of that stand's positions.

| Topic | What was dropped or reversed |
|---|---|
| [Protein](protein-2017.md) | The entire **BCAA section deleted** — 2007 gave numeric leucine dosing, 2017 warns against leucine-only supplementation. Whey+casein blend dropped for whey. Timing demoted to "minimal". |
| [Creatine](creatine-2017.md) | The 2–3 day short-load shortcut deleted; **cycling silently abandoned** with no explanation. |
| [Nutrient timing](nutrient-timing-2017.md) | **The 30-minute anabolic window dissolves.** No protein deadline survives. The fixed 3:1 or 4:1 carb:protein ratio, the "pre-exercise is best of all" claim, and percentage-based carb targets are all gone. |
| [Caffeine](caffeine-2021.md) | **Glycogen sparing "challenged and eventually dismissed."** The claim that caffeine doesn't work in untrained people, and that anhydrous beats coffee — both withdrawn. |
| [HMB](hmb-2025.md) | HMB-FA superiority reversed. The 2025 stand explicitly **excludes its predecessor's evidence base** from informing its conclusions. |
| [Energy drinks](energy-drinks-2023.md) | Guidance for people with cardiovascular or metabolic disease **loosened** — from "avoid unless approved" to "exercise caution and consult" — even as the blood-pressure and QTc evidence got stronger. |

**On the anabolic window specifically**, since our library quotes a 4–6 hour figure elsewhere:
the 2017 stand does not state that number, or any number. It cites Aragon & Schoenfeld
approvingly but never quantifies. What it actually asserts is four incompatible things — MPS
elevated 3–4 h, amino acids 3–5 h, muscle sensitised for at least 24 h, and "eat as soon as
possible." It resolves the tension by declining to, on the grounds that a strategy which may
help and probably won't hurt is worth doing. That is an asymmetric-risk judgement, not an
evidence claim. Anyone citing "4–6 hours" to this stand is citing something that isn't in it.

## Where the stands contradict each other

These are not nuances. They are the same society asserting incompatible things, usually
without acknowledgement.

- **Essential amino acids — a direct reversal.** The [2018 review](issn-review-update-2018.md)
  tells readers to prioritise intact protein over free-form amino acids, citing a trial where
  whey beat matched EAAs. The [2023 EAA stand](essential-amino-acids.md) asserts that EAAs
  stimulate muscle protein synthesis *more than* an isonitrogenous protein isolate. Neither
  document mentions the other's position.
- **Beta-alanine plus creatine.** The [2007 creatine stand](creatine-2007.md) says the
  combination beats creatine alone. The [2015 beta-alanine stand](beta-alanine.md) reviews the
  same two studies, notes neither had a beta-alanine-only arm, and finds no synergy. Stout and
  Hoffman authored both.
- **Omega-3 and recovery.** The [antioxidant stand](dietary-antioxidants.md) ranks omega-3
  top-tier for recovery. The [dedicated omega-3 stand](omega-3.md) treats the same evidence as
  equivocal.
- **Probiotics** received a full position stand in 2019 having never appeared in the 2018
  review's supplement tier table at all.

## Where the position outruns the evidence

Reported honestly in each note's *Strength of the evidence* section. The pattern is consistent
enough to be a reading instruction: **when a stand's body says "equivocal" and its numbered
position says "improves", trust the body.**

- [**Energy drinks 2023**](energy-drinks-2023.md) asserts improved repeat-sprint and anaerobic
  performance while its own sections conclude those are "largely null" and cite a paper titled
  *"…null or negative effect on sprint performance."*
- [**Omega-3**](omega-3.md) does this four separate times — "equivocal" and "inconsistent" in
  the review become "may decrease soreness" and "has been shown to enhance endurance capacity"
  in the position statement.
- [**Tactical athlete**](tactical-athlete.md) lists beta-alanine among supplements aiding
  cognitive performance when the cited study found no cognitive change. Its omega-3
  traumatic-brain-injury case rests on American football players, not military personnel,
  measuring a biomarker rather than an outcome — and concedes "minimal human data exists"
  before quoting an expert panel calling it "unethical not to" supplement.
- [**Meal frequency**](meal-frequency.md) is the thinnest document in the corpus. Its
  body-composition case opens with cross-sectional epidemiology and no trials; the experimental
  literature is 1966, 1971, 1981, 1993 and one 2010 trial. Every "Application to Athletes" box
  admits athletes have not been studied, then recommends anyway.
- [**Nutrient timing 2008**](nutrient-timing-2008.md) states creatine "facilitates greater
  improvements in strength and body composition" directly beneath a paragraph calling the same
  findings "somewhat mixed."
- [**Ultra-marathon**](ultra-marathon.md) is the only stand with per-statement evidence grades,
  and the distribution is telling: **grade A appears exactly once** (caffeine), while the entire
  in-race fuelling protocol — the part readers will actually use — is grade C, from
  observational finisher-versus-non-finisher data. People already failing eat less *because*
  they are failing.
- [**Weight cutting**](weight-cutting-strategies.md) never uses the word *death*, and never
  discusses mortality, heat stroke or renal failure, in a stand on a practice with a documented
  death toll. Its safety argument runs entirely through performance decrement and a 40 °C core
  temperature ceiling.

## Independence and industry ties

Every note carries a *Disclosures and funding* section. Three patterns are worth stating at the
corpus level.

**In 15 of the 27 documents, a declared tie bears directly on that document's own
conclusions.** Not industry ties in general — ties *in the category the stand assesses*:

| Stand | The tie |
|---|---|
| [Creatine 2017](creatine-2017.md) | Prepared at the request of, and funded by, the Council for Responsible Nutrition — a supplement-industry trade association. Structurally the strongest case in the corpus. |
| [HMB 2025](hmb-2025.md) | Lead author employed by MTI Biotech, second author its consultant; both hold HMB patents. |
| [HMB 2013](hmb-2013.md) | An author conducting research for Metabolic Technologies Inc., which commercialised HMB. |
| [Essential amino acids](essential-amino-acids.md) | Two authors hold EAA patents; one also holds equity in The Amino Company. |
| [Beta-alanine](beta-alanine.md) | Lead author funded by Natural Alternatives Inc. (CarnoSyn); a co-author formulates beta-alanine products. |
| [Probiotics](probiotics.md) | Six authors employed by or founding probiotic businesses. |
| [Energy drinks 2023](energy-drinks-2023.md) | Share ownership in an energy drink company and a caffeine-metabolite ingredient firm with patent applications. |
| [Energy drinks 2013](energy-drinks-2013.md) | An author who is Sports Science Advisor to an energy drink manufacturer. |
| [Caffeine 2021](caffeine-2021.md) | Lead author consults for a genetic testing company — material specifically to the genotyping recommendation. |
| [Coffee](coffee.md) | Lead author's company funded by companies selling coffee-related products, on a section built from his own lab's abstract-only studies. |
| [Omega-3](omega-3.md) | Four in-category ties, on a stand whose headline claims already outrun its own review. |
| [Antioxidants](dietary-antioxidants.md) | An author employed by a company supplying astaxanthin — one of the four compounds the stand names as safe to use. |
| [Protein 2017](protein-2017.md) | Two authors employed by a protein supplement company; another advises two more. |
| [2018 review](issn-review-update-2018.md) | Authors holding stock and commission in companies selling ingredients the tier chart grades, and a supplement-industry counsel. It is a tier list with commercial value. |
| [Diets and body composition](diets-and-body-composition.md) | Material to the protein-pacing recommendation specifically, whose evidence base is a co-author's own. |

The honourable exception is [sodium bicarbonate](sodium-bicarbonate.md): its six drafting
authors declare nothing, and the two who name a supplement company also note that it made no
bicarbonate products. That is what a good disclosure looks like. The
[ketogenic stand](ketogenic-diets.md) is the interesting inverse — its authors hold ketosis
patents, and the stand argues *against* the diet anyway.

**One declaration contradicts itself.** The [2023 energy drinks stand](energy-drinks-2023.md)
carries a disclosure statement reading "No potential conflict of interest was reported by the
author(s)" directly above a conflict-of-interest section declaring share ownership in an energy
drink company, co-ownership of a caffeine-metabolite ingredient firm with patent applications,
and the ISSN's own receipt of sponsorship from companies selling caffeine-based ergogenic aids.

**Self-citation is the bigger problem, and it is not a declarable conflict.** The
[EAA stand](essential-amino-acids.md) is substantively a review of its own authors' laboratory
output, and the one published study matching free amino acids to a protein's exact composition
— which the intact protein won — is absent from its 127 references. The
[probiotics](probiotics.md) recovery recommendation rests on two studies by the stand's own lead
author, n=15 and n=29, with no independent replication. The [2025 HMB](hmb-2025.md) kinetics and
safety evidence was largely written by its own authors. The
[2017 protein](protein-2017.md) safety case rests entirely on one lab.

**Six of the older articles carry no retrievable declaration at all**, which should be read as
a limitation of the record rather than evidence of independence.

## What this corpus adds that our other sources don't

- **Populations nobody else here covers.** The [female athlete stand](female-athlete.md) is the
  single most valuable document in the folder for this library, which is otherwise male-default
  throughout — menstrual cycle, hormonal contraception, energy availability and RED-S,
  peri- and post-menopause. It is also unusually honest about absence, stating outright that no
  studies exist on protein requirements across the cycle or on contraceptives, and that several
  of its recommendations are male-derived defaults carried across with a caveat. Note the gap:
  **bone health is essentially missing** — no calcium, no vitamin D, no bone stress injury
  guidance — which is a real omission given bone stress injury is among the commonest
  presentations of RED-S.
- **Endurance and extreme demands** — [ultra-marathon](ultra-marathon.md) fuelling and
  gastrointestinal tolerance, [tactical athlete](tactical-athlete.md) load carriage and sleep
  deprivation. Neither has any counterpart in the lifting-focused material.
- **Combat-sport rapid weight loss** ([stand](weight-cutting-strategies.md)) — adjacent to
  bodybuilding peak week but far more dangerous, and covered nowhere else in the library.
- **A dose reference for compounds the creators skip** — sodium bicarbonate, beta-alanine,
  probiotics by strain, omega-3.
- **The antioxidant hormesis argument** ([stand](dietary-antioxidants.md)), which is the same
  phenomenon as the cold-water-immersion finding elsewhere in this library by a different route:
  reactive oxygen species *are* the adaptation signal, and blunting them blunts the adaptation.
  Implicated: vitamin C 1,000 mg with vitamin E 400 IU chronically, resveratrol 250 mg/day,
  high-dose pre-workout EGCG. Not implicated: astaxanthin, tart cherry, sulforaphane, creatine,
  omega-3.

## Where the ISSN differs from popular evidence-based lifting advice

- **No per-meal protein ceiling.** The 2017 stand gives 20–40 g and cites 40 g beating 20 g
  after whole-body work, ~40 g for older lifters and ~70 g to suppress breakdown. It got there
  in 2017, years before the "20–25 g and the rest is wasted" framing faded.
- **Creatine cycling.** The 2007 stand endorsed it; the 2017 stand dropped it without comment.
  Neither says "never cycle" — that advice has no ISSN citation behind it.
- **Caffeine tolerance.** No stand recommends cycling off, and the 2021 stand finds the tolerance
  literature three-ways contradictory. Critically, **no study tests the actual protocol** —
  abstain, retest, confirm the response is restored. Our library's "tolerance is insurmountable"
  framing is stronger than the ISSN evidence supports.
- **The questions lifters actually ask about creatine are absent.** Neither creatine stand
  mentions DHT, hair loss, non-responders or the caffeine interaction anywhere. The popular
  account sources those from primary literature the ISSN never cites.

## Errors found in the published articles

Recorded in the individual notes. They are worth knowing about because they show that a
position stand's citation list is not self-verifying.

- [**2018 review**](issn-review-update-2018.md): the phosphatidic acid section cites "Joy et al."
  for an 8-week training study; the reference is a paper on seasonal reproduction in vertebrates.
- [**2026 antioxidants**](dietary-antioxidants.md): an **unremoved peer-reviewer comment** sits in
  the published Table 2 footnote — "I recommend revisiting these values and potentially narrowing
  them…" Nobody acted on it or deleted it.
- [**2021 caffeine**](caffeine-2021.md): a bullet claims a genotype-specific basketball effect
  citing a paper titled *"…does not alter the effects of caffeine on basketball performance."*
- [**2017 protein**](protein-2017.md) and [**2017 timing**](nutrient-timing-2017.md): several
  citation-numbering faults and duplicated reference entries.
- [**2007 creatine**](creatine-2007.md): cites a *null* ACL rehabilitation trial in support of
  creatine reducing musculoskeletal injuries.
- [**2015 beta-alanine**](beta-alanine.md): a units error — "4.8–6.8 g/kg/day" should be g/day.
- [**2023 energy drinks**](energy-drinks-2023.md): its inline citation markers drift out of
  alignment with its own reference list by up to three positions, so marker [135] is not
  reference 135. The note maps by author and study rather than by number.
