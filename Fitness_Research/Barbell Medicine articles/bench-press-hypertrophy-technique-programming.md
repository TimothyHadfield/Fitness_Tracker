# The Bench Press Is One Tool, Not a Technique Puzzle — a Short, Uncited Restatement

**Source:** Barbell Medicine, "Bench Press for Hypertrophy: Technique and Programming" ([read](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/blog/bench-press-for-hypertrophy/)) · Barbell Medicine · 2023-03-19 · 1,167 words
**Topic:** Exercise selection & technique

## Bottom line
- **No citations at all** — no reference list, no markers, no links to research. This is a short content page, not an evidence review.
- The position: the bench press trains chest, triceps and anterior deltoid, tolerates heavy loads and a wide rep range, and is **useful but not required** for chest hypertrophy.
- **Grip width and bar path are explicitly deflated.** Small differences in either are said to be unlikely to change hypertrophy outcomes for most people; the useful property is that your setup is *repeatable*, so progress can be tracked.
- Their one geometric recommendation: a **moderate grip, roughly the width that leaves the forearms vertical at the bottom**. A wider grip shortens the range and shifts emphasis to the chest; a narrower one lengthens it and recruits more triceps.
- Arch is presented as a preference, not a technique error — a pronounced arch and a flatter back are both said to work.
- On effort, the same line as the rest of this cluster: sets must be genuinely hard, but **not every set to failure**, because the fatigue cost competes with the rest of the week's training.
- Programming role: use it as the **trackable heavy movement**, then buy additional chest volume more cheaply with machine presses and flyes.

## What is actually being claimed

The article's argument is that the bench press's effectiveness "depends less on small technical details and more on how it is used within a broader training program" — the same demotion of exercise-level detail made in [what drives hypertrophy](drives-hypertrophy-muscle-growth-works.md) and [how to choose exercises](choose-exercises-hypertrophy.md), of which this is the worked example.

The setup they describe is conventional: eyes under the bar, feet planted, upper back in contact with the bench, a controlled descent to the chest or near it, press to lockout. On range of motion they are careful in a way worth noting — touching the chest gives a longer range than stopping short, **but the depth may legitimately vary with shoulder tolerance**. That is load modification framed as technique, and it is the clinical reasoning this source is good at.

Their four named mistakes are treating the lift as essential, over-attending to minor technical detail, training to failure too often, and changing execution frequently enough that progress becomes unreadable.

## What this note cannot do

Every substantive claim here — that grip width does not matter much, that bar path need not be standardised, that arching is a preference — is asserted without a study. **Do not treat this article as the evidence for any of them.**

Barbell Medicine has a much longer and properly referenced article on the same lift, and that is where the outcome data lives: flat versus incline versus decline producing no meaningful difference in pec hypertrophy or pressing strength, push-ups matching the bench press for chest growth, and an explicit statement that EMG differences do not license inferences about growth. See [the bench press, technique, benefits and muscles worked](bench-press-technique-benefits-muscles-worked.md) rather than this page when the question is what the evidence shows.

This page's contribution over that one is narrow but real: it is the only place they state the **programming** role plainly — heavy trackable movement first, cheap volume afterwards.

**They sell the alternative to thinking about it.** The article closes by recommending Barbell Medicine's Hypertrophy I template.
