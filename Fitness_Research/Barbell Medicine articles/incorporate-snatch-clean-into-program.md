# The Olympic Lifts Are Not Assistance Work — Put Them in GPP and Enjoy Them

**Source:** Barbell Medicine, "How-To: Incorporate the Snatch and Clean into Your Program" ([read](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/blog/how-to-incorporate-the-snatch-and-clean-into-your-program/)) · Hassan Mansour · 2020-12-23 · 1,256 words
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- **Their position: snatches and cleans are poor assistance exercises for the squat, bench, press and deadlift.** The stated reason is specificity — contraction velocity, absolute load and bar positions all differ too much from the deadlift for the transfer to be worth the slot. For a bigger deadlift, do deadlifts and closer variants.
- **The justification for doing them anyway is adherence, not adaptation.** Learning a skill is enjoyable, and enjoyable training is more likely to be sustained. That is the whole argument, and they state it as such rather than dressing it up.
- **High-velocity force production is explicitly deflated as a goal** for anyone not competing in a sport that needs it — and they list cheaper substitutes (lighter barbell work moved fast, plyometrics, medicine ball throws).
- **Who should skip them:** beginners and youth athletes without an established strength base, who they say should build strength first and develop power later ([Behm 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28713281/)); anyone in specific competition preparation, rehabbing an injury, or short on recovery capacity.
- **Their entry condition** is a healthy lifter with a strength base, not preparing for a competition, and already meeting the [US adult physical activity guidelines](https://health.gov/sites/default/files/2019-09/Physical_Activity_Guidelines_2nd_edition.pdf).
- **The mechanism for fitting them in is a swap, not an addition:** take the upper-back or ab slot on a GPP day and put the lift there. They must not displace the primary lifts.

## How they actually programme it

Two sampling templates for a lifter training four days plus two GPP days.

*Snatch.* Day 1 — power snatch with a 2-count pause below the knee, **2 reps at RPE 6–7 for 6–8 sets**, then one-arm dumbbell rows as a 7-minute AMRAP and 20 minutes of rowing intervals (20 s sprint at RPE 9–10, 90 s rest). Day 2 — snatch plus overhead squat complex, **2 + 2 at RPE 6–7 for 4–5 sets**, toes-to-bar AMRAP, 30 minutes steady state at RPE 6.

*Clean.* Day 1 — power clean worked up to 2 reps at RPE 6–7, then a **10-minute EMOM of 2 reps**. Day 2 — clean, **3 reps at RPE 7 for 4–6 sets**.

The design logic is worth extracting: **low reps, low RPE, many sets**. The pauses and complexes exist to constrain technique and cap the load — a paused snatch at the knee forces the lifter to stay over the bar, and pairing every snatch with an overhead squat buys exposure to the receiving position. They are explicit that this volume will not drive Olympic-lift strength long term; it is a sampling period.

A third template covers the lifter who decides to compete: a four-day microcycle pairing a classical or partial-range Olympic lift with a squat or pull and a press each day.

## What is evidence and what is coaching

**Almost all of this is coaching judgement**, and the article does not pretend otherwise — the RPE prescriptions, set counts, variant progressions and the swap-a-GPP-slot mechanism carry no citations.

**The one research citation is used loosely.** Behm 2017 is a systematic review and meta-analysis comparing traditional strength training with power training in youth. It is offered here to support "beginners or youth without an established strength base should skip the Olympic lifts and work on power development later" — a recommendation adjacent to what that paper tested, not the finding itself. The advice is reasonable; the citation is doing less work than its placement implies.

**They sell things, and they flag it.** The beginner recommendation comes with an explicit "here comes the plug" for Barbell Medicine's Beginner Program, and the competing-lifter section points at their Olympic weightlifting template. The self-aware plug is unusual and worth crediting.
