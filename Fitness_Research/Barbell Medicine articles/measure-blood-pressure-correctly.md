# How to Measure Blood Pressure at Home, and What the Numbers Mean

**Source:** Barbell Medicine, "How To Measure Your Blood Pressure Correctly" ([read](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/blog/how-to-measure-blood-pressure/)) · Austin Baraki · 2025-09-03 · 966 words
**Topic:** Health & medicine

## Bottom line
- A high **office** reading is not a diagnosis. Guidelines require out-of-office measurements to confirm before treatment starts ([USPSTF 2021](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/hypertension-in-adults-screening)).
- **Cuff size is the biggest source of error you control.** Too small reads falsely high; too large reads falsely low.
- Use a **validated automatic** device — the list is at [ValidateBP.org](https://www.validatebp.org). Not a wrist gadget, not an unvalidated cheap monitor.
- For a home evaluation, take **two readings morning and two evening for at least three days** ([Bello 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30371272/)).
- Healthy resting BP is **below 120/80 mmHg**. If already diagnosed with hypertension, the guideline target is **under 130/80**, ideally under 120 systolic where feasible.
- A high reading without symptoms is a primary-care problem, not an emergency.

## Cuff size by arm circumference
Measure around the upper arm with a tape, then match ([AHA/Target:BP](https://targetbp.org/patient-measured-bp/implementing/smbp-selecting-the-right-cuff-size/)):

| Arm circumference (cm) | Arm circumference (in) | Cuff (width × length, cm) |
|---|---|---|
| 22–26 | 8.7–10.2 | 12 × 22 (small adult) |
| 27–34 | 10.6–13.4 | 16 × 30 (adult) |
| 35–44 | 13.8–17.3 | 16 × 36 (large adult) |
| 45–52 | 17.7–20.5 | 16 × 42 (extra large adult) |

Most modern devices ship with a variable-size cuff spanning small to large adult. Lifters with large arms should check they are not above the supplied range.

## The procedure
1. Quiet room, chair **with back support**, and a table.
2. Cuff on the upper arm with the **artery indicator line over the inner arm**; the lower edge of the cuff **2–3 cm (about an inch) above the elbow crease**.
3. Arm supported on a table, box or pillows so it sits **at heart height**, relaxed, **palm up**.
4. Sit upright, back supported, **legs uncrossed, feet flat on the floor**, and **sit quietly for five minutes** before the first reading.
5. Take the reading while relaxed, then **repeat a second time**.

## How often, and what counts as abnormal
Healthy adults with no hypertension diagnosis: check resting BP **at least once a year** (USPSTF). Anyone under evaluation needs out-of-office readings as well as office ones — the three-day, four-readings-a-day protocol above.

The preferred confirmatory test for **white coat hypertension** (high in clinic, normal outside) and **masked hypertension** (the reverse) is **24-hour ambulatory monitoring**, with a monitor worn continuously and readings taken automatically through the day. Where that is not available, a properly calibrated home machine used carefully is the fallback.

Interpretation is deliberately loose: transiently higher readings during exercise or stress are expected and appropriate, and resting pressures well below 120/80 are often normal in an asymptomatic person. What matters is **the average resting pressure over time**, not any single number.

The urgency rule is the practically useful part: a high reading **without** symptoms can be worked on gradually with a primary-care doctor, and emergency care is rarely needed. Escalate quickly only with chest pain, shortness of breath, vision changes, confusion, or [stroke-like symptoms](https://www.cdc.gov/stroke/signs_symptoms.htm).

## Evidence versus recommendation
Only one primary study is cited, and it supports one specific claim: **Bello 2018** established how many home readings are needed for a reliable estimate. The device, cuff-size and positioning instructions come from guideline and society sources (USPSTF, AHA/Target:BP) rather than from studies quoted in the article, and the "under 130/80, ideally under 120" target is a guideline recommendation, cited here as such without a trial reference. Nothing in the article overreaches — it is a procedure article and the procedure is the standard one.

The article links to Barbell Medicine's own [Guide to High Blood Pressure](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/blog/a-basic-guide-to-blood-pressure-part-i/) for management, which is free content, not a product.
