# Protein Does Not Slow Digestion — a 2014 Forum Rebuttal, Correct and Entirely Uncited

**Source:** Barbell Medicine, "Protein Slows Digestion? Nope." ([read](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/blog/protein-slows-digestion-nope/)) · Jordan Feigenbaum · 2014-03-26 · 1,012 words
**Topic:** Nutrition

## Bottom line
- **This is a line-by-line takedown of a low-quality web article**, reposted from a Starting Strength forum. It is the earliest and least formal item in this source's corpus and it **cites nothing whatsoever**.
- **The central claim it kills:** high protein intake does not slow gastric emptying because "the body has to work to digest it." Protein is broken down by stomach acid and pancreatic enzymes and absorbed rapidly — **whey raises plasma amino acids within about 20 minutes** of ingestion.
- **Carbohydrate-rich and protein-rich foods empty from the stomach at roughly the same rate.** Normal gastric emptying after a meal is **2–6 hours**.
- **What actually governs emptying speed**, on his account: fat content (slows it), fibre type (**soluble slows, insoluble speeds**), total calories (larger meals empty slower), tonicity (**isotonic empties faster than either hypo- or hypertonic**), the hormonal state left by previous meals (ghrelin rises with hunger and increases gut motility), food already in the tract, and drugs. Protein is not on the list.
- **His methodological objection is the best part** and generalises well beyond this topic: the original article never defines a normal transit time, never defines a slow one, and **never separates a pathologically slow state — gastroparesis, ileus, obstruction — from an ordinary meal-composition effect.** Without those definitions the claim cannot be evaluated at all.

## The corrections he makes in passing

*Absorption is not "into the blood and off to the organs".* Amino acids and monosaccharides cross the small-intestinal brush border into the enterocyte, enter the portal vein, and go **to the liver first** before distribution. Fats are absorbed as fatty acids, packaged into chylomicrons with cholesterol and phospholipids, and enter the **lymphatic system** before the venous circulation — going preferentially to tissues rich in mitochondria or peroxisomes for oxidation, chiefly liver and skeletal muscle.

*Diarrhoea is not slow digestion.* It is contents moving too fast. IBS presents as a mixture of diarrhoea, constipation, pain and bloating, so citing it as a cause of sluggishness is incoherent.

*Crohn's disease does not slow transit.* It is dysregulated inflammation in response to gut wall bacteria causing direct mucosal injury. Malabsorption occurs because **the mucosa cannot absorb** what is passing at normal speed — not because the food moves slowly.

*Gastroparesis has known causes*, and he lists them: most commonly diabetes mellitus, idiopathic, and post-surgical (particularly with vagus nerve damage); then medications, alcohol, tobacco and cannabis, infections (mononucleosis, Chagas, rotavirus), CNS injury, Parkinson's and Guillain-Barré, and a tail of systemic disease.

## How much weight this can carry

**None of it is referenced.** Every figure — the 20-minute whey response, the 2–6 hour emptying window, the tonicity and fibre effects, the equivalence of protein and carbohydrate emptying rates — is stated from a physician's recall in a forum post. The physiology is standard textbook material and the conclusion is almost certainly right, but **this article is not the evidence for it** and should not be cited as such.

The tone is worth flagging for anyone reading the corpus in order: this is 2014 Feigenbaum, informal, profane in places, and rhetorically confident in a way the later clinical articles are not. The reasoning is sound; the register is a forum argument, not a review.

**Nothing is sold in this article** — no template, no supplement, no seminar.
