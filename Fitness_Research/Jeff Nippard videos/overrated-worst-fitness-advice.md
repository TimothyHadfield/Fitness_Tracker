# 19 Fitness Topics Rated Overrated or Underrated (with Dr Mike Israetel)

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "OVERRATED: The Worst Fitness Advice Ever" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EEOjsbQ5wYE)) · 2023-12-16 · 14 min
**Topic:** Myths & Evidence

## Bottom line
- Cheat reps, bro splits, natural test boosters, cold showers, NoFap, "locking out is dangerous" and Natty-or-Not content are all **overrated**.
- Training to failure: both agree it is **overrated** — the meta-regression favouring failure only holds at low volumes, low frequency and low training status.
- Diet soda, diet breaks, recomping (Jeff's view) and pumps (Mike's view) are **underrated**.
- 1 g protein per lb bodyweight is **properly rated** — slightly more than needed, which is the point; Jeff would drop to 0.7-0.8 g/lb when bulking.
- Diet break protocol: after losing 5-8% of bodyweight, take 2-3 months at maintenance before dieting again.

## Training

**Cheat reps / ego lifting — overrated (both).** Mike: higher injury risk, lower chance of stimulating the target muscle, and progress becomes untrackable. Jeff partly dissents on isolation work — a little momentum on a bicep curl that buys one more controlled eccentric can be worth it — but still calls them mostly overrated.

**Muscle soreness — split.** Jeff: overrated. It is a reasonable proxy that you hit the target muscle, but the belief that no soreness means no growth is false. Mike pushes back on the fashionable "soreness is only novelty" line: more volume, more intensity, proper progression, proximity to failure, metabolite accumulation, eccentric focus and training at long muscle lengths all increase soreness, and all link positively to hypertrophy. His practical rule: not getting sore is fine, but if you are not progressing and feel under-stimulated, trying to get sore is a reasonable move; if you are sore, stimulus isn't your problem.

**Bro split (each muscle once weekly) — overrated (both).** Mike: small muscles like rear delts or biceps do not need a week to recover, so you are wasting days; train a muscle when it recovers, which is roughly every day to twice a week. Jeff: the latest frequency research shows a bro split is viable, just not optimal — for the same number of gym days you get better results from another structure.

**Training to failure — overrated (both).** [02:34] Jeff's original take: research shows little hypertrophy or strength difference between going to failure and stopping a couple of reps short; you should know what failure feels like, but its importance is overstated. Mike adds that failure advocacy is often psychological catharsis rather than optimisation.

[03:05] Jeff then updates mid-edit: a brand new meta-analysis found training to failure was on average better for hypertrophy than not. That doesn't mean every set — but taking no sets to failure leaves gains on the table. He revises to "properly rated to slightly overrated." He phones Mike, who doubles down: that meta-regression's failure advantage appears **only in people training low volume, low effort, lower training status, and low frequency**. In serious, high-volume, high-effort trainees the same study shows no advantage to failure.

**Mind-muscle connection — overrated (both).** Mike: a quaternary or tertiary variable, and people often pursue it at the cost of good technique, adequate load, correct exercise choice and progression. It matters once technique and progression are already in place. Jeff: a good mind-muscle connection on an insufficiently hard set still won't grow much.

**Pumps — Jeff properly rated, Mike underrated.** Jeff: fun, make training more enjoyable, probably some connection to hypertrophy. Mike: a good gauge of whether the target muscle was hit and where, and a useful gauge of volume.

## Nutrition

**Dirty bulking — overrated (both).** Disproportionately more fat gain than muscle gain. Mike cites his own stretch marks and loose skin as irreversible costs and wishes he had stayed leaner.

**Natural testosterone boosters — overrated (both).** Mike: no such thing. Jeff: even if a herb raised serum testosterone, there is no evidence it would change muscle mass noticeably. Mike's mechanism argument: if the body detects a serum testosterone rise it downregulates its own production, so anything that both raises testosterone and sustains endogenous production is a multi-compound pharmacological drug, not something growing on a tree.

**1 g protein per lb bodyweight — properly rated (both).** Mike: slightly more than needed in almost all cases, and that margin removes the need to fuss over protein quality; it is also easy to remember. Jeff agrees for cutting; when bulking he thinks **0.7-0.8 g/lb** is enough.

**Body recomposition — split.** Jeff: underrated — most of the general population can build muscle and lose fat simultaneously, contrary to "you must bulk and cut." Mike: valid, but effective isn't optimal — anything recomping achieves, dedicated cutting and bulking achieves better, and recomping runs its course in roughly a year, after which you need bigger levers.

**Diet soda — underrated (both).** Mike: essentially inert healthwise by almost all research accounts, and beliefs that it is as bad as or worse than regular soda are unfounded. Jeff: switching from regular to diet alone produces weight loss for many, and it is an underused tool for satiety and craving control while dieting.

**Diet breaks — underrated (both).** Jeff: people rush fat loss; easing off periodically lets training improve and muscles refill — a week or two at maintenance or slightly above pays off long term. Mike's protocol: if you need to lose more than about 10% of bodyweight, you will need a multi-month break for diet fatigue to clear. **Lose 5-8% of bodyweight, take 2-3 months at maintenance, then diet again.**

**Clean eating — overrated (both, with nuance).** Jeff: hard to define, definitions vary, extremes lead to disordered eating; better to take an inclusive approach with a variety of foods including plenty of fruit and vegetables and nothing strictly banned. Mike: it is definable — the absence of intentionally hyper-palatable processed junk — and removing those foods becomes hugely advantageous as a fat-loss phase gets hard, while fun foods are fine during mass gain, maintenance and the early part of a diet. Jeff concedes the fat-loss-phase point.

## Hot topics

**Natty-or-Not videos — overrated (both).** Jeff: innocent until proven guilty; speculating on scant evidence damages reputations, and the viewer gains nothing since you cannot look like anyone else anyway due to genetic differences. Mike: genuine journalistic exposure of someone who commercially committed to being drug-free and is lying is worthwhile; yelling at a camera with zero research is not. Mike states on camera he is not natural.

**"Natural training is completely different from enhanced training" — overrated.** Mike: an excuse for naturals who aren't growing and for enhanced lifters who don't want to read the science. Enhanced lifters do recover better, but the moment they get bigger and stronger that advantage is cancelled out by the recovery cost of carrying more muscle — it levels out, so training principles are training principles.

**Cold showers — overrated (both).** Mike calls them a research-vetted way to reduce hypertrophy; if you need shock therapy for sympathetic drive, you aren't training hard enough. Jeff: lots of hype, very little evidence — except for the blunting of hypertrophy, which does appear real.

**NoFap / semen retention — overrated.** Mike: no research support for the claimed toxicity of masturbation; genuinely unhealthy patterns exist but affect a small minority who should seek treatment. Jeff: no evidence of a substantial long-term testosterone boost or improved training performance.

**"Locking out the knees is dangerous" — overrated.** Mike: neither evidence nor rationale; knees are built to lock out. Control the lockout, don't snap into it or switch the muscles off at the top, and absent a laxity disorder you're fine — you want strong knees. Jeff: part of a broader fragile-body mindset that isn't true.

**Filming in gyms — underrated / over-hated (both).** Fine as long as you respect people who don't want to be on camera and follow gym rules. Mike's argument: public space implies consent to appear in the background of video, increasingly so as AR wearables spread; a private home gym is a different matter.

## Practical application
Don't chase failure on every set — know what it feels like, take some sets there, but stop treating it as the main lever if you already train hard and often. Train each muscle roughly twice a week rather than once. Use momentum sparingly and only on isolation work. Protein at 1 g/lb cutting, 0.7-0.8 g/lb bulking. Use diet soda freely as a satiety tool. Plan diet breaks: 5-8% loss, then 2-3 months at maintenance. Clean up hyper-palatable foods specifically when the cut gets hard. Skip test boosters, cold showers post-training, and Natty-or-Not content.

## Caveats & unknowns
The failure literature is openly conflicting and the two men reach different emphases from the same meta-regression; its pro-failure result appears to be moderated by volume, effort, training status and frequency, which is itself a subgroup interpretation. Soreness as a hypertrophy signal is contested — Mike argues mechanism, Jeff argues it is a poor proxy. Recomping's ceiling (about a year) is Mike's estimate, not a cited finding. Mike is enhanced, which he discloses.
