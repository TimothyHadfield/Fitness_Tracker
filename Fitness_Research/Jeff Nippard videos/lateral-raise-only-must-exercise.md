# Are Squats, Bench and Deadlift Essential? Response to "The Lateral Raise Is The Only Must-Do Exercise"

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Lateral Raise Is The ONLY Must-Do Exercise (MY RESPONSE)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IdNOahFD450)) · 2018-12-22 · 23 min
**Topic:** Exercise Selection

## Bottom line
- Coach Cliff Wilson claimed bodybuilders over-emphasise squat/bench/deadlift and that the closest thing to a mandatory movement is the dumbbell lateral raise. Jeff agrees with the spirit, disagrees on details.
- For bodybuilding there are genuinely no required exercises — the judges (and the beach) only see the physique, not how it was built. Muscle responds to tension, and tension can be created many ways.
- The powerlifts are still excellent bodybuilding tools: highly motivating, huge muscle mass recruited, very time-efficient. Injuries usually come from ego and bad programming, not from the lifts themselves.
- Exercise variation beats a single-exercise approach: Fonseca and colleagues (2014, Jacob Wilson a co-author) found varied leg exercises grew all quadriceps heads, while squat-only produced no significant growth in vastus medialis or rectus femoris ([Fonseca 2014](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/24832974)).
- Jeff's own pick for the single most "must-do" movement would be the **upright row** (side delts plus upper traps), or a cable lateral raise ahead of the dumbbell version.

## The claim being answered
Wilson's post: too many bodybuilders treat squat, deadlift and bench as essential; in bodybuilding no exercise is mandatory, and the nearest thing to one is the dumbbell lateral raise. Jeff's Instagram poll on it split roughly 58% agree / 42% disagree.

Jeff rates each line on a 1-5 agree scale:
- "Too much emphasis on squat/bench/deadlift" — **hard 2 (mostly disagree)**. That bandwagon peaked around 2014-2015 and has faded. Most NPC/IFBB bodybuilders already use huge exercise variety, mind-muscle work, constant tension and mid-range training; many would actually benefit from *more* heavy low-rep compound work in the offseason to build a strength base and allow proper periodisation.
- "People act like these exercises are essential" — **4 (agree)**. Nothing is required; muscle mainly registers tension (plus some damage and metabolic stress), and tension can be delivered many ways.
- "In bodybuilding there are no must-do exercises" — **5 (fully agree)**. Only the finished physique is judged.
- "The lateral raise is the closest to a must-do" — **3 (neutral)**, for the reasons below.

## Efficiency versus effectiveness
Two legitimate camps: coaches optimising results *per unit of time/effort*, and coaches (like Wilson, and Jeff himself) optimising results full stop. Neither is wrong — it depends on whether you want to be a top competitor or just look good and still have a life.

**The maximally efficient routine** Jeff sketches [10:52]: six exercises — squat, Romanian deadlift (he prefers RDL to conventional for bodybuilders), bench press, overhead press, barbell/Pendlay row, pull-up. Train **2 days/week**, three exercises per day (e.g. Mon: squat, pull-up, bench; Thu: RDL, Pendlay row, overhead press), **4-6 sets per movement**, about **1 hour per session** — roughly **2 hours total per week**, hitting every body part twice weekly. His estimate: around **80% of the results of a far more elaborate program for about 20% of the time**.

The effective routine instead asks what can be *added* to fill the gaps rather than what can be stripped away.

## Where the minimalist six-exercise plan leaves holes
- **Side delts** — bench is a front-delt/pec/tricep lift, overhead press is mostly front delt (a little more side delt standing with dumbbells), rows cover rear delts. Nothing there properly loads the lateral delt. This is the real gap, and capped side delts are central to the X-frame that wins shows.
- **Calves** — only stabilisation work from squats/deadlifts. But calves rarely decide a bodybuilding show, so side delts rank higher.
- **Upper traps** — rows hit mid/lower traps well; upper traps get only isometric work from deadlifts. A shrug would fill it, but again traps don't win shows.

## Why upright row over the dumbbell lateral raise
The upright row trains **shoulder abduction and scapular elevation together**, covering both the side delts and the neglected upper traps in one movement. Jeff would also rank the **cable lateral raise above the dumbbell version** because the dumbbell's circular resistance path only gives peak tension near the top, while the cable gives a more consistent resistance curve.

Counterpoint in Wilson's favour: the dumbbell lateral raise is multi-joint-free, uses light loads and is far less fatiguing, so you can hammer side delts **4-6 days per week** with high volume at low recovery cost — provided you manage proximity to failure.

## Practical application
- Don't treat any lift as compulsory. If squats, bench or deadlifts hurt or feel wrong for you, substitute freely; the muscle doesn't know the exercise name.
- Keep the powerlifts if they motivate you and you can progress them without ego-driven form breakdown. Blame reckless programming, not the lifts.
- Vary exercises within a muscle group rather than riding one movement — expect more even growth across all heads.
- Whatever minimal template you run, deliberately add a **direct side-delt movement** (upright row, cable or dumbbell lateral raise). Add calves and shrugs after that if you want completeness.
- Side-delt isolation is cheap to recover from — high weekly frequency is viable.

## Caveats & unknowns
- The Fonseca finding is on quadriceps only; extending it to back, chest etc. is Jeff's reasoning by analogy, and he says so explicitly — no data.
- The wider-grip/long-head speculation and the upright row's superiority are opinion, not tested.
- The upright row involves more joints and heavier loads, so it costs more recovery and may aggravate some shoulders — a real point in the lateral raise's favour.
- The whole "pick one exercise" framing is artificial; in practice you fill gaps as needed rather than minimising.
