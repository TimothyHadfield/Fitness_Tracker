# Core Training: How to Make the Plank Actually Hard

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "How To Build A Better Core & Six Pack Abs: Optimal Training Explained" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1G0y8D5rFDc)) · 2019-08-13 · 8 min
**Topic:** Technique

## Bottom line
- The standard plank is **too easy** to be a strong stimulus (Guinness record: 8 hours, 1 minute). Two modifications from **Schoenfeld's 2014 study** fix that: **move the elbows forward to eye level** and **squeeze the glutes into posterior pelvic tilt**.
- Programming: **3 sets of 20-40 second holds.** When that gets easy, don't add time — make the position harder.
- Progress the plank by **elbows forward → elbows closer together → pull elbows toward toes and toes toward elbows**, not by extending duration or stacking plates.
- A complete core program needs three patterns: **weighted spinal flexion** (crunches), **anti-rotation** (half-kneeling Pallof press), and **anti-extension** (plank).
- The **ab wheel rollout** beat crunches, supine double leg thrusts and side bridges for rectus abdominis activation (Escamilla/Yudice-style 2008 study). Progress it by **increasing range of motion**, not load.

## What the plank actually trains [00:03]
The plank is **anti-extension**: the core contracts isometrically to hold a neutral spine. Relax and the midsection drops and the lumbar spine extends — so the **rectus abdominis**, **transverse abdominis (TVA)** and **obliques** all fire to prevent hyperextension.

Two reasons to keep it in a program even though it's isometric:
1. Core stability carries over strongly to heavy compounds like squats and deadlifts.
2. Several researchers theorise that **core muscle endurance, rather than maximal core strength, is what best combats spinal instability and low back pain** — which favours static endurance holds like the prone plank.

Planks can also drive **hypertrophy**, but only if **progressive overload** is genuinely applied — the point most people miss.

## Setting up the basic plank [01:34]
- Push-up position on a comfortable surface, **elbows directly under the shoulders** so the upper arm is roughly 90° to the floor.
- Up on the toes, body in a straight line head to toe.
- Cue: **pull the elbows back toward your body** to bias the anti-extension contraction.
- **3 sets of 20-40 second holds.** Being able to do this comfortably is a reasonable beginner fitness standard.

## Overloading options he rejects [02:05]
- **Adding duration** — makes training long and boring; almost never recommended.
- **Stacking plates on the lower back/glutes** — awkward to set up alone, and still not very challenging once you're advanced.
- Both are dismissed mainly because they **don't improve muscle recruitment patterns** in the plank itself.

## The EMG picture [02:37]
- Independently published EMG results (Bo Heck / Bret Contreras / Boskey) ranked the plank **fairly evenly across upper abs, lower abs and obliques** — well-rounded but not outstanding on any of them compared with other popular ab movements.
- Likely because the standard front plank is simply easy — **as of 2016 the Guinness plank record was 8 hours 1 minute**.
- **Schoenfeld 2014** found the same: the traditional front plank is not outstanding for core activation.

## The two Schoenfeld modifications [03:37]
Both produced **significantly more activation of upper abs, lower abs and external obliques**:
1. **Extend the lever arm** — walk the elbows forward until they're at **eye level**. This increases hip-to-elbow distance, so the abs must contract harder to resist spinal extension.
2. **Squeeze the glutes hard into posterior pelvic tilt** — the opposite of a "bikini pose" anterior tilt. Per the paper this creates **force coupling between the hip extensors (glutes and hamstrings) and the abdominal musculature**, strongly influencing core activity.

Hold this version for the same **20-40 seconds x 3 sets**. Once you top that out, two further progressions:
3. **Bring the elbows closer together** — narrows the base of support and raises stabilisation demand.
4. **Simultaneously pull the elbows toward the toes and the toes toward the elbows.** The natural tendency is for the hips to pike up; keeping the glutes squeezed in posterior tilt holds them down. Nippard notes that with all cues applied at once he starts shaking within **10-20 seconds**.

## Ab wheel rollout [05:07]
- A **2008 study from Yudice (Escamilla) and colleagues** tested four ab exercises — crunch, supine double leg thrust, side bridge and the ab wheel — and the **ab wheel came out on top for the rectus abdominis**.
- Conceptually it's a **dynamic, long-lever plank**: the abs resist the spine extending as the wheel travels out.
- **Progress by range of motion**, not weight. Stop the rollout at the point where you can no longer hold a neutral lumbar spine, and extend that distance gradually across a training cycle.
- It's an advanced exercise; a short starting ROM is normal. **Neutral spine always beats going all the way down.**

## Common errors [06:08]
- **Hips too high** — a lazy plank that takes the core out of the movement. Fix: squeeze the glutes to pull the hips toward neutral.
- **Hips sagging** — lumbar spine driven into extension; lazy and potentially riskier. Fix: crunch down on the abs, or use the "elbows toward toes, toes toward elbows" cue to lift the hips back to neutral.
- **Rollout: pulling the wheel back with the arms.** Initiate with the core and let the wheel come in as you crunch the abs.

## Practical application
1. Beginner: standard plank, **3 x 20-40 s**, elbows under shoulders, glutes squeezed.
2. Then upgrade to the Schoenfeld version — elbows at eye level, hard posterior pelvic tilt — same 3 x 20-40 s.
3. Then narrow the elbows and add the elbows-toward-toes/toes-toward-elbows cue.
4. Run **ab wheel rollouts alongside** the plank, progressing ROM over a cycle while holding a neutral lumbar spine.
5. Round out the program with **weighted spinal flexion** (crunches) and **anti-rotation** (half-kneeling Pallof press).

## Caveats & unknowns
- The endurance-over-strength theory for preventing low back pain is presented as researcher theorising, not settled fact.
- Plank hypertrophy is conditional on genuinely progressing the difficulty — without that it's a stability exercise, not a growth one.
- Ab wheel rollouts are advanced; don't chase ROM at the cost of a neutral spine.
