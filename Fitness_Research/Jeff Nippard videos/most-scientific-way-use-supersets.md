# Grouped vs Separated Supersets: What the Research Says

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Most Scientific Way to Use Supersets (New Research)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H6QIGfxN6b0)) · 2017-08-01 · 6 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- **Supersets are a time-saving tool, not a growth tool.** If time isn't limited, rest **3-5 minutes** between sets to optimise hypertrophy.
- **Grouped supersets** (two exercises for the *same* muscle back to back) kept creatine kinase — a muscle damage marker — elevated for **at least 5 days** and produced more soreness.
- **Separated supersets** (pairing unrelated muscles, e.g. bench press with leg extensions) are the better default when short on time: they don't degrade training quality or recovery nearly as much.
- The claimed activation benefit of grouped supersets was small, appeared in a **supporting** muscle rather than a prime mover, and in the chest case showed **front delts** taking over rather than more pec activation.
- Best of both worlds: **stagger** rest periods rather than truly superset — insert the secondary exercise mid-rest so total rest for the main lift is preserved.

## Definitions [00:00]

- **Grouped superset** — two exercises for the same muscle performed back to back with no rest (e.g. two chest exercises).
- **Separated superset** — two exercises for unrelated muscles performed back to back (e.g. a set of bench press followed immediately by a set of leg extensions).

Both save time by cutting rest periods. Grouped supersets are additionally promoted by bodybuilders as an intensity technique, on the theory that hitting one body part twice in a row activates more muscle.

## The study [01:03]

Covered in that month's issue of MASS (Monthly Applications in Strength Sport), which referenced over 50 peer-reviewed articles that issue.

- **20 trained men** — familiar with weight training but not competitive lifters ([Brentano 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/27243916)).
- A **single training session** each.
- **Grouped condition:** 5 sets of leg press and leg extension back to back, ~10 reps **to failure**, no rest between any sets. Then 3 minutes rest, then the same for bench press and pec deck.
- **Separated condition:** same volume, but bench press was supersetted with leg extensions (5 sets), 3 minutes rest, then leg press with pec deck.
- Outcomes measured: **EMG muscle activation** and indirect markers of muscle damage — **creatine kinase** and **muscle soreness (DOMS)**.

## Findings [01:34]

- **Creatine kinase** stayed elevated in the grouped superset group at **every time point through 5 days**, and may well have stayed elevated longer than the study measured.
- **DOMS** was higher with grouped supersets, but the difference **did not reach statistical significance**.
- **EMG, legs:** on the final few reps of the fifth set of leg extensions, grouped supersets showed more activation in the **rectus femoris** — but the increase was small, and rectus femoris is a supporting muscle. The prime movers for knee extension are the **vastus medialis, intermedius and lateralis**, which showed no such benefit. Whether this translates to meaningful quad hypertrophy is unclear.
- **EMG, chest:** the **anterior delts** were more active at some time points with grouped supersets — likely because the pecs were fatigued from bench pressing and the front delts compensated. This is a downside, not an upside: people doing pec deck want pec activation, not front delt activation. The study gives no support for grouped supersets as a way to activate the pecs more.

## Practical application

1. **Only superset because you're short on time.** Otherwise rest **3-5 minutes** between sets, per the current hypertrophy data ([Schoenfeld 2016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/26605807)).
2. **When time-limited, use separated supersets.** They preserve training quality and don't blunt recovery for as long.
3. **Avoid grouped supersets on high-frequency programs.** Up to 5 days of elevated muscle damage will degrade performance in later sessions that week.
4. **Grouped supersets have a place on low-frequency programs**, where you're resting a long time between bouts for the same muscle anyway, so the recovery cost matters less. Greg (in the MASS piece) also raises occasional use as a **shock microcycle** — if you want to blast a muscle group, grouped supersets demonstrably create more muscle damage.
5. **Better still — stagger rests instead of supersetting.** Example: on leg press with 3 minutes of rest between sets, don't do tricep extensions immediately after the leg press. Instead rest **1 minute**, do the tricep set, rest another **1 minute**, then return to leg press. Total rest between leg press sets is unchanged, and you get about a minute of global recovery so the tricep work isn't compromised by the leg press that preceded it.

## Caveats & unknowns
- This was a **single acute training session** in 20 trained men measuring activation and damage markers — **not** a long-term hypertrophy study. No conclusions about actual muscle growth can be drawn directly.
- EMG activation is not a reliable proxy for hypertrophy.
- The soreness difference wasn't statistically significant.
- Creatine kinase remained elevated past the final measurement, so the true recovery cost of grouped supersets is unknown.
