# Upright Row Technique: Avoiding Impingement and Maximizing Side Delt Growth

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "How To Fix The Upright Row: Avoid Shoulder Pain & Maximize Growth" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nwkLwMRHMQo)) · 2019-04-09 · 7 min
**Topic:** Technique

## Bottom line
- The upright row may be the **only multi-joint compound movement that heavily targets the lateral deltoid** rather than the front delt.
- Three safety modifications: **keep the bar close to the body**, **pull through the elbows out and up**, and **stop below shoulder height** (~90 degrees of elevation).
- Grip: slightly wide — a 2013 EMG study (McAllister et al.) found wide grip beat shoulder-width and close grip for lateral delt activation. Sweet spot **1 to 1.5x shoulder width**.
- Load in the **8-12 rep range**; going much heavier tends to invite lower-back and hip cheating.
- Best variation in his view: the **standing rope upright row** on a cable.

## Muscles worked [00:32]

- **Lateral delts** via shoulder abduction (arm out to the side, as in a lateral raise).
- **Upper traps** via scapular elevation (shrugging).
- **Biceps** via elbow flexion, though that range of motion is small.

Treat it as a shoulder-and-trap exercise, and modify it to be more shoulder-dominant, since upper traps are better overloaded through shrug variations anyway.

## Shoulder health [01:03]

A **2011 NSCA report** noted that **36% of weight training injuries occur at the shoulder complex**, and the upright row is thought to carry a fairly high risk profile because of potential **subacromial impingement**. Impingement is what happens when the space in the shoulder joint constricts and the bones jam against rotator cuff muscles or tendons, causing inflammation and pain. One reliable way to cause it: raise the arm above shoulder height in an **internally rotated** position (try it and you'll feel tightness at a certain point).

The NSCA report concluded upright rows **can still be safe and effective if proper precautions are followed**. Three modifications:

1. **Keep the bar close to the body.** Keeps tension on the lateral delt and keeps arm elevation more in the scapular plane.
2. **Pull through the elbows, not the wrists** — cue: row **out and up**, not straight up. This also stops the traps taking over by emphasising shoulder abduction over scapular elevation.
3. **Stop below shoulder height.** Most studies show impingement occurring between **70 and 120 degrees of elevation**, so the authors recommend staying just below 90 degrees. End the rep when the arms are parallel to the floor / elbows reach shoulder height.

Individual structure and mobility vary, so ignoring these doesn't guarantee injury — but they keep the shoulder safest for most people.

## Grip width [03:05]

A **2013 study by McAllister et al.** found a **wide grip produced more lateral delt EMG activation than a shoulder-width or close grip**. Because skeletons differ, find the most comfortable grip without going too narrow or too wide; **1 to 1.5 times shoulder width** is the usual sweet spot.

## Variations [03:35]

- **Standing rope upright row (favourite).** More freedom at the shoulder joint than a barbell, and you can cue **pulling the rope apart** as you initiate the concentric, which strongly engages the side delts. Double overhand grip, stable staggered stance just behind the cable, lean slightly back, pull the rope up along the front of the torso until elbows are level with (or just below) the shoulders, then lower under control.
- **Lying rope upright row** on a seated cable machine. Locks you in, prevents cheating, shifts more tension onto the delts.
- **One-arm dumbbell upright row.** Brace against the end of an incline bench with the non-lifting hand, lean slightly forward, lift the dumbbell out to the side. Feels like initiating a dumbbell lateral raise, but the elbow bends, which allows heavier loads.
- **Band upright rows.** Different resistance curve; used as part of a shoulder warm-up or as a high-rep finisher. Because tension is minimal at the bottom, **reverse the rep early** to keep constant tension on the delts.

## Loading [05:06]

**8-12 reps** is the recommended zone. It's a multi-joint compound so it can be loaded reasonably heavy, but in his experience lifters who go down toward **8 reps or lower** end up cheating with the lower back and hips and lose the mind-muscle connection with the side delts. Higher reps to burn out the shoulders at the end of a session are fine as long as form holds.

## Common errors [05:38]

- **Elbows too high.** Unlikely to hospitalise a healthy shoulder immediately, but contraindicated by the literature and worth avoiding for long-term rotator cuff health.
- **Turning it into a front raise.** Shifts emphasis from lateral to anterior delt, which already gets plenty of work from all overhead and horizontal pressing. Keep the bar or rope tight to the body and lift in the scapular plane, arms out to the sides as in a lateral raise.

## Practical application
- Rope or barbell upright row, grip 1-1.5x shoulder width, 8-12 reps, elbows leading out and up, stopping at shoulder height.
- Pair it with an overhead press for overall shoulder strength and lateral raises for isolated side-delt work — the upright row sits between the two.
