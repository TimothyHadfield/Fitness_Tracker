# Staying On Track With Training And Diet While Travelling

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "6 Ways To Always Stay On Track With Training & Diet" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D_HRI56H598)) · 2018-11-20 · 8 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- Collapse your split rather than skipping muscle groups: push/pull/legs becomes upper/lower (4 days) or full body (2 days) so everything still gets hit twice a week.
- When sessions are fewer and shorter, trade volume for intensity — train closer to failure and take more sets to failure.
- Autoregulate load off RPE (target roughly RPE 7-8) instead of chasing prescribed percentages when travel has wrecked your sleep and energy.
- Loosen diet precision one notch: meal plan to macros, macros to ranges, ranges to calories-plus-protein, or straight intuitive eating with the scale as a check.
- A short trip off-plan cannot undo months of habit. Most of the scale jump is water; resume your usual deficit and it comes back off.

## Training

**Default to keeping the program.** [01:00] The first tip is behavioural rather than physiological — build a track record of following through, so that "I'll train anyway" is the automatic answer. He points to friends and to Dwayne Johnson (who reportedly drops luggage at the hotel and goes straight to the gym on landing) as illustrations of the standard, while acknowledging plenty of people can't reasonably train at 2 a.m. in a town with no 24-hour gym.

**Modify the split, don't abandon it.** [02:32] The priority is preserving weekly frequency per muscle group, not preserving the exact template.
- 4 available gym days → upper/lower split
- 2 available gym days → full body
- Either way every muscle group still gets trained twice per week.

**Protect the stimulus with effort.** [03:02] The obvious problem with squeezing a week into two sessions is lost weekly volume. His fix is to raise proximity to failure — train nearer failure and take more sets to actual failure — so the hypertrophic stimulus stays as close to normal as possible even with less total work.

**Autoregulate.** [03:33] If your program prescribes a load or a %1RM, map it to a corresponding RPE, generally RPE 7-8. What produces an RPE 7-8 at home may be a different absolute weight than it is after a flight and bad sleep. Adjust the weight to the day rather than forcing the number and degrading your technique; effort stays matched, so results stay roughly the same.

## Diet

**Increase dietary flexibility by one step.** [04:33] A ladder of loosening, pick the rung that fits:
- On a rigid meal plan → work out its macros and just hit those macros with whatever food is available.
- Already tracking macros → convert them to ranges. His example: a 200 g/day carb target becomes 175-225 g.
- Already using ranges → drop to a calorie target plus a minimum protein goal.
- Or go fully intuitive, eating to appetite and hunger signals, using the scale as a sanity check on the ballpark. This is what he personally does.

**Pre-plan.** [05:35] Pack protein powder and protein bars so you're never short on protein or so hungry you overeat calorie-dense food by default. Specific trick: take a scoop of protein before going out for a big meal — it blunts appetite so you eat less out, and it banks protein toward the daily goal. On arrival, do a grocery run first so you can cook at the Airbnb instead of relying on restaurants for every meal.

**Don't let a bad week become a bad quarter.** [06:36] If you do fall off over three or four days or a week, the failure mode is using that as licence to stay off after returning home. Expect a scale spike from water retention and changed food and digestion, and don't read it as fat gain. Any actual fat added in a surplus comes off again once you resume your normal deficit; give it a week or two of patience. Long-term habits at home decide your physique, not one or two vacation weeks.

## Practical application
- Before a trip, decide how many gym days you realistically have, then pre-write the collapsed split (upper/lower for 4, full body for 2).
- In those sessions, cut set count if you must but push the sets you do keep closer to failure.
- Bring protein powder and bars in the luggage; shop for groceries before the first restaurant meal.
- Pick the loosest diet framework you can still trust yourself with, and set an explicit minimum daily protein number.
- Schedule the return to normal eating for the day you get home, and ignore the scale for the first week back.

## Caveats & unknowns
This is an experience-based tips video with no studies cited — the recommendations are practical heuristics rather than evidence claims. The frequency-preserving and effort-substitution advice lines up with his usual reading of the hypertrophy literature, but no data is presented here that shortened travel blocks maintain muscle equally well.
