# Building and Revealing a Six-Pack: Training, Diet and Supplements

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "Get Abs In 60 Days (Using Science)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tn-XvYG9x7w)) · 2024-01-15 · 9 min
**Topic:** Nutrition

## Bottom line
- Abs become visible around **20% body fat** for men and well-defined by **10%**; the liveable "six-pack sweet spot" is **10-20% for men, 18-28% for women**.
- Train abs like any other muscle with load and progression: **one weighted crunch + one leg raise, 2x per week**, last set to failure.
- Calories = bodyweight (lb) x **10-12**. Protein = goal bodyweight (lb) x **0.8-1**. Fat at least **50 g/day**. Remainder is free.
- Lose **0.5-1% of bodyweight per week**; if the cut runs past 3 months, insert a **2-3 week diet break at bodyweight x 16-18 calories**.
- Only three supplements are worth it: protein powder, **5 g/day creatine monohydrate**, and caffeine.

## Body fat and what you'll actually see
[00:00] At 50% and 40% the abs are buried; at 30% the stomach is flatter but still no definition; 20% is where abs appear; 10% gives a clear six-pack provided the muscle has been built. 6% is stage-lean — Jeff cites a real natural bodybuilder measured by DEXA at 6.2%. Below 8-10% for men or 18-20% for women you start hitting low energy, extreme hunger and reduced libido. Ab shape itself is genetic (Jeff has a four-pack at ~10%) but development is trainable.

## Ab training
[01:02] The "abs are a waste of time to train" argument fails by its own logic — nobody says don't train shoulders because you can't see them when soft. You train abs so there is enough muscle to show when you lean out, and hypertrophied abs pop more.

Fast-paced "fat-burning" ab circuits are essentially cardio: they never bring the abdominals close enough to failure to stimulate growth. Use progressive overload with load instead. Two exercises suffice.

**1. Weighted crunch.** Cable crunch with a rope: kneel, crunch letting the lower back round, squeeze the abs, don't yank with the arms. No cable — hold a plate to the chest and crunch hard. **3 sets of 10-12, twice weekly, last set to failure** (failure = cannot complete a full rep at maximum effort). Add weight or a rep each session with controlled form. This hits the whole six-pack but slightly favours the upper four.

**2. Leg raise** for lower-ab bias. Hanging from a bar, or a Roman chair — Jeff prefers the Roman chair because it locks the lower back and lets him direct attention to the abs. Can't do straight-leg yet? Bent-knee raises. **3 sets of 10-20, twice weekly, last set to failure.** Progress by reps: start at 3x10, add one rep per week to 3x20, then add ankle weights or slow the negative.

Planks are fine for core strength and stability but inefficient for building a six-pack — optional. Anti-rotation work hits the transverse abdominis and obliques well but doesn't load the six-pack muscles with comparable tension.

## Cardio
[04:03] Not required for fat loss, but new research shows combining weight training with cardio produces smaller waists than weight training alone. It also lets you eat more and more active people keep weight off better long term. **2-5 sessions of 30 min low-to-moderate intensity per week.**

## Diet setup
[04:33] Calories: current bodyweight in lb x 10-12. Protein: **goal** bodyweight in lb x 0.8-1. Fat: minimum 50 g/day. Everything left over is free calories from any macro.

Food choice matters far less than hitting the targets — the 6.2% natural bodybuilder ate Pop-Tarts regularly — but bias toward minimally processed whole foods most of the time.

## Common mistakes
[05:35] **Cutting too fast.** Aggressive deficits cost muscle and strength and invite a rebound. Target 0.5-1% bodyweight per week (1-2 lb/wk at 200 lb).

**Skipping the diet break.** Past 3 months of continuous dieting, take 2-3 weeks at bodyweight x 16-18 calories. You may gain a little water or keep losing — responses vary — but almost everyone loses better once the deficit resumes.

**Over-trusting the scale.** It misleads especially during recomposition. Take progress photos in identical lighting at least monthly, and monthly waist measurements.

**Setting too lean a goal.** See the body-fat thresholds above.

## Supplements
[07:00]
- **Protein powder** — not required, but an easy way to hit the daily total. Jeff has a 30-50 g shake after training, purely for convenience, not because of an anabolic window ([Schoenfeld 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30702982/)).
- **Creatine monohydrate, 5 g daily.** Timing irrelevant, no cycling needed, decades of research show no negative side effects, no watery or bloated look. One of the only legal supplements meaningfully boosting strength and muscle mass. Roughly **25% of people are non-responders** — test it yourself.
- **Caffeine** (pre-workout, coffee or tablets). Small thermogenic effect and burns some extra calories, though long-term fat-loss relevance is unclear. Jeff takes it for focus and alertness. Tolerance builds, so use only on days you need it or cycle off for a few weeks every few months.

## Practical application
Twice a week: 3x10-12 weighted crunches and 3x10-20 leg raises, last set of each to failure, adding load or reps. Add 2-5 easy cardio sessions. Set calories at bodyweight x 10-12, protein at goal weight x 0.8-1, fat 50 g minimum. Aim for 0.5-1% weekly loss, photos and waist measurements monthly, diet break at 3 months. Creatine daily, protein powder as needed, caffeine sparingly.

## Caveats & unknowns
Metabolism varies substantially, so the calorie and macro formulas will run high for some and low for others — adjust from real-world rate of loss. Diet-break responses differ person to person. Ab shape is fixed genetically. Whether caffeine's thermogenic effect meaningfully changes long-term fat loss is not established.
