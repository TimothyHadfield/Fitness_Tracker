# Glute Training: Why Squats Are Overrated and What to Do Instead

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "How to Grow a BUTT | The Most Scientific Way to Train Glutes" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XgekALiC8Ik)) · 2017-01-17 · 7 min
**Topic:** Exercise Selection

## Bottom line
- The glutes fire hardest at or near **full hip extension** — which is exactly where squats *don't* load them. Squats are overrated as a glute builder.
- **Hip thrusts** produce more glute activation than squats (Contreras et al., 2015) because they load the glutes at full hip extension.
- **Deep squats do not increase glute activation** once you correct for load; the classic study showing otherwise used the same absolute weight at every depth.
- Train the glutes with **variety** — they perform hip extension, external rotation, transverse abduction and posterior pelvic tilt, so no one movement covers them.
- Structure: pre-activation → hip thrust → lunges → hip hinge → abduction burnout.

## Anatomy [01:02]
Three muscles: **gluteus maximus** (largest, most superficial — the focus here), **gluteus medius** (important hip stabiliser and strong hip abductor) and **gluteus minimus**.

Origins are spread across the pelvis, sacrum, coccyx and some lumbar fascia; insertions are on the upper femur and the IT band. Because of that spread of attachment points the glutes perform many functions: **hip extension, hip external rotation, hip transverse abduction, and posterior pelvic tilt**. That functional variety is the case for training them with a variety of movements.

## Pre-activation [02:03]
Start with a pre-activation movement. Useful for any target muscle, but especially the glutes because people often struggle to establish a mind-muscle connection with them. Options:
- A **circular/looped band** around the legs during a few light or bodyweight squats — opens the hips and gets the glutes firing through active hip abduction.
- A couple of **light, high-rep sets on the abduction machine**.

## Squats: useful, but overrated for glutes [02:33]
Nippard (self-declared powerlifter bias) still considers squats a staple of any program you can safely perform them in. But:

1. **Biomechanics.** A 2001 study (name garbled in captions — *uncertain*) showed the glutes fire hardest at or near full hip extension. Squats load the glutes largely away from full hip extension.
2. **Activation.** Many studies show squats activate the quads far more than the glutes, and **Contreras et al. (2015)** found the hip thrust produced more glute activation than the squat.
3. **Depth doesn't help.** A 2002 study by Caterisano et al. did find deeper squats gave more glute activation — but it had a methodological flaw: the weight on the bar wasn't changed across depths. Same absolute load through a deeper squat is simply harder. When **Contreras et al. (2016)** assigned *relative* rather than absolute loads, the depth differences disappeared.

**Three ways to make a squat more glute-focused:**
- **Point the toes out** (external rotation increases glute activation).
- **Take a wider stance** (Paoli et al., 2009 — *name uncertain from captions*).
- **Sit back / keep the shins more vertical**, as in a Smith machine squat — multiple studies show this increases glute activation.

## Hip thrust [04:05]
Since the hip thrust transfers the most force to the glutes precisely where they're near full hip extension, it makes strong biomechanical sense as a glute builder, and it out-activated the squat in the Contreras data.

**Bar-on-pelvis pain fix:** thick pads help, but the easier workaround is doing them **single-legged for higher reps** with a plate laid on the lap. Benefits of the unilateral version: strict control, a strong squeeze at the top of each rep, the added stabilisation demand during hip extension (which he suspects yields more glute medius activity and may reach regions the barbell version doesn't), and a better mind-muscle connection.

**Cue:** because the glutes posteriorly tilt the pelvis, maintain a **posterior pelvic tilt through the entire range of motion**.

## Walking lunges [05:07]
Three cues to maximise glute activation:
- Walk **forward**, not reverse.
- Take **longer strides**.
- **Minimise the back leg's contribution** by driving the front heel into the floor.

## Hip hinge (deadlift-style) [05:22]
Often thought of as a lower back exercise — it can be, but it's also an effective glute builder. To bias the glutes:
- Slightly **bend the knees**.
- Slightly **flare the feet out**.
- Slightly **round the lower back**.
- **Squeeze the glutes hard at the top** of each rep.

## Hip abduction finisher [05:37]
End where you started. Use abduction as a **burnout** at the end of the session for extra metabolic stress: **20-30 rep sets** on the abduction machine with the butt elevated, or with a cable, or with a plate.

## Practical application
Sample glute session:
1. Band-resisted bodyweight squats or light high-rep machine abduction (pre-activation)
2. Hip thrust — barbell, or single-leg with a plate for higher reps, holding posterior pelvic tilt
3. Squat variation with toes out, wide stance, shins vertical
4. Forward walking lunges, long strides, heel drive
5. Hip hinge with soft knees, flared feet, hard top squeeze
6. Hip abduction burnout, 20-30 reps

## Caveats & unknowns
- Almost all the evidence cited is acute EMG activation, not long-term hypertrophy; higher activation is an assumption about growth, not a demonstration of it.
- The claim that single-leg hip thrusts recruit more glute medius or different glute regions is Nippard's own speculation, explicitly flagged as such.
- Two researcher names (the 2001 hip-extension study and the 2009 wide-stance study) were garbled by the auto-captions; treat as uncertain.
- No set/rep prescriptions are given for the main lifts in this video beyond the high-rep abduction finisher.
