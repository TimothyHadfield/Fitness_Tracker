# A Full Upper Body Workout, Exercise by Exercise

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Best Full UPPER BODY Workout For Max Muscle Growth (Science Applied)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3IQVNjWH60A)) · 2019-05-04 · 10 min
**Topic:** Exercise Selection

## Bottom line
- Session: OHP 2×4 → wide-grip pull-up 3×6 → close-grip bench 2×10 → superset (wide-grip cable row 3×12 + incline lateral raise 3×15) → superset (cable crossover 3×10+10 + rope face pull 3×20) → supinated DB curl 3×12, plus optional weak-point work.
- Overhead press: keep RPE high — failure arrives abruptly, so leaving 2–3 reps in reserve barely feels like a set.
- Progression is by adding sets or reps week to week, then resetting with more weight in week 4.
- Wide-grip cable rows are deliberately made a trap/rear-delt movement because pull-ups already cover the lats.
- Lateral raises: lean forward 15–30°, and stop 10–15° short of the bottom.

## Warm-up
Even on upper days, warm the whole body and break a light sweat before lifting to reduce injury risk and improve performance. Five minutes on an incline treadmill, then a dynamic stretching routine.

## Barbell overhead press — 2 sets of 4
Failure on the OHP tends to arrive suddenly: six or seven reps feel manageable and then the bar simply stops. Because leaving two or three reps in the tank won't feel challenging, it makes sense to run a high RPE here rather than piling on volume.

**Progression:** week 1 = 2×4, week 2 = 3×4, week 3 = 4×4, week 4 = back to 2×4 with more weight.

**Technique cue:** lift the chin and tilt the head back to clear the bar path, rather than pushing the bar forward around the face and arcing it back. The bar should travel in a straight vertical line.

## Wide-grip pull-up — 3 sets of 6
Research from Doma and colleagues (captions render this "doumitt") found no significant difference in lat activation between a pull-up and a pulldown, but the pull-up came out ahead for the **biceps and spinal erectors** — the erectors being what create the appearance of back thickness up the spine. Both movements have a place, but leaving pull-ups out likely costs back thickness.

Loaded here with a 30 lb dumbbell; the requirement is simply to be reasonably close to failure on rep six. Newer or heavier lifters should use bodyweight or an assisted machine.

**Technique cue — tempo consistency.** Most people start with a 1-second concentric and a 1–2 second eccentric, then by rep six they're moving up and down much faster even without visibly cheating. Jeff classes this as a subtle form of cheating. Keep cadence identical from rep 1 to rep 6 and set to set; if you can't, reduce the load.

## Close-grip bench press — 2 sets of 10
**Progression (double progression):** 10 reps week 1, 11 in week 2, 12 in week 3, back to 10 in week 4 with more weight.

The physique advantage of the close grip is upper chest: the majority of EMG studies show a narrow grip produces proportionally more activation of the **clavicular head**.

**Common error:** taking "close grip" too literally. Wrists and elbows should be stacked when viewed from the rear — roughly shoulder-width is what counts as close. Going narrower than that is close to a guaranteed route to wrist pain.

## Superset A — wide-grip seated cable row 3×12 + incline dumbbell lateral raise 3×15
**Cable row.** The wide grip emphasises transverse abduction and scapular retraction over shoulder extension. Arms out to the sides hits **mid traps and rear delts**; elbows tucked down and in with a closer grip turns it into a **lat** exercise. Since pull-ups already fatigue the lats easily, making this a trap-focused movement is the sensible choice — natural lifters more often lack trap development and back thickness than lat width.

**Lateral raise.** Supersetted purely to save time; there's little interference between the two. Jeff normally does leaning cable laterals, but the dumbbell version is fine with two modifications:
1. **Lean forward 15–30°** — braced against a steep incline bench, or hinging at the hips without chest support. This aligns the line of pull with the lateral delt fibres while keeping the raise in the scapular plane.
2. **Stop 10–15° short of the very bottom.** Research from McMahon and colleagues shows the bottom of the range is handled mostly by the rotator cuff, with the lateral delt only taking over later in the ROM. There is also no tension there anyway — with the dumbbell hanging directly beneath the shoulder, gravity can't produce any shoulder-muscle tension.

## Superset B — cable crossover 3×(10+10) + rope face pull 3×20
**Crossovers.** Set the cables low and fly upward to involve shoulder flexion and target the **clavicular (upper) chest** for 10 reps. Then raise the cables to shoulder height and do another 10 reps to burn out the **sternal head**.

**Face pulls.** Swap the D-handles for a rope, 20 reps. The face pull has a short range of motion, so use light weight and high reps with constant tension on rear delts and mid traps. Use an underhand grip and externally rotate at the top to hit rear delts harder and strengthen the rotator cuff. Cue: think of it as a rear double-biceps pose — flexing your biceps from the back on every rep.

## Supinated dumbbell curl — 3 sets of 12
**Grip cue:** hold the dumbbell closer to the outside head so the pinky sits more toward the middle, and drive through the pinky. Widely reported to increase biceps engagement and reduce forearm takeover.

**Imbalance fix:** perform all 12 reps with the weaker/smaller arm first, then match the same reps and weight with the stronger arm. The stronger side ends up at a slightly lower RPE, which should even the two out over time.

## Optional weak-point block — direct neck work
3 sets of 20 slow, controlled crab-walk neck curls supersetted with 3 sets of 20 push-up-style neck reps, using a head harness.

## Practical application
Full session in order:
1. Incline treadmill 5 min + dynamic stretching
2. Barbell OHP — 2×4, high RPE (progress to 3×4, 4×4, then reset heavier)
3. Wide-grip pull-up — 3×6, close to failure, constant tempo
4. Close-grip bench (shoulder-width) — 2×10, adding a rep per week
5. Superset: wide-grip cable row 3×12 (elbows out, traps focus) + incline DB lateral raise 3×15 (leaning 15–30°, cut the bottom 10–15°)
6. Superset: cable crossover 3× (10 low-to-high + 10 at shoulder height) + rope face pull 3×20, underhand with external rotation
7. Supinated DB curl 3×12, weak arm first, pinky-driven grip
8. Optional weak-point volume of your choosing

## Caveats & unknowns
- This is a generalised version of a specific programme; the underlying programme carries more detailed progression, periodisation and week-to-week variation.
- The Doma and McMahon citations come through the auto-captions imperfectly and should be verified before being quoted.
- EMG activation is a proxy for stimulus, not a direct measure of hypertrophy.
