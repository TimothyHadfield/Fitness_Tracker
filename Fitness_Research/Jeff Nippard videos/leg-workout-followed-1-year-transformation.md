# The Leg Workout Behind Nippard's One-Year Transformation

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Leg Workout I Followed For My 1 Year Transformation" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hRZ5MM6gmlE)) · 2025-06-09 · 12 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- Order: lying leg curl → pendulum squat → Romanian deadlift → leg extension → hip abduction → straight-leg calf raise.
- Leg curls go first as a knee and posterior-chain primer, which made his squats feel and perform better.
- Lengthened partials at the end of the last set on leg curls; deep stretch emphasis on calves including a 30 s loaded hold.
- Seat position matters: leg extension seat pushed as far back as possible produced about 170% more quad growth in a 2024 study.
- Two heavy overloadable movements carry the session — pendulum squat (3 × 6-8) for quads and RDL for hamstrings and glutes.

## 1. Lying leg curl (first, deliberately)
Putting curls first was, in his words, a game-changer for how his knees felt when squatting. It prioritises a muscle most people neglect and warms the knees and soft tissue. With the posterior chain slightly activated going in, glutes fire better, knees track more smoothly, and you should be stronger [00:30].

**Lying vs. seated:** he knows the study showing seated leg curls produced 1.6× more hamstring growth than lying curls across 12 weeks, and accepts it. His resolution is to do lying curls on one leg day and seated curls on the other. Two supporting reasons: exercise variety generally develops a muscle more proportionally across its heads, and in that same study the **sartorius** grew better with lying curls. The sartorius matters for bodybuilding — it's the vertical muscle that pops on quad poses when lean — and unlike the hamstrings (more stretched seated), the sartorius is more stretched lying down. (Researchers not named.)

**Technique change this year:** lengthened partials at the end of the last set. Once the pad won't come all the way up, keep going with partials. This exploits the hamstring strength curve — near failure you're still strong in the bottom half.

**Common mistake:** letting the hips pop up, which lets glutes and low back cheat the weight. Drive the hips into the pad and grip the handles hard to pull yourself into the bench. Maximum stability sends all the stack tension into the hamstrings.

## 2. Pendulum squat — 3 × 6-8
His number one quad builder for the year. Like any squat it also hits glutes and adductors.

**Substitutions:** any overloadable squat variation — barbell, Smith machine or hack squat. Quad-bias adjustments: barbell squat, bar higher on the traps and stay upright; Smith machine, feet back underneath you and let knees travel forward; hack squat, same — feet back, deeper knee angle.

**Why he prefers the pendulum:** low back support like a hack squat, plus it's notoriously harder at full depth so you fight harder out of the bottom, which is a large growth stimulus. He also finds the arcing path smoother than a hack squat's linear path, while admitting that's splitting hairs.

**Effort management:** quads are lit up after one set, so don't take every set to failure. Leave 1-2 reps in reserve on the first couple of sets, then give everything on the last set — keep going until you can't get up, then drop it on the safety pin. Note that the pendulum feels brutally hard at the bottom near failure; if you can grind through that sticking point you'll finish the rep, so you can often get more than you expect. Deload every couple of months — necessary if you're taking the last set to failure weekly while adding weight or reps.

**Common mistake:** cutting depth. The quads miss the high-torque zone near the bottom. Go as deep as you comfortably can and film from the side to check, since depth is usually shallower than it feels.

## 3. Romanian deadlift
The single biggest contributor to his hamstring development this year. Chosen because it's easy to add weight to week over week — going from two plates a side for six hard reps to three plates a side for six hard reps means bigger glutes and hamstrings. It complements the leg curl: the curl trains knee flexion, the RDL trains hip extension, the hamstrings' two functions.

**Cues:** push the glutes straight back to keep it a true hip hinge rather than a squat. Keep a slight knee bend but don't let knees drift forward — just enough to spare the knee joint while keeping tension on the muscle. Film from the side; the bar should travel straight up and down over the middle of the foot, not drifting forward.

**Common mistake:** over-reaching at the bottom, chasing range by rounding the spine or letting the bar drift out front. Keep the back tight, bar close, and lower only as far as a neutral spine allows. He stops just below knee level and still gets a full hamstring stretch.

## 4. Leg extension — 1-2 hard sets
His pick for best quad isolation, and it complements the pendulum squat because it hits the rectus femoris harder than any squat variation. Reason: the rectus femoris crosses both hip and knee, so during a squat (both joints moving) its length barely changes; on a leg extension the hips are fixed, so it genuinely stretches and shortens. A 2021 study over five weeks found Smith machine squats did not grow the rectus femoris while leg extensions did. (Researchers not named.)

**Technique change this year:** seat set as far back as it goes — shown in a 2024 study to produce about 170% more quad growth than the standard upright seat position.

He also straps himself in and pulls hard on the handles to keep the glutes planted, so all tension goes to the quads. Optional: a slow 3-second eccentric — not because slow eccentrics are inherently better for growth, but because they let you get the same stimulus with lighter loads, easing knee strain. He needed that after injuring a knee during a strength test for the experiment.

**Common mistake:** letting the butt pop up and down, which loses seat stability and quad tension. Keep hips down; he uses a roughly $5 airline seatbelt extender from Amazon on heavier sets.

## 5. Hip abduction machine
Glutes are the lower-body muscle that most obviously signals you lift, male or female. He used to lean forward on abductions because conventional wisdom said that stretches the glute medius better. He changed after Dr. Bret Contreras responded to his glute tier list video, arguing that the glute medius fibre directions aren't well aligned for leaning seated abduction — instead bridge up into a hip thrust position and do bridged seated abduction to target the medius. Leaning forward biases glute max; bridged biases glute medius.

There's no direct research comparing the two techniques, but Nippard finds the argument sound and suspects varying hip angle periodically is better for overall glute development anyway. Since glute max already got hammered by the pendulum squat and RDL, he does these with hips extended to target upper glute medius fibres.

**Common mistake:** going too light. Many treat this as a fluff/pump exercise. Intermediate to advanced lifters should be maxing out most abduction machines and adding load with a gym pin. His benchmark: if you don't need to sit still for about 10 seconds before standing up, you didn't go hard enough.

## 6. Straight-leg calf raise — 8-10 reps
The exercise everyone skips, which is why calves don't grow. Pick a straight-leg variation you feel well and push it hard, emphasising the deep stretched portion. If calves are a priority, move them to the front of the workout so you're not tempted to skip.

**Common mistake:** short bouncy reps up onto the toes. A growing body of data says a deep stretch is crucial for calf growth. You don't even need to come all the way up on your toes — just rotate up and down over the balls of the feet.

**Finisher:** at the end of the last set, sink into the bottom stretch position and hold for 30 seconds. It feels like the Achilles will snap; it won't. Stretch-under-load appears especially effective for calf hypertrophy.

## Practical application
Run leg curls first as a primer for every squat session. Keep 1-2 heavy overloadable movements (pendulum/hack/barbell squat and RDL) as the progression backbone and take only the last set to failure. Add lengthened partials on leg curls, push the leg extension seat all the way back and strap in, treat hip abduction as a real working exercise, and hold a 30 s loaded calf stretch to finish.

## Caveats & unknowns
- The seated-vs-lying leg curl study favours seated for hamstrings; his lying-curl case rests on sartorius growth and variety, not superior hamstring growth.
- No direct research compares leaning versus bridged hip abduction — that's Contreras's anatomical reasoning plus Nippard's agreement.
- The 170% leg extension seat-position figure comes from a single 2024 study; researchers unnamed.
- The rectus femoris study ran only five weeks.
- Pendulum squat availability is limited; substitutions are explicitly permitted.
