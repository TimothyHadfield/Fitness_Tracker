# Minimalist Full-Body Training Plan: Two Sessions Under 45 Minutes

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Best Science-Based Minimalist Workout Plan (Under 45 Mins)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMjyvIQbn9M)) · 2022-11-01 · 13 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- Two full-body sessions per week, each under 45 minutes, with scaling instructions for 3, 4 or 5 days.
- With so few sets, **every working set must be taken to failure or within one rep of it** — sloppy or submaximal sets are wasted.
- **Drop sets and myo-reps** replace extra straight sets: they add high-tension reps with almost no extra time.
- Prefer **dumbbells and machines** over barbells — less loading/unloading, fewer warm-up sets, and dumbbells give a bigger stretch, which matters when you can only afford one movement per muscle.
- Pick exercises that cover two jobs at once: high-incline press for pecs + delts, overhead triceps extension instead of pressdowns.

## Why these exercise choices
**Dumbbell over barbell press.** Two reasons: dumbbells save time (no plate loading), and they allow more range of motion. Normally a barbell's limited ROM is compensated for by adding a stretch exercise like a cable fly, but on a minimalist plan you cannot afford the extra movement — so pick a press that supplies the stretch itself. **A dumbbell press or a dip are the best options.**

**Overhead triceps extension over pressdowns.** [03:36] The flat dumbbell press already trains triceps in a neutral shoulder position, so pressdowns are redundant. Also, a **2022 study from Mayo and colleagues** found overhead triceps extensions produced about **40% more hypertrophy than pressdowns even when volume-matched**. There has been online pushback claiming a fluke, but the MASS research review judged it well-designed and offered at least three plausible mechanisms. Don't over-weight one study — but if you can only fit one triceps isolation exercise, choose the overhead version.

**Hack squat over barbell back squat.** Machines need fewer warm-up sets. A barbell squat warm-up can take half an hour; the hack squat takes 2-3 quick warm-up sets and he's into working sets within 5-10 minutes.

**Seated over lying leg curl.** A 2020 12-week study showed greater hamstring growth from seated leg curls than lying leg curls, likely because of the greater stretch. Lean forward over the machine throughout the set to accentuate it.

## Full body day 1
Warm-up: 2-3 minutes brisk treadmill walking plus a few arm and leg swings.

1. **Flat dumbbell press — 2 sets:** one heavy set of **4-6**, then a back-off set of **8-10**. Strength set first, hypertrophy set second. Lower with ~30-45° elbow tuck viewed from above, flare on the press; stay tucked throughout if flaring bothers your shoulders.
2. **Dumbbell Romanian deadlift — 2 sets of 8-10.** Push the hips back and let the dumbbells drop straight down under control; don't aim to touch the floor. Reverse when you feel a big hamstring stretch, before the low back rounds. Nippard avoids full lockout to keep tension on the hamstrings; lock out and squeeze if you want more glute involvement. **Use straps** so grip doesn't cap hamstring tension.
3. **Two-grip lat pulldown — 2 sets of 10-12**, a different grip each set: set 1 **overhand, middle grip** (mid-back and lats), set 2 **underhand, close grip** (more biceps, lats from a new angle). After set 2, strip the weight and do **overhead curls** so the high-threshold biceps fibers get high tension too.
4. **Dumbbell step-up — 1 working set of 8-10 per leg.** Main quad/glute movement. Force the front leg to carry the load; the back leg is only for balance. Work one leg at a time so tension isn't lost between reps. Most people stop well short of failure on step-ups and lunges — **go heavier than feels natural** to get within a rep of failure, and use straps.
5. **Overhead cable triceps extension — 1 set of 12-15 plus a drop set.** Lock the elbows in place with the cable just behind the head, squeeze the triceps, lower under control for a big stretch. After failure, **drop the weight 30-40%** and go to failure again.
6. **Machine lateral raise — 1 set of 12-15 plus a drop set.** Nippard uses an Atlantis standing machine for the larger ROM with consistent tension, but a seated machine, cables or dumbbells all work — **taking the muscle to failure with good form recruits the fibers regardless**. Strap in so the thumb/grip doesn't limit you. Stop at 90° (parallel) if the larger ROM is uncomfortable. Drop 30-40%, second failure.
7. **Horizontal toe press (calves) on the leg press — 1 set of 12-15 plus a drop set.** Same straight-leg pattern as a standing calf raise, but seated means the upper body carries no load, which improves the mind-muscle connection. Drop 30-40% and repeat. **Don't start bouncing on the drop set** — hold a ~1 second pause at the bottom and a full squeeze at the top on every rep.

## Full body day 2
1. **Hack squat — 2 sets:** heavy set of **4-6 at RPE 9** (one rep shy of failure), then a back-off set of **8-10**. Rest ~3 minutes between. On the heavy set just move the weight — drive through the heels, keep constant pressure on the shoulder pads. On the back-off set control the negative and focus on feeling the quads. Go to full depth and let the knees travel past the toes — this is perfectly safe.
2. **Antagonistic superset — high-incline Smith machine press (2 sets) + T-bar row (2 sets of 10-12).** Training opposing muscles back to back lets one rest while the other works. Set the bench **45-60°** so the delts get more involved — this substitutes for doing both a vertical and a horizontal press. Take a **slightly closer grip** for more triceps ROM and more upper-pec emphasis (a weak point for most). If the machines are far apart, swap the T-bar row for **chest-supported incline dumbbell rows**, same sets and reps. On rows, do one set wide-grip and one close-grip. **Still rest 30-60 seconds between the two exercises** — supersetting doesn't mean zero rest.
3. **Seated hamstring curl — 1 set of 10-12 plus a drop set.** Lean forward over the machine for extra stretch. To failure, drop 30-40%, failure again.
4. **EZ-bar biceps curl — 1 set of 12-15 plus myo-reps.** Curl out and up, not just up; drive through the pinky to cue supination; squeeze the biceps to move the weight and keep a loose grip so the forearms don't take over. After failure, **rest 3-4 seconds, do 4 more reps, repeat until you can no longer get 4 reps.** Myo-reps pack in many high-tension reps quickly — reps only become maximally hypertrophic near the end of a set, so this skips the low-tension early reps.
5. **Cable crunch — 1 set of 12-15 plus a double drop set.** Kneel with the rope locked over the head, crunch hard and let the whole back round including the lower back. Failure → drop ~30% → failure → strip another 20-30% → failure.

## Scaling to more days
- **3 days:** run full body day 1 as-is, then split day 2 into an upper day and a lower day. Add a set to some exercises or extra movements for weak points.
- **4 days:** split both full-body workouts into upper/lower pairs — upper, lower, rest, upper, lower, rest, rest. Add volume if the sessions feel too short.
- **5 days:** same idea run as an upper / lower / push / pull / legs split.

## Practical application
Keep the exercise list and rep targets fixed; adjust only how the volume is distributed across the week. Straps on RDLs, step-ups and lateral raises. Standard drop-set protocol throughout: reach failure, cut 30-40%, reach failure again.

## Caveats & unknowns
- The overhead-extension-vs-pressdown finding rests on a single 2022 study; Nippard explicitly says not to put too much stock in it, and recommends it mainly because it costs nothing to prefer.
- Flaring the elbows on the dumbbell press is optional and should be dropped if shoulders complain.
- The larger-than-usual lateral raise ROM is a personal preference, not a requirement — failure is what drives recruitment.
