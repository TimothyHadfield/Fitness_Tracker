# Training Arms for Growth: Biceps and Triceps Anatomy, Exercise Selection and Volume

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "How to Train ARMS for Growth | Science Explained (10 Studies)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x4YNi4nRboU)) · 2017-05-08 · 10 min
**Topic:** Exercise Selection

## Bottom line
- Compounds (chin-ups, rows, bench) build arms but won't maximise them — you need direct curls and direct triceps isolation.
- Lead each arm session with a heavy overloadable movement in the **4-8 rep** range (barbell/EZ-bar/dumbbell curl for biceps; bench press for triceps).
- Pair exercises with the shoulder **extended** (preacher-style is the exception — see below) and with the shoulder **flexed** so all regions get hit.
- Volume reference from Dr. Mike Israetel: **8 weekly sets minimum**, **14-20 weekly sets** optimal for most intermediate/advanced lifters, split over **2+ sessions per week**.

## Anatomy [00:00]
Anterior compartment: biceps brachii, brachialis, coracobrachialis. Posterior: triceps brachii (the largest single muscle of the upper arm).

- **Brachialis** sits deeper so it isn't visible directly, but growing it pushes the biceps up and out. It's a *stronger* elbow flexor than the biceps because it doesn't cross the shoulder — all its effort goes to the elbow.
- **Biceps** are triarticular: elbow flexion, forearm supination, shoulder flexion. Long head (outer) assists shoulder abduction; short head (inner) does more adduction.
- **Triceps**: lateral (outer), medial (middle), long (inner). The long head originates on the scapula, so it crosses the shoulder and weakly extends it — which is why pullovers and rows can make triceps sore.

## Biceps training
**Compounds aren't enough.** Chin-ups produce higher biceps EMG than pull-ups ([Youdas 2010](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/21068680)), which fits the simultaneous supination plus elbow flexion. Among horizontal pulls the inverted barbell row is best, at **77% of maximum voluntary contraction**. Nippard's view: these are back builders, and coaches often coach people to *minimise* biceps involvement on them — all the more reason to isolate.

**Load and order.** Higher relative loads raise biceps activation. Biceps are somewhat fast-twitch dominant, so run one heavy curl at **4-8 reps**, and do it **first** in the session since strength is highest early.

**Eccentrics.** Concentric and eccentric actions both drive hypertrophy, but Hather et al. found hypertrophy can't be maximised without eccentric actions ([Hather 1991](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/1835816)), and other work shows eccentrics raise muscle protein synthesis ([Moore 2005](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/15572656)). Practical move: on the last heavy set, use moderate cheating or a spotter to keep doing controlled negatives.

**Grip.** Supinated or rotating (supination and flexion happening together) is most biomechanically sensible. Hammer grip also gives very high biceps activation — contrary to popular belief. The **pronated grip is the weak one**: one source showed under **40%** of the activation of a supinated grip. Reverse curls still have value for brachioradialis and brachialis.

**Grip width.** A slightly wider grip may bias the long head (which contributes to abduction). A very close grip — hands about one hand-width apart — hurt activation by **13%** versus shoulder width in one source.

**Combining exercises.** A 2009 study by Oliveira et al. ([Oliveira 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24150552/)) measured biceps activity across the range on three exercises: the **preacher curl** peaked in the **initial third** of the ROM and dropped off across the remaining two-thirds; the **standing curl and incline curl** were the exact opposite — lowest at the bottom, highest at the top. So combine shoulder-extended positions (arms behind the body) with shoulder-flexed positions (arms in front) to recruit all regions evenly.

**Resistance curve fix.** Peak torque in most curls sits at ~90° of elbow flexion, with less tension above and below. A **cable curl facing away from the machine** gives roughly even tension throughout, so failure comes from motor unit recruitment rather than a mechanical dead spot.

## Triceps training
**Bench press is the single best triceps exercise** in his view — a compound that's easy to overload progressively. Use heavy loads, roughly **4-8 reps per set**.

- **Grip width:** narrower increases triceps activation. Barnett et al. (*name uncertain*) found shoulder-width beat 2× shoulder-width; Lehman (2005) supported this and added an ultra-narrow one-hand-width grip that activated the most of all ([Lehman 2005](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/16095407)). Nippard advises **against** that ultra-narrow grip — wrist strain, limited ROM, not worth it when you're also doing isolation work.
- **Bench angle:** Barnett found **flat** better for triceps than decline, incline or vertical pressing. Smith machine pressing shows similar triceps activation to free bench in at least one study.
- **Isolation:** add one shoulder-extended movement (**pressdown**) and one shoulder-flexed movement (**overhead extension**). Pressdowns bias the lateral head because the long head is shortened at the shoulder and therefore actively insufficient. Overhead work lengthens the long head at the shoulder so it contributes more at the elbow. Because the long head also adducts the shoulder, a narrower grip or tucked elbows increases its contribution in either movement. Unless correcting a visual asymmetry, vary grips so both heads get worked.
- **Finisher:** medicine ball push-ups — multiple lines of research show push-ups on an unstable surface produce higher triceps activation than on a stable one ([Lehman 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16762080/)).

## Practical application
- Biceps day order: heavy curl 4-8 reps first (with forced/assisted eccentrics on the last set) → a shoulder-flexed curl (incline) → a shoulder-extended/preacher-type curl → optional facing-away cable curl for even tension.
- Triceps day order: bench press 4-8 reps, flat, roughly shoulder-width grip → pressdown → overhead extension → unstable-surface push-up finisher.
- **8 weekly sets** is the floor; **14-20 weekly sets** is the target zone for intermediates and advanced. Train arms **at least twice weekly**; up to daily is fine provided weekly volume and intensity targets are hit.
- Triceps volume/frequency can mirror biceps given their similar size and antagonistic role.
- None of it works without enough calories and protein to fuel growth.

## Caveats & unknowns
- Most of the exercise-selection evidence here is acute EMG, not long-term hypertrophy — activation is a proxy, not proof.
- One researcher name (Barnett) remains garbled from the auto-captions and is flagged above as uncertain; do not cite it without checking.
- Frequency and volume prescriptions are stated as depending on training level; the specific figures quoted come from Israetel's guidelines rather than a single trial.
