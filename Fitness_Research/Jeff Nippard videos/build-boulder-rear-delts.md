# Rear Delt Training: Anatomy, Best Exercises and Technique

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "How To Build Boulder Rear Delts: Optimal Training Explained" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qfc70k40318)) · 2019-04-30 · 7 min
**Topic:** Technique

## Bottom line
- Rear delts do two things: **transverse shoulder abduction** (arm out to the side in the horizontal plane) and **external rotation**. Train both.
- Isolation beats compounds here. EMG shows the reverse pec deck out-performing the seated row; Brett Contreras' conclusion is that isolation work destroys compounds for rear delts.
- Jeff's favourite exercise is the **rope face pull** — the only movement combining transverse abduction and external rotation.
- Rep ranges: **15–20** for the externally-rotated face pull, **12–15** for the heavier high-row version.
- The single most common error is turning the movement into a row by over-retracting. Swing the arms **out**, not back.

## Anatomy and why isolation matters
The rear delt's primary function is transverse shoulder abduction — moving the arm out to the side horizontally, as in a reverse fly. The same basic pattern appears in rowing variations like the Pendlay row, where the arms travel out to the side as the elbow bends. So rear delts are highly active during compound pulling.

But EMG data from Botton et al. (auto-captions garble this as "button natal") shows isolation exercises such as the reverse pec deck out-perform compounds such as the seated row for rear delt activation — which led Dr. Brett Contreras to conclude that isolation exercises for the rear delts beat compound exercises decisively. You can build decent rear delts on heavy rows alone, but isolation work is needed to maximise development.

The frequently neglected second function is **external rotation** — rotating the arm outward. Exercises that include it get more out of the muscle.

## Rope face pull — variation 1 (external rotation)
Jeff's single favourite rear delt exercise, because it combines both functions.

**Setup:** cable at shoulder height. Neutral grip with **thumbs** in contact with the handles, not pinkies. Stable stance a few feet back from the machine. Slightly retract the shoulder blades to start from a strong upper-back position.

**Execution:** drive the elbows back while simultaneously pulling the rope toward your forehead. Think about **depressing** (tucking down) the shoulder blades throughout the concentric — this brings in trap musculature and keeps the shoulder in a safer position. The eccentric is a straight reversal: let the rope travel forward and down under control while the shoulder blades stay secure.

**Extra benefits:** strengthens the rotator cuff; excellent assistance work on a push day to balance the pushing demand on the shoulders; helps correct front-to-back shoulder imbalances and may help with forward-slouched posture.

**Loading: light weight, roughly 15–20 reps.**

## Rope face pull — variation 2 (high row)
**Setup:** cable at **eye level**. Pull the rope straight back toward the chin, without the external rotation — squeeze the shoulder blades together and drive the elbows back.

This is essentially a high row, recruiting traps, rhomboids and biceps as well. Because more muscle is involved, **load it heavier in the 12–15 rep range**. Jeff often performs these half-lying with his torso braced against the floor so he can go heavier without losing balance.

## Dumbbell face pull
For when cables aren't available, or just for variety. Bend over at the hips until close to parallel with the floor, keeping a natural arch in the lower back, then copy the external-rotation face pull pattern — **pull your thumbs toward your ears**. Same rear-double-biceps-pose cue applies: lift the arms up and flex the biceps from the back.

## Reverse pec deck
Highest rear delt activation of all exercises tested in the Botton EMG data — though face pulls weren't included in that study, so the comparison is incomplete. Being locked into position lets you do pure shoulder abduction without external rotation, which is good for isolating and burning out the rear delts.

**Grip:** research from Schoenfeld and colleagues found a **neutral grip with the shoulders more externally rotated** produced more rear delt activation ([Schoenfeld 2013](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/23302754)) — plausibly because external rotation is one of the muscle's functions. But some subjects did better with a pronated (palms-down) grip, and Jeff personally gets a better mind-muscle connection that way. Advice: either alternate periodically between neutral and pronated, or use whichever lets you feel the rear delts best.

**Cue:** rounding the scapulae forward via protraction, and thinking about swinging the weight **out** instead of back, can improve the mind-muscle connection.

## Bent-over reverse dumbbell fly
A solid movement with one flaw shared with the dumbbell chest fly: tension peaks when the dumbbells are furthest out to the sides at the top and declines through the rest of the range, hitting essentially zero at the bottom.

**Fix:** stop the dumbbells slightly short at the bottom and work in a constant-tension groove through roughly the **top 3/4 of the range**. Lift the dumbbells out in an arc rather than back, which shifts emphasis off the traps and onto the rear delts. Jeff prefers a pronated grip here, but pick what feels best.

## The most common mistake
Turning a rear delt movement into a row by retracting too much. Rear delts need **transverse abduction** — the arms swing out to the sides, not pulled straight back. Fix it by going wider, paying closer attention, and slowing the movement down as much as necessary until the mind-muscle connection is correct.

## Practical application
- Include direct rear delt isolation; don't rely on rows alone.
- Rope face pull with external rotation, cable at shoulder height, thumbs on the rope, elbows driving back, scapulae depressed: **light, 15–20 reps**.
- Rope high-row face pull, cable at eye level, no external rotation: **heavier, 12–15 reps**.
- Reverse pec deck: alternate neutral and pronated grip, or pick the one you feel most; think "swing out," not "pull back."
- Bent-over reverse fly: stop short of the bottom, work the top 3/4, lift in an arc.
- Face pulls double as push-day shoulder-health and posture assistance work.

## Caveats & unknowns
- The Botton EMG comparison did not include face pulls, so the reverse pec deck's "highest activation" ranking is only relative to what was tested.
- The Schoenfeld grip finding was not universal — individual responses to neutral vs. pronated grips differed.
- EMG activation is a proxy for stimulus, not a direct measure of hypertrophy.
- No set counts or weekly frequency are prescribed in this video.
