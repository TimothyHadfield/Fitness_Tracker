# Barbell Curl Technique: Optimal Biceps Training

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "How To Build Huge Biceps: Optimal Training Explained" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i1YgFZB6alI)) · 2018-12-18 · 9 min
**Topic:** Technique

## Bottom line
- Load light-to-moderate in the **8-20 rep range**; heavier loading just recruits other muscles to heave the weight.
- Focus on **squeezing the biceps**, not moving the weight — a Schoenfeld 8-week training study found an internal focus on the biceps produced significantly more hypertrophy than focusing on the load.
- Curl the bar **out in an arc**, not straight up: this increases the moment arm between the elbow and the bar, generating far more torque and biceps tension at the same load.
- **Actively supinate** by driving pressure through the pinky and ring fingers; supination is the biceps' unique job because the brachialis cannot supinate.
- Barbell, EZ-bar and dumbbell curls are near-equivalent. Use whichever suits your wrists; the barbell's only real edge is finer load progression (2.5 lb/side vs 5 lb/side with dumbbells).

## Anatomy and what the curl actually trains
- **Elbow flexion** is driven mainly by the **brachialis**, not the biceps brachii. It sits under the biceps, and building it pushes the biceps up and out, increasing apparent arm size.
- The **biceps brachii** also crosses the elbow and works hard, assisted by the **brachioradialis** on the back of the forearm.
- **Wrist supination** (turning the palm up) is handled by the biceps and the supinator. This is the key to biasing the biceps, since the more powerful brachialis contributes nothing to supination.
- A small amount of **shoulder flexion** occurs, which is fine — the biceps long head (the peak) crosses the shoulder and assists the front delt, as in a front raise.
- The anterior forearm holds an **isometric wrist flexion** contraction to stop the wrist bending back.

**Targeting the long head / peak:** Dr. Brad Schoenfeld recommends exercises with the upper arm held *behind* the body, putting the long head under greater stretch — e.g. **incline dumbbell curls** or **behind-the-body cable curls** in addition to a basic curl.

## Grip
- Roughly **shoulder width**, supinated, comfortable.
- A **wider grip** may bias the long head slightly (the long head is a stronger shoulder abductor) — but Jeff flags this as his own speculation and notes it costs range of motion.
- A **very close grip** is discouraged: reduced range of motion, and one cited source reports about **13% lower biceps EMG amplitude** versus shoulder-width.
- Grip the bar **loosely** with hands and fingers (opposite of the bench press cue). This reduces forearm elbow-flexor involvement and increases biceps contribution.

## Setup and execution [04:08]
1. Stand upright, shoulder-width stance, braced core, slightly bent knees.
2. Elbows tucked comfortably close to the sides.
3. Before the first rep, flex the shoulders about **10 degrees** (arms slightly forward) and **posteriorly tilt the scapulae** — the biceps short head attaches on the front of the scapula, so posterior tilt pre-loads tension into it.
4. Press through the **pinky and ring fingers** to force supination and shift tension onto the biceps.
5. Curl **out in an arc**, not vertically.
6. Keep a **neutral wrist** throughout. Advanced option: slightly extend the wrist at the top to take the forearms out further — stop if it causes wrist pain.
7. Knees, hips and lower back stay motionless; only the arms move.
8. Jeff doesn't pause at the top (little tension there) but still thinks about squeezing the biceps to move the weight, including at the top.
9. **Eccentric:** lower in an arc, loose grip, forcefully contracting the biceps. Treat the negative as a "failed positive" — actively resisting, not just dropping the weight. The eccentric is at least as important as the concentric for growth.
10. He **resets each rep at the bottom** to regain position and take a breath, which improves tightness, control and total load handled.

## Common errors
- **Going too heavy** — the number one fault, same as with rows. Cheat curls aren't banned on principle (especially with a controlled eccentric) but risk "progressive cheating" rather than progressive overload: more help from the hips and legs each week while biceps tension stays flat or falls. Up to **10-15 degrees of shoulder flexion** to initiate the curl is fine; once hips, knees and ankles join in it's a different exercise.
- **No active supination** — curling with an underhand grip but forgetting to drive the pinky and ring fingers in, so the forearms do most of the work.
- **Arm blaster** can help keep the shoulders back and enforce posture, but learn the movement unassisted first rather than leaning on gear.

## Dumbbell curl as an adjunct
Excellent complement: trains each arm individually (correcting asymmetries), and lets you start with a **neutral wrist and supinate through the concentric**. Cue: place the **pinky against the inner edge of the dumbbell handle** so the biceps must work harder to supinate.

## Practical application
- Bread and butter: barbell or EZ-bar curl, **8-20 reps**, moderate load, loose grip, arcing bar path, active supination, controlled eccentric, reset each rep.
- Add an **incline or behind-the-body cable curl** if peak/long head is a priority.
- Add **standing dumbbell curls** for unilateral work and through-range supination.
- Combine curl variations rather than relying on one, since each has different weak points.

## Caveats & unknowns
- The wide-grip long-head bias is explicitly Jeff's speculation, not evidence.
- The 13% EMG figure comes from "one source" that isn't named in the transcript.
- The Schoenfeld mind-muscle study is described only as an 8-week trial; no further detail given.
