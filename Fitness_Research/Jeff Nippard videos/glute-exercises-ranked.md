# Glute Exercises Ranked S–F: Upper, Middle and Lower Glute Builders

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Best & Worst Glute Exercises (According To Science)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3ryh7PNhz3E)) · 2025-03-17 · 13 min
**Topic:** Exercise Selection

## Bottom line
- Best exercise overall: the **walking lunge** (he calls it a hot take).
- Worst: **donkey kicks** and **fire hydrants** — fine as warm-up/activation drills, useless for hypertrophy.
- Best per region: **machine hip abduction** for upper glutes, **hip thrust machine** for mid glutes, **Romanian deadlift** for lower glutes.
- Train both ends of the range: stretch-biased work (squats, lunges, RDLs) plus shortened-position work (hip thrusts, abductions).
- The barbell hip thrust is effective but overhyped relative to its tier here (high B) — the machine version solves most of its problems.

## Criteria and anatomy
Three boxes: high tension especially in the stretched position; feels good (no knee, hip or low-back pain); a clear way to progress.

Three muscles:
- **Gluteus maximus** — the powerhouse; extends the hip (deadlift) and externally rotates the leg (toes pointed out).
- **Gluteus medius** — about 1/3 the size of the max but still large (roughly the muscle mass of the delts). Sits on the upper glutes and builds the upper-glute shelf. Very active in **hip abduction** (sweeping the leg out) and as a stabilizer, so it works hard on single-leg exercises.
- **Gluteus minimus** — about half the size of the medius; stabilizer, also adds upper-glute size.

The glutes can also be thought of as upper, middle and lower regions.

## The rankings

**S tier**
- **Walking lunge** — hits the entire glute: upper (glute medius stabilizing), middle (hip extension), and lower (deep stretch). Accentuate the stretch with **longer strides** and by leaning forward about **30°**. He prefers walking over stationary because moving through space is more motivating, and he gets more sore from lunges than anything else. **Best of the best.**
- **Front-foot-elevated Smith machine lunge** — a small box under the front foot adds just enough range of motion at the bottom to reach S tier.
- **Machine hip abduction** — directly loads the glute medius and minimus through their primary function. **Lean forward ~30°** to improve the line of pull on the upper-glute fibers and deepen the stretch at the bottom. He uses **15–20 reps**, though heavy low-rep work is also fine. Progress by adding pin weight; when you max the stack, add a gym pin. An extra pad on the inside adds range of motion. **#1 exercise for upper glutes.**
- **45° back extension** — commonly mistaken for a low-back-only exercise, but you're extending the hips, so the whole glute is active in both the lengthened bottom and shortened top. **Round the upper back** to connect with the glutes better. Progress mostly by adding reps; can hold plates to the chest.

**A tier**
- **Hip thrust machine** (high A, e.g. Nautilus Glute Drive) — comfortable belt instead of a barbell, no setup, very stable so you can focus on the glutes. He does these weekly. **#1 exercise for the middle glutes.** Only downside is the lack of a deep stretch.
- **Single-leg dumbbell hip thrust** — whole glute, with extra upper-glute emphasis because medius and minimus stabilize. Fixes side-to-side imbalances. **Don't go heavy — 12–15 reps, controlled.**
- **Barbell squat** — an underrated glute builder; his own glute development largely came from squatting. Activates all regions, especially the middle. Doesn't isolate (big quad stimulus too), so it's not ideal if you want glutes without quads. Make it more glute-dominant by placing the bar a few inches lower, leaning further forward, and driving through the hips. Research shows **squatting deeper matters for glute growth** ([Kubo 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31230110/)) — at least break parallel.
- **Smith machine squat** — feet further forward makes it more glute-dominant, and no balance concerns.
- **Bulgarian split squat** (high A) — deeper glute stretch than a barbell squat and, being single-leg, brings in the upper glutes. To bias glutes: front foot further forward, lean forward, drive through the hips instead of standing up with the knees. Close to S but hard to overload (especially with dumbbells) and very exhausting. He does **2 sets per leg** and stops.
- **Smith machine lunge** (high A) — all the same benefits as walking lunges; only demoted on personal preference for moving through space.
- **Romanian deadlift** (high A) — the best deadlift variant for glute hypertrophy, especially the **lower glutes / glute-ham tie-in**. Hits hamstrings hard too, and as a basic compound it's very conducive to progressive overload. **#1 exercise for the lower glutes.**
- **Cable kickback** (low A) — great for the upper glutes if you kick **up and out diagonally**, since upper-glute fibers do both hip extension (up) and abduction (out). Stable if you brace against the machine or a bench; smooth resistance. Limited stretch. Good choice for isolating glutes without hitting quads.
- **Step-up** (low A) — upper glutes via single-leg stabilization, plus a deep stretch of the whole glute. Set the **box at or just above knee level** to force the glutes to outwork the quads. Grab a handle to keep balance and load more. Awkward at first.

**B tier**
- **Barbell hip thrust** (high B) — one of the most studied movements in exercise science and definitively effective. Activates the whole glute but mainly the middle, since it isolates hip extension. Excellent for overload in the **5–15 rep range** — you don't need a 500 lb hip thrust. Problems: barbell hip pinch (use a thick pad or rolled yoga mats, and place the bar in the hip crease below the ASIS and above the pubic bone), setup time, and no deep stretch — even a deep hip thrust only reaches about **90° of hip flexion** versus the ~**130°** most lifters have available.
- **Sumo deadlift** (high B) — an EMG study found **no difference in glute max activation between conventional and sumo**, but the wider stance and foot flare (abduction plus external rotation) probably hit the glute medius harder and shift emphasis upward.
- **Cable hip abduction** — same idea as the machine but harder to overload, less stable, more annoying to set up.
- **Glute bridge** (low B) — a hip thrust with less range of motion. Easier setup, good for beginners, some people isolate the glutes better by taking the quads out, but he prefers the fuller ROM of the thrust.
- **Curtsy lunge** (low B) — stepping back diagonally demands more balance, so more glute medius. Harder to overload than a standard lunge; an occasional-inclusion option.
- **Conventional deadlift** (low B) — trains hip extension, big lower-glute stretch, amazing for overload, but a **low stimulus-to-fatigue ratio**: huge fatigue elsewhere (especially spinal erectors). Elite strength builder, mediocre glute hypertrophy choice.
- **Cable pull-through** (low B) — good for beginners with little glute strength, but you're eventually limited by how much weight you can hold comfortably, and it starts feeling awkward when loaded.

**C tier**
- **Frog pump** (high C) — externally rotated hips and feet clamped together shift emphasis to the upper glutes via abduction. Decent high-rep finisher, but hard to load and no real stretch.
- **Lateral banded walk** — the band gives essentially zero tension in the stretched position. Fine as a warm-up or to teach glute medius engagement; won't add size beyond the beginner stage.

**D tier**
- **Donkey kick** (low D) — very hard to overload, minimal tension while stretched, and bands don't progress well. Useful as a warm-up/activation drill.
- **Fire hydrant** (low D) — same problems; mobility and rehab uses only.
- **Kettlebell swing** (low D) — good conditioning and some explosive power, not enough tension for hypertrophy.

## Practical application
- His current glute-focused routine: **lots of hip thrusts, hip abductions and walking lunges.**
- Cover all three regions: an abduction movement (upper), a hip thrust (middle), and an RDL (lower).
- Combine shortened-position work (hip thrusts, abductions, kickbacks) with stretched-position work (deep squats, long-stride lunges, RDLs).
- Rep guidance given: hip thrusts 5–15; single-leg dumbbell hip thrusts 12–15; machine hip abductions 15–20; Bulgarian split squats 2 sets per leg.
- Technique cues that matter: lean ~30° forward on abductions and lunges, long strides on lunges, box at knee height or above on step-ups, round the upper back on 45° back extensions, bar low and torso forward on squats.
- Nutrition matters too — training alone won't do it; calories and protein have to support growth.

## Caveats & unknowns
- Whether glutes need a deep stretch is unsettled. A **9-week study in untrained subjects found the hip thrust as effective as the squat** for glute growth despite the squat's greater stretch; other research suggests glutes respond well to deeper training. His conclusion is to use both stretched and shortened exercises.
- The sumo-vs-conventional glute medius advantage is inferred from the wider stance and foot flare, not measured.
- The Smith lunge vs walking lunge ranking is explicitly personal preference.
- Squats are a poor choice if you want glute growth without quad growth.
