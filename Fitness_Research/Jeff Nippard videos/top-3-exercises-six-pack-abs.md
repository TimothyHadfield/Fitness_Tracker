# Top 3 Exercises For Six Pack Abs (Upper vs Lower Abs)

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "Top 3 Science-Based Exercises For Six Pack Abs (Upper vs Lower Abs) ft. Matt Ogus" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2RrGnjxSsiA)) · 2019-08-27 · 7 min
**Topic:** Exercise Selection

## Bottom line
- Visible abs need roughly **8-12% body fat for men, 14-18% for women** — training alone won't reveal them.
- The rectus abdominis flexes the lumbar spine, so effective ab training must **round the lower back through a full flexion range**.
- Pick one exercise pulling the torso down (crunch, biases upper abs) and one pulling the legs up (leg raise, biases lower abs).
- Rep targets: **weighted cable crunch 12-20**, **hanging leg raise 6-15 bodyweight**, **bicycle crunch 15-20 explosive**.
- Don't load lumbar flexion very heavily; keep the crunch moderate-to-light.

## Principles [00:38]
Abs are built in the gym but revealed in the kitchen. Without a low enough body fat percentage — about **8-12% for men, 14-18% for women** — training quality barely matters visually.

The **rectus abdominis**'s primary function is **lumbar flexion**: rounding the lower back. So the six-pack is best trained through a full lumbar flexion range of motion.

**Safety:** weighted spinal flexion has been criticised as risky, but an evidence-based examination of spinal flexion by **Dr. Bret Contreras and Dr. Brad Schoenfeld** argues it's premature to call dynamic flexion exercises like crunches injurious in healthy people.

**Upper vs lower abs:** EMG data (researcher names garbled in the auto-captions as "buh heck Barron's and bus keys" — do not cite without verification) shows crunches and hanging leg raises both hit the whole six-pack, but **hanging leg raises bias the lower abs** and **crunches bias the upper abs**. General rule: include one exercise bringing the **torso down to the midline** and one bringing the **legs up to the torso**.

This video covers dynamic rectus abdominis work; isometric core work (planks, ab wheel) is covered separately.

## 1. Weighted cable crunch [01:39]
Machine or rope attachment on a cable.

- Grab the rope with **pinkies against the handles**, pull it back behind your head, kneel down.
- Before the first rep, **squeeze the glutes into posterior pelvic tilt** so the abs, not the hip flexors, take over.
- Crunch **down and in** — crunching straight down lets the hip flexors dominate; crunching in forces the lower back to round, which is exactly what the abs are for.
- The upper back can stay more or less fixed; there's no need to deliberately curl it, since abs contribute less to thoracic flexion than lumbar flexion.
- Tucking the chin is fine if it helps you focus on flexing the spine. Avoid jerking the head up and down.
- Crunch until you feel a strong contraction along the whole six-pack, then reverse; at the top of each rep reach **full spinal extension** for a genuine full range of motion.

**Loading: 12-20 reps, moderate-to-light.** Heavy lumbar flexion loading may still carry spinal risk; many athletes tolerate it, but he plays it safe.

**Errors:** pulling the rope down with the arms — take a loose grip and treat arms purely as connectors. And flexing only the upper back while keeping the lower back neutral: good lifting advice everywhere else, but the abs are the exception, since rounding the lower back is their job.

## 2. Hanging leg raise [03:42]
- Hang from a pull-up bar with roughly a **1.5x shoulder width grip**. Use **straps** if grip is the limiting factor — less forearm fatigue can improve the mind-muscle connection with the abs.
- Initiate by **rolling the hips forward and up** as the knees swing toward the chest or armpits.
- Think about crunching lower and upper abs together, not simply lifting the legs (which lets the hip flexors take over).
- At the top, lower under control. If the abs are controlling the movement rather than momentum, the **hips stay stable** when viewed from the side.

**Loading: bodyweight for 6-15 reps**, depending on the trainee's abdominal strength — it's genuinely hard when done properly.

**Errors:**
- **Bending the knees up with no hip curl.** The bent-knee version is a fine progression toward straight-leg raises, but you still must roll the hips. Simply lifting the knees only trains hip flexors and is far too easy past rank beginner.
- **Swinging.** Initiate from a dead hang by contracting the abs. If you can't do it without swinging, use a **Roman chair** (removes momentum) or start with **decline reverse crunches / dragon flags**: grab a handle, balance your weight on the upper back and shoulders, swing the legs up while crunching the abs at the top, then let the abs control the negative.

## 3. Bicycle crunch [05:42]
Chosen because it trains the rectus abdominis through spinal flexion **and** brings in the **obliques through spinal rotation**.

- Lie on the floor, hands **behind** the head without grabbing it.
- Bring the left elbow to the right knee by twisting the torso and crunching the abs; alternate sides so the legs pedal.
- Bending the knee itself does nothing for the abs — focus on mindfully crunching and twisting rather than going through the motions.

**Loading: ~15-20 reps**, performed with a faster, more explosive tempo than the other two.

**Error:** moving the arms to reach the knee. Arms stay locked in position; the elbow-to-knee contact should come entirely from spinal flexion and rotation.

## Practical application
- Get body fat to ~8-12% (men) / 14-18% (women) to actually see the result.
- Pair a torso-down movement with a legs-up movement to cover upper and lower abs.
- Weighted cable crunch: 12-20 reps, moderate load, full range including full extension at the top.
- Hanging leg raise: bodyweight, 6-15 reps, hips rolled forward, no swinging.
- Bicycle crunch: 15-20 reps, explosive, deliberate twist.
- Cue posterior pelvic tilt and hip roll to keep hip flexors out of it.

## Caveats & unknowns
The EMG researchers' names are unreadable in the auto-captions. Contreras and Schoenfeld's position is that calling crunches injurious is *premature* in healthy individuals — not a clearance for heavy loading, which is why he caps crunch loading at moderate.
