# Science-Based Chest and Back Upper Body Workout

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Most Effective Science-Based Chest & Back Workout (Full Upper Body) | Science Applied" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7qFp5khuoFA)) · 2019-06-15 · 8 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- Full session: bench press 3x6 (progressing to 8), weighted wide-grip pull-up 3x8 plus 2 forced negatives, barbell floor press 2x8-10, unilateral cable row 3x12, Arnold press 2x12, reverse pec deck 2x(10+10) mechanical drop set, rope pushdown 3x21s, optional preacher curl 2x15-20.
- Progression scheme: 6 reps in week 1, 7 in week 2, 8 in week 3, back to 6 in week 4 with more weight.
- Drive the bar off the chest with **maximum speed** on compound presses when training submaximally — a 2014 paper (via Stronger by Science) found roughly **double the strength gains** versus a slower cadence at equated volume and intensity.
- Avoid failure on the compound presses (1-2 reps in reserve), but take single-joint isolation work essentially to failure, since systemic fatigue cost is low.
- This is day 4 of an upper/lower split; Nippard states the volume across the two upper days is enough for beginners and most intermediates on a four-day split.

## Bench press — 3 sets of 6 [00:32]
His pick if forced to choose one overall pec mass builder. Per the Strength & Conditioning Research database, long-term studies show a program consisting of bench press alone produces considerable chest hypertrophy, and a **2012 study** found bench press 1RM and pec muscle thickness increased in near-perfect tandem across **24 weeks** — a stronger bench and bigger pecs go together ([Ogasawara 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24265879/)).

Because the earlier upper day used a **closer grip with less arch** to bias upper pecs and triceps, this day uses a **bigger arch and wider grip** to bias the sternal head of the mid pecs. The whole pec is highly active in both; it's regional emphasis only.

**Rep speed [01:33].** Citing a Stronger by Science write-up of a 2014 paper, subjects lifting the bar with maximum speed on every rep saw about double the strength gains of a slower cadence, with volume and intensity equated. The caveat: if you're pushing sets close to failure, rep speed stops mattering much — slow and explosive both work. But on heavy compounds trained submaximally, as here, explosive intent makes sense.

**Execution.** Lower the bar to the highest contact point on your chest, pause about half a second, then explode off the chest as if it were a 1RM, pressing up and back while thinking about pushing the floor away. Goal is maximum load with full range of motion and proper position maintained.

## Weighted wide-grip pull-up — 3 sets of 8 + 2 forced negatives [02:33]
Perform 8 normal reps with a full stretch at the bottom and chin clearly over the bar at the top. Then use a box to jump above the bar and lower slowly for **2 forced eccentrics** (or have a partner assist the concentric while you take the negative).

Rationale, from a paper by **Dr. Brad Schoenfeld**: because a muscle isn't fully fatigued by concentric training, heavy negatives may cause greater motor unit fatigue and so add hypertrophic stimulus ([Schoenfeld 2011](https://journals.lww.com/nsca-scj/Fulltext/2011/08000/The_Use_of_Specialized_Training_Techniques_to.11.aspx)).

**Scaling.** If you can't do 8 clean weighted reps, start with bodyweight. If that's still too hard, use the assisted pull-up machine for the 8 reps and then do the 2 forced negatives unassisted via the jump method. On every rep, including the forced ones, feel a strong lat stretch and mind-muscle connection on the eccentric.

## Barbell floor press — 2 sets of 8-10 [03:33]
Called one of the most underrated hypertrophy exercises for the pushing muscles. Powerlifters use it for bench lockout strength; bodybuilders benefit because it hammers the triceps in a way most pressing can't, while retaining carryover to bench strength.

Treat it like a close-grip bench with elbows more tucked to the sides; a standard ~1.5x shoulder-width grip is fine. Because leg drive is eliminated you can't move as much weight — start light and add load over the weeks.

## Unilateral cable row — 3 sets of 12 [04:34]
Most people neglect unilateral back work, which is part of why left-right strength and size imbalances are so common. Start with your **weaker side**, then match its rep count on the dominant side with the same weight, so both contribute equally.

Perform it as a **lat-dominant** row: drive the elbow **down and in** against the side, rather than straight back (which would bias traps and rear delts).

## Arnold press — 2 sets of 12 [05:04]
A vertical press assistance movement complementing the barbell overhead press on day 1. Because the press initiates with horizontal shoulder abduction, the rear delts get involved immediately before it transitions into a standard dumbbell shoulder press. Done **standing**, which forces you to use less weight due to stabilisation demands but recruits the side delts more. A complete front-to-back shoulder builder.

## Reverse pec deck — 2 sets of 10 + 10 (mechanical drop set) [05:34]
First 10 reps: slouch the upper body forward into full scapular protraction. This takes the traps out and isolates the rear delts. Reach near failure here.

Second 10 reps, without resting or reducing the weight: straighten the back and deliberately let the shoulder blades protract and retract on every rep, squeezing them together. Recruiting the traps extends the set and lets the rear delts grind out extra effective reps.

## Rope triceps pushdown — 3 sets of 21s [06:36]
Seven reps in the **bottom half** of the range, focusing on squeezing the triceps at the bottom. Seven reps in the **top half**, focusing on the stretch at the top. Seven **full range** reps to finish.

Unlike the compound presses — where a rep or two is left in the tank — take the triceps essentially to failure here, because a single-joint isolation movement carries much lower risk of accumulating systemic fatigue.

## Optional: constant-tension preacher curl — 2 sets of 15-20 [07:07]
Optional finisher. Biceps are already highly active in the pull-ups and rows, but direct isolation work is worth adding to maximise development and hit them at higher frequency.

Technique cues: no resting between reps, keep hands and forearms as loose as possible, move the bar only by flexing and squeezing the biceps rather than the forearms. Letting the wrists drift into slight extension puts the elbow flexors of the forearm in a weaker position, forcing the biceps to do more of the work.

## Practical application
- Run as day 4 of a four-day upper/lower split; the two upper days together carry enough volume for beginners and most intermediates.
- Bench progression: 3x6 (week 1) → 3x7 (week 2) → 3x8 (week 3) → back to 3x6 with more weight (week 4).
- Vary grip and arch between the two upper days to shift emphasis between upper pecs/triceps and mid pecs.
- Leave 1-2 reps in reserve on compound presses; go to failure on isolation.
- Advanced lifters can add a third weekly upper session targeting a specific weak point.

## Caveats & unknowns
The bench-only hypertrophy claim and the 2012 bench/pec-thickness study come via the Strength & Conditioning Research database summary rather than direct citation, though the 2012 study is listed in the video description as Ogasawara et al.; the rep-speed finding is reported via a Stronger by Science write-up of a 2014 paper whose authors are named nowhere. The forced-negative rationale from Schoenfeld is described as "may elicit" additional stimulus, not as an established effect.
