# Skull Crushers and Triceps Training: Technique and Exercise Selection

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "How To Build Huge Triceps with Optimal Training Technique" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=popGXI-qs98)) · 2018-12-25 · 7 min
**Topic:** Technique

## Bottom line
- Train skull crushers in the **8-20 rep range** with moderate weight to spare the elbow soft tissues.
- Cover the triceps with **three arm positions**: overhead (skull crusher, overhead cable extension), arms at the sides (pressdown), and arm behind the torso (kickback). This hits all three heads and the long head at multiple muscle lengths.
- Lower the bar **back in an arc** past the forehead, not straight down to the forehead — more elbow torque, more triceps overload, less elbow pain.
- Keep constant tension: start each rep with the bar slightly back and stop just short of full lockout.
- Movement must come from **pure elbow extension**. Shoulder movement brings in lats and pecs and reduces triceps loading.

## Anatomy and why the long head matters [00:08]
Three heads: **lateral** (outer), **medial** (down the middle), and **long** (inside of the arm). The long head is the one that beefs up arm mass viewed from behind and in biceps poses; Nippard argues it may be the most important head for overall apparent arm size.

All three heads cross the elbow and produce **elbow extension**. The long head also crosses the **shoulder joint** and therefore assists **shoulder extension** — which is why lat pullovers produce a triceps pump alongside the lats.

**Training implication:** Dr. Brad Schoenfeld has argued that because the long head is more stretched in shoulder flexion, exercises with the arms more overhead train the long head in a greater stretched position. Other research points to **about 90 degrees of shoulder flexion** as the sweet spot for long-head muscle activity — which is roughly where the skull crusher puts you, making it good for all three heads and especially the long head.

## The three-exercise framework [01:38]
For complete triceps development, include one exercise from each category:
1. **Arm overhead** — overhead cable extension or skull crusher.
2. **Arm at the sides** — triceps pressdown.
3. **Arm behind the torso** — kickback.

## Setup [02:08]
- **Load:** moderate, **8-20 reps**, to avoid over-taxing elbow soft tissue.
- **Bar:** straight bar is an option, but the **EZ bar is preferred** — the semi-pronated grip almost always puts less strain on the wrists.
- Position the bar at the top end of the bench and pick it up **overhead using the lats**, with a shoulder-width grip. You can hoist it from your lap, but if you can't take it from overhead off the bench, the weight is too heavy.
- **Floor skull crusher** is an excellent variation: the higher starting position lets you load a bit more, there's no risk of dropping the bar off the bench on a failed rep, and touching the floor each rep enforces an identical range of motion — preventing progressive cheating and ensuring real progressive overload. Downside: you're lying on the gym floor.
- Before the first rep, **retract and depress the shoulder blades** (as on a bench press) for upper-body tightness and shoulder stability.
- **Shift back on the bench so the head is slightly off the top end**, which makes it easier to clear the head on the way down.

## Constant tension and the range-of-motion research [03:10]
Initiate the first rep by moving the bar back over the face; this pulls tension into the long head and keeps tension constant across the range.

A **2017 study** found that training in the **mid-range with constant tension on the triceps produced nearly twice the triceps hypertrophy** of training through the full range while losing tension at the top. Nippard notes the result could be partly explained by differences in relative loading between groups, but as **Greg Nuckols** pointed out in the MASS research review, a nearly two-fold difference jumps off the page even with those limitations.

Applied: start each rep with the bar slightly back, and keep a **slight bend in the elbow at the top** so tension stays on the triceps — without shortening the range so much that you lose the stretch.

## Execution [03:40]
- **Eccentric:** lower the weight **back in an arc**, not straight down to the forehead. The arc increases torque at the elbow, which increases triceps overload.
- **Elbows:** viewed from the front, comfortably tucked and locked in place — no excessive flaring in or out as the elbow extends, though some flare may be necessary depending on your skeleton.
- **Depth:** go as low as mobility comfortably allows; minimum standard is **forearm making contact with the biceps** at the bottom.
- **Concentric:** squeeze the triceps to drive the bar **up and slightly forward**, pressing the middle of the hand into the bar. The shoulder stays reasonably locked — movement should be pure elbow extension, not shoulder extension.

## Common errors [04:41]
1. **Going too heavy** (the underlying cause of most of the rest).
2. **Lowering straight down to the forehead** instead of back in an arc — reduces elbow torque and triceps tension, and more people report elbow pain with this path. Also, dropping or overshooting literally crushes your skull, which is not the intended execution.
3. **Too much shoulder movement** — usually a symptom of excessive load. Recruits lats and pecs, taking work off the triceps.

## Alternative: dumbbell skull crusher [05:11]
Advantages: trains each side individually, helping prevent strength and size asymmetries; lets you adjust internal/external shoulder rotation to a more comfortable position and find where the triceps fire best. Disadvantage: the barbell version allows steadier, more incremental week-to-week load increases and a more consistent technique pattern.

## Practical application
- Skull crushers: EZ bar, **8-20 reps**, bar lowered in an arc past the head, elbows locked in, stop just shy of lockout.
- Build the triceps portion of a program around three arm positions (overhead / at sides / behind torso) in addition to horizontal pressing.
- Use the floor variation when you want heavier loading or a hard ROM checkpoint.
- Control the negative and hold the mind-muscle connection with the triceps on every rep.

## Caveats & unknowns
- The 2017 constant-tension study may be confounded by differing relative loads between groups; Nippard reports it with that caveat rather than as settled.
- The "90 degrees of shoulder flexion" optimum for long-head activation is cited without a named study.
- Some elbow flare is individual — the instruction is comfort and consistency, not a fixed angle.
