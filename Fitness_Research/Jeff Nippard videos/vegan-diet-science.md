# The Science of Vegan Diets: Eggs, Red Meat, Digestion and Vegan Bodybuilding

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "VEGAN DIET SCIENCE: Are Eggs Bad? Vegan Bodybuilding? Is Red Meat Bad?" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_qoyoItiB1E)) · 2018-08-11 · 99 min
**Topic:** Nutrition

Guest: **Kamal Patel**, director of Examine.com — two Johns Hopkins master's degrees, published on vitamin D and calcium, on hiatus from a nutrition PhD. Raised vegetarian, still mostly vegetarian, eats fish. He says he'd eat no animal products if his gut allowed (Ehlers-Danlos syndrome), so his constant "it depends" isn't hostility to veganism. Both object to **factory farming** (~90%+ of US meat) rather than meat-eating; Nippard credits Peter Singer's *The Ethics of What We Eat*. Patel's harm ranking: chickens worst → pastured local dairy cattle much better → wild-caught fish an order of magnitude better again.

## Bottom line
- Neither claims veganism is health-superior. Patel's read: vegetarian/vegan probably edges most outcomes, but the data is too confounded to commit.
- **Digestion is the biggest individual variable.** More plants is a large change to the gut; more animal protein is almost none.
- **Dietary cholesterol has a negligible-to-zero effect on blood cholesterol.** Egg evidence conflicts (RCT vs observational), unresolved.
- **Saturated fat strongly worsens blood lipids, yet RCTs show no effect on heart attacks, cardiac mortality or stroke.** Patel: nobody has explained that gap and nobody can claim to know.
- Red meat's Neu5Gc risk is mechanistic and speculative — act on it only with a family history of autoimmune conditions.
- Vegan lifting is a bioavailability problem, not an impossibility: **above 1.8 g/kg protein**, supplemented sources, plus B12, zinc, iodine, omega-3.

## Does going vegan improve health? `[14:45]`
Plant-based diets aren't monolithic — hummus-and-PB&J and whole-food versions are different interventions. Off the standard American diet it will likely help; off an already-good diet it depends on your conditions and microbiome.
- **Epic-Oxford:** no mortality difference between vegans, vegetarians, omnivores. Pescetarians: lower cancer, slightly higher heart disease (possible confounding).
- **Adventist study:** ~**10–15% lower mortality** in vegetarians. Some live in a Blue Zone (Loma Linda; Okinawa another). Patel warns Blue Zone longevity gets credited to diet when social life, sleep and low stress may explain most of it.
- Nippard cites a 2017 systematic review (24 studies) where nearly every outcome favoured vegan. Patel's counter: **the control group is the flaw** — these compare "eats meat" to "doesn't". Vegetarian-vs-paleo or vegetarian-vs-Mediterranean has never been run.
- Healthy-user confounding: people on "named diets that aren't from the 90s" (paleo, keto, vegan, pescetarian) are simply healthier and more research-literate.

## Digestion `[15:46]`
- The meme that animal protein putrefies in the gut is **backwards**. Animal protein meets stomach HCl and digests near-completely; *every* plant protein escapes small-intestine digestion to some degree (locked in a food matrix) and reaches the colon.
- The gut is an immune-system extension (~**70%** of immune tissue is gut-associated); plants are the "foreign" input and all carry defence compounds, though cultivation bred the worst down (rapeseed → low-acid canola).
- **IBD:** plant-based helps or is neutral. **IBS:** more often hurts — IBD is visible pathology, IBS has unknown pathophysiology (small-intestinal overgrowth, or bacteria protected by biofilm).
- **Stool heuristic** (rule of thumb, not a trial finding): hard, hard-to-pass stools (Bristol scale) usually improve on plants — more bulk and water, fewer hydrogen-sulfide producers. IBS-D usually worsens. More plants for constipation; fewer or more selective plants for diarrhoea.
- **Resistant starch cuts both ways.** Cooking then cooling rice or potatoes leaves ~**20%** of the starch undigested — good for most, but *Klebsiella* feeds on starch and is implicated in ankylosing spondylitis, hence low-starch diets for spinal joint pain.
- **Meat-side mechanism:** very high-fat meals may ferry bacterial **endotoxins (LPS)** across a compromised gut lining via chylomicrons; endotoxins are implicated in inflammatory and autoimmune conditions. Joint pain can worsen in *either* direction.

## Carnivore as elimination `[35:15]`
An n=1 carnivore result can't be argued either way, but the **mechanism is plausible**: the best diet for digestive complaints is fasting, and digestive disturbance correlates with autoimmune symptoms and possibly energy and sleep. A **one- to two-week meat-only trial is low acute risk** and works as an aggressive elimination diet — it tells you the culprit is a plant, then reintroduce. Add dextrose if not going ketogenic. Hedge: no long-term carnivore data exists and never will.

## Processed meat, red meat, Neu5Gc `[38:49]`
- **Nitrates aren't the villain** — likely why leafy greens and beets are healthy (vasodilation, better flow, lower clot risk); boost half-life is a couple of hours, so greens a few times daily sustains it. Fibre's correlation with outcomes is weak; nitrate's is stronger.
- **Processed meat's problem is heat:** heating nitrate-preserved meat forms **N-nitroso compounds**, linked to worse heart health and colorectal cancer.
- **Neu5Gc.** Most mammals coat cells in the sialic acid Neu5Gc; humans mutated ~2 million years ago to Neu5Ac (shared only with guinea pigs and some New World monkeys). Four clues: (1) human tumours can contain very high Neu5Gc, but not all do; (2) **Hashimoto's thyroiditis patients have 7× higher levels**; (3) rheumatoid arthritis patients don't, so it isn't all autoimmunity; (4) a ~2015–16 mouse study feeding high-Neu5Gc pork juice produced tumours at very high rates — at roughly **twice** a meat-only human's dose, in genetically mutated mice.
- **Content:** red meat highest, dairy contains it, chicken and fish none. Narrow recommendation: consider avoiding red meat with a **family history of autoimmune conditions**, or if you feel poorly on it. "A chink in the armour" of carnivore diets, not demonstrated harm.

## Risk in proportion, and ancestral diets `[46:01]`
WHO's **50 g processed meat daily = 18% higher colorectal cancer risk** is *relative*. Baseline ~**4%**, so it goes to ~**5%** — an **absolute increase of about 1%**, for 50 g every day across a lifetime. Patel: **the dose makes the poison**, and headlines never mention dose. Historical intake was episodic — feast, then two days to a month with little animal protein, swinging between **200–300 g protein and 20–30 g**. Daily normal portions sit an order of magnitude below the doses that concern researchers.

Not to copy from paleo: open-flame cooking forms **heterocyclic amines**, highly carcinogenic. WHO placed fresh red meat in **Group 2A** (probably carcinogenic), processed meat in **Group 1** (carcinogenic) — driven by HCAs and nitroso compounds. The "plants offset HCAs" counter fails: greens provide no calories and there's no dressing on a tree. To copy: **variety** and **not eating constantly**. Grains aren't inherently bad; legumes are generally healthy; nuts show excellent trial evidence yet some do badly on them — researchers expected pistachios to improve cycling time-trial performance and the pistachio group did **significantly worse**, probably because nuts and seeds resist breakdown and can leak material through a compromised gut lining.

**Fasting:** Thomas Seyfried reportedly does a seven-day fast at least annually for cancer prevention; Patel thinks **24–48 hours occasionally is pretty safe** and sufficient. Valter Longo's fasting-mimicking diet is **500–700 calories** for a short block, monthly or every second month.

## Eggs `[57:21]`
Patel has **not made up his mind** — the research is genuinely inconclusive.
- **Favourable:** a large 2018 RCT in **diabetics**, **12+ eggs/week vs 1–2**, found **no difference in any cardiometabolic risk factor** (cholesterol, triglycerides, blood sugar). Important because the prior position was "fine unless diabetic".
- **Conflicting:** a meta-analysis ~two years earlier found **more heart failure** at higher intake. Observational, so it can't isolate eggs — but large, and a **hard outcome**, where the RCT measured only intermediate markers. He refuses to declare a winner.
- **Reconciliation is dose.** The RCT compared a couple a day to none; the heart-failure data a lot vs almost none. **Neither had a moderate group.** Five or ten a week is almost certainly safe; **three or four a day he isn't sure about**.
- **Dietary cholesterol was debunked decades ago.** Residual mechanism is **oxidised cholesterol** from high heat, but only ~**1–2%** of yolk cholesterol oxidises — plausible only at 6–12 eggs a day (Ronnie Coleman at 280 lb). Runny yolks don't oxidise; evidence too preliminary to change how you cook.
- **Vegan Gains' criticism**, put to Patel: egg defenders either deny dietary cholesterol raises blood cholesterol or deny blood cholesterol is a CVD risk factor, and the nulls appear only because subjects already had high cholesterol. Patel believes those subjects didn't start high (an IRB wouldn't approve feeding eggs to high-cholesterol patients), but **concedes the broader point** — don't argue dietary cholesterol, argue **saturated fat**.
- Eggs are a leading histamine-reaction food: relevant to people with existing conditions, barely studied, not an issue for the healthy.

## Saturated fat and LDL particles `[65:35]`
- Across RCTs, saturated fat has an **extremely strong** effect on blood lipids — substituted for PUFA, MUFA or carbohydrate, lipids and lipoproteins rise. Exception: high carbohydrate raises triglycerides more than equivalent saturated fat.
- **LDL-C is the wrong number.** Doctors measure cholesterol quantity; what matters is **LDL-P**, particle *number* — more particles, more chance one lodges in the artery wall and oxidises. **Size doesn't rescue you**: 100 large "fluffy" particles are as bad as 100 small dense ones. Heart disease is roughly part lodged lipid, part inflammation.
- **The gap:** observational data say more saturated fat and higher cholesterol markers are worse, but **RCTs of hard outcomes — heart attack, heart failure, stroke, cardiac mortality — show no effect.** Patel is blunt that nobody has explained this and nobody can claim to know; Nippard agrees. Patel's read: a lot of saturated fat probably isn't healthy, a moderate amount seems fine, the evidence conflicts.
- **HDL isn't "good", LDL isn't "bad".** HDL clears, LDL delivers; cholesterol is needed for hormones. The problem is overwhelming clearance — ~**20% of people** have genetic susceptibility to poor clearance (what PCSK9 inhibitors address). Clearest disproof of the simple model: a large HDL-raising trial (he attributes it to Pfizer) raised HDL **20–40 percentage points** and was **stopped because mortality rose**.
- **Where whole-food vegan diets genuinely win:** both mechanisms — fewer LDL particles, plus an anti-inflammatory pattern and healthier long-term microbiome. But **meat is not inherently bad for your heart**.

## Protein and vegan bodybuilding `[09:40]`, `[82:02]`
- Quality scoring moved from **PDCAAS to DIAAS**, using indicator amino acids escaping small- or large-intestine digestion; it rates plant proteins quite differently.
- Bioavailability is low enough to **multiply by two** for the animal equivalent. Nippard's example: 100 g quinoa = 120 calories, only **4.4 g protein**; where ~**20 g** animal protein maximises MPS you'd want **40 g** from quinoa — over **1,000 calories** for one feeding.
- **Targets:** Patel names **1.2–1.4 g/kg** for health and maintenance. **Nippard pushes to ~1.8 g/kg**, and both agree a vegan should be **above 1.8 g/kg** given lower bioavailability and lower leucine — plant protein is inherently less anabolic per gram.
- Whole-foods-only means **four to six meals a day**. For a lean muscular physique you **almost have to supplement** — practically **pea + rice** or pea + soy blends; Nippard adds hemp.
- **Rotate sources.** Not because soy phytoestrogen effects show in trials (mostly they don't), but because detecting adverse effects needs subgroup analyses these trials aren't powered for, and vegan bodybuilders take far larger soy doses than the East Asian tradition. Also contamination: one factory's powder drunk daily raises heavy-metal exposure — same for a daily greens smoothie.
- **Leucine supplementation isn't a workaround:** free amino acids are almost all animal-derived (feathers, hair, via hydrolysis) and extremely expensive. Both flag it as unverified.

## Nutrients to actually watch `[74:56]`
Protein and B12 are what everyone already thinks about, so the interesting gaps are elsewhere. Use **Cronometer**; MyFitnessPal doesn't track these.
- **Omega-3.** ALA→EPA/DHA conversion is genetically variable — ~**18–20% in some, under 10% in others** — governed by copy number of the **FADS** desaturase gene; people of Indian or sub-Saharan African ancestry carry more copies. **Don't supplement unless you have a reason:** eating animal products, usually no need; vegan and risk-averse, or depressed (DHA especially may help), take **algae oil**. **Nippard is more convinced than Patel** of fish oil's cardioprotective effects but prefers food first — fish **2–4×/week**. Patel's scepticism rests on a **Cochrane review published the day of recording**, moving the omega-3/heart-disease link from small-to-negligible to essentially **zero association**. Quality varies (rancidity, heavy metals): batch-tested products, no big Costco bottles, no liquid fish oil. **More is not better** — omega-3s and -6s are long kinked chains vulnerable to free-radical attack; there is **no DRI**, partly weak evidence, partly that excess risk is unstudied. He criticises old paleo-era calculators prescribing eight fish oil pills a day.
- **Vitamin A, vitamin E, magnesium** — named alongside omega-3 as what vegans miss unknowingly.
- **B12.** No reliable vegan source. Some go decades without symptoms, possibly via bacteria on home-grown produce or nori — unpredictable with supermarket food. **Just supplement.**
- **Zinc** is his **number-one deficiency** in vegans/vegetarians, especially athletes (lost through activity); needed for muscle gain, sleep, hormone production.
- **Iodine.** Not a plant problem — most iodine comes from **iodised salt**, and health-conscious vegans swap it for Himalayan pink salt, which isn't iodised. Fix with kelp flakes or sea vegetables.
- **Calcium** is overrated — he worked on the meta-analysis behind the **2009–10 vitamin D guidelines** and read ~**300 studies**. Surprising finding: **no link between calcium alone and osteoporosis prevention or bone density**. It helps once vitamin D, vitamin K (K1 from plants, K2 from conversion or animal sources) and magnesium are adequate — those direct calcium to bone rather than arteries. Misplaced calcium appears as **coronary calcification**, one of the strongest correlates of dying from heart disease.
- **Iron is the opposite problem.** Eat **as little as you can while staying non-anaemic** — it oxidises readily and accumulates with no route of excretion. The **HFE** gene ("high iron") causes hereditary haemochromatosis in ~**1 in 250 American men**; women are protected by menstruation; risk is elevated in white men of Nordic or British ancestry. Asymptomatic for years, then presents seriously; the only real treatment is bloodletting or blood donation. Realistic route to overload is **supplements + fortified foods + cast-iron cooking**. For vegans, low iron is rarely the concern — you'd notice anaemia.

## Practical application
- **Vegan lifter:** **above 1.8 g/kg** protein; **pea+rice** (or pea+soy, hemp) blend; **rotate brands**; supplement **B12**; consider **zinc** and **algae oil**; check **iodine** if you've left iodised salt.
- **Track once, properly** in Cronometer — omega-3, vitamin A, vitamin E, magnesium, not just protein and B12.
- **Use your own body as the study.** Patel's closing advice is an **elimination diet**: strip to tolerated foods (white rice not brown — brown carries more anti-nutrients; berries over higher-fructose fruit; a plain protein, meat on the bone slow-cooked or supplemental protein for vegans), then reintroduce. Otherwise keep a **two-week diet log** tracking bowel movements, energy and sleep. "Studies don't matter if your body does not agree."
- Don't char meat over high heat; don't high-heat cook nitrate-preserved processed meat. Men of British/Nordic descent: test iron status once.

## Caveats & unknowns
- **Almost all diet-comparison evidence is observational** and confounded by the healthy-user effect. Vegetarian vs a *matched* good omnivorous diet has never been run.
- **The saturated fat gap is unresolved** — biomarkers say one thing, hard-outcome RCTs another. Patel: nobody knows, vegan or carnivore. **Egg evidence conflicts** RCT-vs-observational, with no moderate-intake group in either.
- **Neu5Gc is preliminary and mechanistic** — suggestive, not causal; mutated mice at ~2× a human carnivore's dose; rheumatoid arthritis breaks the pattern.
- **Carnivore has no long-term data and never will.** The gut heuristics are rules of thumb. **Omega-3 has no DRI.**
