# Building Bigger Traps: Shrug Technique and Variations

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "How To Build Bigger Traps: Optimal Training Explained" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C6sYjDFuq9I)) · 2019-03-12 · 8 min
**Topic:** Technique

## Bottom line
- A shrug is not just scapular elevation — it's elevation *plus* upward rotation. Pull the shoulders up **and in toward the ears**, not straight up.
- Two governing principles: the upper traps recruit better with the arms at roughly **30 degrees of abduction** (1994 study), and the traps outperform the other shrugging muscles at scapular upward rotation — so bias the movement toward that.
- Barbell shrugs: **8-15 reps**. Cable shrugs: **12-20 reps**.
- Trap bar shrug is Nippard's personal favourite; dual cable shrug is his favourite creative variation and the usual fix for people who can't feel their traps.
- The dominant error is going too heavy, which cuts range of motion — progressively adding weight while progressively shortening ROM is not progressive overload.

## Anatomy [00:03]
The shrug mainly targets the upper trapezius and levator scapulae through scapular elevation. But you shouldn't just push the bar straight up — bringing the shoulders in closer to the neck produces scapular upward rotation, which is handled by the upper and mid traps exclusively. There's also some scapular retraction, so the mid and lower traps are active to a degree.

The traditional barbell shrug is hit-or-miss: some people feel a strong upper trap contraction, others feel it mostly in the levator scapulae along the side of the neck. Nothing wrong with training the neck, but the goal here is isolating the upper trap fibres.

**The 30-degree principle.** A 1994 study found that because of the orientation of the upper trap fibres, they can't elevate the scapula as effectively when the arm hangs neutral. Recruitment improves with the arms roughly 30 degrees abducted ([Johnson 1994](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/23916077)).

## Rep range and load
Use a moderate range, around **8-15 reps**. Loading heavier usually kills range of motion, especially at the top end. A power shrug (leg drive to launch the weight) is an advanced cheating technique — it must still be lowered under eccentric control, used sparingly, and only once strict shrug technique is mastered.

## Barbell shrug execution
1. Set the bar in a power rack above knee height so you can unrack without fatiguing the lower back.
2. Double overhand grip at about **1.25 to 1.5 times shoulder width**.
3. Straps are necessary for most lifters to truly overload the traps. If grip strength is an equal priority, do at least one heavy set strapless.
4. Keep a neutral lower back and drive the hips forward to unrack, like locking out a deadlift. Take two or three steps back if shrugging outside the rack.
5. Feet slightly wider than shoulder width with a slight flare.
6. Lean the torso forward about **10 degrees** by hinging at the hips. Scapulae must not protract — upper back neutral and engaged.
7. Knees nearly straight; a slight bend at knees and hips keeps the bar from hitting your groin on the way up.
8. Elevate the scapulae by lifting the shoulders up *and* pulling them as if trying to touch your shoulders to your ears. To emphasise upward rotation further, think about pushing the arms slightly outward as if doing a lateral raise.
9. Shrug all the way to **terminal elevation** — the point where the scapulae can't rise any further regardless of load. Squeeze at the top; no pause or hold needed.
10. Reverse under control, letting the traps actively stretch while resisting the load.

## Variations
- **Trap bar shrug (Nippard's favourite).** Standing inside the frame automatically puts the arms at roughly 30 degrees abduction, and the bar can't rub the front of the body.
- **Smith machine shrug.** Useful when the lower back is sore or fatigued, since it removes some spinal loading from the forward lean. Shouldn't permanently replace a free weight option.
- **Dumbbell shrug.** Eliminates the bar-rubbing problem like the trap bar. Downsides: holding abduction can make the lateral delt the limiting factor, and available dumbbells may be too light for advanced lifters. Good for high-rep or slow-tempo work.
- **Standing calf raise machine shrug.** Reasonable trap isolation option when grip is compromised.
- **Dual cable shrug (favourite creative variation).** Stand in the middle of a cable rack holding two D handles so that, viewed from the side, the cables run through the mid frontal plane. Lean forward ~10 degrees, shrug up and in toward the ears, slightly abducting the upper arms like a lateral raise to increase the upward rotation range and reach peak upper trap contraction. Mike Israetel is shown doing this with some elbow bend near the top — not required, but it may give a stronger contraction. Load lighter: **12-20 reps**, prioritising mind-muscle connection over heavy overload.

## Common errors
**Going too heavy.** The shrug is not a test of how much you can hold with straps on. Excess load slashes range of motion. Lighten up, fix the form, rebuild gradually while keeping form consistent.

**Rolling the shoulders forward or backward, or letting the scapulae protract.** The upper traps elevate the scapula most effectively from a neutral or retracted position, so hold that position and keep upper back tightness throughout. Rolling creates form inconsistency. If you're protracting to avoid hitting your crotch, fix it with the slight hip-hinge forward lean or switch to dumbbells or another non-barbell variation.

## Practical application
- Barbell/trap bar shrugs: 8-15 reps, straps on, ~10 degree forward lean, shrug up and in to terminal elevation.
- Add dual cable shrugs at 12-20 reps if you struggle to feel your traps.
- Do at least one heavy strapless set per session if grip development matters to you.
- Judge progress by whether you're reaching terminal scapular elevation, not by bar weight alone.
