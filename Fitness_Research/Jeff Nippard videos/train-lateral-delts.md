# How To Train the Lateral Delts: Function, Rotation Cues and Cheat Reps

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "How To Train LATERAL DELTS (Science Explained) | Honeymoon Island Adventure" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iUlOM2Kigpw)) · 2016-09-22 · 8 min
**Topic:** Technique

## Bottom line
- The side delt has **three functions**, and each has a rotation cue that biases it: **abduction and flexion favour internal rotation; transverse abduction favours external rotation.**
- On **front raises, point your pinkies up** (internal rotation) to recruit more side delt. On the **reverse pec deck, point your thumbs up** (external rotation) to make it more side-delt focused.
- **Lateral raises are the best isolation** for the side delts — on that movement the anterior and posterior fibres act only as stabilisers.
- **Controlled cheating works.** Moderate momentum at the *start* of a lateral raise lets you overload the biomechanically strong middle of the range (~30–60° of abduction).
- He runs **multiple lateral raise variations every session** — machine, cable, dumbbell, band.

## The three functions of the side delt [02:39]
1. **Abduction** — arm away from the body, out to the side, as in a lateral raise. **Side delt involvement increases with greater internal rotation.**
2. **Flexion** — arm raised to the front, as in a front raise. Again, **side delt contribution is accentuated with greater internal rotation.**
3. **Transverse abduction with external rotation** — as in a reverse pec deck. Here the side delt is **more active when the arm is externally rotated.**

**Cue 1:** On front raises, **internally rotate — point the pinkies up** — to shift work from front delt to side delt.
**Cue 2:** On a reverse pec deck, **externally rotate — point the thumbs up** — to make it more side-delt dominant.

## Two checks for whether an exercise is actually hitting the side delt [03:40]
1. **The biomechanics check.** Think about the direction the side delt fibres run and ask whether they line up with the load. Raise your arm to the front: with the **thumb up**, the **front delt** is in line with the load; with the **pinky up**, the **side delt** is in line with the load.
2. **The feel check.** Either judge internally by which position gives a better mind-muscle connection, or **physically palpate the muscle** to test whether it fires harder in different positions.

## Machine shoulder press caveat [02:08]
The handles on many machine presses force the elbows **more out in front of you**, which he reasons produces **more front delt and less side delt** work — side delts are more active when the upper arm sits closer to the mid-frontal plane with the elbows out to the side, as in a behind-the-neck press. He explicitly flags this as inference: no data compares handle positions directly.

## Rope upright row: three technique cues [04:11]
- **Initiate the movement OUT, not UP** — this emphasises abduction over flexion (which is what a front raise trains).
- **Actively pull the rope apart** throughout, training abduction isometrically.
- **Externally rotate slightly at the top**, where the movement starts to involve transverse abduction — remember, in transverse abduction the side delt is more active when externally rotated.

## Cheating on lateral raises: Arandjelović 2012 [04:41]
A **2012 paper by Arandjelović** examined how applying **external momentum (cheating)** affects peak torque and what he termed the **total hypertrophy stimulus** for lateral raises. Note the design: it was a **computer-based simulation** of the exercise, not subjects performing it, using anthropometric measures plus data from previous muscle-recruitment work.

Findings and reasoning:
- **Moderate momentum at the beginning of each rep** allows **heavier loads** and **better overload of the muscle in biomechanically advantageous positions**.
- The **biomechanically advantageous position for the side delt is the middle of the range, roughly 30–60° of abduction.**
- The argument: if you supply momentum at the point where the target muscle is in a **biomechanically inferior position** to produce force, you overcome that weak point and can apply **greater force through the range that is better suited to overloading the muscle**.
- An **initial angular velocity of ~57.12° per second** produced the highest peak forces.

**Practical translation** (since nobody can measure the angular velocity of their lateral raises): find the amount of cheating that lets you handle **moderately heavier loads without losing control**, especially on the eccentric. Concretely — **a slight forward lean at the start to get the weight moving, then a more upright posture for the rest of the concentric, and a full controlled eccentric.**

## Practical application
- **Front raises:** pinkies up.
- **Reverse pec deck for side delts:** thumbs up.
- **Rope upright rows:** drive out not up, pull the rope apart, externally rotate at the top.
- **Lateral raises:** the primary side delt builder. Use **slight controlled momentum** from a small forward lean to start each rep, keep the eccentric fully controlled.
- **Run several lateral raise variations per session** — machines, cables, dumbbells and bands — rather than a single one.
- Structurally: **side delts emphasised on push days, rear delts on pull days.**

## Caveats & unknowns
- The Arandjelović study is a **computer simulation with modelled inputs, not a training or EMG study on live subjects** — treat the momentum recommendation as a mechanistic argument, not an outcome finding.
- The claim about machine press handle position biasing front over side delt is **explicitly acknowledged as unsupported by data**.
- The rotation cues are based on function and fibre-line reasoning; no specific EMG studies are cited for them in this video.
