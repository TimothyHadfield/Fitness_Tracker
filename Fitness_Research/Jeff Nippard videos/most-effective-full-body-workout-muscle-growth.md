# High-Frequency Full Body, Day 1: Leg-Focused Workout

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Most Effective FULL BODY Workout For Muscle Growth" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nxisr1AalNc)) · 2020-01-18 · 9 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- Day 1 of a five-day-per-week full-body split; every training day hits the whole body but prioritises one muscle. Today: quads, hamstrings, glutes.
- Never train heavy compounds to failure on a high-frequency split — leave 2-3 reps in the tank on squats or you wreck the next day.
- Only one or two highly demanding movements per day. Everything else is chosen for stimulus-to-fatigue ratio.
- Squat depth: parallel is enough for hypertrophy. Quarter squats are not.
- Alternate upper and lower exercises so each muscle gets long rest between its own movements — longer rest supports greater hypertrophy.

## Warm-up
5-10 min on the elliptical or Stairmaster, 3-4 min of dynamic stretching, then foam roll any tight areas.

## The workout

**1. Squat — 4 x 4 @ 80% 1RM**
[01:01] These must not feel maximal; by set four you should still have 2-3 reps left. Going to failure on heavy compounds is especially costly here because squats recruit huge muscle mass including the spinal erectors, which interferes with everything planned the next day. Focus on perfect technique at the prescribed intensity, not max effort.

*Depth:* A 2002 study found greater glute activation in full squats than parallel or partial, but it used the same absolute load for all three variations. When **Contreras and colleagues** replicated it with relative loading, they found no difference between parallel and full squats for the glutes or any muscle tested. So parallel is sufficient for hypertrophy. That is not licence to do knee bends — **Bloomquist and colleagues** found very shallow quarter squats far inferior to parallel squats for leg growth. If you can reach depth with only slight butt wink, go there; if hip or ankle mobility is limiting, do mobility work and settle for parallel.

*Cue:* After setting the bar (high or low), walking back 2-3 steps, setting a comfortable stance and breathing deep into the gut (not up into the chest), **screw your feet into the floor** so the foot presses firmly into the outer wall of the shoe. Creates a more stable base and helps prevent knee cave.

*Progression:* Weekly undulating. Week 1 heavy 4x4, week 2 lighter 3x6, week 3 back to 4x4 with heavier weight, week 4 lighter 3x6 again. Balances strength gain against accumulating enough effective reps for hypertrophy.

**2. Incline dumbbell press — 3 x 8**
[03:04] Placed here deliberately so the legs recover before the next lower-body movement. Based on a **2017 systematic review** finding longer rest periods promote greater hypertrophy because they allow higher total volume load — so the more rest between exercises for the same muscle, the better.

Hits pecs, delts and triceps in one movement, which is efficient recovery-wise. EMG shows both more upper-chest activity and more deltoid activity as bench inclination increases, especially with a wider grip. Free weights are chosen over a machine because EMG data he cites (researcher name unintelligible in the captions) show lateral delts far more active with dumbbells than the Smith machine, while anterior delts and pecs are similar — the lateral delts are stabilising to stop the dumbbells tipping. Since there's no direct triceps work on day 1, bring the dumbbells down and forward with elbows tucked more than usual, then press up and back toward your face; the added shoulder flexion emphasises upper pecs.

**3. Lying leg curl — 3 x 10**
[05:04] Squats do a poor job on hamstrings — they lengthen at the knee while shortening at the hip. And even hip-extension work like the RDL can't reach every head: the **short head of biceps femoris crosses only the knee joint**, so it is biomechanically impossible for it to contribute to hip extension. To hit all four hamstring heads you need a knee-flexion movement. Choose the less damaging option (lying leg curl) rather than a stretch-loaded one like the glute ham raise, because RDLs are coming on day 2.

**4. Lat pulldown — 3 x 10**
[06:05] Pull-ups might build more back overall, but after heavy squats and a heavy dumbbell press, heavy weighted pull-ups are too much for one day. On a high-frequency split, cap yourself at one or two highly demanding movements per session. Weighted pull-ups appear instead on the back-focused day. No horizontal row today: running both a vertical and a horizontal pull five days a week would push weekly back volume far too high, so alternate vertical pulls (pulldown) with horizontal pulls (row, face pull) across the week.

**5. EZ-bar bicep curl — 3 x 12/12 drop set**
[07:05] The only movement taken to failure on day 1. Intensity techniques are generally avoided on high-frequency work because of the recovery cost, but the biceps are small enough that a drop set won't generate the fatigue that quads or lats would, so it won't compromise the next few days. Swing the bar out in an arc rather than curling back toward the chest, and cue supination by driving the pinky up into the bar.

**6. Hanging leg raise — 3 sets**
[07:36] A complete ab routine needs at least one movement bringing legs toward the chest (leg raise) and one bringing the upper body toward the legs (crunch). Research shows the squat barely activates rectus abdominis or the external obliques, so abs need direct work.

## Practical application
- Run it as listed if time allows. If pressed for time, superset each pair — one set of squats, rest 1-2 min, one set of incline press, rest 1-2 min, back to squats.
- Keep every set except the bicep drop set well short of failure.
- Sequence exercises across the whole week, not just within the day: what you damage today dictates what you can train tomorrow.

## Caveats & unknowns
This is written for intermediate to advanced lifters. With only a year or two of training experience, run a three-day-per-week full-body program instead. The five-day full-body structure only works if fatigue is managed exercise by exercise; the same exercise choices would be unnecessary compromises on a 2-3x/week split, where he says heavy weighted pull-ups on the same day as squats would be perfectly fine.
