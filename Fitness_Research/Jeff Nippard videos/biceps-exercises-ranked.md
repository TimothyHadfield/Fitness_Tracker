# Biceps Exercises Ranked S–F: What Actually Builds Arms

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Best And Worst Biceps Exercises" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GNO4OtYoCYk)) · 2024-10-19 · 16 min
**Topic:** Exercise Selection

## Bottom line
- Best single biceps exercise: the **face-away (Bayesian) cable curl**. Closest runner-up: the **45° preacher curl**. Both belong in a biceps routine.
- Worst: the **waiter curl** (F tier) — wrist strain, awkward, no overload path.
- What separates tiers: high tension *in the stretched position*, comfort (no elbow/wrist pain), and a simple way to add weight or reps.
- High tension in a decent stretch beats a huge stretch with no tension — this is why preacher curls out-grew incline curls in a head-to-head study ([Zabaleta-Korta 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37559762/)).
- For biceps **peak**, hammer curls are his #1 pick (brachialis-driven), and face-away cable curls are the long-head must-include.

## Ranking criteria and anatomy
Three boxes: high tension especially when stretched; feels good (no elbow pain, smooth resistance curve); simple progression.

Two biceps heads — **short head** (inside of arm), **long head** (outer/back of arm, contributes to peak). Underneath sits the **brachialis**, roughly as large as both biceps heads combined; growing it pushes the biceps up and enhances peak.

Biceps functions: elbow flexion and **supination** (turning palm up). Cue for supination: drive through the pinkies as you curl. Both heads cross the shoulder, so the biceps are more stretched when the arm is held behind the torso.

## The rankings

**S tier**
- **Face-away (Bayesian) cable curl** — arms behind the torso means maximum stretch plus even cable tension. Set cable around hand height, step forward 1–2 steps, curl both arms simultaneously (or one at a time on a single stack). Must-include for the long head / peak.
- **45° preacher curl** — angling the upper arm pulls tension into the stretched position; elbows locked so cheating is near impossible. A study comparing preacher vs incline curls found **significantly more distal biceps growth** (near the elbow) with preacher curls ([Zabaleta-Korta 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37559762/)), most likely because incline gives a big stretch but almost no tension there.
- **Machine preacher curl** — locked in; he does these weekly and often works only the bottom half of the range to emphasize the stretch.
- **Hammer-grip preacher curl** — bracing hammer curls on a preacher bench fixes the resistance profile of standing hammer curls.

**A tier**
- **EZ-bar curl** — curved handles ease wrist torque; he uses the outer grip. The angled grip takes you partly out of supination, shifting some work to brachialis/brachioradialis — counter it by driving through the pinkies. Great as the heavy free-weight movement in the **6–8 rep range**. Optimization-wise a B, bumped for convenience and overload.
- **Standing dumbbell curl** (low A) — zero tension at full stretch, but convenient and lets you fix side-to-side asymmetries. Do both arms at once; **alternating** is likely slightly worse because of the long unloaded rest between reps (reasoning from [Goto et al. 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15947720/)).
- **Incline curl** (low A) — solid lengthened-biased option, fast setup, but demoted since the preacher-curl study.
- **Lying dumbbell curl** — an extreme incline curl (seen in Dr. Mike's arm routine); huge stretch high near the shoulder, and more tension in the stretched start than an incline curl. Skip if you have shoulder issues.
- **90°-angle face-away cable variation** (unnamed) — cable perpendicular to the forearm at peak stretch, so maximum tension where the muscle is longest. He feels it's S tier but held it at A pending more time using it.
- **Modified 21s** (low A) — see below.
- **Cheat curl** (bottom of A) — a little momentum to move more load, then control the anabolic negative. Not for beginners; there is **no study** on cheat vs strict form (he plans to run one).
- **Strict curl** (A) — three points of contact against a wall (glutes, upper back, head) so all bar tension goes to the biceps. He suspects it beats cheat curls but can't confirm.
- **Dumbbell hammer curl** (A) — neutral grip removes supination, so less biceps and much more brachialis (brachialis only flexes the elbow, it can't supinate). His **#1 movement for biceps peak**, though he notes there's no hard science on changing peak shape. Held out of S only by the free-weight tension curve.
- **Inverse Zottman curl** (high A) — hammer grip on the way up (brachialis, more load), supinated on the way down (biceps, and biceps are stronger eccentrically).

**B tier**
- **Barbell curl** — hardest at 90° elbow, tension falls off above and drops to zero at the bottom where the biceps are most stretched. Excellent for overload; wrist strain and squat-rack hogging are the downsides. Technically C+, bumped for accessibility.
- **Flat bench curl** (low B) — highest tension at deepest stretch, but tension collapses through the rest of the rep and you stall quickly because you're stuck with light weight and high reps. Fine as a high-rep finisher.
- **Chin-ups** — best back exercise for biceps, real elbow ROM, but the back usually fails first so the biceps aren't the limiting factor. Great for beginners; free.

**C tier**
- **Scott curl** — vertical upper arm removes all tension in the stretch; a worse 45° preacher curl.
- **Drag curl** — pulling the arm back stretches the biceps at the shoulder while shortening it at the elbow, so total muscle length barely changes (same logic as why squats don't build hamstrings).
- **Spider curl** — arms forward puts the biceps in a permanently shortened position; dumbbells clip the bench.
- **Traditional 21s** (high C) — wrong order (long, short, full).

**F tier**
- **Waiter curl** — cranks the wrists, awkward plate, poor overload. Use dumbbells instead; you can still hyperextend the wrist slightly to take the forearms out.

## 21s, done better
Standard 21s = 7 bottom-half, 7 top-half, 7 full. He argues that puts the most anabolic (lengthened) portion first, while you're freshest but it isn't needed there. His order: **short → full → long**. 7 seated curls (top half; seated standardizes the ROM), stand up for 7 full-ROM reps, then 7 bottom-half reps to finish — the set gets easier as you fatigue and ends on lengthened partials. On the last block he goes past 7, continuing until he can't get halfway up.

## Practical application
- Build the routine around one face-away cable curl and one 45° preacher (or machine preacher) curl.
- Add a hammer-grip movement — preacher-braced if possible — to hit the brachialis for peak.
- Use the EZ-bar curl as the heavy overload lift at 6–8 reps.
- Curl both arms simultaneously rather than alternating.
- Cue supination by driving through the pinkies, especially with angled or in-between grips.
- Drop waiter, Scott, drag and spider curls entirely.

## Caveats & unknowns
- No studies exist on strict vs cheating technique; he leans strict but says it could go either way.
- The brachialis-changes-peak-shape idea is mechanistic reasoning, not evidence — biceps genetic structure can't be changed.
- The 90° face-away variation is a personal experiment of about one month; he deliberately under-rated it.
- Cheat curls are not recommended for beginners.
- Lying dumbbell curls may aggravate existing shoulder problems.
