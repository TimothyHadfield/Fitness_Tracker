# Four Shoulder Puzzles (Questions Posed)

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "4 Unsolved Shoulder Mysteries" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=loR7jZ817wg)) · 2017-06-07 · 2 min
**Topic:** Physiology

## Bottom line
- This is a question-only video — four biomechanics puzzles put to viewers, with answers given in a follow-up. No conclusions are drawn here.
- The puzzles all concern apparent contradictions between what biomechanics predicts and what EMG data actually shows for the deltoids.
- Answers are covered in the companion note, `4-shoulder-mysteries-resolved.md`.

## The four questions

**1. Shoulder press and the lateral deltoid.** The shoulder press is a frontal plane movement — the dumbbells travel in the plane dividing the body into front and back halves. The lateral raise operates in the same plane and targets the lateral deltoid. So why doesn't the shoulder press target the lateral deltoid more than the front deltoid?

**2. Front raise vs shoulder press for the front delt.** The front raise trains pure shoulder flexion, and shoulder flexion is the front deltoid's role. So why is the front raise *less* effective than the shoulder press at activating the front delt? (Assume relative loading — the answer cannot simply be "you can go heavier on the press.")

**3. Grip width and deltoid activation.** Does a wider grip or a narrower grip produce greater deltoid activation in pressing?

**4. Neutral vs pronated grip on the reverse pec deck.** **Schoenfeld and colleagues** found a neutral (palms facing each other) grip activated the rear delts better than a pronated (palms down) grip. This was surprising because biomechanically, external rotation of the shoulder should put the lateral delt in a better position to contract through horizontal abduction. Why did the EMG find the opposite of what biomechanics predicts?
