# Best Time of Day to Train, and Cardio Before or After Weights

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "What is the BEST Time of Day to Train? | Should You Do Cardio or Weights First?" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zOMR73FC4Qw)) · 2017-09-02 · 14 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- You can build muscle training at any time of day and in either cardio/weights order, provided training, protein and calories are in place. These are optimisation details, not prerequisites.
- **Consistency beats timing.** Train when your schedule reliably allows it.
- **Order effect:** whatever quality you want to improve, do it first while fresh. Weights first for strength and size; cardio first only if endurance is the priority.
- Evening/afternoon training showed **more hypertrophy than morning training over 24 weeks** (no difference at 12 weeks), with two earlier papers pointing the same direction.
- If cardio and lifting must share a session, put cardio **after** the weights and get a meal or at least a carb source in between.

## The study [04:08]

Reviewed in that month's issue of MASS (Greg Nuckols / Eric Helms' research review). Two papers drew from one large dataset.

- **72 subjects**, late 20s to 30s, recreationally active — had lifted before but had not been on a dedicated program in the past year.
- Four groups in a 2x2: morning vs evening training, crossed with cardio-first vs weights-first.
- Chronotype was measured; nobody was an extreme morning or evening type, and nobody was on night shifts or sleep medication.
- Morning group trained between **6:00 and 10:30 am**; evening group between **4:30 and 8:00 pm**.
- **24 weeks total**, split into two 12-week halves (short-term and longer-term). Each half ran three back-to-back blocks:
  1. **Strength endurance** — light weights, high reps, minimal rest
  2. **Hypertrophy** — moderate loads, ~10-15 reps per set
  3. **Strength** — heavy weights, low reps
- Training was only **2x per week**: one session with low-intensity steady-state cardio, one with HIIT.
- Outcomes: quad cross-sectional area by ultrasound; leg press 1RM and maximal isometric knee extension force for strength. Main lifts measured were leg press, leg extension and leg curl, plus upper body work (pulldowns, presses).

## Findings

**Order effect [07:11].** Doing cardio first improved endurance measures; doing weights first improved strength and size measures ([Küüsmaa-Schildt 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/28704882)). Straightforward specificity — train the priority quality when you're fresh.

Nippard notes a common claim (raised by Greg in the MASS piece) that cardio should go first because cardio is catabolic and would otherwise blunt the post-workout anabolic signal. He is unaware of data supporting this, and this study found the opposite. Go with the data.

**Time of day [10:44].** No difference between morning and evening in the first 12 weeks. In the **second 12 weeks, the evening group gained significantly more muscle** ([Küüsmaa 2016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/27863207)). Two earlier papers cited in the review agree that evening training tends to produce more hypertrophy. The authors didn't speculate on mechanism; Nippard's hypothesis is that it's performance-driven — better hydration, more fed, more glycogen, more awake.

He dismisses the "train in the morning for growth hormone" argument: manipulating hormones within the natural physiological range is a fool's errand (as covered in his testosterone and steroids videos). Structure training around when you actually perform best.

## Cardio nuances [08:12]

- **Cardio as a warm-up is different from cardio as a fat-loss tool.** Some low-intensity cardio before lifting is good for reducing injury risk and improving performance. Nippard does a **minimum of 10 minutes**, and finds even **15-25 minutes** of low-intensity work doesn't measurably hurt his lifting. Keep it low intensity and don't let the duration run away.
- **Cardio after lifting is not a fat-loss advantage.** You may oxidise more fat during that session, but that's an acute, narrow view. What determines fat loss is **24-hour-or-longer energy balance**, not intra-session substrate use. Do cardio after weights for performance reasons, not fat-burning ones.
- **Separate them if you can.** Ideally put at least one meal, or at minimum a carb source, between the lifting session and the cardio session to limit performance interference in both.

## Practical application

- Lift first, cardio second, when they must be in the same session.
- Insert a meal or carb source between the two if separating them by hours isn't possible.
- Only skip the "weights first" rule if endurance is your actual goal, or if the cardio is a genuine 10-25 minute low-intensity warm-up.
- If your schedule is flexible, prefer **afternoon or evening** training for hypertrophy.
- Override all of the above in favour of whatever time you'll actually show up to consistently. Long work or school days leave many people performing better in the morning, and strong morning chronotypes should ignore the evening finding entirely.

## Caveats & unknowns
- Subjects trained only twice a week and were recreationally active, not well-trained lifters.
- No extreme chronotypes were included, so the finding doesn't generalise to strong morning or night types.
- The morning/evening difference only emerged after 12 weeks, and the mechanism is unconfirmed speculation.
- The effect is described as "slightly better" — small relative to consistency and program quality.
