# Growing Horseshoe Triceps by Varying Shoulder Position: Pressdowns and Kickbacks

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "How To Grow \"Horseshoe\" Triceps By Changing Arm Positions (Technique Explained)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=94DXwlcX8Po)) · 2019-08-20 · 8 min
**Topic:** Technique

## Bottom line
- The long head crosses the shoulder as well as the elbow, so changing shoulder angle changes how much it contributes. Train flexed (skull crusher), extended (pressdown) and hyperextended (kickback) positions rather than picking one.
- EMG evidence on which head dominates in which position is mixed, so variety beats trying to "target" a head precisely.
- EZ/straight-bar pressdown: 6-12 reps. Rope and single-arm pressdowns: 10-20 reps. Kickbacks: 10-20 reps.
- The kickback only earns its place if you actively drive the shoulder behind the torso; otherwise it is a worse pressdown.

## Why shoulder position matters
The long head is biarticular — it crosses the elbow and the shoulder — so shoulder angle alters its length and its contribution during elbow extension. The long head sits on the inner arm and makes the arm look thicker from behind; a developed lateral head gives the horseshoe look from the side.

Three positions:
- **Flexed shoulder** (skull crusher, overhead work) — long head under heavy stretch.
- **~90 degrees of shoulder flexion** (bench press) — per the Strength & Conditioning Research database, possibly the optimal angle for long-head activation, which is one reason bench press is a staple for overall triceps size.
- **Extended / hyperextended shoulder** (pressdown, kickback) — biomechanically should favour the lateral head, though EMG results are inconsistent.

## Triceps pressdown [01:32]
**Attachment.** Straight bar or EZ bar, whichever your wrists prefer. Preferred over the rope because the rope is less stable, so most people can't load it as heavily — less total tension. The rope's advantage is that it cues a harder squeeze at the bottom, but you can get that from the bar by controlling the rep and squeezing at lockout. Single-arm pressdowns (gripping the cable head itself) let you address side-to-side asymmetry and allow slight shoulder extension at the bottom for a very strong peak contraction.

**Setup and execution.**
- Set the cable so the bar hangs around eye level. Too low limits range of motion at the top; too high wastes energy getting into the start position.
- Overhand grip, roughly shoulder width. Underhand/reverse grip is a mistake — the triceps do not supinate, so it only cuts the load you can use with no upside.
- Pin the elbows against your sides so the pecs don't take over. Pull the bar into the start position with the forearm touching the biceps (elbow fully flexed).
- Step back from the machine and hinge at the hips so the hips don't block full lockout.
- Squeeze the triceps down; let the elbows travel slightly back on the way down (the long head extends the shoulder, so this recruits it more). Only a few inches, and only if the cue helps. Don't let it turn into a lat pulldown.
- Squeeze at the bottom, then reverse under control, elbows drifting slightly forward.

**Common errors.** Turning it into a whole-upper-body press — getting over the bar and using pecs and front delts to bench the weight down. It moves more weight but shifts work off the triceps. Also standing bolt upright, which limits tension at the top and range of motion at the bottom.

## Triceps kickback [05:06]
Test it yourself: bring your arm from in front of you to behind you and feel the long head harden as the shoulder hyperextends. The kickback never puts the long head under much stretch, but it produces the strongest possible contraction at full elbow extension — done properly it should almost be painful.

**Cable vs dumbbell.** Dumbbells work, but the circular resistance path means maximum tension at the top decaying to zero at the bottom. Cables keep tension more consistent throughout. Use a D-handle, one end of a rope, or grip the cable head directly for a more stable feel.

**Execution.** Pull the handle into position at your side with the elbow at about 90 degrees. Then simultaneously extend the elbow and drive the elbow back, forcing the shoulder into hyperextension so the arm finishes behind the torso. This makes the long head contract actively at both joints at once. Reverse under control, elbow bending as the shoulder comes forward. A more upright standing version works too if it's more comfortable, though the tension curve is slightly worse — just keep the shoulder hyperextended.

**Common error.** Not hyperextending the shoulder, which reduces the movement to an ordinary pressdown and defeats the purpose.

## Practical application
- Rotate all three shoulder positions across the week: skull crushers/overhead extensions (flexed), bench press (90 degrees), pressdowns (extended), kickbacks (hyperextended).
- EZ/straight-bar pressdown: 6-12 reps with moderate weight.
- Rope pressdown, single-arm pressdown, kickback: 10-20 reps, mind-muscle focused.
- Kickbacks are concentric-dominant with little stretch, so DOMS and recovery cost are low — a good place for drop sets and myo-reps.

## Caveats & unknowns
EMG data disagree on whether flexed positions really bias the long head and extended positions the lateral head, so the "use variety" recommendation is a hedge against that uncertainty rather than a proven targeting protocol. The claim that ~90 degrees of shoulder flexion is optimal for long-head activation comes from the Strength & Conditioning Research database summary, not a named individual study.
