# Seven Things That Grew Jeff Nippard's Shoulders

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "7 Things That Helped My Shoulders Grow" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MT9ZeE5JJsI)) · 2025-12-01 · 14 min
**Topic:** Exercise Selection

## Bottom line
- Raise shoulder frequency to 3-4 days per week rather than piling sets onto one or two days — effective volume per session caps out around 6-8 sets.
- Build a genuinely strong vertical press (5-6 reps, add weight weekly, hold that for a year); side delts are more involved than people assume.
- Getting lean does more for the 3D look than any exercise choice.
- Most people train delts almost entirely at short muscle lengths; deepen presses and use cross-body cable raises to add lengthened work.
- Use linear progression on the press and double progression on isolation work, then rotate the exercise when it stalls.

## 1. Take the traps out
If traps dominate, delts lose tension. Some coaches argue trap crossover is fine since most guys want bigger traps anyway, and that going to failure grows delts regardless — Nippard grants some truth to this but says for some trainees the delts go nearly silent, and many female clients specifically want to avoid trap growth.

The cue he's found most effective [01:02]: imagine holding a long shovel and scooping sand out to the side. It forces shoulders down and sweeps the weight out instead of shrugging it up. He tested it informally on random gym-goers against a "just get the weight up" cue; every person reported feeling more side delt with the shovel cue, and several said it felt harder. He notes this is not a scientific survey. Mechanically, initiating this way keeps traps out at the start; they necessarily come in later as the scapula rotates.

## 2. Train shoulders more often
His audience poll: most train shoulders twice a week, 24% once, only 11% three times, and 4% more than three. He calls that a mistake.

Reasoning: effective volume per muscle per session probably caps around 6-8 sets — beyond that, extra sets do little. A meta-analysis from 2025 suggests the cap might be as high as 11 sets per muscle per session, but returns are already sharply diminishing past about six. So the way to add effective volume is more sessions, not fatter sessions — spread it across 3-4 days.

Recovery: anecdotal but consistent in his coaching — shoulders recover fast, almost never get sore, and strength rebounds quickly. After 3-6 hard sets you should be ready again within 24-48 hours, especially with isolation work like lateral raises and rear delt flies. Earlier in his career he did 3 sets of dumbbell lateral raises after every workout, five days a week, and thinks it mattered.

## 3. Get a brutally strong shoulder press
Any overloadable vertical press — machine, dumbbell, or barbell. Load 5-6 reps, add a little weight weekly, and stick with it for a full year.

Mechanism: progressively lifting more increases mechanical tension, the main driver of hypertrophy. Strength doesn't directly cause size, but they're linked — if you're stronger at the same exercise, reps and form, you've almost certainly added contractile tissue.

On the claim that vertical presses only hit front delts, which horizontal presses already cover: front delts do get plenty from horizontal pressing, but side delts are more active on vertical presses than people realise. His own EMG test on the machine shoulder press showed *more* side delt than front delt activation. It also matters because lateral raises can only be loaded so far — a strength-focused press keeps progression available.

## 4. Bring your waist down
The tip he thinks most people will skip and the one with the biggest visual impact. He shows himself at 20% body fat versus 9%: shoulders look broader and rounder because the waist is smaller and there's less fat over the deltoid — deeper cuts, more separation between heads, which creates the 3D illusion. His claim: ignore every other tip, just get lean while training shoulders hard, and they'll look far more 3D.

## 5. Train through a variety of muscle lengths
A muscle is shortened in the contracted half of the ROM and lengthened in the stretched half. Most people train delts almost entirely short: shoulder presses that never go deep, lateral raises where peak tension arrives only when the delt is already short, and rear delt work with very truncated range.

Evidence [07:36]: three studies showed between 130% and 190% more growth from long-muscle-length training in quads, biceps and calves respectively. For delts specifically there's only one eight-week study, comparing dumbbell lateral raises (tension when short) to cable lateral raises (tension when long). The authors hypothesised cables would win on lengthened tension — but there was no difference; both grew side delts about the same.

His read: include both short- and long-length work, but since most people over-do short, correct toward length.
- **Presses:** don't stop at parallel; sink the arms as low as they comfortably go.
- **Lateral raises:** use a cable and sweep it across the body rather than stopping at your side. He notes the eight-week study stopped at the side, which may partly explain the null result — the adduction range is a main advantage of cables and can't be replicated with standing dumbbells.
- **Cross-body cable Y raises:** his favourite, close to the fullest lengthened-to-shortened range available for side delts. No cables? Lie back on a 20° incline bench and do cross-body Y raises with a dumbbell.
- **Rear delts:** cross the arms over the body and sweep back until you feel the squeeze — cables, dumbbells or machine.

## 6. Match the progression scheme to the exercise
Isolation work is hard to overload: starting at 15s and adding 5 lb weekly puts you at 50 lb lateral raises by week eight, which isn't realistic.

- **Heavy shoulder press:** linear progression. Add 5-10 lb weekly at a fixed 5-6 reps. Deload every 4-8 weeks — drop the weight, ease off.
- **Lateral raises and rear delt flies:** double progression. Pick a range, say 10-12. Week one, a weight you can hit for 10 to or near failure. Week two, same weight for 11. Week three, 12. Then add weight and restart at 10. With three sets you don't need to add a rep to all three — one rep on one set keeps progression moving, especially when advanced.
- **When you can't add even one rep without form breaking:** rotate the exercise and restart the progression. Dumbbells → cables → machine, each with its own double progression.

## 7. Target all heads of the delt
Anatomy research dissected 60 cadavers and found that instead of three heads (anterior, lateral, posterior) the deltoid has seven intramuscular segments, each with its own tendon: the posterior head branches into three tendons, the middle head has one, and the front head branches into three. Each segment has a slightly different function. (Researchers not named in the video.)

Practical response: add movements for the planes between the usual front, side and rear raises.
- **A2 and A3 segments:** incline dumbbell Y raises — lifting up and out in a Y puts the resistance line through those in-between fibres.
- **P1 and P2 segments:** 45° cable rear delt pull. Stretch the arm across the body at about 45°, sweep down and back, like drawing an arrow from a quiver.

The shoulder is a ball-and-socket joint with freedom in all three dimensions, so train it in all three.

## Practical application
Restructure rather than add: same weekly set total spread across 3-4 days. Run one press on linear progression at 5-6 reps for a year. Run laterals and rear delt work on double progression with a planned exercise rotation. Deepen presses, sweep cables across the body, add Y raises and the 45° rear delt pull for the in-between planes. Run a cut at some point — leanness is what makes it visible.

## Caveats & unknowns
- The shovel-cue test was informal gym-floor feedback, not a study; he says so explicitly.
- Only one eight-week study has looked at muscle length for delts and it found no difference between cables and dumbbells; his explanation for the null (restricted cable ROM) is a hypothesis.
- The 130-190% long-length growth figures come from quads, biceps and calves, not delts.
- Fast shoulder recovery is his coaching anecdote, not data.
- The 6-8 set effective-volume cap is an estimate; the 2025 meta-analysis puts it as high as 11.
- Cadaver segment anatomy establishes structure, not that training the in-between planes produces more growth.
