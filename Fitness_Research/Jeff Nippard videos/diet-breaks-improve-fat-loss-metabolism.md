# Do Diet Breaks Improve Fat Loss and Protect Metabolism?

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "Do Diet Breaks Improve Fat Loss & Metabolism? (New Scientific Research)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TaaA2fsuXZU)) · 2017-12-04 · 8 min
**Topic:** Nutrition

## Bottom line
- In a 2017 study by **Byrne and colleagues**, the diet-break group lost **50% more fat** than continuous dieters over a matched total caloric deficit — with **no extra muscle loss**.
- Resting energy expenditure dropped **only half as much** in the diet-break group. Metabolism fell in both initially but rebounded with the breaks.
- At **6-month follow-up the gap widened to 80–90% better fat loss**, because continuous dieters regained more weight.
- Recommended protocol: intersperse **1–2 week diet breaks at maintenance calories** throughout a diet, e.g. 2 weeks deficit / 2 weeks maintenance.
- The catch: diet breaks **substantially lengthen the diet** (30 weeks vs 16 in the study), and going from a 33% deficit to maintenance may not feel like much of a break for smaller people.

## Background
The diet break was popularised by **Lyle McDonald** in *The Guide to Flexible Dieting*, drawing on a **2003 paper by Wing and Jeffery** ([Wing 2003](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/12582226)). That study compared 20 weeks of continuous dieting, 20 weeks with a single 6-week break in the middle, and 20 weeks with three evenly spaced 2-week breaks. **Weight loss was about the same across all three.**

That surprised the researchers — they had introduced the breaks expecting to *induce* weight regain. Their speculation was that breaks help with long-term sustainability and long-term weight loss. Then nothing much was published on the question until 2017.

## The Byrne study [01:02]
36 obese men, split into two groups, with **total net caloric deficit matched** across groups ([Byrne 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/28925405)):

- **Group 1 (continuous):** 16 weeks straight at a **33% deficit** — eating 67% of maintenance. Worked example: 3,000 kcal maintenance → 2,000 kcal per day for 16 weeks.
- **Group 2 (diet breaks):** alternating **2 weeks at the same 33% deficit / 2 weeks at maintenance**, repeated for **30 total weeks**. Same example: 2,000 kcal for two weeks, 3,000 kcal for two weeks, repeat.

The dieting period was longer in group 2 precisely so total deficit could be equated despite the maintenance blocks.

**Results:**
- Diet-break group lost **50% more fat**.
- **No difference in muscle loss** between groups.
- Resting energy expenditure dropped **only about half as much** in the diet-break group. Metabolism slowed in both early on, but rebounded in the break group so that by week 16 the diet-break group had significantly faster metabolism.
- **6-month follow-up:** the disparity had widened — diet-break group now showed **80–90% better fat loss**, driven by the continuous group regaining more weight after the diet ended.

## The downsides Jeff raises [04:06]
- **Time cost.** The diet-break subjects dieted for 30 weeks — roughly seven months. Anyone with a deadline or a specific event may not accept that.
- **Shorter aggressive diets still work.** In Jeff's coaching experience, shorter more aggressive approaches without diet breaks produce good results faster. The trade-off: greater risk of post-diet weight regain (so you must be more controlled afterwards) and you may not get the metabolic protection.
- **The "break" may not feel like one.** Moving from a 33% deficit to maintenance is not a licence to eat freely — you're still tracking, still not eating as much as you want, just at maintenance while extending the total diet. For people with lower maintenance calories, especially many women, this might be a bump of only **400–500 extra calories per day**, which may not feel worth the added months.

## Practical application
- If you are not dieting to a hard deadline, intersperse **1–2 week diet breaks at maintenance calories** through a fat loss phase. The study's tested pattern was **2 weeks deficit / 2 weeks maintenance**, repeating.
- Set the break at **true maintenance** — not a free-for-all. You keep tracking during it.
- Budget for the diet taking roughly **twice as long** in total calendar time for the same net deficit.
- If you have an event or just want the weight off fast, a shorter aggressive diet without breaks is legitimate — but plan a controlled post-diet phase, since regain risk is higher.
- Jeff is notably less impressed by the data on **1–2 day acute refeeds** than on full 1–2 week diet breaks, though he thinks refeeds may still have merit.

## Caveats & unknowns
- Subjects were **36 obese men**. Whether the effect holds in **lean, resistance-trained people or in women** is untested. Jeff hypothesises it would, and reports good results using diet breaks with female clients, but explicitly says the study is needed.
- Only two studies exist on this question (2003 and 2017); the evidence base is thin.
- The mechanism for the fat loss difference despite matched total deficit is not fully explained beyond the metabolic-rate sparing.
- The video is based on an article by **Eric Helms** in MASS.
