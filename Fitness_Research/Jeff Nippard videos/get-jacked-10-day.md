# Building Muscle on $10 a Day vs $10,000 a Day

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "How To Get Jacked For $10/Day (Healthy Meals On A Budget)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NS_GeXoboo4)) · 2025-02-15 · 17 min
**Topic:** Nutrition

## Bottom line
- A full day of training, four high-protein meals and supplements came to $8.92. The $10,000 day produced no gains advantage — only convenience, taste and enjoyment.
- Cheapest protein sources, in order: lentils, milk, chicken breast (protein powder ranks 6th). Cheapest carbs: white rice, brown rice, oats; bananas for fruit. Cheapest fats: sunflower seeds and peanut butter.
- Creatine is both one of the most effective and one of the cheapest supplements — 5 g cost 15 cents bought in bulk.
- Plain coffee (~25 c) works as well as a multi-ingredient pre-workout (~50 c–87 c per scoop); a new study found caffeine alone matched a full pre-workout formula ([Kruszewski 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35565718/)). Caffeine tablets are ~5 c.
- A productive hypertrophy session takes 45 minutes: 1–2 exercises per muscle, 1–2 sets each, taken to failure.

## The $10 day, meal by meal
- **Breakfast — peanut butter protein smoothie, $1.50, 48 g protein.** Milk, whey, banana, oats, peanut butter. Milk and whey are among the cheapest high-quality protein sources.
- **Supplements, 20 c.** 5 g creatine monohydrate (15 c) plus a multivitamin (5 c). His view on the multi: probably unnecessary on a generally healthy diet, but cheap insurance.
- **Pre-workout meal — chicken wrap, $2.10.** Chicken breast, brown rice, spinach, cheese, salsa, sour cream, one large whole-wheat tortilla. Bulk-prep the chicken; the wrap assembles in minutes and travels.
- **Pre-workout stimulant — coffee, ~25 c**, taken about 30 minutes before training.
- **Gym, 33 c/day.** $10/month gyms exist: Planet Fitness, Crunch, 24 Hour Fitness in the US; Fit For Less and Planet Fitness in Canada. Home alternative: $40 of bands plus a $30 pull-up bar works out to about 19 c/day over a first year of lifting. He prefers free weights and machines to bands but says bands get the job done, especially for beginners.
- **Post-workout meal — turkey and veg stir fry, $2.83, 46 g protein.** Lean ground turkey, frozen mixed vegetables, soy sauce, brown rice, an egg. No rush on post-workout timing — a whole-food meal about an hour after training is fine. Vegetables look expensive on a per-gram-of-carb basis because you need so much volume; buy them canned or frozen. Frozen vegetables are sometimes more nutritious than fresh that has been in transport or on a shelf.
- **Evening meal — high-protein peanut butter dip, $1.60.** Greek yogurt, peanut butter and honey, with cinnamon apple slices and sunflower seeds on the side.

**Total: $8.92.**

## The $10,000 day and what it bought
- **Sensory deprivation float tank** — first time; genuinely relaxing after ~15 minutes, cheaper than expected, but an n-of-1 impression only.
- **Michelin-trained personal chef, $500/hr × 5 hr = $2,500.** Breakfast was oyakodon (chicken, egg, rice) with 46 g protein — ingredients cost only $6.46, so the dish itself is affordable if you cook it. The pre-workout meal was a seafood risotto with lobster, scallops, shrimp, high-protein rice and truffled caviar; ingredients alone $186.24 (lobster tail was the most expensive protein on his list).
- **Joe Bennett (Hypertrophy Coach), $700/hr × 3 hr = $2,100.** Five-exercise back workout with cues Jeff hadn't used:
  - *Lat-focused cable row:* drive elbows down **and** keep them tucked in — this fixed his inability to feel the lats in the shortened position.
  - *Pull-ups:* slow down toward the bottom stretch and keep elbows tucked rather than flared. Much harder.
  - *Deadlift variant:* an RDL made back-focused with slightly more knee bend and range extended until the back is parallel to the floor — a conventional/RDL hybrid that hammers the spinal erectors.
  - *Chest-supported row:* keep the chest **up** through the whole rep. Rounding the upper back over the pad only stretches the erectors and takes slack out of the mid traps; staying upright actually produced a better mid-back stretch and connection.
  - *Set extension on the last set:* deliberately extend (arch) at the bottom, round over at the top, alternating — smashes the erectors.
  - *Incline cable lat pullover:* let the shoulder blades lift at the top, then pull down and sweep through.
- **DoorDash post-workout chicken pita, $34.40** ($15.23 food, $4.24 delivery, $15 tip). Target was ~500–600 kcal and 35–40 g protein. Benefit over the $2.83 meal: convenience only, no extra gains.
- **Luxury spa recovery package, $1,000** — deep tissue massage on back and legs plus facial massage. Relaxing, supplementary, not mandatory.
- **Meal-prep service salmon dish, $10.40** — salmon, rice, broccoli, mushrooms, onions. Convenient but pricier than buying in bulk and prepping yourself.

He came in ~$4,000 under budget and gave the remainder to a subscriber rather than buy recovery gadgets he considers scientifically unjustified.

## Verdict
- **Food:** identical macros produce identical gains. The chef's real benefit was that macro-friendly food tasted better, which makes dieting feel easier — an adherence benefit, not a physiological one.
- **Coaching:** the one place he saw real value. A recent study found people training with a personal trainer gained significantly more muscle than those without. He expects the gap to shrink over time for anyone who can push themselves hard alone, and calls a trainer a luxury rather than a necessity given how much good free information exists.
- **Recovery:** the float tank, massage and spa help you relax and may aid recovery, but they are supplementary. Research shows you can get recovery benefits from a walk outside, which is free, and sleep is free and probably the single best recovery tool there is.

## Practical application
- Build meals around milk, whey, lentils, chicken breast, eggs and lean ground turkey; rice, oats and bananas for carbs; peanut butter and sunflower seeds for fat. Buy vegetables frozen or canned.
- Take 5 g creatine daily, bought in bulk.
- Use coffee or caffeine tablets instead of branded pre-workout.
- Use a $10/month gym, or bands plus a pull-up bar at home for a first year.
- When time-limited: 1–2 exercises per muscle, 1–2 sets each, all the way to failure, done in 45 minutes.
- Post-workout, eat a normal whole-food meal within roughly an hour. No urgency.
- Prioritise sleep and walking over paid recovery services.

## Caveats & unknowns
The float tank verdict is a personal one-off impression, not evidence. The personal-trainer study shows more growth with a trainer but doesn't establish how long that advantage persists for a self-motivated lifter. Some of the cheapest protein sources on his list are high enough in calories that they're a poor fit when cutting — he flags the lower-calorie options separately for that case.
