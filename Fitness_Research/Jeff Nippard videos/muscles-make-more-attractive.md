# Do Muscles Make You More Attractive? Survey Data and the Research

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "Do Muscles Really Make You More Attractive?" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWC8_vp-bWI)) · 2024-08-04 · 15 min
**Topic:** Myths & Evidence

## Bottom line
- More muscle and more strength both correlate with more romantic partners, and with a roughly **20% higher likelihood of being in a relationship**. Bench press was the single strongest correlate of dating outcomes in the survey.
- Women in the survey overwhelmingly preferred **level 3 of 5** muscularity (41%). Only 10% chose level 5. Men, meanwhile, mostly want level 4 (with 33% wanting level 5) — a large mismatch between what men chase and what women rate as most attractive.
- Body fat had **zero** relationship to partner count (flat line from shredded to overweight), but the highest body fat groups contained the most people rating their romantic satisfaction at the worst possible level.
- The meta-analytic ceiling: across 170,000+ subjects, six stereotypically masculine traits combined explained only **~5% of the variance** in mating outcomes. Strength and muscularity was the strongest of the six — but 95% is other stuff.
- Kindness and intelligence outrank physical attractiveness for women of all orientations. The "nice guys finish last" myth is about neediness and clinginess, not niceness.

## Survey design
Jeff surveyed ~50,000 people from his audience. Respondents self-selected their body type on a grid (muscularity down one axis, body fat across the other), picked the physique they found most attractive, and reported lifetime intimate partner counts, relationship status, romantic satisfaction (1–7), and lifts. He also interviewed people on the street.

## Strength and partners
[01:32] Bench press distribution among the men surveyed: most could bench about two plates per side for a 1RM (intermediate); only **5%** could bench three plates; a tiny fraction had ever hit four. (Jeff's own best is 380 lb.)

Partner count rose with bench press across the board. Men benching over four plates averaged **16 lifetime partners** — by far the highest. Jeff found this genuinely surprising; he'd expected the heaviest benchers to be too gym-focused. His explanations:
- Other research shows heterosexual women perceive stronger men as more attractive.
- The spike at four plates is probably partly steroids, which substantially increase libido (with the accompanying side effects).
- Age is a partial confound but likely insufficient — his audience skews heavily 18–34, with most 25–34.

The same trend held for squat and deadlift, so **the specific lift doesn't matter; being strong is what counts**. Women showed the same trend as men — stronger women had more partners, not fewer. And the scatter is wide: plenty of weak benchers with many partners and strong benchers with few.

## Body fat
[04:34] Jeff expected a leanness sweet spot around level 3 (roughly 10–15% body fat) with the very shredded doing slightly worse. Instead: **a completely flat line** — body fat had no relationship to lifetime partner count from six-pack to overweight.

Romantic *satisfaction* was different. Isolating everyone who rated satisfaction a 1 (worst possible), the highest body fat groups contained the most of them. Same pattern for women.

## Muscularity
[06:36] Partner count rose with muscularity; the most jacked men averaged **11 partners**. Other research links muscularity to mating and reproductive success. Jeff again attributes the top-end spike partly to steroids, since reaching level 8 on his body-fat/muscle chart generally requires them. Women showed the same trend — more muscle, more partners.

More usefully: people with less muscle were less likely to be in a relationship, and people with more muscle more likely — a difference of about **20%**. Levels 5 and 6 on that scale already help, and are well within natural range.

He is explicit that these are correlations. Muscle may not be causal — more muscular people may simply be more confident and more likely to approach people.

## What women actually said they preferred
[08:09] On a 1–5 muscularity scale (4 being roughly the natural ceiling):
- **Level 3: 41%** of women — the most attractive.
- **Level 5: 10%.**
- More women preferred level 2 than level 5.

And this is from his own audience — women presumably already gym-inclined and biased toward muscular. Level 2 is attainable with a reasonably healthy diet and one or two full-body workouts per week. Level 5 requires far more gym time and, realistically, anabolics with their side effects.

Men's stated goals: most wanted level 4, with 33% wanting level 5. So a third of men chase a physique only 10% of women rate as most attractive.

## The broader research
[11:14] The largest study Jeff found was a meta-analysis of **over 170,000 subjects** testing which of six stereotypically masculine traits best predicts mating and reproductive success: height, voice pitch, facial masculinity, testosterone levels, 2D:4D finger ratio, and strength/muscularity ([Lidborg 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35179485/)).

Winner: **strength and muscularity** — ahead of height and voice pitch. But all six combined accounted for only about **5% of the variance** in mating outcomes.

## Kindness beats the alternative
[12:44] Being kind is consistently rated among the most attractive qualities across genders and sexual orientations. A classic study compared "jerks" to "nice guys": only **7% of women picked the jerk**, and the kinder the man was portrayed, the more desirable he was. Even when researchers made the jerk more handsome, he still lost **85% of the time**.

Another study surveyed heterosexual, bisexual and homosexual men and women: intelligence and kindness ranked above physical attractiveness in every group except heterosexual men, who placed physical attractiveness between kindness and intelligence. Being mean or a bully ranked among the least attractive traits.

Jeff's read on the "nice guys finish last" myth: what women picture when they imagine a "nice guy" is someone needy, clingy and desperate. It's the neediness that's unattractive, not the kindness.

## Practical application
- Train for strength across the board — squat, bench and deadlift all track equally with outcomes. The point is being strong, not the specific lift.
- Aim for a muscularity level around 3 out of 5 if attraction is a driver; it's achievable naturally and rated most attractive.
- If you're chasing level 5, be honest that you're doing it for yourself, not for dating outcomes, and weigh the health cost of what it takes to get there.
- Don't obsess over body fat for dating outcomes — but the highest body fat groups had the most severe romantic dissatisfaction, so health-range leanness is worth having.
- The controllable physical stuff that helps most: grooming, smelling good, hygiene, dental care, a hairstyle that suits your face, clothes that fit your body shape.
- Be kind, have goals and other things going on in your life, and don't take yourself too seriously.

## Caveats & unknowns
Everything from the survey is correlational and self-reported, from a self-selected fitness audience — not a representative population. Age is an unresolved confound in the strength-partner relationship, and steroid use is Jeff's speculation for the top-end spikes rather than measured data. Lifetime partner count is a poor proxy for romantic happiness, which is why he adds the satisfaction and relationship-status data. Correlation cannot separate muscle from the confidence that often accompanies it.
