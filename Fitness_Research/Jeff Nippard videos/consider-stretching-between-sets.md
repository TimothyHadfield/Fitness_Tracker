# Stretching Between Sets: Does Inter-Set Stretching Boost Hypertrophy?

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "Why You Should Consider Stretching Between Sets (Science Explained)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuBhECs587w)) · 2019-03-16 · 6 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- Nippard publicly reverses his prior position. He had called inter-set stretching bro science; a 2019 Brazilian trial changed his mind.
- That trial: 30-second stretches of the trained muscle between every set produced roughly 50% more total muscle growth over 8 weeks — though only the vastus lateralis result reached statistical significance on its own.
- Practical protocol: 30-second holds, at or below 30 seconds, stretched to mild discomfort but never to pain, during rest periods you're taking anyway.
- Don't overdo it: stretching to actual pain has been shown to *decrease* hypertrophy, and holds beyond 60 seconds reduce force and power output.
- Nippard's own conservative implementation: stretch the agonist between the first three or four heavy sets of a workout only, not on subsequent isolation work.

## The prior evidence (weak) [00:31]
- **The unpublished calf study.** A grainy PowerPoint slide circulated online claiming a study would show inter-set stretching boosts hypertrophy. The study was never published, though the abstract is still findable and reports significantly more calf growth when subjects stretched their calves 30 seconds between sets of calf raises. Nippard's view: interesting, but don't put much stock in an unpublished study.
- **The 1993 quail study.** Showed over a 300% increase in lat muscle mass in 28 days from weighted stretching alone. Bodybuilding magazines ran with it. The subjects were the right wings of 26 adult quail — birds — and it has never been replicated in humans.
- Despite the thin evidence base, high-profile bodybuilding coaches built training philosophies around inter-set stretching. Nippard names one who has coached Phil Heath and Jay Cutler; the auto-captions render the name as "honey Rambo," which is garbled and should not be relied on as spelled.

## The January 2019 study
Conducted out of São Paulo, Brazil (transcript renders it "South Paulo"). Design:
- 29 subjects, split into two groups.
- Identical full-body program: bench press, triceps extensions, seated rows, biceps curls, leg extensions and leg curls.
- **4 sets of 8-12 reps to failure, 2 days per week, 8 weeks.**
- Control group: no stretching between sets.
- Experimental group: 30-second static stretches of the muscle being trained between every set — e.g. 30-second pec stretches between all four bench press sets. Stretches held just below the point of pain or discomfort.

**Results:** muscle thickness measured at four sites. Summing the four measurements, the stretching group showed about **50% more muscle growth** overall. However, the effect was statistically significant only for the vastus lateralis (outer quad). Biceps, triceps and rectus femoris all favoured the stretching group but did not reach significance individually — only the pooled result did.

Nippard's interpretation: it may work only for the quads, or work best there, or the arm results may simply have needed more subjects or more time. Either way he considers it a well-designed study and impressive for eight weeks — enough to change his recommendation, while noting it's still one study.

## Dosing rules
Two disclaimers drawn from the MASS Research Review coverage:
1. **Intensity:** don't stretch to actual pain. A previous study showed painful stretching can *decrease* hypertrophy. Stay at or below mild discomfort.
2. **Duration:** don't hold too long. Stretches longer than 60 seconds decrease force and power output. Keep holds at or below 30 seconds to be safe.

## Mechanism
Unknown. Nippard speculates it could relate to blood flow and nutrient delivery, loosely analogous to blood flow restriction training — but calls that a stab in the dark. In the MASS article, Greg Nuckols (transcript garbles this as "Greg NACO") speculates it may simply be a matter of exposing the muscle to a greater amount of cumulative tension.

## Practical application
- If you're already resting 1-3 minutes between sets, use 30 seconds of it to stretch the muscle you're training.
- Stretch to mild discomfort only, 30 seconds maximum per hold.
- Nippard's own plan, erring on the side of caution: stretch the agonist between the first three or four heavy sets of the session only. On a push day, stretch pecs between bench press sets but not between pec deck sets; stretch lats for 30 seconds between pull-up sets but not on the subsequent rowing work.
- Rationale for that restraint: he suspects diminishing returns as stretching volume climbs.

## Caveats & unknowns
- One study, eight weeks, 29 subjects. Only one of four measured muscles reached individual statistical significance.
- No known mechanism.
- The supporting calf study was never published; the dramatic 1993 result was in quail, not humans.
- Whether there is a point of diminishing or negative returns from higher stretching volume is untested — Nippard's limited protocol is a precaution, not an evidence-based dose.
