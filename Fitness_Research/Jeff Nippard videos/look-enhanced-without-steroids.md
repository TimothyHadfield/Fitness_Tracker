# The Five Features That Make People Assume You're On Steroids — And How To Build Them Naturally

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "How To Look Enhanced Without Steroids" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2qDA86gP_zg)) · 2025-07-13 · 16 min
**Topic:** PEDs

## Bottom line
- Random strangers judging eight natural lifters consistently flagged the same five features: **side delts, biceps, upper pecs, traps, and leanness**.
- Getting lean is the single biggest lever fully in your control. Nippard gets more steroid accusations now at 155 lb and ~10% body fat than he did 30 lb heavier at ~20%.
- Side delts are the top priority — train them at least twice a week, up to 3–5 times a week if they're lagging.
- Bloodwork on all eight subjects came back natural: testosterone 415–689 ng/dL except one at 928, with normal LH and FSH across the board.
- Within the normal range, higher testosterone did not correlate with more muscle. Only supraphysiological levels drive large size gains.

## The five features

### 1. Side delts (the biggest tell)
"3D delts" is the accusation Nippard hears most. Wide shoulders are essential to the enhanced look, and the side delts are the key. He does lateral raises at least **twice a week**; early in his career he did them at the end of *every* workout. If side delts are lagging, train them more often — **3 to 5 times per week**.

### 2. Biceps
Nearly every interviewee pointed at the biceps of the four men in the "questionable" group. Big peaked biceps may be the easiest way to look enhanced to non-lifters. The subject with 18-inch arms was a competitive gymnast until 14 and now does a lot of **preacher curls with forced reps** — assisting the concentric with the free hand once he can't lift it, then taking the full load on the negative. Nippard credits his own measured (ultrasound) bicep growth over the past year mainly to **Bayesian cable curls**, which emphasise the lengthened position.

### 3. Upper pecs
Nippard coached one subject to the all-time Canadian bench press record for his weight class in drug-tested powerlifting, and rates the bench press highly for chest development despite the science-based community's preference for machines. For upper pecs specifically, **incline pressing is clearly better** — include an incline press or incline fly for complete pec development.

### 4. Traps
Big traps genuinely are a statistical indicator of PED use, because the **upper traps have a higher androgen receptor density** than most other muscles ([Kadi 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10664066/)) — more receptors for injected testosterone to bind to. To build them naturally:
- Shrugs with a **slightly wider grip**, thinking about shrugging **up and in** — upper trap fibres run far more horizontally than most people realise, so this matches fibre orientation.
- Include an overhead raise: a full-ROM lateral raise or an overhead cable lateral raise.
- **Direct neck training** still makes a difference here even though it has fallen out of fashion.

### 5. Being lean (the biggest controllable factor)
With any muscle base at all, leanness makes you look dramatically more jacked. Leanness also brings vascularity, which people wrongly associate with steroids — Phil Heath and Jay Cutler, two of the most gifted bodybuilders ever, weren't vascular. Vascularity is mostly genetics plus low body fat.

## Lighting, posing and the illusion
Two lighting setups were shot on the same group: under standard diffuse lighting everyone read as natural; switching to spotlighting made everyone read as enhanced in a one-second transition. Nippard also shot himself in his kitchen under normal light, no pose, on an iPhone, then ran downstairs, turned on a spotlight, hit a most-muscular and switched to a Sony A7S III — a dramatic visual difference in under a minute. Being placed in a lineup next to genuinely bigger men also strips away the illusion.

## The bloodwork
Nippard's reference framework for judging natural status by testosterone:
- Most healthy young men: **~200–850 ng/dL** ([Zhu 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36282060/))
- **Over 1,000** is slightly suspicious
- **Over 1,200** is very suspicious
- **Over 1,500** is basically impossible without steroid use
- Pro bodybuilders sometimes exceed 5,000, and above 10,000 has been reported, especially without drugs to suppress levels

Results for the eight subjects: **415 to 689 ng/dL**, except one subject at **928**. To check that outlier, Nippard measured his **luteinizing hormone (LH) and follicle stimulating hormone (FSH)** — both drop very low with steroid use, so high testosterone with suppressed LH/FSH would be damning. His LH and FSH sat right in the middle of the reference range, as did everyone else's. 928 is high but unremarkable for an active male in his late teens.

Key finding: **more testosterone did not correlate with more muscle within the natural range.** Research supports this — as long as you're in the normal healthy range, testosterone does far less for muscle growth than people assume ([Morton 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30356739/)). Only supraphysiological injected levels pack on serious size.

## The genetics argument
Two photographic reference points for extreme natural development: Melvin Wells in **1949** (first corroborated reports of testosterone use in lifting were 1952, so almost certainly natural, though Soviet lifters used it in the late 1940s), and an unnamed lifter photographed in **1912** — two decades before injectable testosterone was isolated — with bicep peaks resembling the modern subject who drew the most accusations.

Nippard's framing: muscle growth potential varies as widely as height. We don't accuse a seven-foot man of taking growth hormone, but people jump straight to steroids for muscle. Combine world-class genetics with extremely hard training and you reach a level some people simply won't believe.

## Practical application
- Prioritise side delts: lateral raises minimum 2x/week, 3–5x/week if lagging.
- Bias biceps work toward lengthened-position and intensity techniques: Bayesian cable curls, preacher curls with forced reps and controlled negatives.
- Include incline pressing or incline flies, not just flat and machine work.
- Shrug with a wider grip, up and in; add an overhead raise; consider direct neck training.
- Get lean — this outweighs everything else for the "enhanced" look, and getting there is a nutrition problem, not a training one.
- Recognise that most physique photos you compare yourself to involve spotlighting, posing and a real camera.

## Caveats & unknowns
- A single blood test can't rule out past use — someone could have cycled and come off.
- Nippard's own delt development he attributes substantially to genetics (his mother has notable shoulders at 60, lifetime natural), not just training.
- The androgen receptor density claim about traps explains why traps are a *statistical* signal, not proof in any individual case.
