# Nutrient Timing Around Training: Pre-, Intra- and Post-Workout Nutrition

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "What To Eat Before, During & After Training For Max Muscle Growth" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PM8kiHcAD7Q)) · 2021-10-21 · 13 min
**Topic:** Nutrition

## Bottom line
- The real "anabolic window" is a **4–5 hour bracket** spanning the workout — pre- and post-workout meals shouldn't be separated by more than that.
- **Pre-workout meal:** ~1 g/kg carbs (low-glycaemic source plus a serving of fruit), ~0.5 g/kg protein, 0–20 g fat, eaten **1–1.5 hours before training**.
- **Post-workout meal:** ~0.5 g/kg protein, plus 1–1.5 g/kg carbs when carbs are plentiful or ~0.5 g/kg when cutting.
- **Intra-workout carbs** are optional — but if you train fasted, are lean and in a deficit, train over an hour, or are elite: **0.5 g carbs per minute of training, starting 30 min in**.
- Fat is the timing lever: less fat the closer the meal sits to the session, more fat the further away.

## How big is the window really?
[00:00] Aragon and Schoenfeld's "Nutrient Timing Revisited" ([Aragon 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23360586/)) was widely read as killing the anabolic window entirely. Nippard argues the science crowd overcorrected: that same paper suggests pre- and post-exercise meals shouldn't be separated by more than **3–4 hours**, or **5–6 hours** after a large pre-workout meal. Split the difference and call the window **4–5 hours**.

So timing matters, but it matters more for some people:
- **Leaner lifters** — lower nutrient supply on board.
- **People on lower total calories** — higher risk of muscle loss.
- **Higher-volume bodybuilding-style training** — depletes more nutrients than lower-volume powerlifting work.

## The pre-workout meal
[02:03] Nippard calls it the most important meal of the day, because it determines how effective the training session is, and training drives growth. Two jobs: **fuel the session** (carbohydrate) and **create an anabolic environment during training** (protein).

**Targets:**
- Carbs: **1 g/kg body weight**, ideally a lower-glycaemic source **plus a serving of fruit** — different carbs use different transporters and therefore absorb at different rates, giving more sustainable energy across the session.
- Protein: **0.5 g/kg body weight**.
- Fat: **0–20 g**, scaled to timing. Fat slows digestion, so meals close to training get less fat and meals further out get more.

**Timing:** 1–1.5 hours before training is the sweet spot. Too close and undigested food makes you lethargic or bloated; too far and you risk low energy or feeling flat. If you're forced into the gym on short notice, eat a **lower-calorie meal of faster-digesting foods**. If you know training is more than 1.5 hours away, eat a **higher-calorie meal with slower-digesting foods**.

**Worked examples:**
- *80 kg (175 lb) male, training in 1–1.5 h* → ~80 g carbs, 40 g protein, 10–15 g fat. Protein oatmeal: 80 g oats, one scoop protein powder (a casein/whey blend), sliced banana, half a tablespoon of peanut butter, cinnamon (may help keep blood glucose stable into the session) and a pinch of sea salt if you sweat heavily. Vegan swap: pea/brown rice protein, drunk separately as a shake if the texture doesn't work in oats.
- *55 kg (120 lb) female, training in ~2 h* → ~27 g protein, 55 g carbs, 10–15 g fat, deliberately **higher in fat and fibre** to slow digestion so nutrients are still available at training time. 4 oz salmon, medium sweet potato, mixed green salad with light goat cheese, apple slices with cinnamon.
- *Training in 20–30 min, hasn't eaten in hours* → something light and fast with little fat or fibre: **whey shake plus a banana**.

## Training fasted
[06:05] About a third of Nippard's audience trains fasted. Some carbohydrate before training generally benefits performance, but from coaching experience some people genuinely perform better on an empty stomach — typically very early-morning trainees who have no appetite or feel nauseous eating that early. It isn't a deal-breaker: world-record powerlifter Russel Orhii trained fasted for years and still reached elite strength and size (he has since moved away from it). **If you skip the pre-workout meal, intra- and post-workout nutrition become much more urgent.**

## Intra-workout nutrition
[06:36] Not required for everyone, especially with a well-timed pre-workout meal. Worth doing if you:
- train fasted,
- are in a caloric deficit and already very lean,
- train longer than an hour, or
- are an elite trainee optimising details.

**Dose:** a liquid carb source (Gatorade, Kool-Aid, Tang) at **0.5 g carbohydrate per minute of training, beginning ~30 minutes into the session** — so 30 g of carbs across a 90-minute workout. When cutting and unwilling to spend carbs on liquid sugar, even **5–10 g of carbohydrate** can stabilise blood glucose and rescue a bad session. Optional extra for fasted trainees or anabolic insurance: **5–10 g EAAs**, or simply sip **~10 g whey** instead.

## The post-workout meal
[08:09] The urgency has been overstated. Given the 4–5 hour bracket: eat 1 hour pre-training and train for an hour, and you still have 2–3 hours to get the post-workout meal in. But eat 2 hours pre-training and train for 2 hours, and you should eat **as soon as possible**.

- **Protein: 0.5 g/kg** — ~40 g for an 82 kg (180 lb) lifter.
- **Carbs:** relatively less important — you no longer need the immediate fuel, typical weight sessions deplete less glycogen than bodybuilders claim, and research shows lost glycogen is replenished within **24–48 hours** as long as you eat carbs at some point after training ([Knuiman 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26697098/)). Still, glycogen resynthesis rates are highest in the first few hours, so include some — especially with full-body training or two-a-days where you hit the same muscle again within a day or two. Target **1–1.5 g/kg** when carbs are available, **~0.5 g/kg** when cutting. Include another serving of fruit.
- **Fat:** largely a non-issue post-workout since digestion speed no longer matters much. If you're at the outer edge of the 4–5 hour window, keep fat **under 10–20 g** so digestion isn't slowed excessively.

**Worked examples:**
- *80 kg male* → 40 g protein / 80 g carbs / 10–15 g fat: extra-lean ground turkey, jasmine rice, low-fat sour cream, low-fat shredded cheese, sriracha, a kiwi, mixed green salad; add milk or extra turkey if protein falls short.
- *55 kg female* → 4 oz chicken breast (or protein-matched tofu), 300 g baby golden potatoes, steamed Brussels sprouts, a small handful of salted pistachios, 100 g mixed blueberries and raspberries.
- *Fasted trainee with a commute before the next real meal* → protein shake immediately after training plus fruit (banana or grapes), and a couple of rice cakes depending on total carb intake; first whole-food meal a couple of hours later.

## Practical application
1. Work out your body weight in kg and set pre-workout at 1 g/kg carbs + 0.5 g/kg protein, post-workout at 0.5 g/kg protein + 0.5–1.5 g/kg carbs.
2. Eat the pre-workout meal 1–1.5 hours out. Adjust food choice, not the macros, when the gap is shorter or longer — faster/lower-fat when close, slower/higher-fat and fibre when distant.
3. Keep pre-workout fat at 0–20 g and post-workout fat under 10–20 g only if you're near the edge of the window.
4. Add intra-workout carbs only if one of the four conditions applies; otherwise skip it.
5. Check the gap between your pre- and post-workout meals stays inside 4–5 hours. That's the only timing rule that really has to hold.

## Caveats & unknowns
- Nutrient timing matters most for lean lifters, low-calorie dieters and high-volume trainees; for a well-fed lifter on moderate volume the effect is much smaller.
- Fasted training is not disqualifying — individual response varies and some people simply perform better on an empty stomach.
- The 4–5 hour figure is Nippard's midpoint between the 3–4 h and 5–6 h ranges given in the Aragon/Schoenfeld paper, not a directly measured number.
- Post-workout carb urgency is largely theoretical for typical lifters; it matters more for full-body or twice-daily training.
