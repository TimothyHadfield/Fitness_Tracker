# The 6-Day Push/Pull/Legs Routine: Full Week, Exercise by Exercise

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Smartest Push Pull Legs Routine (Fully Explained)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qVek72z3F1U)) · 2021-06-14 · 19 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- Six days: quad-focused legs, chest/tri push, lat-focused pull, posterior-chain legs, delt-focused push, mid-back pull. Each muscle hit twice weekly.
- Main barbell lifts are kept sub-maximal (RPE 5–8, percentage-based); the isolation and machine work is where you push to RPE 8–10 and use intensity techniques.
- Machines are deliberately used on hypertrophy work so sets can be taken closer to failure without taxing stabilisers and joints already loaded by heavy free-weight work.
- Long-muscle-length training is a running theme: seated leg curls over lying, dips for the pec stretch, overhead extensions for triceps.
- Intensity techniques appear on **last sets only** — drop sets, myo-reps, lateral raise 21s.

## Day 1 — Legs (quad focus) [00:30]
3–4 min dynamic warm-up, then a pyramid warm-up before working sets.

| Exercise | Sets x reps | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Back squat | 3x4 @ 80% 1RM | RPE 5–6 on set one, rising to 7–8 by the last set |
| Romanian deadlift | 3x10 | Keep light in week 1 — huge muscle-damage potential; push hips straight back, add load in later weeks |
| Unilateral leg press | 3x15 per leg | First real hard set of the day — take to RPE 8–9 |
| Eccentric-accentuated leg extension | 3x10–12 | 3–4 sec lowering; **seat set further back** |
| Seated hamstring curl | 3x10–12 | Last set: 10 reps to failure, cut load in half, failure again (he got 14 more) |
| Standing calf raise | 3x10–12 | Myo-reps on the weaker (left) side after each set: 4–5 reps, shake off, repeat until he can't get 4 clean |
| Decline plate crunch + long-lever pelvic-tilt plank | superset | Planks 30 sec/set, elbows under eyes, glutes squeezed into posterior pelvic tilt |

**Squat cue:** driving the knees out on the ascent pushes the hips toward the bar, shortening the hip moment arm and helping break through the sticking point. Safe as long as knees track in line with the toes rather than being shoved as far out as possible.

**Rectus femoris rationale:** it's the only quad head crossing both hip and knee, so it shortens at the hip while lengthening at the knee during squats and leg presses and gets little stimulus there. Moving the leg-extension seat back reduces hip flexion and lets it contribute to knee extension.

**Seated vs lying leg curl:** a high-quality study by Mayo and colleagues found seated leg curls produced significantly more hamstring growth than lying. The MASS review attributed this to training at long muscle lengths — the hamstrings are more stretched when seated. Lying curls are still useful for variety.

## Day 2 — Push (chest/triceps focus) [04:34]

| Exercise | Sets x reps | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bench press | 3x8 @ 72.5% 1RM | Quarter-second chest pause on anything above 6 reps |
| Machine shoulder press | 3x12 | Machine chosen so sets can go closer to failure with less joint/stabiliser cost; sub standing dumbbell Arnold press |
| Dips | 3x12–15 | Recovers the range-of-motion deficit of powerlifting-style bench; big pec stretch at the bottom. Sub deficit push-ups |
| Eccentric-accentuated skull crusher | 3x8–10 | 3-second lowering, no full lockout — constant tension |
| Egyptian cable lateral raise | last set myo-reps | 12 reps to RPE 9–10, then rest a couple of breaths and hit 4 more, repeating until 4 reps aren't possible |
| Cable triceps kickback | 2x20–30 | Strict 1 sec up / 1 sec down |

**On tempo:** research shows tempo doesn't matter much for hypertrophy provided you control the weight ([Schoenfeld 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25601394/)). He monitors it on high-rep sets only, because form degrades late in long sets.

**On side delts:** easily neglected on a powerbuilding approach, hence extra stimulus on the exercises that do hit them.

## Day 3 — Pull (lat focus) [07:06]

| Exercise | Sets x reps | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Weighted pull-up | 3x6 | Upright torso for near-pure shoulder adduction (lats + teres); legs straight down, scapulae depressed, chest up, full ROM. Assistance is fine |
| Seated cable row | 3x10–12 | MAG or neutral close grip; elbows tucked, drive them down toward the stomach, don't pull past the midline |
| Kneeling cable pull-over (constant tension) | to tempo failure | 1 sec up / 1 sec down; no rep target — end the set when the tempo breaks |
| Hammer cheat curl | — | Deliberate body English to handle heavier loads; grip the handle hard to load forearm flexors and brachioradialis |
| Strict incline dumbbell curl | — | Elbow back and locked, supinate by driving through the pinkies; isolates the biceps now that the forearms are pre-fatigued |

## Day 4 — Legs (posterior chain focus) [09:38]

| Exercise | Sets x reps | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Deadlift | 3x3 @ 80–85% 1RM | Sub-maximal but RPE 6–8 by set three. Bar path straight up/down over midfoot; pack the lats, scrape the shins |
| Machine hack squat | 3x10–12 | Main quad movement; let the knees travel forward past the toes (safe absent knee pain); match barbell stance width and foot flare. Leave 2–3 reps in reserve. Sub goblet squats |
| Unilateral hip thrust | — | Squeeze one glute at a time, posterior pelvic tilt at the top, chin tucked |
| Nordic ham curl + prisoner back extension | superset | Nordics set up on a lat pulldown with assistance; hands behind head on back extensions to lengthen the hip moment arm |
| Unilateral leg press calf raise | 3x8–10 | Start with the weaker leg, stretch it between sets |
| Weighted L-sit hold | — | 10–25 lb plate on the thighs via a pull-up belt; 10–15 sec is hard at first, build toward 20–30 sec at the same weight |

## Day 5 — Push (shoulder focus) [12:11]

| Exercise | Sets x reps | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Overhead press | 4x4 @ 80% 1RM | Arch the upper back, tuck the glutes — no energy leaks |
| Close-grip bench press | 3x10 @ RPE 7–8 | "Close grip" = move each hand in **one hand's width**, not as narrow as possible; elbows tucked slightly more, steady cadence |
| Low-to-high cable crossover | — | Upper pec bias. Last set only: failure at 10–12, drop the load 50%, failure again |
| Overhead cable triceps extension | — | Elbow locked, full stretch at the bottom, squeeze at the top; cables give steady tension so use full ROM |
| Dumbbell lateral raise 21s | 3 sets | 7 full-range, then 7 top-half-to-three-quarters, then 7 bottom-half only — a mechanical drop set exploiting the dumbbell tension curve (easy at the bottom, hard at the top) |
| Neck machine (8-way) | — | He trains neck directly 1–2 days a week as a minimum effective dose |

**On drop sets:** current research doesn't show much benefit beyond possible time efficiency. He still uses them periodically because they calibrate what "all out" actually feels like — a check on whether your normal sets are as close to failure as you think.

## Day 6 — Pull (mid-back focus) [15:13]

| Exercise | Sets x reps | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Omni-grip lat pulldown | 3 sets | Set 1 wide grip, set 2 moderate (just outside shoulder width), set 3 reverse close grip — increasing biceps assistance offsets accumulating lat fatigue, so the same weight and reps hold across sets |
| Chest-supported row (machine) | 3x10–12 | Mike Israetel style: full scapular protraction at the bottom, squeeze hard at the top, chest allowed to lift slightly off the pad |
| Rope face pull | — | Pull slightly upward and externally rotate for rear delts and rotators, rather than pulling elbows straight back (which shifts work to mid traps) |
| Incline dumbbell shrug | optional | For stubborn upper traps; use straps so grip isn't limiting |
| Rear delt raise | 2x15 (+ extension) | 15 reps with scapulae protracted, sweeping out but not back; rest a few seconds, then 10–15 more actively squeezing the shoulder blades to let mid traps assist |
| EZ-bar pronated curl → EZ-bar supinated curl | superset | Pronated grip fatigues the forearm elbow flexors first, so the biceps must take over on the supinated set |

Mid-back volume looks low because this week is nested in a program where full-body strength weeks supply heavy squatting and deadlifting; add more mid-back work if that isn't your context.

## Practical application
- Run the six days as written; treat main-lift percentages as sub-maximal work and save true failure for machines, cables and isolation.
- Apply the intensity techniques (drop sets, myo-reps, 21s) to the **final set** of an exercise only.
- Prefer seated leg curls, dips and overhead triceps extensions where you want a stretched-position stimulus.
- Weak or lagging side? Add myo-reps or unilateral work for that side only, as he does for his left calf.

## Caveats & unknowns
- Tempo has little independent effect on hypertrophy — the tempo prescriptions here are mostly about controlling execution.
- Drop sets aren't clearly superior for growth; they're used as a psychological calibration tool.
- Machine substitutions reduce stabiliser demand, which he notes is not purely a benefit — those stabilisers still need training.
- The mid-back volume is intentionally low for this specific program context, not a general recommendation.
- Loads shown are his own (385 lb squat, 275 lb bench); use the percentages, not the numbers.
