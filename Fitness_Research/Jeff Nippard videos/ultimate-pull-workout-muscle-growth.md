# Ultimate Pull Workout for Back, Biceps and Rear Delts

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Ultimate PULL Workout For Muscle Growth [Back, Biceps, Rear Delts] (2023)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=spKGN0XzErU)) · 2023-02-12 · 11 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- Six-exercise pull day: lat pulldown (feeder sets to failure), chest-supported row with rotating grips, bottom-half pullovers supersetted with lat stretches, omni-directional face pulls, heavy EZ-bar curls, bottom-half preacher curls.
- Every pull day should contain at least one vertical pull and one horizontal pull; the rest is angle variation.
- Varying grip and pulling direction set to set is deliberate — the back is many muscles pulling on the skeleton in different lines.
- Biceps get both a heavy low-rep movement (6-8) and a stretch-position partial movement.
- The series is bodybuilding-focused rather than powerbuilding, with only minimal squat/bench/deadlift work programmed.

## Warm-up
Five minutes on a treadmill or StairMaster, 10 arm circles per side, 10 cable external rotations per side.

## 1. Lat pulldown — 4 feeder sets of 10, then failure work
Feeder sets are a warm-up method Nippard credits to John Meadows [01:33]: build up across four or five sets of 10, ramping RPE roughly 4-5 → 6-7 → 7-8 → 10 (failure), resting a minute or two between. Two purposes: groove the movement so the lats are engaging properly by the time you hit the failure set, and calibrate load — if feeder three already feels heavy you barely add weight; if it feels easy you jump more.

He rests 2-3 min and does a second set to failure with the same load, aiming to match the rep count (often ends up 7-8 due to fatigue). Then a mini drop set: strip about 30% and grind 4-5 more reps. Reason: on pulldowns you often hit technical failure while the back could still do more with a bit of cheating, so the drop set gets the fibres to genuinely high activation without breaking form.

Technique: middle overhand grip, roughly 1-1.5x shoulder width. Too wide shortens ROM; too close lets biceps take over. Keep grip consistent across feeder sets since the point is grooving. Use straps on failure sets so grip isn't the limiter and mind-muscle connection improves; a thumbless grip is the fallback cue. He also notes IFBB pros at his gym don't train stiff and rigid — allowing some controlled lean and rock lets you feel the target muscle better, provided the negative stays controlled.

## 2. Omni-grip chest-supported row — 3 × 10-12
"Omni-grip" = a different grip each set: wide, then slightly closer, then neutral or underhand. Any chest-supported machine works, or a T-bar row, or a Helms row (dumbbells with the chest braced on an incline bench). Pulldowns emphasise lats and teres; rows emphasise traps and rhomboids, especially with an active scapular squeeze [05:39].

## 3. Bottom-half dumbbell pullover + lat stretch superset — 2 rounds
Pullover set, then 30 s static stretch on the right lat, 30 s on the left, 30 s rest, second pullover set, then stretch left then right (alternating which side goes first). Only the bottom half of the pullover ROM is used because tension on the lats is essentially zero at the top.

On the ongoing debate that pullovers are a poor lat exercise due to weak leverage overhead [06:40]: leverage isn't optimal, but because the bulk of the fibres sit behind the active joint the lats still get stretch and tension. His reasons for keeping it are honest proxies rather than proof — he always feels a strong stretch, and his lats are sore for a day or two afterwards. He notes the settling evidence would be a longitudinal study measuring lat thickness in pullover vs. no-pullover groups, and says no such study exists as far as he knows.

Stretching: grab something solid, lean the hips back until one whole side of the back stretches, hold 30 s at about 7/10 intensity (just short of pain), then switch.

## 4. Omni-directional face pull — 3 × 12-15
Cable low and pull low-to-high on set one; cable at shoulder height and pull straight across on set two; cable high and pull down toward the eyes on set three. A vertical plus a horizontal pull are all you strictly need for back growth, but hitting more angles is likely better for maximising development.

## 5. EZ-bar curl — 3 × 6-8
Most people neglect low-rep biceps work and only chase the pump. Treat this like a bench or deadlift: track load, ensure weight or reps climb week to week. A little hip drive to start the bar is acceptable if the negative is controlled on every rep.

## 6. Bottom-half preacher curl — 2 × 10-12
Partials in the stretched position only. Justified by a 2021 study from Sadle (name as spoken in the captions — spelling uncertain) and colleagues: 30-32 subjects split into a group curling only 0-50° of elbow flexion, a group curling only 80-130°, and a non-training control. After five weeks the bottom-half group gained more than twice the muscle thickness of the top-half group, indicating the lengthened portion of the preacher curl is the more hypertrophic half. The authors also cited a wider body of literature supporting long-muscle-length training.

He does these one arm at a time, starting with the weaker arm and matching reps with the stronger one, which limits asymmetry and lets him focus tension on one biceps.

## Practical application
- Use feeder sets on mind-muscle-connection exercises like pulldowns; use short warm-ups on lifts where you're chasing load.
- Rotate grip within an exercise on machine rows and rotate pulling angle on face pulls.
- Program biceps across two rep zones: heavy 6-8 tracked for overload, plus stretched partials.
- Strap in on any back set taken to failure.

## Caveats & unknowns
- No longitudinal evidence that pullovers grow lats; his case rests on stretch sensation and soreness, which he explicitly says are weak individual proxies.
- Feeder sets can add fatigue before the working set — he restricts them to exercises where grooving matters more than maximum load.
- The preacher-curl study is five weeks with a small sample; the researcher's name is garbled in the auto-captions.
