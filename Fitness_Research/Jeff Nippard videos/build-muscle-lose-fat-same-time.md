# Body Recomposition: Building Muscle While Losing Fat

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "How to Build Muscle and Lose Fat at the Same Time | Body Recomposition Science Explained" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wkBtHOBmpb0)) · 2017-09-26 · 6 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- Recomposition is real and well documented — dozens of studies show it — but meaningful recomp reliably happens in only four groups: beginners, detrained lifters, the overweight/obese, and steroid users.
- The recipe in those groups: moderate calorie deficit, high protein, and getting progressively stronger.
- Advanced natural lifters can grab a temporary window of recomp immediately after a cut, but it always runs out.
- Once it runs out, intermediates and advanced lifters should stop chasing it and run discrete cut and bulk cycles instead.
- Target gaining rate on a lean-gain phase: **1-3 lb per month**, checked against visual progress.

## The evidence

A 2015 study on resistance-trained young men and women found that eight weeks of heavy weight training produced simultaneous fat loss and muscle gain, and the effect was stronger on a **higher-protein diet**. Nippard flags the limitations himself: short duration, questionable reliability of the Bod Pod, and possible compliance issues.

A 2011 study from **Garthe** (name partly garbled in the captions — likely Garthe et al.) and colleagues showed even elite athletes gained lean mass and lost body fat over a 2-3 month window. Caveat: the effect was mostly in the female athletes, possibly because they started with higher body fat, and the athletes were elite at their sports (football, volleyball, hockey) rather than elite at resistance training — so some novice-gains effect may have been in play.

## Why the four groups work

- **Beginners** — untrained muscle responds to almost anything.
- **Detrained lifters** — the muscle memory effect. When you stop training, the **myonuclei** added to muscle fibres persist. Restart training and those myonuclei can ramp muscle protein synthesis back up far faster than building from scratch.
- **Overweight/obese** — enough stored body fat to fuel the energetically expensive process of building muscle, so fat oxidation can effectively subsidise growth even in a calorie deficit.
- **Steroid users** — anabolics work through a similar mechanism to muscle memory: they help fibres acquire more nuclei, and acquire them faster.

## The advanced recomp window

Nippard's own case: roughly 10 lb of lean gain in about six months after ten years of training (153 lb before, 163 lb at 8.4% body fat after, with body fat dropping). Four conditions made it work.

1. **Cut first.** If you are advanced and above roughly **12% body fat**, this strategy fails — you won't finish lean enough to look dramatically bigger.
2. **Don't be too lean either.** Attempting this straight off a contest-prep diet is a bad idea, because the body needs some fat regain just to recover properly. The sweet spot for ending the cut is roughly **8-10% body fat**.
3. **He was detrained.** He was coming off an injury and had spent several months at very low volume and intensity, so raising both again triggered the muscle memory effect. You don't need an injury — an occasional few weeks off training may spark new growth.
4. **Anabolic hormone levels were favourable.** Gain too slowly and testosterone stays suppressed; gain too quickly and it also stays suppressed. Hence the **1-3 lb per month** guideline with regular visual assessment. (He also cites, half seriously, research that new relationships raise testosterone, and that a five-minute conversation with an attractive woman can raise it by ~30%.)

Ride the window as long as it lasts, but expect a point where fat gain starts outpacing muscle gain. At that point either move to maintenance (slow or no progress) or commit to a proper bulk, accepting some fat gain as the price of better performance and faster growth.

## Practical application

- **Beginner / detrained / overweight:** moderate deficit + high protein + progressive overload. Recomp is close to a sure thing.
- **Advanced, post-cut:** finish the cut at 8-10% body fat, then eat to gain 1-3 lb per month and exploit the window until fat gain overtakes muscle gain.
- **Advanced, long term:** compartmentalise into cuts and bulks.
  - Preference for maximum muscle: **bulk 6+ months**, with correspondingly longer cuts. Extra fat is acceptable if it buys more muscle.
  - Preference for staying lean year-round: **bulk 2-4 months**, interspersed with **2-4 week mini cuts**.

## Caveats & unknowns
- The 2015 recomp study was short (8 weeks) with imperfect body composition measurement.
- The elite athlete study may have been confounded by relative inexperience with resistance training, and the effect concentrated in women.
- The recomp window in advanced lifters is transient by nature — it cannot be a permanent programming strategy.
