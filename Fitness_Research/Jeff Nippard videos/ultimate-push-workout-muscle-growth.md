# Ultimate Science-Based Push Day (PPL Series): Chest, Shoulders, Triceps

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Ultimate Push Workout For Muscle Growth [Chest, Shoulders, Triceps] (2023)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c3pbe3qzatQ)) · 2023-01-02 · 13 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- Bench press strength is minimalist: **one near-max top set of 3-5 reps per week**, then two back-off sets at ~75% of that weight for hypertrophy.
- Seven exercises: bench, Larson press, Arnold press, cable press-around (superset with pec stretches), crossbody cable Y-raise, a partial-rep triceps superset, and crossbody triceps extension.
- **30-second inter-set static stretching** of the trained muscle appears to boost growth for free — one study saw 9.4 mm vs 6.2 mm muscle thickness gain (~50% more); a recent Schoenfeld-led review concluded it may enhance adaptations without adding training time.
- A complete push day needs **both a horizontal press (pecs) and a vertical press (delts)** — only collapse them into a single incline press when time-limited.
- Static stretching does not impair strength unless held **longer than 60-90 seconds**.

## The session

**Warm-up.** ~5 minutes treadmill/StairMaster plus upper-body dynamic drills — arm circles, cable external rotations — then a full warm-up pyramid before the heavy bench set.

**1. Bench press — 1 top set of 3-5 reps.** Build an arch that is comfortable; size matters less than being tight and stable. Dig the upper back into the bench, pinch the shoulder blades together and down, roll the elbows forward for extra stability. Keep three points of contact: butt, upper back, head. Have a spotter lift the bar out softly rather than yanking. Lower down and slightly forward to the highest contact point on the chest, then press slightly back and up as explosively as possible.

Elbows tuck slightly on the descent and may optionally flare on the press. Because of how the pec fibers fan out, **anything from 0 to 30 degrees of elbow tuck lines up with the majority of pec fibers** — pick the amount within that range that you feel in your chest, are strong with, and that causes no discomfort.

No spotter? Unrack with your butt off the bench (legal in competition), then settle down and start.

Difficulty target: the last reps grind, bar speed clearly slows, form stays tight, and maybe one more rep was available — no more.

*IPF rule note:* the federation tightened rules against extreme arching and reduced range of motion — the bottom of the elbow must now go below the top of the shoulder joint for a lift to count. Not an issue unless your arch is enormous.

*Substitution:* flat dumbbell press or a machine press, same sets and reps.

**2. Larson press — 2 sets of 10 reps** at **~75% of the top-set weight** (295 lb top set → ~225 lb). Same setup and arch, but the legs lie flat on the bench — Nippard rests his feet on another bench or box for balance. Removing leg drive isolates pecs, front delts and triceps. Take a slightly closer grip to increase range of motion and shift emphasis toward upper pecs and triceps. Unlike the explosive top set, use a smooth **1-2 second negative and 1-2 second positive** with a soft chest touch, feeling the pecs and triceps stretch and contract.

**3. Standing dumbbell Arnold press — 3 sets of 8-10 reps.** Start with palms facing in, flare the elbows out as you press to full extension, reverse under control. Nippard used to treat this as a light mind-muscle/pump movement (30-40 lb dumbbells) but now loads it heavily (up to 60 lb for 8-10) by treating it like a barbell overhead press — squeeze the glutes, drive through the heels. **A belt and wrist straps make a real difference here.**

**4. Cable press-around superset with static pec stretch — 2 sets of 12-15 reps, each paired with a 30-second stretch hold.** [05:37] The press-around is a single-arm hybrid of a cable fly and a cable press where you press *around* the body past the midline. Nearly every other chest exercise — presses, flys, dips — stops at or before the midline, but the pecs are not fully contracted until well past it, so this targets an end range almost nothing else hits.

After each arm's set, statically stretch that pec for 30 seconds, then switch.

*Evidence for inter-set stretching:* the study Nippard covered showed a 30-second hold between sets at ~7/10 intensity (just short of pain) increased growth by roughly 50% — **6.2 mm muscle thickness gain without stretching vs 9.4 mm with**. A newer review by **Schoenfeld and colleagues** concluded inter-set stretching may enhance muscular adaptations without increasing time spent exercising, since it happens during rest. No strength interference here since it is the last pec exercise — and in any case static stretching only interferes with strength when held beyond 60-90 seconds.

**5. Crossbody cable Y-raise — 3 sets of 12-15 reps.** Learned from natural bodybuilder **Alberto Nunez**. The rationale: on a lean-away Egyptian lateral raise the body blocks a deep stretch, but pulling the arm across the body stretches the side delt much more — the Y-raise does that under active tension. Cue it like drawing a sword at the bottom, then flicking it up and out. **Do not turn it into a front raise** — lift out and back on a diagonal plane.

*Anatomy:* the deltoid is usually described as three heads (anterior, lateral, posterior), but the latest anatomy literature suggests **seven intramuscular segments**. Segments like D4/D5 may be hit better by something between a front raise and a lateral raise — hence a Y-raise. Practical takeaway: raising the arm at varied angles and planes is more likely to develop the whole shoulder and create a round, 3D delt.

**6. Triceps superset — 3 sets of 8 squeeze-only pressdowns + 3 sets of 8 stretch-only overhead extensions**, back to back with no rest between. Both are deliberate half-reps: pressdowns use only the **second half** (the squeeze), overhead extensions only the **first half** (the stretch). Credit to **Hypertrophy Coach**, who calls the pressdown principle "staying where it's hardest" — the second half of a pressdown is the hard part. You cannot just do half reps everywhere, because the stretched portion is probably the most important for growth, and pressdowns give a poor triceps stretch — so the overhead extension supplies stretched partials.

**7. Crossbody triceps extension — 2 sets of 10-12 reps.** Extra triceps volume at an unconventional arm angle. Most people always train triceps with the arms tucked at the sides; since the long head crosses both elbow and shoulder, shoulder position changes which region is emphasised.

## Practical application
Bench 1×3-5 (near max) → Larson press 2×10 @75% → Arnold press 3×8-10 → press-around 2×12-15 supersetted with 30 s pec stretches → crossbody Y-raise 3×12-15 → pressdown/overhead extension partial superset 3×8 each → crossbody triceps extension 2×10-12. Use the rest periods you already have for stretching — it costs nothing. Strap and belt up for Arnold presses.

## Caveats & unknowns
- Whether the extreme end range of the press-around actually matters for hypertrophy has, as far as Nippard knows, never been directly studied — but he sees no downside as long as the stretch isn't sacrificed.
- Which shoulder position best targets which triceps region is not established, and there are likely individual differences; his recommendation is simply to rotate several arm positions over time.
- Elbow flare on the bench is uncomfortable for some shoulders; stay tucked if so.
