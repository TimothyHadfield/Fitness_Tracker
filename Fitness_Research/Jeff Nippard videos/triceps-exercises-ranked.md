# Triceps Exercises Ranked S–F: Why Overhead Work Wins

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Best & Worst TRICEPS Exercises (Ranked Using Science)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OpRMRhr0Ycc)) · 2024-05-24 · 14 min
**Topic:** Exercise Selection

## Bottom line
- Best exercise overall: the **overhead cable triceps extension**. A 2023 study found overhead extensions produced roughly **40% more triceps growth than press-downs** ([Maeo 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819335/)).
- Worst: the **dumbbell kickback** — zero tension where the triceps are stretched (though he thinks it's over-hated online).
- Pressing alone will not maximize the triceps, especially the long head. Do direct isolation work.
- Hit the triceps from **at least two or three shoulder angles**: one overhead, one with arms at the sides (press-down), and ideally one with arms behind the torso (cable kickback).
- Bars beat ropes for overload. Same weight, same technique: 16 reps with a bar vs 12 with a rope vs 10 reverse-grip.

## Criteria and anatomy
Three boxes: high tension especially in the stretched position; feels good (no pain, smooth resistance); simple progression (add weight or a rep weekly).

Three heads. **Lateral** head gives the arm its outer sweep, **medial** head runs down the middle, **long** head builds the arm from the rear. Only the long head crosses the shoulder as well as the elbow, so it also performs **shoulder extension** (moving the arm down/back). Consequences:
- Long head is most **stretched** with arms overhead.
- Long head is most **contracted** with arms behind the torso (kickbacks, dips).
- Long head is why you feel triceps on dumbbell pullovers.
- On presses the long head lengthens at the shoulder while shortening at the elbow, so its length barely changes — presses under-train it.

## The rankings

**S tier**
- **Overhead cable extension (bar)** — arms overhead puts the long head under deep stretch *and* the cable gives high tension there. Backed by the 2023 study showing ~40% more growth than press-downs ([Maeo 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819335/)). His best of the best.
- **EZ-bar skull crusher** — very high tension at deep stretch, especially if you **arc the bar back behind your head** rather than stopping at the forehead. EZ bar spares the wrists; easy to overload. Fix the uneven tension curve by starting with elbows angled slightly back instead of straight up, which pre-loads the fibers. His favorite free-weight triceps exercise.

**A tier**
- **Bar press-down** — stable, even cable tension throughout, easy overload, easy to feel. Only held out of S because it never gets the triceps into a big stretch. He does it weekly.
- **Overhead cable extension with rope** — same long-head benefit, slightly less overload potential.
- **Katana extension** — cross-body cable overhead extension (grab each cable with the opposite hand, then turn 180°, or start crossed in front and duck through). With cables at or just above waist height you end up ~30–40° forward of straight out to the side, i.e. the **scapular plane**, which is comfortable for most people. Trains each arm individually while doing both at once. A personal favorite held at A only for the setup and learning curve.
- **One-arm dumbbell overhead extension** — fixes the French press's bulk and lets you angle the elbow comfortably. Best dumbbell-only option.
- **Dumbbell skull crusher** — on paper S, but free-moving dumbbells feel awkward to control in the lying overhead position.
- **Smith machine JM press** — the fixed bar path makes it easier to keep the triceps, rather than pecs and delts, driving the weight.
- **Cable kickback** — one of the few movements training the triceps with the arm behind the torso, giving a full long-head squeeze. Smooth even cable tension; lateral and medial heads still get decently stretched. He prefers **higher reps** with a hard contraction. Bending forward vs staying upright is biomechanically the same thing.
- **Close-grip bench press** — outstanding for progressive overload, consistent tension, feels good, and he engages the triceps better here than on dips. Demoted from S only because the long head won't grow as well as from overhead work.

**B tier**
- **Rope press-down** — spreading the rope at the bottom mostly happens at the wrists; it's a mind-muscle-connection benefit rather than real extra contraction. The loop rope is less rigid, so tension leaks into stabilizing it (12 reps vs 16 with a bar).
- **Seated dumbbell French press** — good long-head stretch, faster setup than cables, and he does them seated for stability. Downsides: circular resistance path (lots of tension at the bottom, little at the top — not fatal since the stretch matters more), and as you get strong the dumbbells get bulky and hard on the wrists.
- **Barbell JM press** — a hybrid of close-grip bench and skull crusher, lowering the bar to the chin. Overrated in his view: elbow discomfort every time he's tried it, and the arms aren't far enough back for a real long-head stretch. Still a solid lift with bench-press carryover.
- **Close-grip dip** — strong long-head contraction (arms behind torso) but never a deep stretch. Excellent for overload with a belt. Dropped from A/S because his shoulders get cranky and pecs/delts take over.
- **Machine dip** — a more locked-in dip; still lets his pecs and delts take over on some machines.
- **Diamond push-up** — narrow base makes it much harder than it looks and most people feel the triceps more from the flared elbow position. Good as an all-out finisher.

**C tier**
- **Reverse-grip press-down** — the underhand grip barely changes anything biomechanically but limits strength (10 reps vs 16 with a bar), with grip often the limiter. Some people feel it more because the forearm bones move out of the way at the top; not worth the lost overload.
- **Dumbbell kickback** — zero tension when the triceps are stretched, tension rising as they shorten: exactly backwards. Less bad than the internet says, because kickbacks are already a shortened-position exercise, but use cables if you have them, or overhead dumbbell extensions.
- **Bench dip** — takes pecs and delts out well, but the fatal flaw is overload: soon too easy, and loading plates on your lap is awkward.
- **Close-grip push-up** — same press biomechanics (lateral/medial biased) but very hard to overload past beginner stage.

No F-tier triceps exercises in this list.

## Practical application
- Anchor the routine on one **overhead** movement (cable extension with a bar, katana, or one-arm dumbbell) — this is the non-negotiable one.
- Add a press-down with a **bar**, not a rope or reverse grip.
- Add a **cable kickback** to cover the shortened, arm-behind-torso position, using higher reps.
- Keep a heavy press (close-grip bench) for long-term overload, but don't rely on it for the long head.
- Skull crushers: arc back behind the head, elbows starting slightly back, EZ bar.
- Progress by adding weight or a rep week to week.

## Caveats & unknowns
- The long-muscle-length research is real but, in his words, not strong or robust enough to throw out every shortened-position exercise — hence keeping kickbacks and dips.
- Rope vs bar likely doesn't matter much long-term if you push hard and track progress.
- Dips and machine dips are personal downgrades: plenty of people do them with no shoulder issue and their ranking would rise.
- Katana extensions were held back for setup/learning-curve reasons, not effectiveness.
