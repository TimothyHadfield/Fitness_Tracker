# Science-Based Leg Day on a 6-Day Upper/Lower Split (2019)

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Most Effective Science-Based Leg Day 2019 (New Upper/Lower Split)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_kLBi8tF6Kk)) · 2019-04-20 · 10 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- The split moves from push/pull/legs to upper/lower run **6 days per week** — upper 3x, lower 3x. Per-session volume drops; the frequency bump is the new growth stimulus.
- Session order: heavy compound first (back squat), then progressively more isolation and intensity techniques.
- Progression runs in **3-week wave loading cycles**: week 1 = 4 reps (low RPE), week 2 = 5 reps (moderate), week 3 = 6 reps (hard), week 4 resets to 4 reps and the wave repeats. Three cycles = 9 weeks.
- Squat stance width does not change quad activation, but a wider stance does recruit glutes more.
- Abs trained 2x/week here rather than daily, because frequency is already high elsewhere.

## The workout

**1. Back squat — 3 sets of 4/5/6 reps (by week)**
Chosen not as the best glute builder but because it trains quads, glutes and spinal erectors while building raw strength that carries over to other lower-body work in a way nothing else does.

- High-bar position used to keep the torso more upright and offload the lower back. He notes he is roughly **10% stronger low-bar**, which he says is typical, and plans to trial low-bar mid-program.
- Depth: hip crease below the knee joint (a powerlifter's standard). For pure hypertrophy, **parallel or just below is fine**.
- Stance slightly wider than shoulder width, and he calls that about as narrow as he'd go. Research comparing **75% vs 140% of shoulder-width stances found no difference in quad activation**, but glutes were better activated with the wider stance — consistent with their hip abduction role. Glute-focused lifters may want to widen slightly; otherwise pick the stance where you feel strong and can hit depth.
- Bracing cue: breathe **down into the gut**, not up into the chest. Viewed from the side, the bar should not rise as you take air in.

**2. Eccentric-accentuated Romanian deadlift — 3 x 10** [04:04]
Four-second lowering phase; do not squeeze or fully lock out the glutes at the top, keeping constant tension on the hamstrings. He notes hamstrings respond unusually well to a strong mind-muscle connection. Caveat given honestly: research on accentuated eccentrics has **not** shown much hypertrophy benefit so far. His rationale is that slowing the negative gives at least the same stimulus with lighter loads, which makes it easier to keep the work on the hamstrings instead of letting glutes and lower back take over.

**3. Walking lunge drop set — 3 x (8 + 8) per leg, 16 total strides** [05:05]
A single **50% drop**. Pace out 16 strides beforehand, heavy dumbbells at one end, 50%-lighter dumbbells at the other; 8 reps per leg heavy, swap, 8 reps per leg light coming back. Citing **Dr. Brad Schoenfeld**, the jury is still out on drop sets but available evidence suggests potential benefit when integrated properly; that paper advises **against very large drops of 60-70% and against very small drops of 5-10%**, while allowing a range in between. Beginners can use bodyweight reps as the drop.

**4. Eccentric-overloaded leg extension — 3 x 10** [06:07]
Concentric with both legs, then remove one leg and lower the whole load unilaterally, squeezing the quad and controlling the negative. Justification: **eccentric strength is roughly 20-50% greater than concentric strength**, so one leg can handle the lowering that two legs raised. If one quad is noticeably weaker, do all 10 eccentrics with it first and match with the stronger side; otherwise alternate legs each rep.

**5. Lying leg curl finisher — 2 sets of 8 + 12** [07:08]
8 reps with a 3-second lowering phase, then without racking the weight, 12 more reps with constant tension through the active mid-range. Only two sets, so both must be taken seriously.

**6. Superset: straight-leg calf raise + cable crunch** [07:39]
Calves done on the leg press for convenience — with a straight leg the biomechanics are essentially the same standing or seated. Superset purely to save time; abs and calves don't interfere. Cable crunch cue: **flex and extend the spine**, don't just hinge at the hips — let the lower back round at the bottom and squeeze the abs together like an accordion.

**Warm-up:** general cardio (StairMaster or steep incline treadmill) to break a light sweat, then 3-4 minutes of dynamic stretching and foam rolling.

## Practical application
- If you're running an upper/lower split, 6 days/week means lower per-session volume but 3x lower-body frequency. Five days/week works with modification; if you can only train four, run an upper/lower split with more conventional programming first.
- Load the wave: build effort across three weeks, taper, repeat. Nine weeks is a full run, and most lifters short of 10-15 years' experience can repeat the cycle and keep progressing.
- Use intensity techniques (drop sets, accentuated eccentrics) sparingly and deliberately — as weak-point tools and engagement, not as the default.

## Caveats & unknowns
- Accentuated eccentrics have not demonstrated a clear hypertrophy advantage in research; the case for them here is practical (better targeting at lighter loads), not evidence of superiority.
- Drop-set evidence is still unsettled per Schoenfeld.
- Lunge drop sets are advanced; scale to bodyweight drops while acclimating.
