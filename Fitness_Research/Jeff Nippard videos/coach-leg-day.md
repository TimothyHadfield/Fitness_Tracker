# Coaching Cues for a Lower Body Day

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "How I Coach a Leg Day | The Best Lower Body Cues for Growth" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mSuoTz8_cwM)) · 2017-11-25 · 15 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- A hotel-gym leg session Nippard coaches live for a training partner with about 18 months to 2 years of lifting experience *(name rendered as "Rashon" / "Ra'Shon" by auto-captions — uncertain)*. Four movements: plate-loaded leg press, dumbbell Romanian deadlift, walking lunge, calf raise.
- The RDL cues are the meat: initiate with the hips, keep the dumbbells travelling in a vertical line, keep weight in the heels.
- On lunges, step length is the variable that shifts the emphasis — short step is quad-dominant, long step recruits glutes and hamstrings.
- Foot position on RDLs is worth experimenting with; toes pulled back is usually stronger, toes pointed down usually gives a better hamstring feel.
- Two calf-raise tweaks: pause at the bottom of every rep, and train barefoot.
- Explicit warning that this intensity is for an intermediate, not a beginner.

## Leg press `[01:08]`

Working sets of **8-10 reps**. Nippard doesn't know the weight standard on the unfamiliar machine, so he deliberately starts too light and adds plates until the load matches the rep target rather than guessing.

Form checkpoints he calls out `[02:47]`: depth going right to the point of parallel, and knees tracking out rather than caving in.

## Dumbbell Romanian deadlift `[03:47]`

The sequence: hold the dumbbells in front, **set the hips back** with a slight knee bend, lower the dumbbells to just below the knees, then extend up. Flat back throughout.

- **Range of motion is capped by hamstring flexibility** `[04:52]`. If the athlete can't reach mid-shin without rounding, stop at kneecap level — that's enough of a stretch — and work toward more range over time.
- **The most common error** `[05:23]`: pushing the dumbbells out in front of the body, because a straight vertical drop would hit the knees. Swinging them forward means the hips never went back. The fix is to initiate with the hips so the knees clear themselves, letting the dumbbells travel **straight up and down in a vertical line**, close to the body.
- Why it matters: if the dumbbells drift forward, weight shifts onto the toes and balance goes. **Keep the weight in the heels.**
- **Foot position** `[06:25]`: most people are stronger with the toes dorsiflexed (pulled back toward the shins), but pointing the toes down can produce a stronger hamstring contraction. A practical trick — start the set with toes pointed down, then switch to dorsiflexed near failure to extend the set. Nippard personally uses toes-back for strength. He flags this as a preference, not a rule: go with where you feel it best.

## Walking lunge `[08:10]`

- **Step length sets the muscle bias** `[08:40]`. A short, narrow step is quad-dominant. A longer forward step brings in far more glute and hamstring — which is the point of the exercise as he programs it.
- **The back leg is a stabiliser only.** Drive the front heel through the floor to push yourself forward. Pushing off the back leg adds momentum and turns it into a cheat rep. Cue: train as if the back leg isn't there.
- **Don't step directly in front of yourself** `[09:12]` — place the foot slightly to the side to create a wider base and stabilise better.
- Balance is largely a practice effect; it improves with reps.

## Calf raises `[10:20]`

- **Pause briefly at the bottom of each rep.** The Achilles tendon is extremely elastic, so a bounce out of the stretched position lets stored elastic energy do work the calves should be doing. Let it dissipate first.
- **Take your shoes off.** Better range of motion, and a more solid base — the foot slides around inside a shoe.

## Practical application
- Leg press: 8-10 reps, load found by working up rather than guessing; depth to parallel, knees out.
- RDL: hips back first, dumbbells vertical and close, weight in heels, stop where your hamstring flexibility ends rather than rounding the back.
- Try the toes-down-to-toes-back switch mid-set on RDLs as a way to squeeze out extra reps.
- Lunge: long step for glutes and hamstrings, front-heel drive, back leg passive, foot placed slightly wide.
- Calves: dead-stop pause at the bottom every rep, shoes off.

## Caveats & unknowns
- **This intensity is not for beginners** `[09:49]`. Nippard makes the point explicitly: his partner is an early intermediate with 1.5-2 years of training, so pushing him hard is appropriate. A genuine novice should be introduced to the movements slowly, or they'll be wrecked for a week.
- The foot-position advice on RDLs is presented as individual — "it ultimately comes down to where you feel it best," not a general recommendation.
- No sets, rest periods, or weekly frequency are given, and the session was constrained by limited hotel-gym equipment and a hard time cut-off.
- Nippard notes his own calf training science video was old at the time of filming and due to be redone, so treat the calf cues as coaching preference rather than a summary of the evidence.
