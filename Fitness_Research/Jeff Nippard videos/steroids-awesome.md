# Steroids: How Well They Work and Everything Else They Do

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "Steroids Are Awesome" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lmClPGvdWTI)) · 2024-06-17 · 16 min
**Topic:** PEDs

## Bottom line
- Steroids work far beyond anything natural. Creatine at 5 g/day for a year adds roughly **2 lb** of extra muscle; a normal bodybuilding steroid dose adds roughly **20 lb** in a year, more depending on genetics and dose.
- The honest cost list: cardiac hypertrophy and heart failure risk, accelerated brain ageing, anxiety, measurable intelligence decline, hair loss, gyno, testicular atrophy and possible lifelong dependence, acne, abscesses from underground-lab gear, tendon injuries, aggression, and higher mortality.
- A 2024 paper following 1,000+ steroid users for 11 years (average age 27) against 60,000 matched non-users found **2.8% mortality vs 1.0%** — the authors called the excess death rate in this young population "striking."
- Mike Israetel's recommended earliest age for even considering supervised use: **25**. Jeff's rule: **at least 10 years of hard, smart natural training first**, which lands most people in their mid-20s anyway.
- "TRT" at 150–200 mg/week is not replacement — it's perpetual mini-enhancement, above what you could achieve naturally.

## The size of the gap
[00:30] Jeff, lifetime natural, tested at **485 ng/dL** — mid-range for his age. Enhanced bodybuilder Chase Irons, who is openly honest about his use, tested **above the assay's 10,000 ng/dL ceiling**.

He argues use is rising because influencers now admit their use. In theory that lets viewers reset their expectations; in practice many just started taking steroids instead. The bigger problem is partial transparency — influencers disclose the use and show the gains, but almost never show the costs, so the only available conclusion is that steroids are amazing.

## What actually happens after an injection
[02:02] Testosterone enanthate is a testosterone molecule with a carboxy group attached, dissolved in vegetable oil with an aromatic compound (e.g. benzyl benzoate) that prevents bacterial growth and aids dissolution. Injected intramuscularly, the oil forms an elongated bubble between muscle fibres and releases testosterone into the bloodstream over hours. Esterases cleave the carboxy group; binding proteins carry the testosterone to muscle cells, where it signals the DNA to grow the cell.

**Heart.** The heart is also muscle, so the same signal applies. The left ventricle in particular thickens, and the extra bulk makes it harder for the heart to both squeeze and relax — impaired pumping and increased heart failure risk.

**Brain.** A 2021 study took 3D MRI scans of over 200 weightlifters, roughly half natural and half enhanced. The natural lifters' brain age matched their actual age; the enhanced lifters showed a significantly larger **brain age gap** — their brains looked older than they should. Mike Israetel adds that some types of intelligence degrade with steroid use, measured both in current users and former users, and that most people are entirely unaware of this.

**Anxiety.** Dose-dependent and compound-dependent. Trenbolone causes far more than milder compounds like Primobolan. Mike disclosed that he currently scores, on a validated anxiety scale, as having severe anxiety disorder — the most severe category short of panic disorder.

**Body dysmorphia.** Steroids make you bigger but may not make you feel better about how you look, and can worsen dysmorphia. Taking gear doesn't mean you'll look like Sam Sulek. When you come off you rapidly lose a chunk of the size, feel smaller than ever, and the pull to go back on at higher doses becomes very hard to resist.

**Hair.** Some testosterone converts to DHT, which shrinks hair follicles faster — why so many bodybuilders are bald in their 20s.

**Gyno.** Some converts to estrogen, increasing breast tissue formation: soft, puffy nipples. The reliable fix is surgery. Most users take additional drugs to block conversion, and nearly every bodybuilder deals with it at some point.

**Testes.** Exogenous testosterone signals the testicles to stop producing, and they shrink to about **half** normal size. Natural production shuts down, which is why some users end up injecting for life.

**Skin.** Sebaceous glands enlarge and overproduce sebum, causing acne. Acne drugs used to counter it carry their own side effects.

**Abscesses.** Because most gear in the US and Canada comes from underground labs, quality varies wildly. Mike has been hospitalised at least once for a non-infectious but severe immune-response abscess — his quad swelled about half a foot out to one side. Treatment is cutting the skin open and draining it.

**Aggression.** "Roid rage" rarely manifests as actual violence, but the intrusive aggressive thoughts are constant and disturbing.

**The puberty effect.** [08:05] A common anecdote is the first 6–8 weeks feeling like being 16 again — which is a problem when you're an adult with responsibilities and a teenager's libido.

**Injury.** When strength climbs fast, connective tissue can't keep pace. If your bench goes up 50 lb in 6 weeks the weight feels light and you keep pushing, but tendons and ligaments haven't adapted — hence the brutal injuries seen in bodybuilders.

## Dose and "healthier" use
[09:40] On "I'll just do sport TRT, 150–200 mg/week": the term TRT has become conflated with a baby cycle. Even if total testosterone reads normal on paper, it isn't representative of what you could produce naturally — you're in perpetual mini-enhancement territory. That's a risk/reward judgement, not automatically bad.

Is there a healthy way? Everything is a spectrum — there are healthier and less healthy approaches, but not a safe one. The healthier version:
- Start with very small amounts.
- Regular blood work (every few months, or at least twice a year).
- Heart scans and related procedures.
- Consult a professional coach who does this for a living.
- Keep a medical doctor on retainer.
- Monitor blood pressure constantly.
- Cycle properly, on and off.
- Take the smallest dose that achieves the effect you want.

Mike's analogy: you can design a race car, a suit and crumple zones to be safer, but past 200 mph "safer" is the only word available — it was never safe.

## Age cutoffs
[11:40] Two reasons not to start young:
- Steroids can **close growth plates early**. Some men keep growing until 22; starting at 16 means ending up shorter than your genetics allowed.
- The brain develops in high-level ways well into the late 20s. Mike puts the probability of significant stunting of brain development at high through the mid-20s.

Mike's cutoff for the conversation about responsible, medically supervised use by a serious individual: **age 25**. Jeff's version: at least **10 years of hard, smart natural training** first — you'll know what works for your body, and you'll be mid-20s by default.

## Jeff's alternative
[13:43] Instead of enhancing, Jeff ran a year-long experiment with **McMaster University** to see how much muscle he could still build naturally this deep into his lifting career, doing everything perfectly: tracking every set, taking the last set of every exercise to true technical failure, skipping no sets or workouts, tracking food daily and hitting calorie and protein targets every day. He reports solid gains and more comments on added size at his gym than he's had in years.

His argument to anyone who thinks they've hit their natural limit: most people haven't been as diligent as they could be. Dial training and nutrition to 100% first, then make the decision.

## Practical application
- If you're under 25, or under 10 years of serious training, this isn't a decision you're ready to make.
- Before concluding you're at your natural limit, run a genuine 100% year: every set tracked, last set to technical failure, no missed workouts, calories and protein hit daily.
- If you do use: smallest effective dose, regular blood work and blood pressure monitoring, heart imaging, proper cycling on and off, medical supervision, experienced coaching.
- Expect gyno, acne, hair loss and testicular shutdown as likely rather than possible, and budget for the possibility of lifelong injections.

## Caveats & unknowns
Exactly how much steroids shorten lifespan can't be stated precisely — the 2024 cohort study establishes excess mortality but not the magnitude of life shortening, and everything is dose-dependent. Response varies enormously with genetics and dose. Aggression is real as intrusive thought content but rarely produces violence. The brain-age and intelligence findings are observational. Mike Israetel is himself an enhanced user and says so.
