# Exercise selection

What movement goes in the slot. Forty-nine notes, 2015–2026, organised by the decision you're
actually making rather than by video.

A note on dates matters more here than anywhere else in the library. The **S-to-F tier lists are
2024–2025**; the per-muscle "Most Scientific Way To Train X" videos are **2016–2019** and run on
EMG activation data. Where they disagree — and they disagree on the best chest exercise, the best
glute exercise, the best hamstring hip-extension exercise and the best calf raise — the newer note
is his current position, and the [disagreements section](#where-the-notes-disagree) lays them out
rather than picking one silently.

The framing he never drops: exercise selection is **variable #6 of 8**, below effort, progressive
overload, volume and frequency. From [top 10 worst exercises](top-10-worst-exercises.md): there are
few inherently good or bad exercises, only better and worse choices for a given goal. And from
[the lateral raise response](lateral-raise-only-must-exercise.md): **in bodybuilding there are no
mandatory exercises at all** — only the finished physique is judged, and muscle registers tension,
not exercise names.

## The three criteria

Every tier list from 2024 onward scores an exercise on the same three boxes — identically worded in
the [chest](chest-exercises-ranked.md), [back](back-exercises-ranked.md),
[shoulder](shoulder-exercises-ranked.md), [biceps](biceps-exercises-ranked.md),
[triceps](triceps-exercises-ranked.md), [quad](quad-exercises-ranked.md) and
[glute](glute-exercises-ranked.md) notes:

| # | Criterion | What fails it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **High tension, especially in the stretched position** | Automatic **F tier**. Hex press, kickbacks, banded work, standing plate press |
| 2 | **Feels good** — no joint pain, smooth resistance profile, decent pump and mind-muscle connection | Dips and bench for cranky shoulders; barbell JM press for elbows |
| 3 | **Simple progression** — you can add a rep or a plate | Inverted rows, bench dips, bodyweight push-ups, sissy squats |

The earlier framework, from [choosing the best exercises](choosing-the-best-exercises.md) (2018),
was structural rather than mechanical: **build on compounds, fill gaps with isolation**, and cover
six patterns — squat, deadlift variation, horizontal pull, vertical pull, horizontal press, vertical
press — plus isolation for rear delts, biceps, triceps, hamstrings, calves and abs. That template
still holds. What changed by 2024 is the tie-breaker: **where in the range the tension sits** now
decides most rankings, and it's why the machine chest press outranks the bench, why the cable
lateral raise outranks the dumbbell, and why overhead triceps extensions outrank pressdowns.

### The six properties that actually move an exercise up or down

**Resistance profile.** The single most common demotion. Dumbbells and bands give you zero tension
where the muscle is longest and peak tension where it's shortest — the flaw shared by the standing
dumbbell lateral raise, the bent-over reverse fly, the dumbbell fly, the dumbbell kickback and the
barbell curl ([shoulders](shoulder-exercises-ranked.md), [biceps](biceps-exercises-ranked.md)).
Cables and good machines fix it.

**Stretch position.** He treats loaded lengthening as the most anabolic part of the range, hedged as
"most likely" rather than settled. The evidence he leans on: **three studies showing 130–190% more
growth** from long-length training in quads, biceps and calves respectively
([7 things that helped my shoulders grow](7-things-helped-shoulders-grow.md)). The hedge is real —
he deliberately keeps a fully-shortened movement in each program (cable press-around for chest,
super-ROM lateral raise for delts, cable kickback for triceps) "as insurance against the stretch
literature being incomplete."

**Stability.** Tension diverted into balancing is tension the target muscle doesn't get. It's why
chest-supported rows beat freestanding ones, why seated and lying face pulls beat standing, why the
seated cable fly beats the crossover, and why the renegade row is F tier
([back](back-exercises-ranked.md)). The extreme case is unstable-surface training: per the CSEP
position stand, **unstable devices should not be used when hypertrophy, strength or power is the
goal** ([worst exercises](top-10-worst-exercises.md)) — the only entry on that list he says to avoid
outright.

**Loadability.** Rankings held down purely by a ceiling: one-arm dumbbell rows and flat dumbbell
presses (you max the rack at ~100 lb), inverted rows, bench dips, goblet squats, horizontal leg
press, bodyweight push-ups (**30–50+ reps to reach failure**), the Atlantis lateral raise (he's
nearly maxing the stack).

**Joint comfort.** Openly personal. Dips, the bench press, flared-elbow pressing and the barbell JM
press all get docked on his own shoulders and elbows, and he says explicitly they'd rank higher for
someone who tolerates them.

**Ease of progression.** Distinct from loadability — it's whether adding load is *practical*.
45° back extensions, planks and ab wheel rollouts all progress awkwardly; hack squats and pulldowns
progress by moving a pin.

### Why EMG is a weak basis for choosing

Almost every 2016–2019 per-muscle note is built on activation data, and almost every one carries the
same caveat: **EMG is a proxy for stimulus, not a measure of growth**. The specific failure modes he
and his guests name:

- **Loads aren't equated.** The classic case, from [episode 8 — hamstrings](episode-8-hamstrings.md):
  a squat-depth study used the **same absolute load** for partial and deep squats, so the partial
  condition was trivially easy and of course the deep squat "activated more." That's the study
  cited for years to justify ass-to-grass. The same flaw sits under the
  [Pizzari shrug finding](neck-and-trap-training.md) and the
  [Youdas ab comparison](get-six-pack-abs.md). [Grow a butt](grow-butt.md) traces it fully: the
  depth advantage vanished once Contreras assigned *relative* loads.
- **Electrodes only read what's underneath them.** Andrew Vigotsky's explainer in
  [episode 8](episode-8-hamstrings.md). The rear-delt neutral-grip finding may partly be surface
  electrodes picking up rotator cuff, since the cuff also externally rotates
  ([shoulder mysteries resolved](4-shoulder-mysteries-resolved.md)).
- **It reports group averages**, so it can't tell you what maximises *your* activation
  ([back width vs thickness](back-width-vs-thickness.md)).
- **Activation is not hypertrophy.** The leg extension wins outright on quad EMG but has a low
  overload ceiling ([quads](most-scientific-way-train-quads.md)); the deadlift ranks #1 for hamstring
  activation and Vigotsky still calls it "not a huge hamstring exercise."
- **Study designs contradict each other.** Calf foot position: one 2011 paper for toes-out, three
  2017 papers finding nothing. Free weights vs machines for delts: two papers finding no difference,
  one finding a large one at 60% 1RM that disappeared at 80%
  ([free weights vs machines](free-weights-vs-machines-big-delts-explained.md)).

The practical consequence is that the 2024–2025 tier lists are built on **biomechanical reasoning
plus coaching experience**, not head-to-head trials, and he says so in every caveats section.

## The 25 exercises you actually need

From [the only 25 exercises you need](only-25-exercises-need.md) (2026), his most recent and most
compressed statement. The top 10 are the ones to keep if you only get ten.

| Rank | Exercise | Why it earns the slot |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Squat** | Highest muscle-mass return — **~55% of male, ~58% of female muscle mass is in the legs** (Janssen 2000). Squat strength raises the deadlift, not the reverse |
| **2** | **Pull-up** | Can't be faked, needs only a bar. He built to **bodyweight +75 lb × 6**. Pulldowns are equally S-tier if you actually overload them |
| **3** | **Incline bench press** | A vertical and horizontal press in one. **20–45° all work**; most studies use ~45° |
| **4** | **Romanian deadlift** | Every program needs a hinge. Less fatiguing than a conventional pull for equal or greater glute/ham/erector stimulus |
| **5** | **Chest-supported T-bar row** | Chest support removes the low back as limiter. Elbows at **~45°** |
| **6** | **Lateral raise** | Side delts may be the most important muscle aesthetically; presses shortchange them |
| **7** | **Preacher curl** | His single pick if limited to one biceps exercise — impossible to cheat, high tension at a still-lengthened position |
| **8** | **Overhead cable triceps extension** | **~40% more triceps growth than pressdowns** over 12 weeks (Maeo 2023) |
| **9** | **Leg extension** | Only way to load the rectus femoris properly. **Set the seat back.** Safe for knees |
| **10** | **Seated leg curl** | Hamstrings are **~40% of thigh mass**. **~50% more growth than lying curls** in a 12-week study |

Ranks 11–25, in his order: **bench press, walking lunge, overhead press, deadlift, Bayesian cable
curl, Nautilus glute drive / barbell hip thrust, weighted dips, reverse pec deck, machine pec deck,
cable crunch, neck curls and extensions, dumbbell wrist curls and extensions, standing calf raise,
dumbbell shrugs, machine lat pullover.**

Two structural points he makes about that list. First, **deadlift, overhead press and bench press
are ranked on movement-pattern importance, not hypertrophy efficiency** — he says outright they're
"probably not optimal" for size. Second, several rankings are held down by **equipment access**
(lat pullover machine, glute drive), not effectiveness. And the commonly skipped visible muscles —
**calves, forearms, neck, abs, traps** — need direct work and respond fast once you start.

## The exercises to drop

| Exercise | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|

| **Behind-the-neck pulldown and press** | Lower lat activation than pulling to the front; less overload, more risk, no upside | [worst exercises](top-10-worst-exercises.md) |

| **Barbell guillotine press** | He rates the *movement* highly — F tier purely on hazard: dropping the bar on your neck | [chest](chest-exercises-ranked.md) |
| **Cross-body standing dumbbell fly** | Zero tension in the stretch; it's effectively a front raise | [chest](chest-exercises-ranked.md) |
| **One-arm dumbbell press** | No advantage — each pec already works independently on a normal dumbbell press | [chest](chest-exercises-ranked.md) |
| **Waiter curl** | Only F-tier biceps movement: wrist strain, awkward plate, no overload path | [biceps](biceps-exercises-ranked.md) |
| **Scott, drag and spider curls** | All three remove tension from the stretched position | [biceps](biceps-exercises-ranked.md) |
| **Standing dumbbell external rotation** | Rotation is transverse-plane, gravity is sagittal — the load provides essentially no resistance. Use cables or bands | [worst exercises](top-10-worst-exercises.md) |
| **Above-the-knee rack pull** | A deadlift with less ROM; F tier for damaging equipment. Low block pulls for strength, hip thrusts for glutes, shrugs for traps | [worst exercises](top-10-worst-exercises.md), [back](back-exercises-ranked.md) |
| **Donkey kicks, fire hydrants, banded lateral walks** | Warm-up and activation drills only. No overload path, no stretch tension | [glutes](glute-exercises-ranked.md) |
| **Jump squats, kettlebell swings** for hypertrophy | Calories and power, not enough tension | [quads](quad-exercises-ranked.md), [glutes](glute-exercises-ranked.md) |
| **Cable squat** | Cable pulls you forward into the machine — a bodyweight squat with a balance tax | [worst exercises](top-10-worst-exercises.md) |
| **Dumbbell pullover as a chest exercise** | 1.5 stars / D tier. It's a lat exercise. Pecs contribute on paper; nobody feels it | [chest 2022](best-worst-chest-exercises-build-muscle.md), [chest 2024](chest-exercises-ranked.md) |

Two entries on the worst list are **etiquette, not biomechanics** — curling in the squat rack and
slamming rack pulls around. And the **ass-to-grass squat** sits at #7 on that list, which reads
oddly next to the depth argument elsewhere; his actual position is "squat as deep as you comfortably
can," and past your structural limit the extra depth is spinal flexion, not extra knee or hip range.

### Chest

**Best: the machine chest press** — deep stretch, high tension across the range, locked in for
mind-muscle connection, trivially easy to overload, safe to take to failure
([chest ranked](chest-exercises-ranked.md), 2024). The **seated cable pec fly** is the only other S
tier and his favourite isolation lift.

The A tier is deep and interchangeable: barbell bench, incline bench, flat and incline dumbbell
press, dips, Smith bench, deficit push-ups, cable crossover, pec deck, dumbbell fly, cable
press-around. The **bench press is A tier, not S** — three deductions: the bar stops on the rib cage
so the stretch isn't as deep as dumbbells, some people get cranky shoulders (usually from too much
weight), and not everyone feels their pecs on it.

**Incline over decline as the default.** Chaves 2020 found incline matched flat for mid and lower
pec growth *and* beat it for upper pec growth — confirmed with longitudinal data where it had
previously been biomechanical inference only
([chest 2022](best-worst-chest-exercises-build-muscle.md)). Decline is B tier: more people struggle
with upper pec development than lower.

Two technique fixes worth their own line. **Dumbbell flys as lengthened partials** — cut the top
half where tension disappears. **Deficit push-ups with a 2–3 second pause** — if you're going to do
push-ups, do them this way, because a plain push-up bottoms out on the floor before the pecs are
fully stretched and needs 30–50+ reps to approach failure.

The 2016 note ([chest for growth](most-scientific-way-train-chest-growth.md)) adds one thing the
later ones drop: the **internally rotated (Bayesian) fly grip** — thumbs toward each other, palms
down — on length-tension grounds, plus an end-of-session **stretch iso-hold** at ~50% of your 5RM,
starting at 50–60 seconds and adding ~5s weekly. Chest **2× per week**.

### Back

**Best: the chest-supported row** — every positive of a freestanding T-bar with a stable base, so
all tension goes into the back. Six movements reach S tier
([back ranked](back-exercises-ranked.md), 2024): wide-grip pulldown, neutral-grip pulldown,
half-kneeling one-arm pulldown, Meadows row, chest-supported row, and cable rows (close and wide).

**The deadlift is C tier as a back builder.** It barely stretches the lats or midback, the tension
they get is isometric, and there's no back pump. He keeps it for erector thickness and total-body
strength, and notes it would be **A or B tier ranked as a lower-body exercise**.

**Width vs thickness is largely a myth.** From [back width vs thickness](back-width-vs-thickness.md)
(2019): Lehman found the seated row **beat the lat pulldown for both lat and trap activation**, and
the Andersen data shows lats and traps are both highly active in the pulldown regardless of grip.
Width appearance is lat development plus **waist circumference — and waist size is a diet variable**,
not a training one. What grip actually does:

| Movement | Grip | Emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Vertical pull | Medium-to-wide **pronated**, ~1.5× shoulder width, to the front | Lats via shoulder adduction |
| Vertical pull | Close / neutral | Lats via shoulder **extension**; more trap and rhomboid |
| Row | Close or V-bar, elbows **down and in** | Lats / width |
| Row | Wide, elbows **back**, squeeze the blades | Mid traps and rear delts / thickness |
| Row | Medium, elbows back and out at **~45°** | The best single blend |

The grip evidence chain is worth knowing because each study fixes the last one's flaw: Signorile
2002 favoured wide-to-the-front but confounded wrist position; Lusk 2010 held wrist constant and
found **width didn't matter while pronated beat supinated**; Andersen 2014 used relative loading and
put the **medium grip (1.5× biacromial)** on top, though the lat finding was a *trend*, not
significant ([back width vs thickness](back-width-vs-thickness.md),
[most scientific back](most-scientific-way-train-back.md)). No EMG data exists on row grip width at
all — that half of the table is biomechanical reasoning only.

Practical extras: **face pulls seated or lying, not standing** — standing loses tension to balance.
**Pull-ups and pulldowns give equal lat activation** at matched relative load (Doma 2013); the
pull-up just adds biceps. Chin-ups drop to B tier for the back specifically because the supinated
grip shifts tension to the biceps. If you can't feel your lats, **2–3 light pre-activation sets**
(one-arm lat pull-ins, cable pullovers) measurably help — coaching cues alone raised lat EMG in
Snyder 2009 ([most scientific back explained](most-scientific-way-train-back-explained.md)). Back
**2–3× per week**, and be careful above **25 working sets per week** (Israetel).

### Shoulders

Treat the three heads as three muscles. The volume split is the headline: Dr. Mike Israetel argues
**70–90% of delt volume should go to side delts**
([top 3 shoulder exercises](top-3-shoulder-exercises-3d-delts.md)), because front delts are already
hammered by chest pressing and rear delts by proper back work. The observational basis — bodybuilders
carry roughly **5× the front delt development of untrained people, 3× the side delts, and only
10–15% more rear delt** ([most effective shoulders](most-effective-way-train-shoulders.md)).

**Front delts: machine shoulder press** ("A tier plus", his #1 front delt pick and go-to vertical
press). Dumbbells beat barbells — faster setup, more ROM at both ends, and more side delt
involvement since the delts stop the dumbbells drifting (Saeterbakken 2013). The
**standing barbell OHP is B tier** and he's careful about it: "overhated by the science crowd,
overrated by the barbell crowd." **Skip front raises** unless you genuinely can't press.

Two clean mechanical answers from [shoulder mysteries resolved](4-shoulder-mysteries-resolved.md)
(the questions are posed in [4 unsolved shoulder mysteries](4-unsolved-shoulder-mysteries.md)):
the press hits the front delt rather than the side because the shoulder sits **externally rotated**,
putting the anterior head in the better position against gravity; and the front raise underperforms
the press because the front delt produces **maximum force above horizontal**, exactly where the
front raise stops.

**Side delts: the cable lateral raise, cable set at about hand height** — S tier and best shoulder
exercise overall, because tension peaks when arm and cable form 90°, i.e. when the delt is most
stretched. Also S: the cable Y raise and the behind-the-back cuffed raise. The **standing dumbbell
lateral raise is B tier** on resistance profile alone — zero tension at the bottom where the delt is
longest, peak tension at 90° where it's shortest. If dumbbells are all you have,
**lean into a bench or rack**, which is "the most effective way to do lateral raises with dumbbells."

**Rear delts: the reverse cable crossover** — huge stretch, especially crossing the arms at the top;
cue **sweeping the cables out, not yanking them back**. The **sideways one-arm reverse pec deck** is
the other S-tier pick, and the reasoning is worth quoting from
[7 amazing exercises](7-amazing-exercises-no-one.md): a standard reverse pec deck is
**"effectively half reps in a shortened position"** — the rear delts don't approach a stretch until
the arm reaches across the body. The single most common rear-delt error, from
[boulder rear delts](build-boulder-rear-delts.md), is **turning it into a row by over-retracting**:
the arms swing **out**, they don't pull back.

Rep and progression guidance from [7 things that helped my shoulders grow](7-things-helped-shoulders-grow.md)
(2025), his most current shoulder note: **train shoulders 3–4 days a week**, not 1–2, because
effective volume caps around **6–8 sets per session** (a 2025 meta puts it as high as 11, but returns
diminish sharply past six) and shoulders recover in **24–48 hours**. Run **linear progression on the
press** (5–6 reps, add 5–10 lb weekly, hold for a year) and **double progression on isolation work**,
rotating the exercise when you can't add a rep without form breaking. The "take the traps out" cue he
found most effective: **imagine scooping sand out to the side with a long shovel.** Older volume
guidance from 2017: **8 isolation sets/week minimum for each of side and rear delts, 16–22 for a
real push.**

And the tip he thinks most people will skip: **get lean.** He shows himself at 20% versus 9% body fat
— the 3D look is mostly a smaller waist and less fat over the delt, not exercise selection.

### Biceps

**Best: the face-away (Bayesian) cable curl** — arms behind the torso for maximum stretch plus even
cable tension. Closest runner-up and the more foolproof movement: the **45° preacher curl**. Both
belong in a routine ([biceps ranked](biceps-exercises-ranked.md)).

The principle that separates them from the alternatives: **high tension in a decent stretch beats a
huge stretch with no tension.** That's why preacher curls out-grew incline curls in a head-to-head
study (Zabaleta-Korta 2023, significantly more *distal* biceps growth) — the incline curl gives a
big stretch but almost no tension there. The same logic demotes the **flat bench curl** (highest
tension at deepest stretch, but tension collapses through the rest of the rep) and kills the
**Scott, drag and spider curls** outright.

For **peak**, his #1 is the **dumbbell hammer curl** — the neutral grip removes supination, so less
biceps and much more **brachialis**, which is roughly as large as both biceps heads combined and
pushes the biceps up as it grows. He flags there's no hard science on changing peak shape.

Small technique items that recur: **cue supination by driving through the pinkies**; **curl both arms
simultaneously**, since alternating gives a long unloaded rest between reps; **EZ-bar curl at 6–8
reps** as the heavy overload lift. And his rebuilt **21s — short, then full, then long** (7 seated
top-half, 7 full standing, then bottom-half reps to failure), because standard 21s put the most
anabolic portion first where you don't need the freshness.

The 2017 note ([train arms for growth](train-arms-growth.md)) adds the pairing logic: Oliveira 2009
found the **preacher curl peaks in the first third of the range and the standing/incline curl in the
last two-thirds** — opposite profiles, so pair them. Volume: **8 weekly sets minimum, 14–20 optimal**,
over 2+ sessions.

### Triceps

**Best: the overhead cable extension with a bar.** The evidence is the strongest single
exercise-selection finding in the library: Maeo 2023 found overhead extensions produced
**~40% more triceps growth than pressdowns** over 12 weeks
([triceps ranked](triceps-exercises-ranked.md)).

The anatomy that explains it: only the **long head crosses the shoulder**, so it's most stretched
with arms overhead and most contracted with arms behind the torso. **On presses the long head
lengthens at the shoulder while shortening at the elbow, so its length barely changes** — which is
why pressing under-trains it and why close-grip bench is A tier rather than S.

Program **at least two or three shoulder angles**: one overhead (non-negotiable), one with arms at
the sides (**bar** pressdown), and ideally one with arms behind the torso (cable kickback, higher
reps). The bar-over-rope call is concrete: same weight, same technique, **16 reps with a bar vs 12
with a rope vs 10 reverse-grip** — the rope leaks tension into stabilising itself, and the
reverse grip just costs you load.

The other S-tier pick is the **EZ-bar skull crusher**, with two fixes: **arc the bar back behind your
head** rather than stopping at the forehead, and **start with the elbows angled slightly back** to
pre-load the fibres. On the kickback, from
[horseshoe triceps](grow-horseshoe-triceps-by-changing-arm-positions.md): it only earns its place if
you **actively drive the shoulder into hyperextension** — otherwise it's a worse pressdown. That note
also sets the rep split: **6–12 for bar pressdowns, 10–20 for rope, single-arm and kickbacks.**

### Forearms

Direct work isn't mandatory — heavy pulling loads them hard — but it's necessary if they're a weak
point, and [only 25 exercises](only-25-exercises-need.md) puts wrist curls and extensions at #22 on
the grounds that **forearms are the calves of the upper body**: always visible in a t-shirt.

Cover three functions: **wrist flexion, wrist extension, and grip**, plus a **reverse-grip curl** for
the brachioradialis. The brachioradialis point is subtle
([build huge forearms](build-huge-forearms.md)): it's **equally active in neutral, supinated and
pronated positions**, so a pronated grip doesn't recruit it more — it just takes the biceps *out*.
And because peak brachioradialis torque sits at **100–120° of elbow flexion** versus ~80° for the
biceps, **top-half partial reps after 12–15 full reps** bias it further.

Five movements cover everything ([forearm training](forearm-training.md)): **reverse curl (thumbless
grip, crush the bar), wrist roller, barbell towel hold (30s up to 45s, then add weight and restart),
plate pinch (two 5–10 lb plates up to 25–35 lb), and dumbbell wrist curls/extensions at 15–20 reps.**

Dose: **2–3 exercises, 2–3 sets each, 10–20 reps, at the end of an upper body session, 2–3× per
week.** Volume-equated research found **10×/week is no better than 3×/week** (Boyes 2017). He warns
*against* adding fat grips to lifts you already do — it cuts the load and lets your back and shoulder
work suffer for the sake of forearm work.

### Quads

**Best: the hack squat.** Everything the barbell squat offers plus faster setup, a more locked-in
position, and free choice of foot position without a balance tax. If your gym lacks one, the
**high-bar back squat** ([quads ranked](quad-exercises-ranked.md)). S tier: barbell back squat, hack
squat, pendulum squat, Smith machine squat, Bulgarian split squat.

**The rectus femoris problem.** It's the only quad head that also flexes the hip, so on squats it
shortens at the hip while lengthening at the knee and its overall length barely changes. Fix:
**a leg extension with the seat set back** — hips fixed, so all four heads are highly engaged, and a
recent study found significantly more overall and rectus femoris growth with the seat back. The
"leg extensions wreck your knees" claim is called thoroughly debunked; ease off only if you already
have knee pain.

**Depth is where the quad tension lives.** Bloomquist 2013 — 12 weeks, MRI — found the deep group grew
more at **every site**, while the shallow group showed **no growth at four of six sites**
([quads 2017](most-scientific-way-train-quads.md)). Use yoga blocks on the shoulders if a hack squat
won't let you reach depth. **Stance width is a non-factor** — three studies, 75% to 140% of shoulder
width, no difference. **Front squats** raise quad demand via a more upright torso (~21% more vastus
lateralis than back squats in Contreras 2016) at lower knee compressive load, so they're a reasonable
pick for cranky knees; A tier only because many find the rack position awkward.

**Bulgarian split squats at 2 sets per side and stop** — he's explicit about the cap, because they're
so fatiguing physically and psychologically. Volume: **12–18 weekly sets**, above ~20 usually creating
recovery problems, at **1.5–3× per week**; his own preference is 2× — one heavy 4–8 day and one
8–20 day.

### Hamstrings

The single organising principle, unchanged from 2015 to 2026: **train both hip extension and knee
flexion. Don't dichotomise.** The anatomical reason is the **short head of biceps femoris, which
crosses only the knee** — a program of pure hip extension never trains it at all
([episode 8](episode-8-hamstrings.md), [hamstring training](hamstring-training.md)).

**Squats are a bad hamstring exercise** — Ebben ranked the squat *worst* of six movements tested, and
squat hamstring EMG comes in around **20% of MVC**. The mechanism: the hamstring shortens at the knee
while lengthening at the hip, so total muscle length barely changes. Deadlifts are only modestly
better; Vigotsky's blunt summary is "the deadlift itself isn't a huge hamstring exercise, neither is
the squat."

The knee-flexion pick has moved. The 2017 note says he has **no strong preference between lying and
seated leg curls** (EMG similar); [only 25 exercises](only-25-exercises-need.md) reports a 12-week
training study finding **~50% more hamstring growth with seated versus lying**, and ranks the seated
curl #10 overall. The mechanism was already in the 2015 podcast: on a seated curl the hips are flexed
so the hamstring is lengthened at the hip and doesn't hit **active insufficiency** at the knee.

Regional work that holds up: **point your toes in the direction of the fibres you want**
(Lynn and Costigan — toes in for medial, toes out for lateral), and the **lying leg curl beats the
stiff-leg deadlift for distal activation** with no difference proximally (Schoenfeld 2015).
**Ankle position is a non-issue** once load is equated — you're stronger dorsiflexed only because the
gastrocnemius comes out of active insufficiency and helps ([hamstrings tips](hamstrings-tips.md),
[episode 8](episode-8-hamstrings.md)).

Template: **hamstrings 2× per week, under 20 sets weekly.** Day 1 heavy stiff-leg deadlift 4–8 + light
leg curl 8–20; Day 2 heavy leg curl 4–8 + light hip extension 8–20 — with Vigotsky's caveat that the
rep ranges are interchangeable between the two patterns. For strain prevention: **eccentric
long-length work** — Nordics, good mornings, RDLs. His good-morning paper found it stretched every
hamstring muscle **more than sprinting does** at every load tested.

### Glutes

**Best: the walking lunge** — he calls it a hot take himself
([glutes ranked](glute-exercises-ranked.md), 2025). It hits all three regions: upper (medius
stabilising), middle (hip extension), lower (deep stretch). Accentuate it with **longer strides and a
~30° forward lean**.

Best per region:

| Region | Exercise | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Upper glutes** | Machine hip abduction | **Lean forward ~30°**, 15–20 reps, add a gym pin when you max the stack |
| **Middle glutes** | Hip thrust machine (Nautilus Glute Drive) | Comfortable, stable, no setup — he does these weekly |
| **Lower glutes / ham tie-in** | Romanian deadlift | Best deadlift variant for glute hypertrophy |

The **barbell hip thrust is only high B** — effective and heavily studied, but bar pinch, setup time,
and no deep stretch: even a deep hip thrust reaches only about **90° of hip flexion** against the
~**130°** most lifters have available. Barbell squats are **A tier and "an underrated glute builder"**;
make them more glute-dominant with the bar a few inches lower, more forward lean, and hip drive.

Cover both ends of the range: shortened work (thrusts, abductions, kickbacks) plus stretched work
(deep squats, long-stride lunges, RDLs). He's honest that **whether glutes need a deep stretch is
unsettled** — a 9-week study in untrained subjects found the hip thrust as effective as the squat
despite the squat's greater stretch.

The 2017 notes add detail the tier list doesn't: **toes in for glute medius, toes out for glute max**
(Lee 2014); the **barbell hip thrust with a neutral pelvis beat the American and banded versions** for
upper-glute activity, though 2 of 13 subjects did better on the variants
([build an amazing butt](build-amazing-butt.md)); and Contreras' recovery framework —
**stretchers 3–4 days, activators 2–3 days, pumpers 1–2 days** — which is what makes 3–4 glute
sessions a week workable.

### Calves

**Run a straight-leg and a bent-leg variation.** The gastrocnemius crosses the knee, so it's trained
with the leg straight; the soleus crosses only the ankle, so a bent knee puts the gastroc into
**active insufficiency** and hands the work to the soleus — which is the **larger** of the two by
volume ([calf training](calf-training.md)).

**Tempo is the whole exercise** ([force calves to grow](force-calves-grow-smarter-training-methods.md)):
**1–2s pause at the top, ~2s eccentric, and a mandatory 1–2s pause in the fully stretched bottom
position.** The bottom pause exists to stop the elastic Achilles bouncing the load for you — bouncing
calf raises look hard and are mostly tendon work. If your current technique is a bounce, drop to
**bodyweight only for a few weeks** and rebuild the tempo.

Rep split: **heavy day 6–12, light day 12–20+**, with the seated/soleus work skewing higher still.
**Foot position doesn't matter** — the 2011 toes-out finding wasn't upheld by three 2017 papers, and
the biomechanical reason is that foot rotation comes from the hip and the gastroc doesn't cross the
hip. Other useful bits: **flex the quads to lock the knees** before each rep so you can't squat the
weight up; use a riser so the heels drop below the toes; **barefoot is fine** if shoes restrict
plantarflexion.

On stubbornness: no direct research exists. The four candidate explanations
([science of calf training](science-calf-training.md)) are pre-training from daily walking, skeletal
structure and insertion height, **soleus fibre composition (70–96% type I)**, and **lower androgen
receptor density** — the last of which he calls speculative at best. His own view is that people
mostly just get discouraged and stop training them properly.

### Abs and core

**You build abs with training and reveal them with diet.** Being lean won't show undeveloped abs, and
you need roughly **8–12% body fat as a male and 14–18% as a female**
([top 3 ab exercises](top-3-exercises-six-pack-abs.md)) — a 2025 note puts it at 10–12% and ~20%.

**Squats and deadlifts are not ab exercises.** Willardson measured roughly **10% MVC for the overhead
press and under 5% for squat and deadlift**, at 75% 1RM ([six pack abs](get-six-pack-abs.md)). The
corollary Contreras draws: if you want abs you must isolate them — and if you *don't* want a blocky
midsection, drop the direct oblique work rather than the compounds.

Pair **one torso-down movement (crunch — biases upper abs)** with **one legs-up movement (leg raise —
biases lower abs)**. The **cable crunch** is the pick because it's one of the very few ab exercises you
can progressively overload by adding weight weekly. Technique: **posterior pelvic tilt before the
first rep**, crunch **down and in**, and round the lower back deliberately — the abs are the one place
where "keep a neutral spine" is the wrong cue, because lumbar flexion is their job. Keep the load
moderate; heavy loaded lumbar flexion is where he plays it safe.

A complete core program needs three patterns ([ab and core training](ab-and-core-training.md)):
**weighted spinal flexion, anti-rotation (half-kneeling Pallof press), and anti-extension (plank)**.
The plank as normally done is too easy — the record is **8 hours 1 minute** — and Schoenfeld's two
modifications fix it: **walk the elbows forward to eye level** and **squeeze the glutes into posterior
pelvic tilt**, both producing significantly more activation. Progress by making the position harder,
never by adding duration: elbows forward, then elbows closer together, then pull elbows toward toes
and toes toward elbows. **3 x 20–40 seconds.**

The **ab wheel rollout** topped Youdas' comparison for rectus abdominis; progress it by **range of
motion, stopping where you can no longer hold a neutral lumbar spine**. And from
[7 amazing exercises](7-amazing-exercises-no-one.md), the **dragon flag** at **4–8 reps** is his
preferred alternative to hanging leg raises, because a hanging raise leaves the torso unstabilised so
you swing and lose tension.

Dose: **3–6 sessions per week, 2 exercises per session, 3–4 sets**, at **6–12 reps** on loaded
movements and **15–30** on bodyweight ones. There is **no research supporting ultra-high-rep ab
training**, and he pairs ab work with an equal amount of lower back work.

### Neck

**The neck is just another muscle** — and the evidence is unusually clean. Conley 1997: 22 men,
12 weeks, a rigorous 4-day program including squats, RDLs, mid-thigh pulls, rows and shrugs. The
compound-only group showed **zero increase in neck cross-sectional area**. The group that added
**nine weekly sets of neck extensions gained 13%** ([neck and trap training](neck-and-trap-training.md)).
Heavy deadlifts and shrugs do not grow the neck.

Train **flexion and extension**; lateral flexion is optional (the SCM wraps around the side of the
head, so flexion work covers the side dimension), and rotation matters only for combat athletes.
**10–20 reps, up to 30, 2–3× per week, 40–70 reps per session** — heavier loading isn't worth risking
the cervical spine's connective tissue when close-to-failure high-rep work does the job
([build a thicker neck fast](build-thicker-neck-fast.md)).

Practical: a **1–2 minute dynamic warm-up every session, non-negotiable**, or you risk a "zinger"
that leaves you unable to turn your head for days. On neck curls, take a **loose grip on the plate**
so the arms can't assist and **hook your legs under the bench** so the abs can't crunch the weight.
A **head harness** is near-essential for extension, because the neck is strong there and a single
45 lb plate stops being enough. Stop at **neutral head position** — never hyperextend. He gained
about **two inches of neck circumference in a year**. The anatomy behind all of it is in
[can you build your neck](build-neck.md): four patterns only — flexion, lateral flexion, extension,
and shrugging.

The dissenting protocol is Alex/AlphaDestiny's in [bear mode](go-bear-mode-look-enhanced-naturally.md):
**4 sets of 25, flexion and extension, every 48 hours, building to 90 lb.** Far more aggressive than
Jeff's own prescription, and presented as anecdote rather than evidence.

### Traps

**A shrug is elevation plus upward rotation.** Pull the shoulders **up and in toward the ears**, not
straight up ([build bigger traps](build-bigger-traps.md)). The governing finding is Johnson 1994:
upper trap fibres run much closer to horizontal than vertical, so they can't effectively elevate the
scapula with the arm hanging neutral — hence **shrug with the arms roughly 30° abducted**. The trap
bar does this automatically, which is why it's his favourite version.

**Barbell/trap bar shrugs 8–15 reps; cable shrugs 12–20.** The dominant error is going too heavy,
which cuts range of motion: progressively adding weight while progressively shortening ROM is not
progressive overload. Judge progress by whether you reach **terminal scapular elevation**, not by
bar weight.

Other trap work worth having: **rope upright rows** (elevation plus abduction — traps and side delts
at once, and Israetel's tip is that running them straight after shrugs destroys the traps); **rope
face pulls with retraction**, loading them in a different plane; and the **Kelso shrug**, which
completely isolates the mid traps and rhomboids without the biceps limiting you
([7 amazing exercises](7-amazing-exercises-no-one.md)). The evidence for that last one is neat:
**after failing on a normal row you can usually crank out another 4–5 Kelso shrugs** — your biceps
and rear delts failed, your mid back didn't. Done right the weight travels only **4–5 inches**.

Volume: **12–20 sets per week** for most people, or **6–10 extra weekly sets** of an isolation you
aren't already doing, on top of what back and shoulder work already provides.

## Free weights vs machines

The tribalism is overdone, and it's the one question with a clean meta-analytic answer:
**free weights and machines build muscle roughly equally as long as you take sets close to failure**
(Haugen 2023, cited in [quads ranked](quad-exercises-ranked.md)).

What each does better, from [choosing the best exercises](choosing-the-best-exercises.md) and
[free weights vs machines for delts](free-weights-vs-machines-big-delts-explained.md):

| | Free weights | Machines |
|---|---|---|
| **Advantage** | Better strength transfer; more stabiliser involvement | Easier to learn, safer to push to failure, all tension to the target |
| **Disadvantage** | Harder to learn; balance steals tension | Fixed path may not fit your skeleton; quality varies enormously |
| **Delt activation evidence** | Two studies found **no difference**; a third found free weights much higher — but only at **60% 1RM**, vanishing at **80%** | — |

His practical rule: **use both, and put the free-weight movement early in the session** when you're
fresh. And the 2024 tier lists lean noticeably more machine-friendly than the 2016–2018 notes — the
best chest exercise, best front delt exercise, best quad exercise and best mid-glute exercise are all
machines now, precisely because machines deliver stable tension in the stretched position.

One caveat he repeats: **machine rankings assume a good machine.** The Atlantis lateral raise and a
Panatta press aren't interchangeable with whatever your gym has.

## The underrated S-tier work almost nobody does

From [7 amazing exercises no one does](7-amazing-exercises-no-one.md) (2025) — seven movements he does
weekly, all of which fix a tension-profile or range-of-motion problem in a standard exercise:

| Exercise | The problem it fixes | Prescription |
|---|---|---|
| **Dead hang** | Shoulder health, grip, spinal decompression | His max is 2:45. Chalk, loose grip first, pace it like a race |
| **Zottman hammer curl** | You're 40–50% stronger eccentrically, so normal curls under-load the negative | 8–10 reps |
| **Incline dumbbell Y raise** | Dumbbell laterals have zero tension at the bottom | Bench at **20–30°**; hits the fibres between front and side heads |
| **Sideways one-arm rear delt fly** | Standard reverse pec deck is half reps in a shortened position | Sweep across the body, one arm at a time |
| **Dragon flag** | Hanging leg raises leave the torso unstabilised | 4–8 reps; legs to **60–80°** |
| **Kelso shrug** | Rows fail on the biceps before the mid back | Locked elbows, **1s pause**, weight travels 4–5 inches |
| **Seated cable deadlift** | Barbell hinges cost more fatigue than the stimulus is worth | Glutes planted, go lower than a barbell allows |

He's careful about the framing: these are **substitutions and accessories, not replacements for the
fundamentals**, and he explicitly doesn't claim they'll dramatically increase gains — the pitch is
"not leaving gains on the table."

Two more from elsewhere. The **barbell 45° hyperextension**
([most underrated exercise](most-underrated-exercise-get-yoked-hits-traps.md)) trains four things at
once — hip extension, isometric thoracic extension, lumbar extension, and an isometric lat/triceps
long-head pull-in — with an inverted-U torque curve that's harder at the midpoint than the good
morning, horizontal back extension or reverse hyper. **6–10 reps** as a compound; round the back and
hold a dumbbell instead and it becomes a high-rep glute movement. And the **Meadows row**, the one
back movement he singles out for an unmatched midback pump.

## Building the yoke

The "look big in clothes" problem is a different question from the bodybuilding one, and the answer
is a specific short list of muscles rather than a training method.

Jeff's five ([the yoke workout](yoked-ultimate-trap-neck-delt-workout.md)): **neck, traps/upper back,
side delts, upper chest, and the triceps long head** (plus glutes, which that session skips as it's
upper-body only). Alex/AlphaDestiny's six ([bear mode](go-bear-mode-look-enhanced-naturally.md)):
**neck, traps, upper back, shoulders, forearms, glutes** — chosen because these are the areas that
blow up on enhanced lifters, so training them directly is how a natural fakes that look.

The two approaches differ sharply. **Jeff's yoke session** is 7 exercises × 3 sets, moderate-to-high
reps, RPE-autoregulated, with **neck placed first** on the priority principle (Simão 2005: fatigue
from earlier exercises measurably degrades later performance, regardless of muscle size). **Alex's**
is four heavy loaded-stretch movements — **rack pull above the knee, power shrug (5×20 with leg
drive), cheat row, and direct neck work** — trained full-body twice a week with weekly exercise
rotation, at **15–20% body fat rather than single digits**. Nippard flags the whole thing as
anecdote-driven and pushes back on the recomposition claims; the nucleus overload hypothesis in that
note is untested speculation and probably confounded by genetics.

Where they agree: **the neck is arguably the single most important muscle for looking muscular in
clothes**, and it needs direct work because nothing else trains it. The glute case is the same
argument from a different angle ([5 reasons to train glutes](5-reasons-everyone-want-huge-glutes.md)):
side and rear are three of the four directions people see you from, so undeveloped glutes leave most
of your visual impression flat — plus a real injury-risk payoff at the knee from abduction and
external rotation work.

## Where the notes disagree

The live contradictions, newest position stated first.

**Best chest exercise — three different answers in four years.** 2022
([best & worst chest](best-worst-chest-exercises-build-muscle.md)): **bench press #1 at 5 stars**,
machine chest press 4.5. 2024 ([chest ranked](chest-exercises-ranked.md)): **machine chest press is
S tier and crowned best**, bench press drops to A. 2026
([only 25](only-25-exercises-need.md)): **incline bench at #3, bench at #11**, and no machine press in
the top 25 at all. The rubric changed underneath the answer — 2022 scored enjoyment, tension,
overload and convenience; 2024 scored stretch tension first; 2026 scored movement-pattern importance.

**Best glute exercise, and whether squat depth matters for glutes.** 2017
([grow a butt](grow-butt.md)) argues **squats are overrated for glutes** and that the depth advantage
is a load-equating artefact that disappears under relative loading. 2025
([glutes ranked](glute-exercises-ranked.md)) puts the **walking lunge at #1**, calls the barbell
squat "an underrated glute builder", and cites **Kubo 2019 that squatting deeper matters for glute
growth**. It also demotes the barbell hip thrust — the 2017 hero movement — to **high B**. Treat 2025
as current, but note he never explicitly retracts the 2017 depth argument.

**Best hamstring hip-extension movement.** 2015 ([episode 8](episode-8-hamstrings.md)): Vigotsky's
single pick is the **45° hyperextension**, because hip-extension torque stays highest through the
whole range, and both agree it ranks above the conventional deadlift. 2026
([only 25](only-25-exercises-need.md)): he prefers the **RDL to the 45° back extension** specifically
because it's **easier to overload**. Same movement, opposite verdicts, on different criteria.

**Seated vs lying leg curl.** 2017 ([hamstring training](hamstring-training.md)): **no strong
preference**, EMG is similar, rotate both — and [hamstrings tips](hamstrings-tips.md) (2016) actually
favours the *lying* curl for distal emphasis. 2026: a 12-week trial showing **~50% more growth
seated**. The newer note resolves it.

**Standing vs seated calf raise.** 2016–2019 notes: **do both**, standing for gastroc and seated for
soleus, with the soleus deserving deliberate attention because it's the larger muscle. 2026 cites
**Kinoshita 2023 — standing straight-leg raises significantly more effective than seated bent-leg** —
and makes standing the main lift. Both can be true (do both, prioritise standing), but the emphasis
has flipped.

**Calf foot position.** 2016 ([science of calf training](science-calf-training.md)): **feet straight
ahead — turning them out can roughly halve activation.** 2018 and 2019: **no meaningful difference,
use what's comfortable.** The newer position is better supported (one 2011 paper against three 2017
papers) and is the one to keep.

**Cables vs dumbbells for lateral raises.** 2024 ([shoulders ranked](shoulder-exercises-ranked.md)):
**cable lateral raise S tier, dumbbell B tier**, entirely on resistance profile. 2025–2026: an
8-week study found **no difference** between cable and dumbbell lateral raises for side delt growth
(Larsen 2025), and he concedes dumbbells "still grow side delts fine if trained hard"
([7 things](7-things-helped-shoulders-grow.md), [only 25](only-25-exercises-need.md)). His hypothesis
for the null — the study stopped the cable at the side rather than sweeping across the body — is
explicitly a hypothesis. He still prefers cables; the evidence no longer requires you to.

**Front raises.** [Worst exercises](top-10-worst-exercises.md) (2019) has them at #3 worst;
[shoulders ranked](shoulder-exercises-ranked.md) (2024) names them the worst of the worst. But in
[top 3 shoulder exercises](top-3-shoulder-exercises-3d-delts.md) (2023), **Dr. Mike Israetel's front
delt pick is the EZ-bar front raise at 10–20 reps** with a 4–5 second negative — a rare front-delt
isolation movement, useful when triceps volume is high or the shoulders are irritated from pressing.
That's a guest's view Jeff doesn't share, not a change of mind. The 2016
[lateral delt](train-lateral-delts.md) note also keeps front raises around, just with pinkies up to
bias the side delt.

**The reverse pec deck.** 2017 ([shoulders](most-effective-way-train-shoulders.md)): the standout
rear delt movement, **~90% EMG across three studies**, corroborated in
[boulder rear delts](build-boulder-rear-delts.md) (2019). 2025
([7 amazing exercises](7-amazing-exercises-no-one.md)): the standard version is **"effectively half
reps"** and needs to be done sideways, one arm at a time. Same exercise, reassessed on range rather
than activation.

**Best triceps compound.** 2017 ([train arms](train-arms-growth.md)): **the bench press is the single
best triceps exercise**, and the flat bench beats other angles. 2024: close-grip bench is A tier and
explicitly demoted because **the long head won't grow well from pressing**. The Maeo study landed in
between.

**Best biceps exercise.** 2024 rankings: **Bayesian cable curl #1**, preacher second. 2026: if limited
to one, **the preacher curl**, because it's easier to execute and harder to mess up — the Bayesian
falls to #15. Not a contradiction so much as a different question (best vs most foolproof), but the
headline answers differ.

**The upright row.** 2018 ([lateral raise response](lateral-raise-only-must-exercise.md)): his own pick
for the single most "must-do" movement, because it covers side delts and upper traps at once. 2024:
**B tier as a delt builder**, because the upper traps take over before the delt fibres exhaust. Still
defended as "overhated" and a great yoke builder.

**Whether the glutes or the quads are the largest muscle.**
[Build an amazing butt](build-amazing-butt.md) (2017) says the gluteus maximus is the largest muscle
in the body; [only 25 exercises](only-25-exercises-need.md) (2026) says recent anatomy research
suggests **the quads are** — bigger than glutes or lats. Trivial for programming, but flagged so the
two aren't quoted side by side.

**Full ROM vs constant-tension partials.** The library's general position is that full range wins.
But [the yoke workout](yoked-ultimate-trap-neck-delt-workout.md) cites a 2017 study where mid-range
constant-tension triceps work produced **nearly twice the hypertrophy** of full-range work where
tension was lost at the top — with a loading confound the MASS review flagged. It's consistent with
the narrower rule (constant-tension partials beat full range only on free-weight isolation lifts with
a slack zone), but the note states it far more strongly than that.

## Things to treat carefully

- **The cheat-rep case for lateral raises** ([train lateral delts](train-lateral-delts.md)) rests on
  Arandjelović 2013, which is a **computer simulation**, not subjects performing the exercise. The
  "57.12°/second initial angular velocity" figure is unusable in a gym and he says so.
- **The pinkies-up lateral raise cue** is a genuine risk/reward trade-off with no consensus. His
  11 years without injury is one anecdote.
- **Neck training and sleep apnea**: no evidence in natural lifters; the football-player association
  doesn't separate muscle from fat, and newer evidence points to tongue fat.
- **Brachialis growth changing biceps peak** is mechanistic reasoning, not evidence.
- **Nucleus overload** ([bear mode](go-bear-mode-look-enhanced-naturally.md)) is an untested
  hypothesis, likely confounded by genetics — both parties say so.
- **The one-arm dumbbell row's** clean bill of health is argued from an *absence* of concern among
  other professionals, not positive evidence ([back ranked](back-exercises-ranked.md)).
- **Note-library housekeeping**: `most-scientific-way-train-back-explained.md` is the **2016** video
  and `most-scientific-way-train-back.md` is the **2017** one, so the "-explained" filename is the
  *older* note. `4-unsolved-shoulder-mysteries.md` is questions only — the answers live in
  `4-shoulder-mysteries-resolved.md`. `build-neck.md` is pure anatomy with no sets, reps or
  citations; the program is in `build-thicker-neck-fast.md`. And `hamstrings-tips.md` is a 2016
  shoulder-and-hamstring workout vlog, not a hamstring guide.
