# Spot Reduction: Can You Lose Fat From Specific Body Parts?

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "Can You Lose Bodyfat From Specific Bodyparts? (It Might Be Possible)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XM1JPtF_vdA)) · 2018-06-26 · 8 min
**Topic:** Myths & Evidence

## Bottom line
- Three studies (1971 observational, 2007, 2013) found no evidence of spot reduction; one 2018 study from the University of Rome found strong evidence for it.
- Nippard's verdict: **not busted, but not confirmed** — the data are equivocal and he remains highly sceptical.
- The one condition under which it might work is low-intensity cardio performed immediately after training the target body part. Training the body part alone almost certainly does nothing.
- He does not confidently recommend chasing this. The three pillars of fat loss remain a sustained calorie deficit, progressive resistance training and sufficient protein.
- In his coaching experience, stubborn fat areas are simply the last areas to lean out. Stay in the deficit and they eventually go.

## Why spot reduction shouldn't work mechanistically [00:32]
It feels intuitive that the body would pull fat from directly above the working muscle. But stored fat is triglyceride, and muscle cannot burn triglyceride directly. It must first be broken down into glycerol and free fatty acids, which enter the bloodstream — and the bloodstream circulates the whole body. So fat used to fuel exercise is drawn from general circulation, not from the local depot.

## The evidence [01:33]

**1971 — tennis players (observational).** The first test of the hypothesis. Researchers measured subcutaneous fat thickness on the dominant (swinging) versus non-dominant arm of tennis players. The active arm was more hypertrophied but showed **no difference in subcutaneous fat thickness**. Strike one — but it is observational, so no strong conclusion on its own.

**2007 — single-arm training (interventional).** 104 subjects (45 men, 59 women) trained the non-dominant arm only for 12 weeks with preacher curls, triceps extensions, concentration curls and similar. Arm fat volume was measured by MRI. **No difference in subcutaneous fat volume change between trained and untrained arms** in either sex. Strike two — but very little fat was lost overall, so the study was poorly powered to detect the effect.

**2013 — single-leg training.** A high-volume 12-week single-leg program that *did* produce significant total body fat loss, making it a better test. **No difference in fat mass change between the trained and untrained leg.** Strike three, and this was the scientific consensus for a long time.

**2018 — University of Rome.** 12 weeks, but instead of one limb, subjects were split into an **upper-body-only** and a **lower-body-only** group, and — crucially — every training session was followed by **30 minutes of light cycling**. The upper-body group lost far more arm fat and far less lower-body fat; the lower-body group lost far more leg fat and far less arm fat. Whole-body fat loss was the same in both groups. This is the opposite of the earlier findings.

## The proposed mechanism [04:36]
Covered in MASS Research Review, where Dr Eric Helms suggested that local muscular work increases blood flow to the adjacent fat tissue, increasing mobilisation of those stores. Normally, mobilised fatty acids that aren't immediately burned can simply be re-stored. But low-intensity aerobic exercise predominantly uses fat for fuel, so the cardio done straight afterwards is what actually oxidises the freed fatty acids. Helms also cautioned strongly: it is a single study with a small sample and the result could be a fluke.

## Practical application
- Do not expect crunches, high-rep triceps extensions or similar to strip fat from that area on their own — that part is not supported.
- If you want to try it anyway, the only version with any support is: train the target body part, then do roughly 30 minutes of **low-intensity** cardio immediately afterwards. It costs little and might work.
- Nippard explicitly does not make this a confident recommendation. Prioritise the three pillars: sustained calorie deficit, progressive resistance training, adequate protein.
- Expect stubborn areas to lean out last. Keep dieting and getting leaner and they will follow.

## Caveats & unknowns
- The positive result rests on one study with a small sample; the three negative studies each had their own limitations (observational, or too little total fat loss).
- The negative studies used single-limb designs while the positive one used upper-vs-lower-body splits plus post-workout cardio — so they are not testing quite the same thing, and the cardio component may be the deciding variable.
- Nippard's stated position is uncertainty rather than endorsement: "we just can't say for sure."
