# Cable Glute Kickback: Technique for Isolated Hip Extension

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "Build Bigger Glutes With Perfect Training Technique ft. Stephanie Buttermore (Glute Kickback)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dJa_Nf4zdik)) · 2019-01-22 · 7 min
**Topic:** Technique

## Bottom line
- The kickback is near-pure hip extension, so it isolates the glutes better than compounds but has a low ceiling for loading.
- Train it in the **12-20 rep range**, not heavy.
- Because weight increases run out fast, progress instead through mind-muscle connection, technique mastery, and pump/rep accumulation (add one rep whenever form allows).
- Think "swing back and up in an arc," not "kick" — kicking recruits the quads via knee extension.
- Use a velcro ankle strap, lean the torso forward 45-60 degrees, and brace the upper body against the machine.

## What the movement trains
Hip extension: taking the hip from flexed to straight. Same basic pattern as the hip thrust, deadlift and squat, except the torso leans forward and the leg travels back behind the body instead of the hips driving forward. Some knee extension (quadriceps) also occurs, and because the goal is glute isolation, that component should be consciously minimised.

Because it crosses one joint and uses essentially one muscle group, its capacity for overload is limited compared with every other exercise in the Technique Tuesday series so far.

## Why bother with an isolation movement [02:08]
Two reasons:

1. **Volume without systemic cost.** It lets you accumulate more training volume for a priority body part without adding recovery demand to other muscles or joints. If you want the glutes to grow faster than everything else, heavy compounds alone make that hard — some isolation work is likely necessary.
2. **Activation drill.** A strong mind-muscle connection built on the kickback tends to carry over to heavier, more complex lifts like the hip thrust.

## Progressive overload without adding weight [01:08]
Three avenues, in order of emphasis:
- Improve mind-muscle connection over time.
- Improve/master technique over time.
- Improve the pump over time, including adding reps — one extra rep whenever you can hold good form and the connection.

You can add weight once you top out a rep range (say past 20 reps), but past a point extra load only produces form breakdown; from there, revert to the three avenues above.

## Setup and execution [02:38]
- **Attachment:** use a velcro ankle strap. Other attachments can cause pain or discomfort — a 2005 study by Farina and colleagues (name garbled in the auto-captions as "fariña"; treat the attribution as uncertain) found that pain inhibits muscle excitation, so training through unnecessary pain works against glute development.
- **Position:** hook the ankle, line the leg up with the cable, and lean the torso forward roughly **45-60 degrees**. Standing fully upright loads the glutes only at the bottom of the range; leaning forward gives a more consistent tension curve throughout.
- Brace against the machine frame, lean slightly away from the working leg, knees slightly bent.
- **Concentric cue:** pull the lower glute toward the upper glute. Swing the leg back and up in an arc rather than kicking it. Keep the hamstrings soft with a relaxed knee so the glutes stay the primary hip extensor.
- **Upper glute bias (optional):** swing the weight slightly out laterally. But priority goes to finding the movement path that gives the best mind-muscle connection over forcing an awkward position for a theoretical benefit.
- **Eccentric:** treat the negative like a failed positive — resist the weight actively, feeling the glute stretch under tension, rather than letting the leg swing back down.
- **Tempo:** since reps are high, find a comfortable swinging groove and keep speed identical from rep 1 to rep 15. Don't let the reps get faster and sloppier as fatigue builds.

## Common errors [05:11]
- **Kicking too much.** The signature error. Shifts emphasis to the quads. Fix: focus on squeezing the glute to drive the weight out and up in an arc, not just backward.
- **Too much upper-body movement.** Some is fine, but don't use torso momentum as a counterbalance to swing the leg up. Brace against the frame, stay stable, initiate every rep from the glute.

## Alternatives
Donkey kickbacks set up on a lying leg curl or Smith machine are reasonable, but those machines lock you into a fixed path and remove the freedom to find the movement path that suits your skeleton. Cable kickback is preferred.

## Practical application
- Program as accessory glute volume alongside heavy hip extension work (hip thrust, deadlift, squat), **12-20 reps**.
- Load light enough that the swing-back arc and glute squeeze stay intact; if technique breaks, the weight is too heavy.
- Progress by adding a rep at a time; when you clear ~20 reps cleanly, add a small increment and rebuild.
- Can be used as a pre-lift activation drill before heavy hip thrusts.

## Caveats & unknowns
- The 2005 pain/muscle-excitation citation is garbled in the auto-captions; the finding as stated is that pain inhibits muscle excitation, but the researcher's name should be verified.
- No study is cited comparing the kickback's hypertrophy outcome against other glute exercises — the case for it here is mechanical and practical, not trial-based.
- The lateral-swing upper-glute cue is presented as theoretical, and Nippard explicitly deprioritises it below comfort and connection.
