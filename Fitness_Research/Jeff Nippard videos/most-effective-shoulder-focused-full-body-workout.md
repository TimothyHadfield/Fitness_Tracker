# Shoulder-Focused Full Body Workout (High Frequency Split, Day 5)

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Most Effective Science-Based Shoulder Focused Full Body Workout" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FbWfA_s0XL8)) · 2020-09-16 · 9 min
**Topic:** Exercise Selection

## Bottom line
- Day 5 of a 5-day high-frequency full-body split, with delts prioritised by hitting them first and giving them extra volume.
- The overhead press is **not** redundant with horizontal pressing — side delts get significant activation and rear delts some, plus it's a fundamental movement pattern worth training.
- OHP progression when weight jumps stall: **3x6 → 4x6 → 5x6 at the same weight over three weeks, then back to 3x6 with more weight**.
- Manage fatigue through exercise selection: **stretch-loaded** movements cause more muscle damage, **contracted-position-loaded** movements less. On a high-frequency split, rotate them.
- Quads and glutes get **no direct work** this day — they already have plenty from the other four sessions.

## The session

| Exercise | Sets x reps |
|---|---|
| Barbell overhead press | 3 x 6 |
| Dumbbell lateral raise | 3 x 15 |
| Seated cable row | 3 x 12 |
| Lying leg curl | 3 x 12 |
| Dumbbell concentration curl | 3 x 12 |
| Cable crunch | 4 x 15 |
| Seated calf raise | 4 x 15 |
| Push-ups | 2 x AMRAP |

## Barbell overhead press — 3x6
[00:30] The OHP has been criticised as redundant: it's front-delt dominant, and front delts already get plenty from horizontal pressing. Nippard disagrees. A figure from a study he credits to "Sederbacken and colleagues" (caption spelling, likely Saeterbakken) on the standing barbell press shows that while front delts are most active, **side delts still show significant activation and rear delts some activation**. He also sees a vertical press as one of the few fundamental movement patterns that belongs in any complete program — for injury prevention through overuse avoidance and for raw frontal-plane strength. Dumbbells are a fine substitute if more comfortable; the point is that *some* vertical press should be prioritised on a shoulder day.

**Cues.** Push through the **outside of the hands** rather than the insides. This stops the grip creeping inward and biases shoulder **abduction** — the same reason a dumbbell press up-and-out is harder than up-and-in. The bar is fixed so the hands can't actually travel, but the cue keeps the shoulders in their strongest position through the range. Second cue: elbows tucked to about **45 degrees** at the bottom, then flared out a bit once the bar clears the face, which gets the bar back over the shoulder joint, shortening the moment arm and making the lift more efficient.

**Progression.** Start **3x6**; add one set per week to reach **5x6** at the same weight by week 3; in week 4 return to **3x6** with added weight. Useful specifically for OHP, where advanced lifters hit a wall adding even one rep or a small weight jump week to week — accumulating sets first keeps strength moving.

## Dumbbell lateral raise — 3x15
[03:05] Chosen deliberately for fatigue management. Exercises loading the **stretched position** cause more muscle damage; exercises loading the **contracted position** cause less. The previous session used the **cable lateral raise**, which loads both the bottom stretch and the top contraction. The dumbbell version has near-zero tension in the stretch and maximal tension in the contraction at the top — so it can be pushed much closer to failure for less fatigue cost. He notes this probably doesn't matter much for a lateral raise specifically, but the principle generalises. On a high-frequency split where the same muscles are trained on consecutive days, don't take too many sets to failure and moderate effort, especially when first starting the protocol.

## Seated cable row — 3x12
[04:05] A mind-muscle-connection movement for the back that can be steered trap-dominant or lat-dominant. **For lats:** think about pulling the elbows primarily **down** with elbows tucked to the sides — you may need to drop the weight, but the lat contraction is intense. Leaning slightly forward on the eccentric adds lat stretch (optional; staying upright works, it's just harder to get maximum stretch). **For mid traps:** cue driving the elbows **back** and squeezing the shoulder blades together at the top.

## Lying leg curl — 3x12
[04:36] Along with calves, the only leg work today — no direct quad or glute work, since they got plenty over the other four days. You could swap in glute ham raises for extra glute volume, but deadlifts were day 4, so check the posterior chain is recovered first.

**Common mistake:** letting the pads pop off the ankles, especially near the top, which means you've lost control and are using momentum. Fix: let every other muscle in the leg — including calves — go completely loose and direct all attention to the hamstrings, gradually applying pressure into the pads through the concentric rather than heaving.

## Dumbbell concentration curl — 3x12
[06:09] Elbow pinned against the leg, squeezing the bicep to move the weight. Train both bicep functions: **elbow flexion and supination** — start with a neutral grip at the bottom and supinate by driving through the pinky so the palm faces up at the top. Take a deliberately **loose grip** so the biceps rather than the forearms do the work.

## Cable crunch — 4x15
[06:39] One of the few exercises where you *should* round the lower back — **spinal flexion** is the primary function of the rectus abdominis. Lock the shoulders and arms in place so movement is initiated only from the abs. Don't just bend at the hips; squeeze the abs together like a sponge or an accordion.

## Seated calf raise — 4x15
[07:09] He rotates a bent-leg option (this) with a straight-leg option (day 3) for variation, since they likely emphasise slightly different calf muscles. Day 3 used **4x8** for a mechanical-tension stimulus, so today's higher reps emphasise the **metabolic stress** pathway. Cue: think about **rolling back and forth on the balls of the feet** rather than fixating on getting up on tiptoes — the calf's job is plantarflexing the ankle. Pause at the bottom to dissipate elastic reflex, and finish each rep with a strong squeeze at the top.

## Push-ups — 2 all-out AMRAP sets
[07:40] Finishing the week with a max-effort set is a chance to dig deep, and since a day or two of complete rest follows, the fatigue can't interfere with the next session. Still use good control: standardise reps by touching **nose, chest and stomach** to the floor every rep. Reps will drop on the second set from fatigue — the goal is to add **one rep to both sets** over the remainder of the program.

## Practical application
Run this as the fifth full-body day of the week with delts front-loaded. Track weekly per-muscle volume totals across all five days so you can nudge numbers up for weak points — spreading volume across full-body days is the main advantage of the split, because it maximises per-session performance and recovery.

## Caveats & unknowns
The stretch-vs-contraction damage distinction is offered as a general principle, and he explicitly says it "probably doesn't make a huge difference" for something like a lateral raise. The bent-leg/straight-leg calf rotation rests on "likely" different muscle emphasis. Swapping leg curls for glute ham raises depends on your posterior chain being recovered from the day-4 deadlifts.
