# Training Like a Natural Pro: Alberto Nuñez's Upper-Body Cluster-Set Session

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "How To Train Like A NATURAL PRO | Alberto Nunez Collab" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MCZyYJ_WerQ)) · 2018-04-26 · 20 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- Filmed as an unplanned upper-body session in an unfamiliar hotel-area gym, so the theme is how a natural pro salvages a good workout with bad equipment and poor sleep.
- Cluster sets are the central tool: one hard set, rack the weight, take ~4-5 deep breaths, then keep going for 3-4 mini-sets. High quality volume in very little time.
- Open with a row rather than a press — rows are the easiest movement to set up and progress cleanly when travelling.
- Nuñez favours movements where cheating is structurally impossible (dumbbell seal row) and where the resistance profile never goes slack (kettlebell lateral raise).
- Effort target is roughly 1 rep in reserve, and on pull-ups he argues you can judge reps-in-reserve far more accurately than on most other lifts.

## The session

**1. Dumbbell seal row — 3-4 sets, higher reps (roll into ~4-6 rep territory as fatigue accumulates)**
Chest-supported prone rowing on a raised bench. Nuñez calls it his bread-and-butter and "the cleanest row you'll ever feel": you have to actively look for a way to cheat, so an added rep or added kilo is genuine progress rather than more body English. Second benefit — it spares the torso and lower back, keeping you fresh for squat and deadlift days.

Cues: lead with the scapula, the shoulder blade moves first. Watch head position — people creep the head forward to fake tension across the upper back, which pulls the body toward the bar instead of the bar toward the body. Keep the chin tucked back; with the seal row the bar is right in your face, which naturally encourages the double-chin position and leaves the scapula free to move fully. Setup takes about five minutes if the gym has movable benches.

**2. Slight-incline chest press — cluster sets**
First set of 8-12 reps taken close to failure, then rack, take 4-5 deep breaths, and go again. Each cluster then feels like the last few reps of the first set. Three to four clusters total.

Rationale: on short sleep and unfamiliar equipment, pressing is the first thing to fall apart. Clustering guarantees at least one extended, high-quality effort. Nuñez stops slightly before he actually wants to stop — that, he says, is the sweet spot. Cluster difficulty ramps fast.

**3. Superset: wide-grip pull-ups (clustered) + a short-head triceps movement**
Pull-ups taken to about 1 rep in reserve — Nuñez notes that with pull-ups you can feel exactly how many reps remain in a way you can't with other exercises. First set ~8 controlled reps, then clusters of ~2 reps with a few breaths between.

Grip: Nippard prefers neutral; Nuñez prefers wide, finding it harder but far more lat-dominant. Because the pull-up already loads the long head of the triceps through shoulder extension, the paired triceps movement is chosen with the arms up/out to the side to bias the short head.

**4. Superset: kettlebell lateral raise + kettlebell curl**
Kettlebells are used deliberately. With a dumbbell you eventually reach a near-zero-gravity position at the top where the load stops challenging the delt; with a kettlebell the mass always hangs below the hand, so tension never disappears.

Lateral raise cues:
- Lean forward slightly and raise forward-and-out (a scaption-style path) rather than straight to the sides — going straight out runs you out of room quickly, and the forward angle gives noticeably more range.
- On the way down, stop with the hand roughly above the belly button. The first few inches from the bottom are dominated by elastic recoil from the humerus rotating, so cutting them out keeps you in the loaded mid-range.
- Slight elbow bend — it shortens the lever, and since the deltoid inserts high on the humerus the far end of the arm is dead weight anyway. Same logic as putting the leg-extension pad closer to the knee.
- Wrists slightly in front, machine-like plane of motion. High reps, and Nippard describes it as feeling like a completely different exercise from a machine raise, with the delts also working isometrically to hold the forward lean.

Curls: ~15-20 reps, then a cluster to finish.

## Practical application
- Travelling or stuck with poor equipment? Lead with a row, use cluster sets, and choose exercises that self-limit cheating. You get real volume without the "just chase a pump" default.
- Cluster template: one set of 8-12 near failure → rack → 4-5 breaths → repeat 3-4 times. Works on presses, pull-ups and curls alike.
- Progress the seal row by reps or load and trust the number, because form drift can't inflate it.
- Rotate out of fixed machine planes periodically — the free-weight versions load positions machines can't, which is where the novel soreness comes from.
