# Four Steps To A Bigger Bench Press (Bar Path, Grip, Frequency, Top Sets)

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Fastest Way To Blow Up Your Bench Press (4 Science-Based Steps) + Sample Program" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ptpmRrzRtWQ)) · 2019-10-06 · 8 min
**Topic:** Technique

## Bottom line
- Press the bar **back toward your face and then up** — not straight up. This is what separates elite from novice benchers.
- **Widen your grip** gradually, about one finger-width per workout, toward a max-legal (index fingers on the rings) width.
- Bench **3 days per week** — the sweet spot — arranged as daily undulating periodization (DUP).
- Add **one heavy top set of 2-3 reps at ~90% 1RM, once per week**, before your main work.
- Set up some arch first; without one you're leaving strength on the table.

## Step 1: fix your bar path [00:31]
Unlike the squat and deadlift, where a vertical bar path over midfoot is the goal, the bench press should **not** move straight up.

The biomechanics: benching is mostly horizontal shoulder adduction (pecs and front delts bringing the arm across the body, as in a fly). But unless you're doing a guillotine press to the neck, there's also **shoulder flexion** — raising the arm as in a front raise, just through a shorter range.

Unracked, the bar sits over the shoulder joint, where the front delt has no moment arm to fight. Lowering the bar down and slightly forward (traditional bench) shortens total bar travel and is stronger than lowering straight down to the neck — but it creates a **moment arm between the bar and the shoulder**. Pressing straight up from there keeps that moment arm in place, forcing the front delt to do a partial front raise while you're at the hardest point of the lift.

The fix: **shove the bar back first, then up**, killing the moment arm as fast as possible so that by the sticking point a few inches off the chest the front delts are barely loaded. It also shifts emphasis onto the pecs via horizontal adduction.

Data from **Dr. Thomas McLaughlin** confirms this plays out in reality: most benchers correctly lower the bar down and slightly forward in a smooth arc, but **only elite lifters drove the bar back then up** — novices pressed straight up first and then back.

**How to apply:** film sets from the side; use an app like Iron Path or Dartfish to track the path. His cue: think about pushing the floor away while simultaneously driving the bar back and up off the chest. It feels awkward at first.

## Step 2: widen your grip [04:03]
Ease your grip out by roughly **one finger-width per workout over the next few weeks**. Most of the world's top bench pressers use a max-legal grip with index fingers on the rings. It won't suit everyone, but wider is usually stronger for three reasons:

1. Shorter range of motion.
2. The midpoint and lockout get easier because the pecs aren't as contracted — more stretch on the pecs means more forceful contraction through the range.
3. Easier to hold a tight upper back; a wide grip forces more scapular retraction and depression, giving a more stable base.

## Step 3: bench more often [05:04]
Strength is a skill — practice it more. But practicing bad habits doesn't help, which is why technique came first.

He has personally run 4-5x/week bench programs, but those demand tightly controlled per-session volume and intensity to avoid overtraining. For most people, **3 days per week is the sweet spot**, set up as **daily undulating periodization** targeting a different quality each day. A DUP study from **Dr. Mike Zourdos** showed this design increased 1RM strength ([Zourdos 2016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/26332783)).

Sample week:
- **Day 1 — hypertrophy:** sets of 8
- **Day 2 — power:** lighter speed work
- **Day 3 — strength:** heavy sets of 5

## Step 4: weekly heavy top set [06:06]
Optionally add a heavy top set before the hypertrophy work on day 1: **~90% of 1RM for 2-3 reps**, then back off to that day's main work. **Once per week only**, ideally before your lightest training day.

Purpose: get comfortable handling heavy weight regularly and build confidence without frying recovery. Strength is specific — you need heavy weight in your hands often — but don't max out regularly on these.

## Practical application
- Establish at least a moderate arch (a moderate arch is fine, especially for hypertrophy goals).
- Film from the side; consciously press **back, then up**.
- Move the grip out one finger-width per session toward max-legal width, as comfort allows.
- Bench 3x/week: 8s (hypertrophy) / speed work (power) / 5s (strength).
- One weekly top set of 2-3 reps at ~90% 1RM before the hypertrophy day.

## Caveats & unknowns
Max-legal grip width isn't comfortable or appropriate for everyone — shoulder comfort limits it. Very high bench frequency (4-5x/week) requires careful volume and intensity management. Much of the video's material is drawn from the Stronger By Science bench press guide.
