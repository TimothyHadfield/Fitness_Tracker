# Pull-Ups for Back Width: Optimal Technique and Progression

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Best Way To Do Pull Ups For A Wide Back (Optimal Training Technique)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hdc7Mw6BIEE)) · 2019-01-01 · 8 min
**Topic:** Technique

## Bottom line
- Load pull-ups **heavy, in the 5-10 rep range** — the overload potential is high (elite lifters use bodyweight plus 100+ lb).
- Grip: **pronated, double overhand, ~1.5x shoulder width**. Wider than that is a bad idea — shorter ROM, harder loading, wrist pain.
- Keep one grip for **at least 8 weeks** and progress by adding reps or weight before changing anything.
- Full range every rep: near dead hang at the bottom, chin over bar (upper chest touching) at the top, elbows pulled in to the sides.
- Pull-ups beat lat pulldowns for total musculature; a 2013 study found **equal lat activation but more biceps and spinal erector activation on the pull-up**.

## Muscles involved
Primary: **lats, rhomboids and teres** muscles via shoulder adduction (bringing the arm down to the side). **Brachialis, biceps and brachioradialis** handle elbow flexion. **Lower traps** produce scapular depression. The **rectus abdominis** works isometrically, resisting spinal extension so the torso doesn't bend backward through the range.

## Why the pull-up [00:37]
- **More total musculature than the lat pulldown.** Doma and colleagues (2013 — name uncertain in the auto-captions) found no significant difference in lat activation between pulldown and pull-up, but the pull-up came out ahead for the **biceps** and the **spinal erectors**.
- **High overload potential.** It is not a bodyweight-only exercise; incrementally adding external load strengthens and grows all the pulling muscles.
- **Better for teres major and minor** than chin-ups or rows, which are primarily shoulder-extension based.

## Loading and progression [01:38]
Rep range: **5-10, heavier end**. Elite standard: bodyweight plus upwards of 100 lb added.

Progression ladder for people who can't yet do 5:
1. Use an **assisted pull-up machine** to master technique through a full range — nothing wrong with this.
2. Progressively reduce the machine's assistance until you reach **5 bodyweight reps**.
3. Add reps until **10 bodyweight reps**.
4. Then add external load via a weighted belt or a dumbbell held between the legs by a partner.

Master full-ROM technique before adding any load.

## Setup [02:38]
- Use a straight bar or a kinked bar, ideally high enough to hang without the feet touching the floor.
- **Don't jump to the bar** — jumping produces uneven, inconsistent grip width and loading problems. Use a bench or aerobic riser placed **1-2 feet behind the bar** so your feet don't catch it on the way down.
- **Grip width:** pronated double overhand at roughly **1.5x shoulder width**. Alternative method: make a 90-degree angle between upper arm and forearm, raise the arm straight up, and grip where the hands land.
- A **2016 Massey University study** compared muscle activation across four pull-up variations and found **no significant difference between them**, except that the **pronated wide grip was slightly better for the mid traps**. Underhand or narrow grips aren't wrong, but they may let the biceps take over for some lifters. Going wider than 1.5x shoulder width is counterproductive.
- **Consistency beats variety:** keep the same grip for at least 8 weeks, progressing by reps or load without changing form. After ~8 weeks you can switch grip to hit a slightly different angle and repeat the overload process.
- **Straps** are fine, especially if they let you feel the back better — but do at least one heavy back movement per week without straps so grip strength and forearm size aren't compromised.

## Execution [04:08]
- Start from a **dead hang**. Initiate by **depressing the shoulder blades**; cue: *pull your elbows into your back pocket*.
- Simultaneously **raise the chest slightly** toward the bar — a slight puff only. Don't exaggerate it into what looks like an inverted row.
- Keep pulling the elbows **down and in** until they're as close to your sides as possible. Minimum standard: chin over the bar, which for most people means the **upper chest touches the bar**.
- **Keep the hips extended** to prevent kipping (swinging the legs forward or up). Legs either dead straight or crossed and tucked behind — either way, **squeeze the glutes**.
- **Eccentric:** lower under control, letting the elbows travel up and out. You should feel the lat actively stretching under tension. Cue: think about **pulling the bar apart** on both the concentric and eccentric to feel the lats better.
- **Attentional focus:** with heavy low-rep work, an **external focus** on simply executing the movement is best for most people. On higher-rep sets, an internal focus on squeezing and activating the lats works well too. Both can be effective.

## Common errors [05:42]
1. **Inadequate range of motion** — cutting short at both ends. You don't need a complete dead hang (a slight elbow bend is fine), but you must go all the way up, leading with the chest, finishing with elbows pulled in. If you can't get extra reps at full ROM, don't do them.
2. **Lack of full-body rigidity** — letting the torso and legs go slack as reps get hard, which invites cheating and momentum. Glutes flexed, hips extended, no swinging.

## Alternative: wide-grip lat pulldown [06:13]
Reasonable substitute, especially for beginners — lat activation is virtually identical even though the pull-up edges it out for the back overall. Pull the elbows down and in, touch the bar to the upper chest, let the lats stretch on the negative. But don't default to it just because it's easier: seated, the core is far less involved, and avoiding hard fundamental movements is a bad habit to build.

## Practical application
- Program pull-ups as the primary vertical pull, **5-10 reps**, weighted once 10 clean bodyweight reps are in the bank.
- Fix grip for an 8-week block; log reps and added load; only then change grip.
- Set up from a riser 1-2 feet behind the bar, never a jump.
- Every rep: shoulder blades down first, chest to bar, glutes tight, controlled negative to a near dead hang.

## Caveats & unknowns
- The "Doma 2013" attribution comes from auto-captions and may be misspelled — verify before citing.
- The Massey 2016 study found only a mid-trap difference between grips, so grip choice matters less than consistency and ROM.
- These are EMG/activation findings, not direct hypertrophy trials.
