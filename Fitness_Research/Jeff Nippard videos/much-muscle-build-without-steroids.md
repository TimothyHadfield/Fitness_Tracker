# How Much Muscle You Can Build Naturally vs On Steroids

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "How Much Muscle Can You Build With & Without Steroids?" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VD9p9tEP9RE)) · 2023-07-31 · 13 min
**Topic:** PEDs

## Bottom line
- Natural men top out after gaining roughly 20-40 lb (9-18 kg) of muscle; natural women roughly 12-24 lb. Most of it arrives in the first five years.
- Steroids roughly double or triple that. A moderate dose adds another 20-40 lb; a high dose plus good genetics can add 20-40 lb more on top.
- In Bhasin's 1996 trial, 600 mg/week of testosterone with no training beat natural training: 7 lb of muscle in 10 weeks vs 4.4 lb.
- Maxing out natural potential takes 5-10 years of consistent hard training plus a moderate surplus and adequate protein — 5-15 sets per muscle per week, 0.7 g protein per lb (1.6 g/kg).
- Do not judge training advice by how big the person giving it is; that comparison is confounded by drugs and genetics.

## The natural ceiling [00:30]
Nippard's own curve, as a Canadian natural bodybuilding national gold medalist training 15 years at 5'5" (160 lb lean, 180 lb bulked): ~20 lb of muscle in the first five years, ~5 lb in the next five, 1-2 lb in the five after that — 27 lb (12 kg) total. Faster early, asymptotic later.

Research puts men's natural maximum at 20-40 lb (9-18 kg) of gained muscle and women's at 12-24 lb. Advanced training and diet strategies can nudge the ceiling, but only marginally.

**What "doing things right" means:** train hard and regularly, approaching failure; consistent technique; enough volume (5-15 sets per muscle per week is a good ballpark); a variety of exercises; add weight or reps over time. On the diet side: a moderate caloric surplus for at least half your lifting career, and at least 0.7 g protein per lb bodyweight (1.6 g/kg). Do that for 5-10 years and you'll almost certainly gain 20-40 lb of muscle.

## Real-world natural examples [02:00]
- **Alex Leonidas** — ~40 lb of muscle in 13 years at 5'6", 120 lb to 160 lb at similar body fat. He also ran extended bulks up to 200 lb at ~20% body fat, so the 40 lb did not go on lean. Leonidas argues that time in a surplus at a higher body fat is required to max out natural potential.
- **Jeffrey Verity Scofield** — ~43 lb across 10 years at 6'0", 150 lb to 193 lb at similar body fat, peaking at 224 lb during bulks before cutting back down.

**Pre-steroid era proof [03:02]:** anabolic steroids were not isolated and synthesized until the early 1930s and not injectable until 1935, so strongmen and bodybuilders before that date were natural as a matter of historical fact — and some were extremely muscular and lean. That said, they almost certainly do not represent the natural ceiling, since training and nutrition have improved and far more people compete now.

## What the steroid studies show

**Bhasin et al., 1996 [04:04]** — 43 subjects, four groups, 10 weeks, 600 mg testosterone per week:
- No training, no steroids: negligible
- Trained natural: **+4.4 lb** muscle
- Trained + steroids: **+13.4 lb** — more than triple the natural trained group
- Steroids, no training at all: **+7 lb** — over 1.5x the natural trained group, without lifting

**The 2001 dose-response follow-up [06:34]** — same research group, 61 subjects, five doses (25, 50, 125, 300 or 600 mg/week) for 20 weeks, no lifting. Muscle gain scaled with dose: the lowest dose added just under 1 lb; the 600 mg group added roughly **20 lb in 20 weeks — one pound of muscle per week, on average, without training.**

Individual response varied enormously in that trial: the worst responder gained 9 lb over 20 weeks, the best 31 lb.

## Dosing context [05:34]
From an anonymous anabolics expert Nippard interviewed:
- **100 mg/week** — representative of therapeutic hormone replacement
- **150-200 mg/week** — "sports TRT": borderline supraphysiological free testosterone perpetually, while total T looks normal on paper
- **300+ mg/week** — clearly enhanced territory
- **500-600 mg/week** — entry-level bodybuilder cycle
- **1,000 mg/week minimum, up to 4,000-5,000 mg/week** — competitive professional level

So the 600 mg used in the famous studies is high by TRT standards but moderate-to-low against high-level competitive bodybuilding.

## Real-world enhanced examples [08:06]
- **Shaun Clarida** — reigning 212 Mr. Olympia. Before turning pro he competed at the WNBF, the most stringently drug-tested event in the world, weighing 130 lb on stage at 5'2". Rough math suggests he added ~20 lb naturally (110 to 130 lb) while already training extremely hard (900 lb leg press reps). After enhancing he added another **60 lb — three times what he built naturally.**
- **Chris Bumstead** — reigning Classic Physique Mr. Olympia. Started at 14 weighing 170 lb at 6'1", reached 225 lb by 19 while natural (a 55 lb increase; Nippard believes the before/after images predate his documented first cycle). Enhanced, he now sits at 264 lb off-season at similar body fat — about **40 lb added**. Notably he built most of his mass naturally, which suggests he is not running huge doses and could grow considerably more by raising them. Mostly this illustrates the power of genetics.

## The genetics point [10:10]
Response to natural training varies widely — some gain very little, some a great deal, most land in the middle — and response to anabolics varies just as much. The 2001 study's spread (9 lb to 31 lb in 20 weeks without lifting) makes the point.

## Side effects [11:11]
From the NIH list: well-established elevated risk of high blood pressure, blood clots, heart attack, stroke and artery damage in regular users; plus gynecomastia, balding, acne, abscesses and oily skin. Moderate controlled dosing with regular hormonal monitoring by a physician mitigates some of this, but it remains a health gamble.

## Practical application
If you are considering enhancing, max out your natural potential first — that process forces you to learn how your body responds to the training and nutrition levers, and buys you time for an informed decision. Natty maxing generally is not possible before at least 10 years of hard, smart training, so he suggests a decade under the bar before even considering it. If health is the priority, stay natural unless a medical professional recommends hormonal treatment.

## Caveats & unknowns
Nippard frames this as neither pro- nor anti-steroid — he thinks people should be able to do what they want with their own bodies, provided they understand the tradeoff. The natty status of any modern natural bodybuilder will always attract skepticism given steroid availability. The Bumstead pre-cycle timeline rests on coach statements and photo dating, not hard proof.
