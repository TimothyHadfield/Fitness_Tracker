# Cable Fly Technique for Isolating the Chest (Upper Pec Focus)

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Best Way To Isolate The Chest For Growth (Upper Chest Focus)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-EIhKMDSjBY)) · 2019-04-16 · 6 min
**Topic:** Technique

## Bottom line
- The best chest builder is still a big bench press; if you add one accessory, make it a weighted dip. Flies are for **extra chest volume at low fatigue cost** when the pecs lag or are disproportionately developed.
- If you pick one fly, pick the **cable fly** — it's the only version that lets you add both shoulder flexion (upper pec) and internal rotation (a lesser-known pec function).
- **Set cables low** to bias the upper chest; arms out at roughly a 15-30 degree angle at the start.
- Train flies **light and high-rep: 15-20 reps**, with scapulae retracted and depressed throughout.
- Most common error is letting the triceps assist by changing the elbow angle instead of controlling the weight with the pecs.

## Why a fly, and why cables

A fly is primarily **horizontal shoulder adduction** — bringing the arm across the body — the same basic pattern as a bench or dumbbell press. The difference is that the **elbow angle stays essentially constant**, taking the triceps out and isolating the pecs. A press flexes and extends the elbow under load, which recruits more total muscle mass but is harder to recover from.

**Upper chest:** the clavicular head of the pec assists **shoulder flexion** (raising the arm forward, as in a front raise). Adding a flexion component biases it — which is also why an incline bench angle hits upper pec fibres better.

**Internal rotation:** a less appreciated pec function. Put a hand on your chest and rotate the arm inward and you'll feel the pec contract. Cables let you use it; dumbbells and the pec deck largely don't.

**Against dumbbells:** peak tension comes at the bottom where torque and stretch are greatest, then falls progressively through the concentric until there is basically **zero tension at the top**. A partial fix is stopping short of full adduction to hold a constant-tension mid-range groove, but you then lose the peak contraction.

**Against the pec deck:** the resistance curve is more consistent, but the shoulder is locked into pure adduction — no shoulder flexion for the upper pecs, and no free internal/external rotation.

## Cable fly setup and execution [02:37]

1. Set cables **low**. Grab handles so arms are out at about **15-30 degrees**.
2. Step forward one or two steps until you feel a slight pec stretch; plant a stable stance — a **staggered stance** works well for balance.
3. Before the concentric, **retract and depress the scapulae** exactly as you would benching. Keeping the scapulae back produces a far stronger pec contraction than letting the back round forward, which slackens the pec.
4. **Externally rotate** the shoulders first — this puts the pec in its most lengthened position.
5. Draw both cables up and across the body, thinking about **pulling the insides of your elbows toward each other**, internally rotating as you go, finishing with **thumbs facing one another** at the top.
6. Reverse under control on the negative, letting the pecs stretch while externally rotating back.

## Loading and creative variations [03:39]

- **15-20 reps** is the usual zone — single-joint, pump- and mind-muscle-focused work.
- **Cable height sequence:** cables high for set 1 (more lower pec), middle for set 2 (mid pec), low for set 3 (upper chest).
- **Cable fly 21s:** 7 reps in the top half of the range, 7 in the bottom half to the stretched position, 7 full range. Reserve this for the **end of a workout** for extra metabolic stress so it doesn't interfere with heavier pressing volume.

## Common errors [04:41]

- **Not controlling the movement** — the most frequent fault. People bend and extend the elbows to muscle the weight down instead of squeezing the pecs. The arms needn't be locked straight, but the elbow angle should be identical top to bottom.
- **Losing scapular retraction.** Shoulders rolling forward weakens pec recruitment noticeably and can cause shoulder pain that's avoided simply by locking the upper back into position first.

## Practical application
- Bench and dip drive chest size; add cable flies only to fill a volume or weak-point gap, placed at the end of the session.
- 3 sets, 15-20 reps, cables set low for upper chest, scapulae locked, external rotation at the stretch and internal rotation at the finish.
- Rotate cable height across sets to vary emphasis; save 21s for a finisher.
