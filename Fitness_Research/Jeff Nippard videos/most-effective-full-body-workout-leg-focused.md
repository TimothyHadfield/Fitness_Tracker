# Leg-Focused Full Body Workout (High Frequency Series, Day 4)

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Most Effective Full Body Workout: Leg Focused" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tQ2LSSP_0GQ)) · 2020-07-15 · 10 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- Session layout: reset deadlift 3x5 @ 75% 1RM, weighted dip 3x8, leg extension 3x20, unilateral lat pulldown 3x12/side, then a giant set of rope face pull + overhead cable triceps extension + Egyptian lateral raise, 3x15 each.
- On a high-frequency full body plan, deadlift progression alternates week to week: sets of 5 on odd weeks, sets of 2 on even weeks, with linear load increases within each rep scheme.
- Reset the bar fully on the floor between deadlift reps — touch-and-go uses plate bounce as assistance.
- Deliberately separate leg exercises with an upper body movement so the quads are fresher for isolation work.
- Isolation lifts get more benefit than usual on full body days because they aren't buried at the end of a brutal leg day.

## Deadlift (3x5 @ 75%)
[00:30] "Reset deadlift" has two meanings. A full reset — hands off the bar, stand, rebrace each rep — helps lifters whose first rep is noticeably harder than reps two and three, i.e. those with setup problems. Nippard's own usage is looser: just bring the bar to a dead stop on the floor each rep. He rates the deadlift highly for hypertrophy of the whole posterior chain, traps down to hamstrings, and thinks a full body program without one is incomplete if you can pull safely.

**Sumo vs conventional.** Differences are small. He leans sumo on full body work because research shows it is slightly more quad dominant and slightly less lower-back dominant, which buys quad stimulus without piling recovery cost onto the lower back — a running concern across this series. An in-house analysis of the 2018 and 2019 World Powerlifting Championships by The Strength Athlete coaching team found stance preference tracks body size: lighter lifters favour sumo, heavier lifters conventional, clearly in men, less clearly in women. Head coach Bryce Lewis suggested weight classes are height classes in disguise — shorter lifters have shorter femurs and a more vertical back position, which suits sumo. Practical advice: test both, use the one you're stronger with, and switch occasionally to work weak points.

**Lat cue.** Citing an article by Greg Nuckols: the lats don't attach to the upper thoracic vertebrae at all, so they cannot be what keeps the upper back from rounding. What they do is keep the bar close to the body, shortening the moment arm and reducing external torque on glutes, hamstrings and upper back. Cue: pull the bar toward your shins, like a pullover against the bar.

**Progression.** Sets of 5 build volume and technique; heavy doubles build maximal strength largely through neural adaptation.

## Weighted dip (3x8)
Shoulder-friendly setup: shoulder blades retracted and depressed as on a bench press, and limit range of motion to what feels comfortable — about 90 degrees of elbow bend is enough to load chest, delts and triceps without over-stressing the joint. Dips are commonly written off as lower-chest only. A 2010 solo EMG experiment by Bret Contreras found the dip produced higher peak upper-chest activation than an incline press with 225 lb. Lower pec was still the most active region within the dip itself, but the movement hits the whole pec well. Accentuate it by leaning forward roughly 30 degrees and driving the hands down into the handles as if pressing, keeping posture consistent through the range.

## Leg extension (3x20)
Cue: before the first rep, keep glutes, hamstrings and calves as loose as possible while flexing the quads hard, then squeeze the quads to move the weight rather than kicking it out. Keep flexing the quads on the eccentric. Done this way the burn may force you to stop earlier than usual with the same load — that's acceptable. The aim here is a strong mind-muscle connection producing high tension without needing heavy loading. Research supports combining exercises for overall quad development rather than relying on squats alone.

## Unilateral lat pulldown (3x12/side)
Most full body programs go heavily compound and minimalistic. That saves time but can under-supply loading variety and repeatedly tax the same joints in the same way — a real risk at high frequency. Unilateral work breaks that pattern and builds the mind-muscle connection on the non-dominant side, which bilateral lifts tend to let coast into asymmetry. Technique: combine shoulder extension and adduction by driving the elbow down *and* in, covering both primary lat functions in one movement.

## Finishing giant set (3x15 each)
- **Rope face pull.** Two setups: a mid-trap version (drive elbows straight back, squeeze shoulder blades, like a high row) and a rear-delt/rotator-cuff version (externally rotate at the shoulder, pull the rope to your forehead, think rear double biceps pose). Because the program already has plenty of rowing, he uses the rear-delt version.
- **Cable overhead triceps extension.** Rope, both arms at once to save time (unilateral with the cable end also works). The cable's more consistent tension curve lets you allow the triceps further back for extra stretch at the bottom; squeeze to full elbow extension.
- **Egyptian lateral raise.** Cable dropped to the lowest setting, D-handle, cable run between the legs. Unlike a dumbbell lateral raise — where he cuts the bottom range short since there's no active tension there — the cable keeps tension on the delt throughout, so let it travel down between the legs for a genuine stretch.

## Practical application
Run the session in order as listed. Alternate deadlift rep schemes weekly (5s / 2s) with small linear load jumps. Keep at least one upper body exercise between leg exercises. Push isolation work with intent — squeeze rather than count.

## Caveats & unknowns
Sumo vs conventional hypertrophy differences are described as small; stance choice is individual and best settled by testing. The Contreras EMG work was a single solo experiment, and EMG activation is not a direct measure of growth. The whole setup assumes familiarity with his high-frequency full body rationale.
