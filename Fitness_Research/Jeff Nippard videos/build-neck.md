# Neck Anatomy and How to Train It

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "Can You Build Your Neck? | Neck Hypertrophy 101" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4vnoeOARGW4)) · 2016-02-21 · 6 min
**Topic:** Exercise Selection

## Bottom line
- **The neck is just another muscle.** Train it and it hypertrophies; don't and it won't. That's Nippard's whole premise.
- Baseline for the experiment: his neck measured **13.5 inches**; his arms were around **16 inches**. Target: **~15 inches over about 6 months** of direct neck training. He is openly unsure it's achievable.
- The old gym-lore heuristic he's working from: **neck and biceps should measure about the same**.
- Only **four movement patterns** matter for training the neck: **neck flexion, lateral flexion, neck extension, and scapular/clavicular elevation (shrugging)**.
- Most of the small anterior neck muscles (the hyoid group) contribute little force and can be ignored for hypertrophy purposes.

## Anterior neck: sternocleidomastoid [01:06]
The largest neck muscle and the main contributor to forward flexion of the neck. Its name maps its anatomy: it **originates at the sternum and clavicle**, runs up the side of the neck, and **inserts at the mastoid process** — the bony protuberance at the back and side of the skull. It fans out toward the clavicle at its lower end.

Because it runs up the *side* of the neck rather than straight up the front, shortening it produces **both forward flexion and lateral flexion**. So to hypertrophy it, train **neck flexion and lateral flexion**.

## Lateral flexion: the scalenes [02:38]
Three scalene muscles, sitting deep to the SCM. They **originate on the cervical vertebrae** (upper spine) and **insert on the first and second ribs**. Contracting, they **assist lateral flexion** — so they're covered by the same lateral flexion work as the SCM.

## The muscles he deliberately ignores [03:08]
A group of small muscles running up and down the front of the neck: the **sternohyoid** (sternum to hyoid bone), the **thyrohyoid**, and the **omohyoid**. These are **hyoid depressors** and contribute little force to forward flexion, so Nippard excludes them from training consideration entirely.

## Posterior neck: upper trapezius [03:39]
The main muscle for the back of the neck. The trapezius is fan-shaped, **originating at the occipital bone** at the back of the skull, with fibres running down and out to **insert on the clavicle and scapula**.

For the neck specifically, only the **upper trap fibres** matter — those running from the occipital bone out laterally to the clavicle and scapula. Shortening them brings origin toward insertion, which **extends or tilts the neck back**.

Two training implications:
1. **Neck extension**
2. **Clavicular/scapular elevation — i.e. shrugging**

## The capitis muscles [04:41]
Two more posterior muscles, both originating on the cervical vertebrae:
- **Semispinalis capitis** — runs **straight up** the neck and inserts at the base of the occipital bone. Pure **neck extension**.
- **Splenius capitis** — runs **up and out to the side**, inserting at the mastoid process of the temporal bone. Contributes **neck extension plus neck rotation**.

## Practical application
- Cover four patterns: **neck flexion**, **lateral flexion** (both sides), **neck extension**, and **shrugs**.
- Flexion and lateral flexion hit the SCM (with scalenes assisting laterally); extension hits the capitis muscles and upper traps; shrugging adds upper trap work.
- If you want a bigger neck, you have to **train it directly** — the argument is simply that no direct work means no stimulus.
- Neck rotation is a function of splenius capitis but he doesn't prescribe rotation work; extension work covers it.

## Caveats & unknowns
- This is a **pure anatomy video**. It contains **no sets, reps, loads, frequency, exercise names, or study citations** — the anatomy is the entire content, and the actual program was sold separately as a download.
- The "neck should match your biceps" rule is **gym lore Nippard reports hearing**, not evidence. He offers no source.
- The video is framed as the **setup for a 6-month self-experiment (n=1)**, and he says outright he doesn't know whether the 15-inch target is possible. **No follow-up results are in this video.**
- Auto-caption garbling of anatomical terms was heavy ("stero CL mastoid", "spenis capitus", "master wave process"); names above are the standard corrected spellings.
- No discussion of safety, contraindications, loading progression, or neck-training risk.
