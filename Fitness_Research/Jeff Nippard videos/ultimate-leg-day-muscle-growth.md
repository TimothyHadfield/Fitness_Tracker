# Ultimate Science-Based Leg Day (PPL Series): Full Session Breakdown

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Ultimate Science-Based Leg Day For Muscle Growth (2023)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H6mRkx1x77k)) · 2023-02-21 · 11 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- Squat strength is trained minimalistically — **one hard top set of 2-4 reps at 85-90% 1RM, RPE 8-9, once per week** — then two back-off sets for hypertrophy.
- Six lower-body exercises cover quads, hamstrings, glutes and calves, plus one direct ab movement.
- Seated leg curls beat lying leg curls (a 2021 12-week study found **~56% more hamstring growth**), because they load the hamstrings in a longer position. Lean forward to accentuate it.
- Foot rotation genuinely changes which part of the calf grows — a 2020 study (22 subjects, 9 weeks) confirmed it with direct measurement, not just EMG.
- Abs should be **progressively overloaded with weight** like any other muscle, not blasted with endless circuits.

## The session

**Warm-up.** 5-10 minutes on treadmill or StairMaster (more important on leg day since it is half the body), then ~2 minutes of dynamic stretches.

**1. Back squat — 1 top set of 2-4 reps.** Pyramid up over 4-5 warm-up sets to a working weight of 85-90% 1RM. Example: a 405 lb max means roughly 345-365 lb for the top set. This should be RPE 8-9, not a max-effort grinder — no more than 1-2 reps left, but since it is the only heavy set it must be genuinely hard.

*Substitutions:* if you only care about hypertrophy, can't squat, or don't want to, swap in **hack squats** (first choice) or **Bulgarian split squats** (second choice) and raise the rep range to **4-6** instead of 2-4.

**2. Paused squat — 2 sets of 5 reps.** Drop to about **75% of the top-set weight** (365 lb top set → ~275 lb). Big breath at the top, sit back and down between the legs, hold the breath, pause 1-2 seconds in the hole, then drive up explosively with the bar tracking in a straight line over mid-foot.

Why pauses: the pause kills stored elastic energy in the tendons, so quads and glutes must overcome the load from a dead stop. Five paused reps are far harder than five normal ones, meaning **the same hypertrophic stimulus from lighter loads and less joint/systemic stress**. Pauses also guarantee consistent depth and reinforce good technique for heavier work.

**3. Barbell Romanian deadlift — 3 sets of 8-10 reps.** Unrack, step back 2-3 steps, push the hips back as the bar drops, and reverse once the bar is between just-below-knee and mid-shin — or earlier if the lower back starts to round. Chest up, hips forward, slight knee bend but a roughly vertical shin, bar tight to the legs over mid-foot.

*"I only feel it in my lower back."* Usually the bar is drifting forward, forcing the low back to pull it back in — keep the path strictly vertical. A belt can help: EMG shows a belt doesn't meaningfully reduce lower-back muscle activity, but Nippard finds it redirects attention to the hamstrings and reduces the uncomfortable low-back pump. The erectors must contract on RDLs regardless, so you can shift emphasis but never remove them.

*Hamstrings vs glutes.* For glutes: think about squeezing the glutes to move the weight, optionally squeezing them together at lockout — treat it almost like a vertical hip thrust. For hamstrings: **cut out the top 10-25% of the lift entirely**, keeping emphasis on the stretched bottom half, and optionally pause at the bottom.

**4. Dumbbell walking lunge — 2 sets of 10 reps per leg** (20 strides per set). Three errors to avoid:
- **Cutting depth.** The rear knee should softly touch the floor on every rep; depth usually collapses late in the set. Consistent reps beat an arbitrary rep count of shortened ones.
- **Free-falling on the negative.** Control and resist the eccentric like any hypertrophy movement — rushing it discards the most important part of the lift.
- **Letting grip cap the load.** Most people go far too light and stop at fatigue rather than near failure. Use straps; you are not training forearms here.

**5. Seated leg curl — 3 sets of 10-12 reps.** [06:12] The 2021 12-week study ([Maeo 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33009197/)) found seated curls produced **~56% more growth than lying curls**, most likely because the seated position loads the hamstrings at a longer muscle length. Sit far enough forward that you feel a real stretch before the set starts — a seat set too far back throws that advantage away; raise the seat or lean forward over the machine until you feel passive tension.

Because the hamstrings cross both hip and knee, foot rotation matters: EMG shows **toes pointed in → more inner hamstring, toes pointed out → more outer hamstring** ([Beuchat 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31277009/)). EMG has never been validated as predictive of long-term hypertrophy, so treat this as experimentation. With three sets: set 1 toes slightly out, set 2 slightly in, set 3 straight ahead. If one position clearly feels better, use it; drop any that feel awkward.

**6. Leg press toe press (calves) — 4 sets of 10-12 reps.** Placed here so you can sit down and rest the back and hips after heavy leg work. [08:46] A **2020 study, 22 subjects, 9 weeks** ([Nunes 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32735428/)), compared toes-out, toes-in and straight-ahead calf training and measured growth directly: **toes out grew the medial (inner) calf more, toes in grew the lateral (outer) calf more, straight ahead grew both evenly.** So point toes *in* for outer calves, *out* for inner calves. Across four sets: set 1 out, set 2 in, sets 3-4 straight. If a position is awkward or badly limits load, use the strongest and most comfortable one.

**7. Decline plate crunch — 3 sets of 10-12 reps.** Abs go on leg day because most ab work also involves the hip flexors, already warm. Hold a plate to the chest and squeeze the abs together, letting the lower back round — do **not** just hinge at the hips, which mostly trains hip flexors. Log weight and reps and add one or the other each session.

## Practical application
Run it in order: squat 1×2-4 → paused squat 2×5 → RDL 3×8-10 → walking lunge 2×10/leg → seated leg curl 3×10-12 → leg press calf raise 4×10-12 → decline plate crunch 3×10-12. Strap up for lunges. Use the top-set weight to set back-off loads at 75%. Rotate foot position across sets on curls and calves.

## Caveats & unknowns
- EMG-based foot-rotation advice for the hamstrings is unproven for hypertrophy; the calf version has direct long-term evidence and can be stated with more confidence.
- The glute squeeze at RDL lockout probably does little mechanically — it is a cue that helps some people find the muscle.
- Substituting hack squats or Bulgarians is fine only if strength on the squat is not a goal.
