# Mini-Cutting: When It Helps, When It Wrecks Your Long-Term Progress

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "Minicutting 101" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U4J3oJXf_bM)) · 2015-05-15 · 20 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- A mini cut is a short deficit phase — roughly **2 to 8 weeks** — as opposed to a full contest prep of **24 to 36 weeks**.
- Two situations where you should *not* mini cut: **within 6 months of a competition**, and **when you're already under 10% body fat**.
- Three legitimate reasons to run one: to restore a dead appetite after a long bulk, to keep offseason body fat inside a workable range, or simply to get lean for summer.
- Deficit size depends on the goal: **20-25% below maintenance for 2-6 weeks** to reset appetite or for the leanness-struggler, **10-15% below maintenance for 5-10 weeks** for the easy-gainer trimming an offseason.
- Nippard's own strongest gains came from **two straight years without any mini cut** — young/newer lifters chronically mini-cutting are most at risk of stalling.

## When not to mini cut

**After a show.** Post-competition binges are near-universal among competitors and the weight gained in that first week (people report 10, 30, even 40 lb) is largely water, not fat. Damage-controlling it with a mini cut sets up a binge → mini cut → binge yo-yo for the rest of the offseason. Instead: raise water intake, drop calories back to maintenance, and fix the binging behaviour. The weight re-equilibrates on its own. He sets a rule of thumb: **no mini cut within 6 months of a show.** That window is when the body is most primed for anabolism — good pumps, rising strength, carbs finally back in — and should be spent adding tissue.

**Already under 10% body fat.** Below that, you're better off in a surplus. If you're at 8%, eat your way up to 10-12%, then run a mini cut of about a month, then build back. Staying ultra-lean permanently is fine *if* calories are high, gym progress is continuing and strength isn't suffering — and if you're experienced. Newer, younger lifters who still have headroom to raise calories should use it. He is explicit that you *can* progress under 10% and even in a deficit, but the **rate** of progress is likely blunted by time spent in a deficit that could have been spent in a surplus.

**His own case study.** First show 2009 at 140 lb, provincials 2010 at 139 lb, Canadian natural Nationals 2012 at 151 lb — 12 lb of stage weight in two years, with visibly better conditioning. Zero mini cuts in that period; he stayed in a surplus and peaked at just over 180 lb offseason (40 lb above stage weight) before cutting 30 lb back down. He calls that surplus more aggressive than he'd now recommend, but credits the bulk of his long-term development to it.

## When a mini cut is warranted

**1. Appetite reset.** After a long bulk, appetite dies and weight stalls. He cites a **2008 study following 7 natural bodybuilders over 11 weeks of contest prep**, with hormone panels at 11 weeks out, 5 weeks out, and 3 days out during peak week. By **6 weeks of dieting**, leptin had significantly fallen and ghrelin had significantly risen — the combination that makes you very hungry. Over the *next* 6 weeks ghrelin rose only ~6% further and leptin was essentially flat, implying hunger doesn't keep escalating much past the 6-week mark. Caveat he raises himself: those subjects started very lean (he recalls roughly 9-10% body fat; the caption reads "99.6%", clearly garbled), so it's unclear whether someone starting at 12-15% shows the same hormonal shift. Prescription: **20-25% below maintenance for 2-6 weeks**, then back to gaining.

**2. Offseason body-fat control.** Top natural bodybuilders now stay lean offseason and conditioning standards keep climbing. Getting sloppy either makes the next cut brutal or puts a given conditioning level out of reach for the season. He splits people into two archetypes:

- **Competitor 1 — gets shredded easily.** Can carry more fat offseason. Ceiling around **12-15% for men**. Bulk as long as possible with calories as high as possible, then run a **longer, gentler mini cut: 5-10 weeks at 10-15% below maintenance, aiming to drop 5-10 lb**. If you need to lose much more than 10 lb, you overshot the bulk.
- **Competitor 2 — struggles to get lean.** Stay closer to stage condition. Ceiling around **10-12%**. Use **shorter bulks (1-2 months)** interspersed with **shorter, sharper mini cuts: 20-25% below maintenance for 2-3 weeks**.

The logic for separating them: letting the hard-gainer-of-condition bulk too long turns the "mini" cut into a full-blown long cut, eating into gaining time; giving the easy-conditioning person too many mini cuts makes them spin their wheels, since 1-2 months of bulking may not be enough to progress.

**3. Getting lean for summer.** Not everyone cares about a stage. Set a **10-25% deficit** depending on how much time you have — more time means you can use a smaller deficit, and the bigger the deficit the more lean mass you risk. Personal preference governs here.

## Diet vs cardio for the deficit
It probably doesn't matter mechanically, but he leans toward **dietary restriction over cardio**: cardio expenditure is harder to track accurately, and too much of it — especially high-intensity — will eventually interfere with recovery and weight-training performance. Use cardio sparingly, as on any cut.

## Transition / "hardening" phases
An idea he attributes to Lyle McDonald (and credits Lyle McDonald and Lane Norton as sources for several concepts here): spend **2 weeks at maintenance** between a mini cut and a surplus, and again between a bulk and a mini cut. Nippard's verdict: he can't identify a physiological rationale for it, but it may help adherence — stepping 4,000 → 3,600 → 3,000 kcal is less of a shock than jumping straight down, so you're less likely to cheat.

## Practical application
- Never mini cut inside 6 months of a show, or under 10% body fat.
- Appetite reset: 20-25% deficit, 2-6 weeks.
- Easy-conditioning lifter: bulk to 12-15%, then 5-10 weeks at a 10-15% deficit, target 5-10 lb lost.
- Hard-conditioning lifter: bulk 1-2 months to a 10-12% ceiling, then 2-3 weeks at a 20-25% deficit.
- Prefer food restriction over cardio; keep cardio sparing.
- Optional 2-week maintenance bridge either side of the cut, purely for adherence.

## Caveats & unknowns
- Body-fat percentages are eyeball guidelines for men; he gives no separate female numbers beyond saying the same framework applies.
- The 2008 hormone study's subjects were already very lean, so the 6-week hunger plateau may not generalise to someone starting at 12-15%.
- The pound targets (5-10 lb) are round numbers and scale with bodyweight.
- He is emphatic that mini cutting doesn't "kill gains" and that elite naturals stay lean year-round and still progress — the concern is specifically about newer lifters wasting their highest-growth-potential years bouncing between phases without a plan.
