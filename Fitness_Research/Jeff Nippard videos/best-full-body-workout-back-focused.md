# High-Frequency Full Body, Day 3: Back-Focused Workout

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Best Science-Based Full Body Workout: BACK Focused" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=didU4ZwAkPI)) · 2020-02-29 · 8 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- Day 3 of a five-day full-body split. Lats and traps are prioritised by hitting them first and with slightly more volume.
- Inter-set stretching (20-30 s at 7/10 intensity) may add hypertrophy and at worst does nothing — you're resting anyway.
- The cable upright row is a two-for-one: side delts via abduction plus upper traps via scapular elevation and upward rotation, replacing a lateral raise *and* a shrug.
- Upright row safety concerns are overblown according to Brad Schoenfeld, provided you stay at or below roughly 80-90 degrees.
- Save going-to-failure for the last exercise, when the heavy back work is done and recovery interference no longer matters.

## The workout

**1. Weighted pull-up — 3 x 6 @ RPE 8**
[00:30] This is the third consecutive day hitting back in some capacity, so extra fatigue or soreness in the first week or two is normal. Warm up properly: one controlled set of six with bodyweight or assistance to lock in technique and mind-muscle connection, then a second warm-up set of 4-5 reps with some weight added, then three working sets.

Tempo matters: rep six should look like rep one. Neither the concentric nor the eccentric should speed up as you fatigue. If you can't complete all six with consistent form, drop the weight for the next set.

**Inter-set lat stretching.** [01:31] A **2019 study** gave subjects the same full-body program twice per week; the group that stretched 30 seconds between sets saw roughly **50% more muscle growth** across the four muscles measured ([Evangelista 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/30688865)). Important limitation: looking at individual muscles, the difference only reached significance for the quads and when all muscles were pooled. **Chris Barakat** has since run a new study in his lab (unpublished at the time) that may add further support. Protocol: hold 20-30 seconds at about **7/10 intensity** — a deep stretch, not painful. His read is that inter-set stretching is either slightly positive or neutral as long as holds aren't excessive, so it's worth doing; at minimum it improves the pump.

**2. Humble row — 3 x 10 @ RPE 7-8**
[02:02] Deliberately lower RPE because execution matters more than load here — the whole purpose is limiting momentum and cheating. Take any row too close to failure and you start swinging or pulling with the arms, which is fine in some contexts but not this one. (He first saw the exercise on Matt Jensen's Instagram; Phil Snelgrove named it, because it humbles the weight you can use.)

To make it mid-trap and rear-delt dominant, emphasise transverse shoulder abduction by driving the elbows straight out to the sides. Use a towel or knee sleeves for cushioning on the bench edge, and set bench height so the torso sits roughly parallel to the floor.

**3. Leg press — 3 x 15**
[03:02] The opposite approach to day 1's heavily loaded, externally cued squats: lighter weight, higher reps, internal focus on feeling the quads contract. He doesn't make high-rep work the bread and butter, but it does two things. It builds work capacity — learn to tolerate and recover from sets of 15 and lower-rep sets feel easier. And while there's no scientific consensus on how much metabolic stress matters for hypertrophy, most of the literature still points to it contributing, even with mechanical tension as the main driver ([Schoenfeld 2013](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/23338987)).

*Cueing:* For quad dominance, keep constant tension without locking the knees at the top and place the feet lower on the platform. For glute dominance, wider stance, feet higher, toes flared out. His preference: run all 15 reps without pausing or locking out, smooth and consistent.

**4. Standing calf raise — 4 x 8**
[04:35] They don't have to be standing — what matters is that the **leg is straight**. If short on time, superset toe presses between leg press sets; otherwise use a separate machine. He now places calves mid-workout rather than at the end, because at the end he's more tempted to skip them — a useful accountability trick for chronic calf-skippers. Keep the knees locked by flexing the quads, and **pause at the bottom of every rep** to stop a highly elastic Achilles tendon from taking over.

**5. Cable upright row with rope — 3 x 10**
[05:05] A very time-efficient exercise: it hits side delts through shoulder abduction and upper traps through scapular elevation and upward rotation at once, for a small recovery cost — knocking off two muscles instead of programming a separate lateral raise and shrug.

On safety, he spoke with **Dr. Brad Schoenfeld**, who has published research in the area: the concerns are largely overblown, and if you're not going above roughly **80-90 degrees** the risk is very limited. Some individuals' genetics may mean it hurts them, but the evidence doesn't support the blanket warnings.

*Cueing:* Initiate by sweeping the weight out almost like a lateral raise while trying to pull the rope apart, then squeeze the traps up at the top like a shrug. Think of it as a lateral-raise/shrug hybrid rather than a true upright row.

**6. Hammer curl — 3 x 10, taken to failure**
[06:35] Biomechanically ideal for the **brachioradialis**, which sits on the back of the forearm and is better positioned in the line of pull with a neutral or pronated wrist. Despite the reputation as a brachialis/brachioradialis move, EMG evidence shows the **biceps are also very active** on hammer curls — the reverse (palms-down) grip is the only hand position genuinely inferior for the biceps. So it gives a good mix of forearms, brachialis and biceps, and should build the whole back of the arm.

*Technique:* Grip the dumbbell in the middle of the handle rather than letting the top head rest passively against your hand, which removes some forearm involvement.

Take these to failure — the heavy back work is done, so recovery interference isn't a concern at the end of the session. He notes RPE is hard to gauge on biceps because a little cheating buys extra reps, so he keeps it simple and goes all out while keeping form reasonably intact.

## Practical application
- Prioritise a muscle by placing it first and giving it slightly more volume, not by restructuring the split.
- Stretch the trained muscle 20-30 s at 7/10 between sets — free upside during rest you're taking anyway.
- Move calves to the middle of the session if you habitually skip them.
- Replace a lateral raise plus shrug with cable rope upright rows when time is short.
- Push isolation and end-of-session work to failure; keep the heavy compounds at RPE 7-8.

## Caveats & unknowns
The 2019 inter-set stretching study reached statistical significance only for the quads and for the pooled muscle data — the 50% figure should not be treated as settled. Barakat's follow-up was unpublished at the time of filming. There is no scientific consensus on how much metabolic stress actually contributes to hypertrophy. The upright row may still cause discomfort for some individuals regardless of the evidence base. Elevated fatigue in the first week or two of back-to-back back training is expected and shouldn't be read as overtraining.
