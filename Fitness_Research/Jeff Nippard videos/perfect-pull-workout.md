# The Perfect Pull Workout: Six Back and Biceps Movements

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "The Perfect Pull Workout (According To Science)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DXL18E7QRbk)) · 2023-07-10 · 8 min
**Topic:** Programming

## Bottom line
- Lead with 3x12-15 one-arm half-kneeling lat pulldowns, stopping the pull at the midline where the lats lose leverage.
- One all-out AMRAP set of pull-ups per session — beat or match last week's reps in identical form.
- Controlled "body English" on rows is a feature, not a flaw: back exercises are easy at the bottom and hard at the top, so ultra-strict form can end sets before the back is actually exhausted.
- Sweep the weight out and back on reverse pec deck to keep the rear delts working instead of the mid traps, and rotate hand positions across sets.
- Shrug up **and in**, not straight up — upper trap fibers fan out more horizontally than people assume.

## Session structure
5 min general warm-up (treadmill or StairMaster) plus quick dynamic stretches.

1. One-arm half-kneeling lat pulldown — 3x12-15
2. Pull-up — 1 all-out AMRAP
3. Kroc row — 3x10-12
4. Cable shrug — 3x10-12
5. Reverse pec deck — 3x10-12
6. Overhead cable biceps curl — 3x10-12

## One-arm half-kneeling lat pulldown [00:00]
Half-kneeling stance, non-working hand braced against the knee of the same leg. The more stable base directs tension onto the lats. Keep the cable and your forearm in a straight line — if the forearm meets the cable at an angle, the cable is set too high or low, or you are pulling too far back. **Stop the range of motion at the midline**, since the lats lose all leverage once the arm travels behind the torso.

**Pulldown vs pull-in [00:30]:** the one-arm lat pull-in (cable pulled in from out to the side) targets the lats through shoulder adduction; the one-arm pulldown to the front targets them through shoulder extension. Both work. Nippard has shifted toward the pulldown because more people report a better mind-muscle connection with it — possibly because the teres muscles overpower the lats for some people on the pull-in, though both muscles perform both functions, so he suspects it is mostly individual. No direct hypertrophy study comparing the two exists. Pick whichever you feel in your lats more, or rotate them if they feel equal.

## Pull-up [01:31]
He normally does multiple pull-up sets on a pull day, but since the cables already hit the lats, this is one all-out AMRAP. His framing: pull-ups are worth including even though they are harder than pulldowns, so the compromise is one set only — but it has to be everything you've got.

**Progression:** each week match or beat last week's rep count in the same form. Bulking, matching reps is already an overload because you're heavier. Cutting, add at least one rep per week, since your falling bodyweight reduces the resistance.

**Technique:** grip 1.5x shoulder width, chest up, think about pulling elbows down and in until they are as close to your sides as is comfortable. On a straight bar, at minimum get your chin over the bar or your chest level with your hands. Can't do bodyweight? Use an assisted machine, or loop a band over the bar and hook a foot in the bottom.

## Kroc row [03:01]
A looser dumbbell row with a slightly more upright posture and controlled cheating, named after record-holding powerlifter Janae Kroc.

Nippard's argument: science-based lifters are often stricter than they need to be. Strictness is right for beginners learning technique and it keeps progression tracking clean, but once you've mastered the basics, controlled body English on selected movements can let you overload the target muscle more effectively — most top bodybuilders sway and move rather than lifting stiffly. The non-negotiable is staying in control of the weight, especially the negative.

**Why it matters most on back work [04:02]:** the strength curve of back exercises is unusual — very easy at the bottom, much harder at the top. If you terminate the set at the first loss of the full top squeeze, you may stop well short of actually stimulating the back. A little momentum at the bottom lets the back approach true exhaustion. Use it as an occasional intensity technique, not on every exercise. If cheating feels uncomfortable, stricter form with lighter weight is fine.

## Cable shrug [04:32]
Convenient (no plate loading) and the cable angle lines up better with upper trap fibers, which fan out more horizontally than most people think rather than running vertically. **Shrug up and in**, not straight up and down, to reach as many of those fibers as possible. Same reasoning behind using a slightly wider grip on barbell shrugs — it forces the up-and-in path.

## Reverse pec deck [05:03]
Focus on **sweeping the weight out and back, not just back.** Pulling straight back shifts emphasis onto the mid traps — fine in isolation, but the pull-ups and Kroc rows already handled those, so isolate the rear delts here, since they get overpowered by larger back muscles in compounds.

**Rotate hand position across sets:** palms facing each other (neutral) on set one, palms down (pronated) on set two, slightly internally rotated on set three if your shoulders tolerate it. Nippard feels it best in positions two or three.

**Evidence [05:34]:** a 2013 study from Brad Schoenfeld and colleagues found average rear delt EMG was higher with a neutral/palms-in position than pronated ([Schoenfeld 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23302754/)) — but the individual data showed substantial variation, with some subjects showing greater activity with internal rotation and some with neutral, and some differences were large. Usual EMG caveats apply, but the takeaway is that if you have only ever used one hand position you may be leaving rear delt growth on the table.

## Overhead cable biceps curl [06:04]
Kneel and curl with the arm out to the side and up overhead. Nippard normally avoids overhead curls as awkward, but picked them up after seeing natural pro bodybuilders Alberto Nunez and Brian Minor at the N1 Training lab comparing arm-down vs arm-overhead curls: both showed an apparent bias toward the **long head of the biceps** with the arm held overhead. The long head is the outer portion and contributes more to the biceps peak.

He is careful not to overstate it — EMG limitations plus the individual variability the Schoenfeld paper demonstrated — but calls it worth experimenting with, especially since peak is a weak point for him.

This is usually his only biceps movement on this day. If you need more direct arm volume, add a couple of sets of curls with the arm down at the side or stretched back behind the torso.

## Practical application
The whole session is six exercises and roughly 16 working sets. Progress the pull-up AMRAP and the Kroc row load week to week; treat the pulldown, reverse pec deck and overhead curl as feel-driven quality work. Since biceps volume here is minimal, this day assumes another arm-focused session elsewhere in the week, or you add the extra curl sets.

## Caveats & unknowns
No hypertrophy study compares the lat pull-in with the lat pulldown, so that choice is preference. The overhead curl long-head bias rests on lab EMG observation of two lifters, not a training study. EMG activation patterns are highly individual, which is precisely why he recommends experimenting rather than prescribing.
