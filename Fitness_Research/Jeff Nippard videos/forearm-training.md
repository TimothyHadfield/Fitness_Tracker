# Forearm and Grip Training: Five Exercises and How to Load Them

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "How To Build Huge Forearms: Optimal Training Explained (5 Best Exercises!)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MfMxT_jXcPE)) · 2019-11-05 · 7 min
**Topic:** Exercise Selection

## Bottom line
- Five movements cover the whole forearm: **reverse curl, wrist roller, barbell towel hold, plate pinch, and dumbbell wrist curls (flexion + extension)**.
- **Direct forearm work isn't mandatory** — pulling and deadlifting already load them heavily — but it *is* necessary if forearms are a weak point.
- Starting dose: **1-2 of these movements, 1-2x/week**, then assess.
- On the reverse curl, use a **thumbless grip** and actively crush the bar; letting it rest in the palm or on the thumbs is the most common mistake.
- Isolation wrist curls: **15-20 reps**, since it's a single joint and a small muscle group.

## Anatomy [00:32]
The forearm is not one muscle — there are **over 20 muscles from wrist to elbow**, split into two compartments:
- **Anterior** — wrist flexion and finger flexion. Anything you grip trains the front of the forearm.
- **Posterior** — wrist and finger extension. The most prominent muscle here is the **brachioradialis**, which flexes the elbow alongside the biceps.

The brachioradialis is active in normal supinated curls, but because it sits on the back of the forearm it becomes **more active when the wrist is pronated**, since pronation puts the biceps at a biomechanical disadvantage.

## 1. Reverse grip curl [01:02]
- EZ bar or straight barbell — the semi-pronated EZ bar grip is often easier on the wrists.
- **Thumbless grip**, so the bar can't rest passively on the thumbs; this pulls more of the anterior forearm in.
- Crush all eight fingers into the base of the hands as hard as is comfortable.
- Elbows pinned at the sides; curl to maximum elbow flexion, lower under control.
- Some lifters get a better mind-muscle connection with the wrist in **slight extension or slight flexion** — worth experimenting.
- **Marineau (Marinean) et al.**: peak brachioradialis torque occurs at **100-120° of elbow flexion**, while peak biceps torque is nearer **80°**. This implies **top-half partial reps** bias the brachioradialis over the biceps.
- **Intensity technique**: after **12-15 full reps**, extend the set with top-half-range reverse curls.
- Common errors: excess momentum, and letting the bar sit in the hand/on the thumbs (not wrong, but easier and less forearm work).

## 2. Wrist roller [02:35]
Nippard's pick as one of the most effective single movements — as one hand goes into flexion the other goes into extension, so it trains front and back together.
- Stand on a box or riser so the rope can hang lower.
- Load **light**. Twist the wrists backward to raise the plate, then **actively untwist** on the way down rather than letting the plate drop.
- **Progression**: add small increments of weight, or keep the weight and increase total travel distance by adding rounds.
- Common error: holding the roller far out in front — the front delts fatigue before the forearms. Keep the arms in at the sides with a bent elbow, which also isometrically taxes the brachioradialis and biceps.

## 3. Barbell towel hold [03:37]
The most "functional" of the group, with carryover to heavy deadlifts and rows; Nippard calls it one of the most underrated pure grip-strength builders.
- Set a barbell in a rack **just above knee height** so you don't have to deadlift it up first.
- Wrap **two small gym towels** around the bar so the ends overlap in your hands, crush the towels, and lift to full lockout.
- **Start light with ~30-second holds. Progress by adding time up to 45 seconds, then add weight and restart at 30 seconds.**
- Can also be done hanging from a pull-up bar — bodyweight alone is hard at first.
- Why towels over farmer's walks or plain barbell holds: you get the same grip stimulus **without loading as much total weight**, saving time and sparing assistance muscles from extra fatigue. Plain barbell holds also let the bar sit in the palm, so they train finger flexion less.

## 4. Plate pinch [04:39]
Trains **pinching strength** — treated by some athletes as a separate quality from palm gripping — and specifically the **digit flexors of the anterior forearm**.
- Grab two plates side by side and pinch them off the floor by squeezing the fingers together as hard as possible.
- **Start with two 5 lb or 10 lb plates, progressing to 25 lb or even 35 lb plates.**
- Advanced variation: load with **thumb plus one finger at a time** (thumb + index, thumb + pinky) to specialise.
- Trains each side independently, so it exposes and corrects **left/right grip imbalances**.
- Use chalk if fingers slip.

## 5. Dynamic wrist work [05:39]
The grip exercises above are largely **isometric**, so add movements that take the wrist and fingers through an active range:
- **Hand gripper** — trains crushing strength through an active ROM; possibly redundant if you're doing the above, but convenient at home.
- **Dumbbell wrist curl** — Nippard still likes this for lagging forearms. Hits the anterior forearm well, simple, and easy to overload by adding reps or weight. **15-20 reps.**
- **Dumbbell wrist extension** — flip the arm over, wrists just off the bench, **15-20 reps with slightly lighter dumbbells**.

## Practical application
- If forearms aren't a weak point, indirect work from pulls and deadlifts may be enough.
- If they are: pick **1-2 of these movements and run them 1-2x/week**, then reassess.
- A balanced pairing would be one grip/isometric movement (towel hold or plate pinch) plus one dynamic movement (wrist roller or wrist curl + extension).
- Reverse curls slot in as an elbow-flexion movement that also builds the brachioradialis; finish with top-half partials after 12-15 full reps.

## Caveats & unknowns
- Nippard is explicit that **direct forearm training isn't required for everyone**, given the indirect load from pulling work.
- The top-half-partial recommendation is an inference from torque-angle data, not a direct hypertrophy finding.
- Wrist-position tweaks on the reverse curl are framed as individual preference ("some trainees find..."), not established.
