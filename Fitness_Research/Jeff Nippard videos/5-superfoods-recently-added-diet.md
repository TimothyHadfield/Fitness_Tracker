# Five "Superfoods" Worth Eating, and the Science Behind Each

**Source:** Jeff Nippard, "5 'Superfoods' I Recently Added To My Diet (Science Explained)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hMmUc8NpC7k)) · 2019-05-25 · 7 min
**Topic:** Nutrition

## Bottom line
- "Superfood" is a marketing term with no scientific definition — PubMed barely recognises it. But some foods genuinely are more nutritious than others.
- Blueberries: half a cup to a cup daily, for anthocyanins and brain/cardiometabolic effects.
- Garlic: add it liberally, or 600–1500 mg/day in capsule form. Reduces cold severity and lowers total cholesterol over 2+ months.
- Mussels/shellfish 1–2× per week: 18 g protein per 150 g at 129 kcal, ~7% leucine, ~1 g omega-3, plus zinc for testosterone support.
- Leafy greens daily: nitrates → nitric oxide → better blood pressure and measurably more bench press volume.

## Why the word "superfood" is a problem
It sits alongside "cleanse" and "detox" as a buzzword with no established definition. Science-Based Medicine argues the trend is actively unhelpful: it makes meal planning more complex by focusing people on single magic ingredients instead of overall dietary quality. The real levers are unglamorous — a caloric deficit for fat loss, a nutritious diet plus exercise for health, and modern medicine for disease.

## Blueberries
Per cup: ~85 kcal, 21 g carbs (about 4 g fibre), 24% DV vitamin C, 36% DV vitamin K. What sets them apart from other berries is a very high concentration of phenolic compounds and anthocyanins.

**How antioxidants work:** metabolising food, exercising, smoking, pollution and even sunlight generate free radicals, which react with molecules such as DNA to cause oxidative stress — implicated in cancer, heart disease and Alzheimer's. Antioxidants defend against this. Dr. Steven Novella's point is that the term entered marketing and public consciousness long before the science was understood. Crucially, the benefits appear when antioxidants come from food, not supplements — some evidence suggests antioxidant supplements may do net harm. Whole foods only.

On phenolic content blueberries beat raspberries, strawberries and blackberries, sitting just behind black currants (US currant farming was banned in 1911, which is why Russia dominates the crop today). Aronia berries also rank very high.

Anthocyanins are the blue pigment and can cross the blood-brain barrier, which may explain the cognitive effects:
- A 2016 study by Bowtell and colleagues (captions render this "Bothwell") found an anthocyanin-rich blueberry concentrate improved brain perfusion and activation in regions tied to cognitive function.
- Work from Krikorian et al. (captions garble this as "Krekorian natal") showed blueberry supplementation improves memory in older adults.
- Other research found significant reductions in diastolic blood pressure and LDL cholesterol plus improved insulin sensitivity, with no difference in caloric intake between blueberry and control groups.

**Dose: 0.5–1 cup per day.**

## Garlic
Examine.com's database rates including garlic cloves as one of the healthiest dietary habits available.
- A study from Nantz et al. (captions: "Nancy tall") found 90 days of garlic reduced cold and flu symptom severity and produced significantly fewer days missed from school and work.
- A meta-analysis of 39 trials found garlic reduces total cholesterol when used for at least two months.

**Dose: add it to food where possible (garlic powder in eggs, diced cloves in stir-fries); if you dislike the taste, 600–1500 mg/day in capsules.**

## Kiwi fruit
Not a new addition for Jeff, included as a reminder. Kiwis beat apples, oranges and bananas on essentially every vitamin, yet make up under 1% of global fruit consumption. Newer research points to the **peel** being especially beneficial: eating it increases fibre intake and preserves much of the vitamin C, and it may be responsible for kiwi's positive effect on sleep — one study concluded kiwi peel could be useful for developing natural sleep aids.

## Mussels (and other shellfish)
Per 150 g: 18 g protein for 129 kcal. The protein is ~7% leucine, putting its quality on par with chicken and beef for stimulating muscle protein synthesis. A serving also carries nearly 1 g of omega-3 fatty acids and is high in potassium, magnesium and zinc.

Zinc matters specifically for lifters: trainees and athletes can become deficient relatively easily, especially heavy sweaters, and natural testosterone production appears zinc-dependent — so shellfish may help preserve or support testosterone. Scallops and other shellfish do the same job.

**Frequency: once or twice per week.** Preparation: mussels boiled in salt water for 2 minutes, eaten fresh; scallops pan-fried in olive or macadamia oil with garlic.

## Leafy greens
The lifting-specific reason to eat greens is their very high nitrate content. Nitrates raise nitric oxide production; nitric oxide is a vasodilator, so it improves pumps, lowers blood pressure and protects the heart.

A paper by Mosher et al. (captions: "Moser") found **400 mg of beetroot nitrates per day for six days increased total reps and total weight lifted on a bench press program.**

**Frequency: spinach, kale and similar every day, as often as you can manage.**

## Practical application
- Daily: 0.5–1 cup blueberries; leafy greens at as many meals as practical; garlic wherever it fits (or 600–1500 mg capsules).
- Weekly: mussels or scallops 1–2×.
- Eat kiwi with the peel on for the fibre, vitamin C retention and possible sleep benefit.
- Take antioxidants from food, not pills.

## Caveats & unknowns
- Several researcher names come through the auto-captions distorted; the corrections above are best-guess reconstructions and should be verified before citing.
- The blood-pressure/LDL/insulin-sensitivity blueberry findings are attributed only to "other research" with no named authors.
- The 400 mg beetroot nitrate finding is a single study on bench press volume; it is not a general performance guarantee.
- "Superfood" framing aside, none of these substitutes for total diet quality and energy balance.
