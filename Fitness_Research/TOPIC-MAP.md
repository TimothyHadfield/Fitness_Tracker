# Topic map — which file answers which question

A router, not a summary. Find your question, open the files listed in the order given. Nothing
here states a finding; it points at the file that does.

**The general rule.** For anything contested, read
[WHAT-TO-BELIEVE.md](WHAT-TO-BELIEVE.md) first — it says which source is right and how confident
that deserves to be. Then go to the domain summary for the detail. Only open individual notes when
you need a study's own numbers.

**The layers.** `WHAT-TO-BELIEVE.md` (cross-source verdicts) → `<source>/SUMMARY.md` (that
source's framing and routing) → `<source>/SUMMARY-<domain>.md` (study-by-study detail) → the notes
themselves.

## Which source to trust on what

- **ISSN position stands** — supplements and sports nutrition. Formal consensus, densest citation
  base in the library. Caveat: the numbered position statement is often firmer than the evidence
  review directly above it. When they disagree, trust the body.
- **House of Hypertrophy** — hypertrophy mechanisms and single training variables. The most
  careful reasoning here. Caveat: 26 videos have no retrievable reference list.
- **Jeff Nippard** — the broadest coverage, and the best claim-to-source mapping. Caveat: 199 of
  279 notes are from 2014–2019, and he has publicly retracted several positions since.
- **Barbell Medicine** — injury, pain, labs, clinical medicine. The only source qualified here.
  Caveat: check the byline; the unbylined listicles are a different publication.
- **Menno Henselmans** — read for the dissent, not the consensus. Where he disagrees, the
  disagreement is the content. Characteristic failure: absolute claims with no source.
