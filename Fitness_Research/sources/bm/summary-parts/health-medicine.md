## Health, labs and medicine

Material no other source here touches, written by practising physicians — and where the gap between *what the evidence shows* and *what we recommend* is widest. Several of these articles carry no citations at all.

### My blood test came back abnormal

The most useful cluster here: a lifter's out-of-range creatinine, transaminases and CK are routinely muscle-derived rather than disease.

**Creatinine.** [It is a muscle metabolite used as a kidney test](abnormal-labs-exercise-part-i-kidney-function.md) — the breakdown product of creatine in skeletal muscle — so a rise means either filtration fell or muscle mass rose, and CKD-EPI cannot tell which. The ceiling: even people who train will not exceed **1.5 mg/dL (133 µmol/L)** without impairment, and values above it **"should always be evaluated further"**, with muscularity permitted only after other causes are excluded. **1.4–1.5 against a 0.7–1.2 range** is the realistic muscularity case; below it, impairment is still possible. Two things discriminate: a **prior value** (0.8 → 1.4 warrants evaluation even if muscle was gained in between — trajectory beats threshold), and **cystatin C**, a filtration marker unaffected by muscle mass, so it stays normal beside a muscular lifter's creatinine-driven "abnormal" eGFR while real impairment raises both. *Evidence:* the physiology. *Recommendation:* the 1.5 threshold and the compare-to-a-prior sequence — conventional, and untested as a pathway.

**Liver enzymes, and a defect worth recording.** AST and ALT are not liver-exclusive; [both leak from skeletal muscle](exercise-affect-liver-function-tests.md), and the magnitude rests on one experiment — **Pettersson 2008, n=15 untrained men, one session, no control**. The [longer case-based article](elevated-liver-enzymes-after-exercise-means.md) adds the timeline that traps a regular lifter (still rising at 48 h, peak 4–7 days, baseline only at 10–12 days), the discriminators that hold (**albumin and INR are unaffected by exercise**; a normal INR effectively excludes acute liver failure), and a physician survey finding **56% did not list exercise-induced muscle damage in their differential**.

But it gives **three different escalation thresholds**: the body sets "substantially less plausible" at **>5× ULN**, the FAQ sets urgent evaluation at **>10×**, and the patient scripts invoke **<3×** as the expectant-observation range. In an article whose whole purpose is telling a reader whether to worry, that is a real defect — someone taking the FAQ number could sit on a 7× elevation. Its key takeaway also claims **100% of resistance trainees show elevation** on the strength of fifteen untrained men, and **its reference list is internally corrupt** — at least five entries' author and title point at a different paper than the PMID beside them. Its recommendation — rest a week, retest with CK and LDH, before imaging or referral — is sensible judgement with no trial behind it. The article links to their **paid Medical Consultations**.

**CK.** [Rhabdomyolysis is a clinical diagnosis, not a lab value](rhabdomyolysis-causes-treatments-return-sport.md). The classic triad appears in **fewer than 10% of cases**. **CK >5× ULN is sensitive but not specific** — below it you can be confident it is absent, above it you have learned little, because athletes routinely exceed it in normal training, and discharge is not decided on it either. The four-phase return protocol is explicitly labelled expert opinion.

### My cholesterol is high

The four-article lipid series is the best-cited work here. The organising claim is **cumulative exposure — "the lower, the longer, the better"** — which licenses their [rejection of the 10-year risk window for younger people](cholesterol-causes-prevention-treatment.md), a policy preference, not a guideline position. **ApoB is the target** (under 90); **HDL-C is demoted to a marker**, since trials raising it do not reduce events. Diet reduces to **fibre ≥30 g/day** and fat *type*, not fat total.

[Part 2](cholesterol-myths-misconceptions.md) is where the evidence is densest: LDL-C tracks plaque linearly inside the normal range (**1,779 people with no conventional risk factors; plaque 11% at LDL-C 60–70, 64% at 150–160**), and **1 mmol/L of lowering yields 22–24% fewer events regardless of how achieved**. Its weakness is asymmetric scrutiny — null saturated-fat studies get three pages of dissection, supporting ones none — and its **"18–20% vs <10% of calories" threshold** is an established breakpoint in neither cited source.

The [action plan](lower-heart-disease-risk-action-plan-cholesterol.md) carries the drugs: CTT's **170,000 participants across 26 trials, 25–30% fewer events per 38 mg/dL**, each statin doubling buying only ~6%, so **early combination beats dose escalation**. It rejects red yeast rice, citrus bergamot and OTC fish oil — notable from a company with a supplement line. But the sentence closing its case for aggressive therapy — **"a 1% reduction in plaque volume is associated with an 18% reduction in major cardiovascular events"** — has no source at all, and is a surrogate relationship doing an outcome's work. [Lp(a)](lipoprotein-levels-testing-treatment-heart-disease.md) is **>90% genetic, fixed by age 5, test once**; rule out below 30 mg/dL, rule in above 50; **no approved therapy lowers it and reduces events**, so management is compensatory. It calls Lp(a) "causal" flatly, then concedes the hypothesis is untested — and links twice to their **paid physician consultation**. [Statin muscle symptoms](statins-muscle-they-affect-strength-growth.md) run 1–5% above placebo in blinded trials against 10–20% in clinic; STOMP (**420 adults, six months, atorvastatin 80 mg**) found no strength difference — but that article also claims no difference in *hypertrophy*, which STOMP never measured, and seven of its references link to unrelated papers.

### My blood pressure is high

[Naci 2019 — a network meta-analysis of 391 RCTs, 39,742 subjects](effect-resistance-training-high-blood-pressure.md) — is the whole basis. In hypertensive subgroups exercise lowered SBP **8.96 mmHg** against **8.8** for the drug trials; resistance training alone **−7.83**; across all 197 exercise trials including normotensives, only **−4.84**. Two caveats travel with that headline. **8.96 vs 8.8 is not a difference**, and it is an **indirect comparison across separate populations** — the drug trials were larger, older and started ~18 mmHg higher; nobody has been randomised to exercise or a drug. And the outcomes differ in kind: the drug literature has stroke and mortality endpoints, the exercise trials measured **millimetres of mercury over weeks**. "Exercise slightly beats medication" is a surrogate comparison, not a substitution. The [longer guide](blood-pressure-causes-prevention-treatment.md) is unusually good on drugs — doubling one agent adds ~1.5 mmHg on top of 8.7, while **four drugs at quarter dose deliver ~22.5 mmHg** with fewer side effects. But "lifting weights does not cause hypertension", the sentence its audience most wants, is the one empirical claim in it with **no citation**. [Measurement technique](measure-blood-pressure-correctly.md) is the standard procedure done properly: cuff size is the biggest error you control, and a high reading without symptoms is a primary-care problem, not an emergency.

### I'm on a GLP-1

[The "40% muscle loss" headline is largely a measurement problem](glp-1-muscle-loss-ozempic-wegovy-mounjaro.md), and the argument is good: DXA "lean soft tissue" contains water, glycogen, organs and intramuscular fat, assumes a fixed 73% hydration that breaks during 15% weight loss, and moves 1–2% from glycogen or creatine manipulation alone. The STEP-1 **placebo** group also lost DXA lean mass despite minimal weight change. Where function was measured it held or improved (SEMALEAN: fat −18%, lean −5%, **grip +4.5 kg**).

Two cautions. **Their Table 1 is a model presented as measurement** — it decomposes a 3 kg lean-mass loss into liver glycogen, liver fat, intramuscular fat and **only 200–500 g of contractile muscle**, with a footnote conceding these are "bounded estimates" from compartment modelling, and a stated total (1.5–3.9 kg) that does not pin to the 3 kg it decomposes — the article's most quotable claim is its least measured one. And **SEMALEAN is a single-arm cohort with no control**, carrying most of the weight for "function improves." No pair-fed human trial exists, so "the drug is catabolic" is untested; the one pair-fed comparison is mostly mice. Their protein target, **1.2–1.6 g/kg**, is extrapolated from non-GLP-1 deficit literature and clashes with the ISSN stand, which treats 1.6–2.4 as the floor. On stopping, [three randomised withdrawal trials converge](gain-weight-back-after-ozempic-data-shows.md): roughly two-thirds regained within a year, SURMOUNT-4's placebo arm regaining ~14% of body weight. Their reframe is fair — lifestyle does not hold weight off better, it holds off less weight. Both articles route to **Coaching, the paid Beginner Template and Whey Rx**.

### Should I get my testosterone checked? (Mostly: no)

[The most valuable clinical content in the batch is a refusal](where-priorities-improve-health.md). The seven priorities are ordinary; what distinguishes the article is the flat instruction against **routine lab or imaging screening in asymptomatic people — naming vitamin D, testosterone, thyroid, cortisol, stress tests and whole-body scans** — plus no sleep trackers and no default keto or fasting. A physician position on the harms of over-testing, argued from USPSTF, in a market whose business model is selling biomarker panels. (Its "~90% of what matters" headline is invented, and coaching is linked three times.)

The testosterone articles carry it through. [More than half of men meeting deficiency criteria at one visit are normal on a repeat draw with no treatment](low-testosterone-men-symptoms-causes-testing-trt.md) (MMAS, >1,700 men). Requiring symptoms *plus* labs drops prevalence from **38.7% to 2–6%** — the six-fold spread overprescribing lives in. Only **three sexual symptoms** cluster reliably with biochemical hypogonadism; fatigue and low mood, the complaints that drive men into clinics, are worthless as discriminators. Most adult cases are **secondary and reversible**: visceral fat (**−147 ng/dL with obesity; +84 for 10% weight loss**), sleep restriction (**−10–15% at 5 h/night for a week**), opioids — and **sleep apnoea**, where exogenous testosterone worsens the actual problem. The [sleep apnoea guide](basic-guide-sleep-apnea.md) makes the same case. TRAVERSE (**5,246 men; MACE 7.0% vs 7.3%**) retires the cardiovascular scare without showing benefit. The [companion piece](low-testosterone-causes-diagnosis-management.md) has the best line on harm — spermatogenesis suppressed within 10 weeks, **4–10% never fully recover** — but is unsourced throughout and defers roughly ten times to their **paid Testosterone Course**.

### Measuring the body, and the metabolic chain

[BMI's real failure is under-diagnosis](bmi-accurate-bmi.md), not the muscular outlier: **~97% specificity, ~42% sensitivity at 30** (25 studies, ~32,000 people), catching only 36% of men and 49% of women who genuinely carry excess fat — a positive is trustworthy, a negative is not. It survives on outcome data (**239 prospective studies, >10 million people**), not precision. Pair it with [waist measured at the iliac crest](measure-waist-circumference.md), where the NHANES cut-points come from — though that article's bolded 40″/34″ is undercut by its own footnote conceding 37″/31″ screens better. [The BRI article](new-number-health-body-roundness-index-replace.md) misstates its own formula — BRI is **waist and height only** — so its case that BRI accounts for ethnicity fails. [The visceral-fat piece](visceral-fat-vs-weight-waist-matters-more.md) is better, and its winning theory undercuts its own headline: visceral fat is a **marker** that subcutaneous storage capacity has been exceeded, with fatty liver doing the damage. That chain runs through the **personal fat threshold** in [type 2 diabetes](know-about-type-2-diabetes-part-1.md) — one model presented as the model, with no effect sizes or designs — to [fatty liver](fatty-liver-disease-causes-symptoms-treatments.md), whose most actionable sentence is that **ALT and AST are normal in more than half of MASLD patients** and do not exclude advanced fibrosis. That article's numbers are almost entirely uncited.

### Getting older, and being pregnant

[Sarcopenia has moved off mass onto strength](sarcopenia-symptoms-prevent.md): mass declines 0.5–1.0%/year after 50, strength 1.5–3.0%, because the loss is neural. Its "walking is not enough" is sound size-principle reasoning stated as established finding, with no study comparing walking to lifting. [Osteoporosis](basic-guide-osteoporosis.md) is the opposite extreme — **it cites nothing at all**, including its one load-bearing safety claim, that high-intensity lifting rebuilds bone in older patients without causing fractures. Such trials exist; a reader cannot check that this rests on them. [Strength tracked cancer mortality where self-reported lifting did not](strength-cancer-related-mortality-enough-exercise-actually.md) in one NHANES analysis (n=2,773) — but a dynamometer beats a two-question self-report through measurement error alone.

[Training through pregnancy](exercise-during-pregnancy-safety-training-guide.md) is safe in **uncomplicated, average-risk** pregnancies, on ACOG plus a 2025 BJSM meta-analysis pooling 47,619 participants; the Valsalva fear is answered by two small acute studies (one a **22-woman** 3D Doppler) that test the mechanism directly but measure surrogates. Guidelines say **RPE 4–6**; the article prescribes **RPE 6–8** from a non-pregnant meta-analysis. Its worst gap: the sentence introducing conditions to stop for is followed by nothing, and no warning-signs list appears anywhere. [Cycle-phase periodisation is not supported](women-menstrual-cycle-athletic-exercise-performance.md) — cycling estradiol runs ~3× post-menopausal levels and the dip lasts days not decades, and all four periodisation studies are confounded by tapers and contralateral crosstalk. [The sex-and-gender article](shades-gray-sex-gender-fairness-sport.md) is strongest on the history of failed sex testing; its stretch is using within-sex testosterone variation to argue about the between-sex gap. Two loose ends: [exercise triggers migraine in 22% of sufferers yet every intervention reduced frequency](exercise-affect-headaches.md) in a 21-trial network meta-analysis, and [CBD has good evidence only for two rare paediatric epilepsies](science-cbd-oil-health-performance.md), none for performance, with **only 31% of 84 tested products accurately labelled and THC in 21%**.
