## How to read this source

House of Hypertrophy is the most densely cited channel in this library and the narrowest. The
median video is twelve minutes on a single training variable. Across the channel he cites 1,557
references, 977 of them distinct.

The creator is **self-taught and has no formal credentials**, which he says himself. Judge him
on the work, and the work is unusually good in a specific way: he presents the study that
contradicts him, states sample sizes and training status, distinguishes a statistically null
result from a demonstrated absence, and **publicly retires his own positions when the evidence
moves**. This summary records several of those reversals, because the reversals are the strongest
evidence that the rest is worth trusting.

Three cautions apply throughout, and they are the reason this summary keeps repeating the same
three questions.

**Was growth measured, or something that stands in for it?** Enormous parts of this channel —
almost every exercise ranking, every "best exercise for the side delts" tier list — rest on EMG,
moment-arm modelling or acute signalling rather than on measured hypertrophy. He usually says so.
Readers usually don't notice.

**Were the subjects trained, and was the program complete?** Much of the volume literature ran
on programs training one or two muscle groups for around ten weeks. That is not a program, and
he now says so about work he previously cited without the caveat.

**Is there a reference list at all?** **Twenty-six videos have none**, and they are
disproportionately his longest and most confident: the 73-study biceps guide, the 87-study
mechanisms video, the 63-study triceps guide, the 36-study lats guide. Those cite on screen
only. Where a claim in this summary traces back to one of them, it is unverified, and it is
marked.

## The short version

- **Mechanical tension is the driver**, and the case for it is an elimination argument built on
  signalling studies rather than a direct measurement. The negative findings are better evidenced
  than the positive one: microtears, the pump, lactate and post-workout hormone spikes each fail
  the same way — the thing rises and growth does not follow.
- **More volume keeps working further than most people think.** The best current meta-regression
  finds no plateau out to 43 weekly sets per muscle. He still recommends 10–20, and is candid
  that this is his prior overriding the curve.
- **Rep range barely matters between about 5 and 35 reps** when sets are taken near failure — and
  his sharpest single argument is why the studies claiming otherwise are broken: they gave the
  low-rep groups more sets and longer rest.
- **Effort matters more than tinkering.** Training to failure and stopping 1–3 reps short grow
  much the same at normal volumes; drop sets, rest-pause, supersets and tempo tricks are time
  levers, not growth levers.
- **Lengthened partials are at least as good as full range of motion and probably not better**
  in trained lifters — a position he built aggressively and then largely walked back in 2024.
- **Exercise selection has one usable predictor**, and it is not activation: a two-joint muscle
  grows poorly when one of its actions opposes the compound movement, and better when the other
  joint holds it at length.
- **Almost everything else is a rounding error** — time of day, free weights versus machines,
  cardio at ordinary volumes, exercise variation, pre-sleep protein, and creatine's effect on
  lean mass once you measure it directly rather than through body water.
