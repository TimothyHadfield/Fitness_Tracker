## Where he departs from the rest of this library

This library holds five sources. Where they disagree, the disagreement is the useful part, and
this channel disagrees more often than its narrow subject range suggests.

**On volume ceilings.** Jeff Nippard's [junk volume](../Jeff%20Nippard%20videos/junk-volume.md)
position treats roughly six hard sets per muscle per session as the point of diminishing returns,
resting on a Krieger meta-analysis. The meta-regression this channel reports finds growth still
climbing past 20 sets per session and 43 per week, with no inverted U anywhere. The two are not
strictly incompatible — per-session and per-week are different questions, and the high-volume
studies mostly trained one muscle group — but they pull hard in opposite directions, and a reader
following Nippard's number is training well below what the newer analysis supports. Menno
Henselmans sits closer to this channel, doubting that a ceiling has been demonstrated at all.

**On the Barbalho papers.** Both this channel and Menno Henselmans report that the Barbalho volume
trials were **retracted for implausible data**. Those trials were one of the three legs of the
volume-ceiling dispute this library records, and the Nippard material predates the retraction and
does not mention it — a correction has since been applied to the Nippard summaries. This channel
handles it best: it removes the papers from an argument they would have supported.

**On rep-range floors.** This library's older material puts the equivalent band at 5–35 reps.
This channel argues for a floor of 6 rather than 5, on the grounds that the trials defending very
low reps systematically gave the low-rep group more sets and longer rest. That is a better
argument than the number it produces, and it applies to any source repeating the 5–35 figure.

**On lengthened partials.** Nippard's material and this channel's own earlier videos lean toward
lengthened partials beating full range of motion. The 2024 trained-subject studies found no
advantage — and one of the null studies was **funded by Nippard**, which this channel discloses.
Where the library still asserts a partials advantage in trained lifters, it is behind the
evidence.

**On EMG.** This channel produced the library's hardest anti-EMG argument — the triceps long head
shows high activation in the bench press and grows minimally from it, which is exactly the
inference EMG is used to make. He then softened that stance in later videos and built whole
exercise rankings on activation data. Both positions are in this folder. The earlier one is
better reasoned.

**On creatine.** The headline "one third faster muscle growth" is a reinterpretation, not a
measurement. The direct-measurement meta-analysis found an effect size of about **0.11**, which
its own authors called trivial; the reclassification to "small-to-medium" uses hypertrophy-specific
thresholds taken from a blog post. The methodological objection is reasonable. The headline
outruns it, and he does not say so.

## Known problems in this material

Recorded here rather than quietly fixed, because a reader needs to know where the floor is thin.

- **Twenty-six videos have no retrievable reference list**, including the four most confident
  guides on the channel. Every claim traced to them is unverified. They are marked in their own
  notes and in this summary, but they are also the videos most likely to be quoted.
- **Reference lists are occasionally wrong.** One video describes a strength *frequency*
  meta-analysis and links the *set volume* meta-analysis instead. Flagged in the note rather than
  corrected.
- **The same study is reported with different durations in different notes** — one trial appears
  as 8 weeks in one video and 9 in another. His numbers, not a transcription error.
- **Titles inflate study counts.** A video titled "11 Studies" rests on two chronic trials and
  nine acute or mechanistic papers; a video titled "18 Studies" is carried by three. The notes say
  so where it matters.
- **187 reference links could not be resolved to a citation.** Most are congress abstracts,
  strength-and-conditioning journals that are not machine-indexed, and PDFs rehosted on private
  domains. They appear in the bibliography as the bare link he gave, which is honest but not
  followable in every case.
- **Caption transcription mangles researcher names**, so every name in these notes was taken from
  the description's reference list rather than the audio. Where a video has no reference list and
  a name was only spoken aloud, the note describes the study without naming an author.
