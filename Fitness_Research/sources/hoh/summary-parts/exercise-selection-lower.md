## Back, legs, and how to choose exercises at all

### First decision: does the equipment matter?

No, and it is the best-supported claim here. [Free weights vs machines](free-weights-vs-machines-muscle-hypertrophy.md) covers Schwanbeck 2020 — 36 subjects averaging 2 years training, 8 weeks, every set to failure, matched movement for movement — with similar biceps and quadriceps thickness gains. [These 3 exercises](3-exercises-don-t-build-more-muscle.md) adds Hernández-Belmonte 2023, the strongest: 36 trained men, 8 weeks, similar quadriceps growth at two sites plus pec major and rectus abdominis. Aerenhouts 2020 and push-up trials (Kikuchi 2017, n=18 trained; Kotarsky 2018, n=23) agree. [The best training mode](best-training-mode-muscle-growth-most-get.md) closes the escape routes: free weights produce larger acute hormone spikes, but those do not predict growth; variable resistance is null across four trials; eccentric overload won in only **4 of 11 studies**. Recruitment follows the *movement*, not the equipment.

Two caveats: Schwanbeck measured **only two muscles by ultrasound over 8 weeks**, and a null in an underpowered trial is thin ground for "equivalent"; and his band scepticism is **entirely mechanistic** — no band hypertrophy trial exists, he says so, then states the takeaway more firmly than that supports.

Where modality bites is progressive overload. [Bodyweight training](bodyweight-training-bad-muscle-growth-true-false.md) locates it: upstairs is fine, but there is **no proper bodyweight hip-extension exercise** for the hamstrings, and calf raises and hip thrusts blow past 35 reps for novices. "You probably cannot maximise lower-body hypertrophy with calisthenics alone" is hedged as speculation; that note's regional section leans on EMG, which it flags.

### Second decision: compounds only, or isolation too?

[Are isolation exercises useless](isolation-exercises-useless-muscle-hypertrophy-19-studies.md) is the reference note. Three trials (Gentil 2015, Gentil 2013, França 2015, 8–10 weeks) found adding single-joint work produced no extra arm growth despite the isolation groups doing *more* volume. His rebuttal: every one measured **only elbow flexors or arm circumference at a single mid-muscle site**, which cannot detect regional differences. Four further Barbalho studies pointed the same way and he discards them for improbable data — throwing out evidence that helped the side he opposes. The affirmative case is mostly mechanistic and the note says so: lateral delt, calves, knee-flexion hamstring function, rectus femoris and triceps long head are argued from joint mechanics, with **only Costa 2021 (22 detrained men, 8 weeks)** testing variety directly.

### Third decision: how often do you change exercises?

Never randomly. [Switch exercises every session](switch-exercises-every-session-more-muscle-hypertrophy.md) covers the only direct test: Baz-Valle, **19 men with 2+ years training**, 8 weeks, an 80-exercise app randomising selection each session versus a fixed list, 3×6–12 to failure. Quad growth was statistically indistinguishable, some raw percentages favouring the *fixed* group. His case against switching — skill, repeated bout effect, tracking — is entirely mechanistic, and its recovery limb is contradicted by the study's own null. The real positive: variation-group motivation rose, the fixed group's declined.

That sits in tension with [Underrated selection principles](underrated-exercise-selection-principles-muscle-growth.md), which endorses Rauch 2017 — **17 men, 5.6 years training, 9 weeks** — where an autoregulated group free to pick exercises beat a fixed programme on lean mass. That note and [The best exercise](best-exercise-muscle-hypertrophy-science-says.md) both argue individuals recruit differently on the same lift (Boyer 2021, hamstring heads; Stronska, bench press) — **all surface EMG**, which he elsewhere says cannot predict growth. He even defends Stronska's normalisation seven months after demolishing it, and his "isolate a muscle to raise its compound involvement" principle has **no growth data at all**.

### The principle that actually predicts things: biarticular muscles and length

[Maximize muscle growth](maximize-muscle-growth-new-study-exercise-choice.md) is the best video here. Burke 2024, **28 trained subjects, within-subject** — one leg leg-pressed, the other did leg extensions; one did straight-leg calf raises, the other bent-leg, all to failure at 8–12. Rectus femoris grew better with the leg extension, vastus lateralis with the leg press. Two principles: a biarticular muscle grows poorly when one of its actions *opposes* the compound (rectus femoris flexes the hip; the leg press extends it), and positioning the other joint so the muscle is **longer** grows it more.

The within-subject design is the rigour — your left leg does not sleep more than your right — and his refusal to resolve the parallel biceps case, laying out three counterarguments to his own principle and ending on the fence, is him at his most honest. Two flags: the neural down-regulation story is **inference layered on an outcome**, since the study measured growth only; and calf outcomes came from a **single region per muscle** versus three for the quads.

### Quads

Converged and well-evidenced. [Is the squat enough](squat-enough-leg-growth.md) found the gap early — Fonseca (49 untrained men, 12 weeks): squat-only groups failed to significantly grow rectus femoris; varied groups grew all four heads. Kubo (17 untrained men, 10 weeks) found **neither** 90° nor 140° depth grew it. [Bigger quads](bigger-quads-new-study-epic.md) supplies the fix: **22 untrained men**, within-subject, leaning-back leg extension (40° hip flexion) versus normal (90°), same knee range, **1.4–2.7× greater rectus femoris growth** at both regions, vastus lateralis similar — the internal control that makes it credible. [Unilateral vs bilateral](unilateral-vs-bilateral-training-muscle-growth.md) replicates it in **63 untrained women, 8 weeks**: leg extensions beat squats for rectus femoris at all three regions; squats won for vastus lateralis distally only. Run both. Overreach: the reverse-Nordic and sissy-squat picks rest on length reasoning with **no comparative growth data**, and Mitsuya 2023 (9 bodybuilders) is MRI *activity*, not growth.

### Glutes, hamstrings and deadlifts

[Squats vs hip thrusts](squats-vs-hip-thrusts-glute-growth.md): 34 untrained subjects, 8–12 reps to volitional failure — **no difference** in gluteus maximus growth at upper, middle or lower regions. Squats additionally grew quads and adductors; neither grew hamstrings. The Barbalho result claiming a 2.5× squat advantage is discarded. Limitations stated: untrained subjects, vague failure criterion. Hamstrings are the hole — Kubo found no significant growth at either squat depth, Bloomquist minimal increases and some decreases.

[Trap bar vs conventional](trap-bar-deadlift-vs-conventional-deadlift-depth.md) is **entirely biomechanical modelling and acute data**, and he says so. Its best move: subjects lift **8.4% more** on the trap bar, so scaling Swinton's moments by that erases the hip and spinal differences and leaves the knee difference intact — quads more, glutes and erectors similar. Its hamstring claim has **no data at all** behind it.

### Calves

[Bigger calves](bigger-calves-ultimate-guide-32-studies.md) is where he **reverses himself** — the clearest disagreement in this set. [Maximize muscle growth](maximize-muscle-growth-new-study-exercise-choice.md) concluded straight-leg raises may be all you need; the 2025 guide, on three growth studies plus a swelling study all pointing the same way for the soleus, calls bent-leg work a defensible addition. Straight-leg primacy for the gastrocnemius is unchallenged. Lengthened partials produced **126.9% greater medial gastrocnemius growth** than full ROM (Kassiano 2023, untrained women, 3×/week, 8 weeks, 15–20 reps to failure); lengthened supersets 43.3% greater (Larsen 2025) — ranking the two by those figures compares across studies, a hint not a ranking, as the note flags. Rep range does not matter (Schoenfeld 2020: 6–10 versus 20–30 reps to failure grew all three calf muscles similarly, killing the slow-twitch/high-reps argument). Volume: 12 > 9 > 6 weekly sets in **61 untrained women**, with diminishing returns.

### Back: the domain with no growth data

[Build your lats](build-lats-ultimate-guide-36-studies.md) is titled "36 studies" and contains **zero direct measurements of lat growth** — he states this twice, unprompted. Everything is moment arms, isometric sEMG, and analogy from quad, glute and triceps trials. **The video has no retrievable reference list**: sources appear on screen only, unnamed by author or journal. Same for [Pullovers](pullovers-not-train-lats-uh-oh.md), a clip of it; and [Is the squat enough](squat-enough-leg-growth.md), whose seven sources are dead `bit.ly` links.

His organising move is good — classify by joint action, so close-grip vertical and horizontal pulls are both shoulder extension and should develop the lats comparably, while wide-grip rows are horizontal extension where lat leverage is poor. The regional-bias claims are the weak link: a moment-arm model chained to isometric sEMG at a joint angle **not actually reached in the exercise**, then to unmeasured regional growth. The pullover ranking (machine > cable > free weight) is a resistance-profile argument, not a result. Note too that here he argues *more* range is not automatically better — partial skull crushers and preacher curls beat their longer rivals — which sits awkwardly beside the long-length principle driving the quad and calf sections.
