## What actually makes a muscle grow

Twenty-six videos cover this, the channel's home ground and where its method is most visible: set an evidential bar, apply it to your own preferred stimulus first, and say which findings are measured and which are inferred. They agree on the headline and disagree on how confident it should be.

### The one stimulus left standing

Mechanical tension is his answer, reached by elimination rather than demonstration. In [the 87-studies mechanisms video](most-important-muscle-building-stimulus-87-studies.md) he sets three criteria — a plausible mechanism, protocols raising the stimulus producing more growth, and the ability to isolate it — then discards the classic animal evidence *for tension* because it fails the third: tenotomy, stretched-limb immobilisation and weighted chicken wings all confound tension with electrical signalling, metabolites and damage. Throwing out his own side's evidence first is the most credible move in the domain.

What replaces it is four newer studies, and their type matters enormously. A 2019 Danish experiment blocked active force production while leaving electrical input intact: activation of the key growth-signalling complex did not occur; the same group equated peak tension between eccentric contractions and pure passive stretch and found equal activation, so total tension registers rather than its origin. A 2008 US study raised active force at constant electrical input and saw signalling rise; a 2001 Canadian study found peak tension linearly related to signalling protein activation. **All four measure signalling over hours, not size over months.** He says so. The human-level support — protocols "presumed" to equate or elevate tension producing equal or greater growth — is inference, because tension cannot be measured in a training human. That is the load-bearing distinction in the whole channel: mechanism is measured in cells, outcome in people, and nothing measures both at once.

Two studies push at the definition. [Counts's no-load curls](biceps-curls-no-weight-build-muscle-optimally.md) — 13 untrained subjects, 6 weeks, one arm doing 4×20 maximal squeezes with no weight, the other 4×8–12 at 70% 1RM — produced similar biceps thickness gains at all three sites: external load is a convenient forcing function, not the stimulus. [Warneke 2023](stretching-builds-muscle-similar-lifting-weights.md), 69 subjects, found six weeks of **one hour of daily static calf stretching at 7–8/10 pain** matched 5×10–12 calf presses three times weekly for gastrocnemius thickness. Passive tension is still tension. Both videos are far more careful than their titles. [The mind-muscle note](mind-muscle-connection-help-build-more-muscle.md) is adjacent: Schoenfeld's 27 untrained men gained significantly more elbow flexor thickness with an internal focus over 10 weeks, but showed **no difference for the quads**.

His definition ends on the two levels most people miss: tension must last a decent duration and fall within one time window — ruling out 1RM work and 50-rep sets. [The 2024 myths video](90-still-believe-builds-more-muscle.md) turns that into his cleanest correction: **time under tension is not mechanical tension.** A long slow set spends most of its time far from failure; what matters is time under *high* tension, reachable anywhere in roughly 6–35 reps.

### The mechanisms that turn out not to drive growth

**Microtears.** The repair-it-bigger story is carried by two videos with 40 million combined views and no outcome data. [The microtears video](biggest-muscle-building-myth-ever-microtears.md) dismantles it using Schoenfeld's own 2012 review as the case's strongest statement. Direct evidence: a 2011 study of **14 untrained men** where a three-week ramp-in group had little damage or soreness across an eight-week eccentric cycle-ergometer programme and grew comparably to the naive high-damage group; **Damas 2016**, where the myofibrillar protein synthesis spike correlated with hypertrophy only *after* damage attenuated; and **Foley 1999**, six men, five sets of ten eccentric curls at 110% of concentric 1RM, where muscle volume finished roughly **10% below baseline** for weeks. [The repeated bout effect note](most-unappreciated-muscle-building-principle.md) closes it from the other side: maximal eccentric quad work, 5×15 weekly for 10 weeks in untrained men, saw DOMS, creatine kinase and C-reactive protein fall to near-nothing by weeks 8–10 while training continued. Damage disappears; growth does not.

**The pump.** [The pump video](pump-best-indicator-hypertrophy-true-false.md) separates measured from inferred exactly: the supportive evidence — drop sets, short rest on isolation work, constant-tension partials — comes from studies where **the pump was never measured**, and he flags that each time. The one direct test (Hirono) correlated acute quadriceps swelling with six-week hypertrophy at rho of only **0.4–0.6**. Against it: 30% 1RM gives a far bigger pump than 80% with similar growth; BFR at 20–40% 1RM gives an enormous pump and matches 70–90% loading (Lixandrão); 2.5+ minutes rest beats 90 seconds on compounds despite a smaller pump.

**Metabolic stress.** [The lactate video](huge-muscle-building-myth-22-studies.md) supplies the strongest disconfirmations. Infusing lactate into training humans raised blood and muscle lactate without raising anabolic signalling above saline — and he volunteers the design flaw favouring him (infused into the arm, not the working leg). The dissociations need no mechanism: 400 m running and sprint cycling match five sets of lower-body work or three sets of curls to failure for muscle lactate, and build almost nothing. Cuffing 3–5 minutes *after* sets added nothing and **reduced** growth in women (Dankel 2016).

**Hormones.** Four notes converge. [Acute spikes](spike-testosterone-growth-hormone-naturally-via-lifting.md): six correlation studies, four null, the positives disagreeing about which hormone matters; the arm-trials conflict (Ronnestad n=11 positive distally, West n=12 null); short rest spikes GH more and grows less. [Resting levels](lifting-weights-not-increase-testosterone.md): two meta-analyses and six trials find no chronic rise, even in men aged 60–78 starting at bioavailable testosterone around **129 ng/dL**. [Between people](myth-testosterone-muscle-hypertrophy.md): across McCall (n=11), Morton (49 trained men) and Mobley (67 untrained men), resting and intramuscular free testosterone fail to separate high from low responders. [Within a person](science-increasing-testosterone-muscle-strength.md): raising it works, but **additively** — Bhasin's four-group trial gave 6.7% quadriceps CSA for testosterone alone, 5.4% for training alone, 13.7% combined against a 12.1% sum; Finkelstein puts a realistic natural-range rise at **2–4%**.

### The open questions

The taxonomy comes from [the definitional video](science-muscle-growth-actually.md): five routes — myofibrillar hypertrophy, sarcoplasmic hypertrophy, myofibrillar packing, hyperplasia, increased length — the first three being one continuum defined by the ratio of myofibril to sarcoplasm growth. [The myofibril video](stronger-bigger-muscles-myofibrillar-packing-hypertrophy.md) is blunt that **no human studies examine the myofibril level at all**; thickness and splitting rest on chicken and mouse ultrastructure. Only fascicle length transfers, responding to long-length ROM and eccentric overload.

**Sarcoplasmic hypertrophy** is where his own position moved. The [33-studies tally](sarcoplasmic-vs-myofibrillar-hypertrophy-packing-33-studies.md) counts 15 studies finding myofibrillar hypertrophy, 11 packing, 7 sarcoplasmic — the striking cell being **zero of 12 specific-tension studies finding the decrease sarcoplasmic hypertrophy predicts**. [The dedicated video](science-sarcoplasmic-hypertrophy.md) frames high volume (21+ weekly sets) as a lever, on three of five studies classified after the fact, without asking whether a lifter should want proportionally less contractile tissue. A year later [the dense-vs-puffy video](low-reps-dense-muscles-high-reps-puffy.md) deflates it: in MacDougall's data triceps fibre CSA rose **23%** while myofibrillar relative space fell **2%**, which he doubts is visible. That is a genuine reversal of emphasis, and the later note is right. [The muscle-confusion study](muscle-confusion-produces-sarcoplasmic-hypertrophy.md) — 20 trained men, within-subject — found myofibrillar space holding at ~70% in the constant leg and falling to ~61% in the varied leg, before he catches an internal contradiction in the paper and notes unmeasured swelling could explain all of it.

**Hyperplasia.** [The fibre-number video](gain-more-muscle-fibers-training.md): animal meta-analysis gives +11.6% under weight training (five studies, all positive), +21% stretch, +5.4% ablation. A human biceps holds an estimated **198,000–419,000** fibres, nobody has counted them, and every human study infers backwards from biopsies. He lays out three defensible positions and picks none.

**Deceleration.** [The 16-studies video](muscle-growth-slows-down-fast-16-studies.md) rejects Counts's three-month-plateau claim: four studies show quads levelling at 6–8 weeks, but a 1970 study found no biceps plateau across 100 days and Narici found quads rising across six months. His reinterpretation — that untrained people grow for three months while ignoring nutrition and variation — is untested. [The newest video](much-muscle-gain-causes-growth.md) puts lifetime natural gain at roughly **5 / 12 / 21 kg** fat-free mass for low, average and high responders, and warns against building programmes on speculation about what maximises tension when outcome data says the variable doesn't matter — him correcting his own framework.

### Rigour, and where to hold the line

Rigorous: leading with the study contradicting the popular one ([androgen receptors](hidden-key-building-muscle-legit.md), where Morton's 49 trained men and Mobley's 67 untrained men disagree and he picks no winner); killing the systemic-hormone explanation for [cross-education](cross-education-effect-training-one-limb-increases.md) with his own hormone evidence; demolishing surface EMG normalisation in [the activation video](change-muscle-activation-any-compound-exercise-maybe.md) before conceding what survives it.

Where it outruns evidence: his damage position hardened then softened — the 2022 microtears video treats it as settled while the 87-studies video from the same year, the 2024 myths video and the 2025 review all allow a threshold role, and Schoenfeld, whose reviews supply the demolished case, stays more agnostic than any. [The calf video](calf-training-worth.md) rests entirely on between-study comparisons.

The citation problem is worst where the stakes are highest. **The 87-studies video — the intellectual centre of the channel — cites on screen only; its description carries no reference list, so all eighteen studies underpinning the tension case are unverified.** The same holds for the sarcoplasmic, hyperplasia, myofibrillar-packing, 2024-myths and calf videos. Those claims may be sound; they are not checkable.
