## Range of motion, stretch and tempo

### The arc, 2021 to 2025

This is the domain where the channel's own position visibly moved, and the notes date every step.

**June 2021 — the opening move.** [Full range of motion is NOT optimal](full-range-motion-not-optimal-building-muscle.md) is the founding argument. Six prior studies had compared full to partial ROM; four favoured full, and in all four the partial trained the muscle at a **short or neutral** length. His conclusion is narrow and correct: those test short-length partials, not partials as a category. Pedrosa — **45 untrained women, 12 weeks, knee extension**, five groups, each group's 60% 1RM retested fortnightly against its *own* range — found the long-length partial (100°–65°) grew best overall, beating full ROM at 50%, 60% and especially 70% of femur length. He proposed mechanical tension (voluntary activation rising from **93.6% at 50° knee flexion to 98.5% at 100°**, plus passive titin force), explicitly rejecting metabolic stress and damage, then spent three minutes hedging: one study, untrained women, two of four quad heads, and a knee extension whose resistance profile flatters the result.

**April 2022 — peak optimism.** [TRIPLE hypertrophy](triple-hypertrophy-training-muscle-like.md) is the high-water mark. Sato's preacher curl trial — **32 untrained subjects, 5 weeks**, 0–50° elbow flexion versus 80–130° — gave the long group roughly **three times** the average elbow flexor thickness gain, driven almost entirely by the 70% site. He flagged immediately that there was **no full-ROM group**, and that the full-versus-lengthened-partial claim still rested on Pedrosa alone.

**October 2022 — the sweep.** [Stretch-mediated hypertrophy](key-more-muscle-stretch-mediated-hypertrophy.md) generalised the pattern to quads, glutes, adductors, hamstrings, biceps and triceps, largest **distally**, while stating plainly there was then **no direct research at all** on back, shoulders, calves, abs or chest. He still refused to say lengthened partials beat full ROM.

**August 2024 — the regional framing.** [Optimize muscle growth](optimize-muscle-growth-most-people-don-t.md) recasts ROM as a regional question. Short-length partials grow proximal and middle regions decently but **fail distally** — in Bloomquist's squat trial the distal thigh actually *lost* size. All three studies then favouring lengthened partials used **untrained** subjects, and he says so.

**September 2024 — the turn.** [It's finally here](finally-here-new-study-muscle-growth-epic.md) covers Wolf et al.: **25 trained individuals, 4.9 years average experience, 8 weeks**, within-subject, one side full ROM and one lengthened partials on every exercise. Elbow flexor and triceps thickness at 45%, 50% and 55% of humerus length grew **similarly** — the first trained-lifter trial, and null. He still reads it as informative (the peak squeeze at the shortened position is *dispensable*) and raises the sharpest caveat himself: **only the 45–55% regions were measured**, precisely where the effect is smallest. Worth recording — the study was **funded by Jeff Nippard**, another creator in this library, disclosed in [Most important studies of 2024](most-important-muscle-building-studies-2024.md).

**April 2025 — the counterweight consolidates.** [Training beyond failure](training-beyond-failure-new-study-epic.md) puts both trained-subject nulls side by side: **n=25** and a full-body trial with **n=297**, neither finding an arm or thigh difference. He does not simply accept them — Wolf has an open dispute over how the partials were standardised, the 297-subject trial used low volumes, and **both measured only mid-region** — but he stops recommending lengthened partials generally. Only calves survive as a confident recommendation.

**September 2025 — the reconciliation.** [Faster muscle growth](faster-muscle-growth-new-study-epic.md) tests the distal hypothesis directly: **13 trained lifters, ~6 years experience, 8 weeks**, unilateral preacher curls, full ROM (0–140°) versus lengthened partial (0–70°). The 50% region was a dead heat; the 70% favoured partials at **Bayes factor 4.87** — moderate evidence, not a win. Of six studies, all three in untrained favour partials and all three in trained show similar growth — but two of those three could not have seen a distal effect.

### Where it stands

Lengthened partials are **at least as good as full ROM, occasionally slightly better distally**; nothing has found them inferior; and the untrained/trained split plus the mid-region measurement problem explains most of the contradiction. Full ROM remains a defensible default. Separately — and he is emphatic on the distinction — **exercise selection reaching longer maximum lengths still wins**: seated over lying leg curls (Maeo 2021, with biceps femoris short head as internal control), overhead over neutral triceps extensions, leaning-back over normal leg extensions. He flags a possible **threshold**: lying leg extensions lengthen rectus femoris more still, and were *not* better.

The related results hold up independently. [Partial squats](partial-squats-superior-deep-squats-size-strength.md): 60° knee flexion grows only the proximal thigh; 90° suffices for quads but leaves **glute max and adductors** on the table (Kubo, 17 men, 10 weeks). [Half bench press reps](half-bench-press-reps-more-strength-size.md): Martínez-Cava, **49 men, 10 weeks**, range-specific 1RMs — full-range training beat the partials at full-, two-thirds- *and* one-third-range 1RM, inverting specificity. [Isometrics](they-said-worst-way-build-muscle.md) fits the same logic: **23 trained subjects, 6 weeks**, long-length (~125°) isometrics matched full-range knee extensions.

### Tempo and time under tension: a separate question, cleaner answer

Stretch is about *where* in the range you load, tempo about *how fast*. Unlike ROM, the tempo answer has not moved since 2021.

[Time under tension is overrated](time-under-tension-overrated-10-studies.md) supplies the decisive methodological point. The three studies that appear to show slow tempo builds more muscle (Usui, Watanabe 2013, Watanabe 2014) **fixed load and rep count**, so the slow group was necessarily closer to failure: they measure effort, not tempo. When effort is controlled — Lacerda, **within-subject, 14 weeks**, 2s versus 6s reps to failure; Chaves, 4s versus self-selected ~1.8s — growth is equal, and Schoenfeld's meta-analysis puts **0.5–8 seconds per rep** at parity. Morton 2019 backs it mechanistically: a **threefold TUT spread** across four conditions to failure produced no difference in glycogen-depletion fibre recruitment. [The most overrated principle](most-overrated-muscle-building-principle.md) kills the **30–60 second rule** — Lacerda's fast sets ran 25–38 s and grew the same.

[The best rep speed](best-rep-speed-size.md), October 2025, closes it: a meta-analysis of **14 studies, 278 subjects**, restricted to trials manipulating one phase in isolation, found **no clear advantage either way** — eccentric SMD **0.06**, about 0.9 percentage points, credible interval spanning zero. Both conditions grew **5–6%**, the literature-wide norm. The single exception across every tempo note is **super-slow**: Schuenke's 10s/4s protocol underperformed for fibre CSA, and [SuperSlow training](superslow-training-produce-super-fast-gains.md) dismantles the force-velocity argument for it — with a fixed load, moving slower *reduces* acceleration and therefore force. [Regional tempo effects](repetition-tempo-impact-regional-muscle-growth.md) is the weakest note here: three studies, one significant regional difference, and the flagship Earp comparison confounds tempo with load and effort.

### Rigour and overreach

He is unusually rigorous in three recurring ways: he explains statistics before using them (forest plots, credible intervals, converting SMD 0.06 into a usable percentage); he discards evidence that would help him, throwing out the squat-versus-hip-thrust glute paper over data-validity concerns in [Stretch-mediated hypertrophy](key-more-muscle-stretch-mediated-hypertrophy.md); and he dismantles the study that best supports his own premise, as with the Japanese concentric-versus-eccentric trial in [Mentzer](fastest-way-build-muscle-says-mike-mentzer.md).

Where conclusions outrun evidence: the chest section of [Stretch-mediated hypertrophy](key-more-muscle-stretch-mediated-hypertrophy.md) builds a hypertrophy conclusion entirely on a **strength** study with no growth measurement, and [Half bench press reps](half-bench-press-reps-more-strength-size.md) repeats it — "almost certain" full-range benching grows more, on zero bench hypertrophy data. [Constant tension](constant-tension-key-building-more-muscle.md) reclassifies Pedrosa post hoc to keep alive a technique his own data (two null Tanimoto trials, a Goto/Pinto contradiction) suggests is inert. [Biceps growth](have-talk-about-biceps-growth.md) leans on an arm-circumference result from **n=7** in the same breath as calling circumference the crudest measure available. And the 9-second TUT floor and 84-second ceiling in [The most overrated principle](most-overrated-muscle-building-principle.md) rest on one study plus an author's Instagram comment.
