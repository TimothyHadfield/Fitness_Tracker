## Chest, shoulders and arms

### Which pressing variation should I use?

Whichever you like. The bench press has the best growth data of any upper-body exercise here: [Is the Barbell Bench Press Enough for the Chest?](bench-press-enough-chest-growth-24-studies.md) assembles five longitudinal trials and benchmarks them against Wernbom's pooled elbow-flexor rate (0.20%/day). Ogasawara's 24-week study (untrained men, 3×10 at 75% 1RM, 3×/week) produced +43% pec thickness, 0.26%/day; Yasuda's six-week trial hit 0.42%/day; Davies' trained lifters (n=10, 4×5 at 85%, 8 weeks) reached 0.18%/day at the mid pec. He flags every time that thickness and cross-sectional area are being compared against a CSA benchmark.

Everything else is a tie. One direct hypertrophy comparison found machine chest press ≈ flat barbell bench. Push-ups match too, in [Bench Press vs Push Ups](bench-press-vs-push-ups-which-best.md) and [Push-Ups vs the Bench Press](push-ups-vs-bench-press-muscle-hypertrophy.md): Kikuchi (18 trained men, 40% 1RM, 3 sets to failure, 2×/week, 8 weeks — similar pec and triceps thickness) and Kotarsky (23 novices, 4 weeks — neither group significant). The real constraint is load availability, not stimulus: growth holds down to ~30% 1RM, roughly a 35-rep set, and a standard push-up loads ~64% of body weight. Smith machine rests on **five EMG studies and zero hypertrophy data**, which he labels a "tentative hypothesis" in [The BEST Chest Exercises](best-chest-exercises-building-muscle-33-studies.md). Dips have neither.

### Do I need incline work? Yes — but the reason is arithmetic

[How Effective Is the Flat Bench Press for Upper, Middle, and Lower Pec](effective-flat-bench-press-upper-middle-lower.md) is his cleanest piece of reasoning in the domain. Ogasawara's regional CSA gains were upper 36.3%, middle 37.3%, lower 40.0% — near-identical percentages. But baseline upper pec CSA was ~15 cm² against 25–27 cm² for middle and lower, so the same percentage bought **5.5 cm² versus ~10 cm²**. Flat pressing doesn't skip the upper chest; it grows a smaller muscle by the same proportion. Lower-pec work is correspondingly unnecessary — that region has no arithmetic problem.

Which upper-pec exercise is a different matter, and here everything is **EMG**: 5 of 7 studies favour incline for upper-pec activity, 4 of 8 favour a closer grip, 1 of 2 a reverse grip, and the two head-to-head comparisons he leans on are non-significant results from a single paper ([Blow up Your Upper Chest](blow-upper-chest-exercises.md)). Angle is unresolved — 30°, 44°, or no difference.

**Where his position changed.** Chavez et al. (47 untrained men, 1×/week for 8 weeks, incline vs flat vs both) is treated in 2021 as "the one longitudinal study" and the basis for recommending incline. By [Grow Your Chest](grow-chest-most-detailed-guide-internet-72.md) in 2024 he dismantles it: 54–62% thickness gains across **eight training sessions** is ~4.6% growth per workout against a literature averaging 33 sessions, and only 30 of the 47 completers appear in the hypertrophy data, unexplained. Throwing out the study that best supports his own recommendation is the most creditable ninety seconds in the domain.

### If I bench, do I still need direct triceps work?

Yes, and this is the best-evidenced claim in the domain. Brandão 2020 (43 untrained men, four groups, 80% 1RM to failure, 3 min rest, 2×/week for 10 weeks, MRI by head) is covered in [Is the Bench Press Enough for Triceps Hypertrophy?](bench-press-enough-triceps-hypertrophy.md) and [Build Your Triceps Faster](build-triceps-faster-science-simplified.md): bench grew the **lateral head** well and the **long head** barely at all; skull crushers did the reverse; the groups doing both grew all three. The mechanism is clean: the long head alone crosses the shoulder, and shortens there during the press eccentric.

Two cautions: he twice overrides non-significant whole-triceps and medial-head results by calling them type II error, in a study with ~10–11 subjects per cell; and the combined-exercise groups did double the set volume, so only bench-only vs skullcrusher-only is volume-matched.

### Which triceps isolation exercise?

Overhead extension. Maeo (21 untrained adults, within-subject, 12 weeks, 5×10, ROM matched 0–90°, alternating which arm went first) found whole-triceps volume **1.4×**, long head **1.5×**, and medial+lateral combined **1.4×** favouring overhead — despite heavier absolute loads on the pushdown ([The BEST Triceps Hypertrophy Exercise](best-triceps-hypertrophy-exercise.md)). The medial/lateral result has no accepted explanation. Stasinaki (n=9, 6 weeks) was null and always ran pushdowns first each session, a confound he identifies himself.

**Two position changes converge here.** [The ULTIMATE Guide to Building Triceps](ultimate-guide-building-triceps-63-studies.md) (2021) had only the n=9 null and hedged correctly; he adopted Maeo by 2023. That same guide treats the pushdown's hardest-at-lockout profile as a *virtue*, complementary to the skull crusher; by the 2024 chest guide he argues that tension near the shortened position adds little. Both readings cannot stand.

**Reference gap.** The 63-study triceps guide has **no retrievable reference list**. Its 30–45 weekly-set recommendation, its five-study frequency table pointing four different ways, and the fibre-subpopulation argument for 2–3 isolation exercises are all unverifiable.

### Do rows and pulldowns cover my biceps? His answer moved

In 2021, [Rows and Lat Pulldowns Are Not Enough](rows-lat-pulldowns-enough-biceps-growth.md) reports Mannarino (10 untrained men, within-subject, 8 weeks, 4–6 sets of 8–12 to failure, thickness averaged at 25/50/75%): curl arm ~11%, row arm ~5%. In 2025, [Compound Exercises Are Enough for the Biceps](compound-exercises-enough-biceps.md) reports the same team's follow-up (9 untrained men, 8 weeks) splitting it regionally: **curls won at 75%, rows matched them at 25%**. He emailed the authors and confirmed subject overlap between the two papers. His conclusion softens from "rows are not enough" to "back work is not a null stimulus, but isolation is still needed to maximise". The honest reading is his own: nine untrained subjects, and non-significance is not equivalence. One inconsistency — the Gentil 2015 pulldown-vs-curl trial (29 untrained men, 10 weeks, no difference at a single site) is used without comment in 2021 and 2025, while [The ULTIMATE Guide to Biceps Growth](ultimate-guide-biceps-growth-73-studies.md) raises a research-integrity concern about that author.

### Which curl?

His 2023 hypothesis — a curl with the shoulder extended, for the long-head stretch — rested on quadriceps isometrics, preacher-curl partials (Sato and Pedrosa: initial-position partials beat final-position, concentrated at the 70% region) and the Maeo triceps analogy ([The Most EFFECTIVE Biceps Exercise](most-effective-biceps-exercise-not-think.md)). No direct test existed.

Four now do, and he handled the sequence unusually well. [Incline vs Preacher Curls (2023)](incline-vs-preacher-curls-biceps-growth-2.md) opens by saying the first trial **raises the possibility he was wrong** — Zabaleta-Korta's 31 women trended toward preacher at all three regions, though nothing was significant, including the incline group's own gains. Vendruscolo (21 trained men, BFR, **3 weeks**) was null. Kassiano 2025 (63 untrained women, 3×8–12 to failure, 2×/week, 8 weeks) split it: 50% favoured incline, 60% similar, 70% favoured preacher ([2025 note](incline-vs-preacher-curls-biceps-growth.md)). Attarieh (15 untrained men, within-subject, 10 weeks, **resistance profiles matched at ~60° elbow flexion**, biceps and brachialis measured separately) found no significant difference anywhere ([Bigger Biceps: This NEW Study](bigger-biceps-new-study-interesting.md)).

His muscle-by-muscle resolution — incline for biceps, preacher for brachialis and brachioradialis — rests substantially on **Kobayashi, a conference abstract with no full text**. He flags it, but he also rejects the obvious alternative reading (the two studies favouring incline used untrained subjects, the two nulls used trained) in favour of weighing study quality. That is a judgement, and the confound remains available.

Two things dwarf curl choice, both from the 73-study guide, which — like the triceps guide — has **no retrievable reference list at all**. Effort: Martorelli (89 untrained women, barbell curl only, 70% 1RM, 10 weeks) found failure beat stopping 5–6 reps short by a large margin, and a fourth non-failure set barely closed it. Measurement: the biceps is only **46%** of elbow flexor volume (brachialis 37%, brachioradialis 17%), so most of this literature measures something half made of another muscle.

### Side delts, rear delts and forearms: rankings without growth data

This is where confidence and evidence diverge most. [How to Build 3D Shoulders](build-3d-shoulders-25-studies.md), [Bigger Side Delts](bigger-side-delts-ranking-best-exercises-using.md), [How to Build Your Rear Delts](build-rear-delts-28-studies.md) and both forearm notes ([guide](build-forearms-faster-most-detailed-guide-web.md), [ranking](bigger-forearms-ranking-best-exercises-using-science.md)) contain **not one hypertrophy outcome study on the muscle being ranked**. Everything is moment-arm modelling, EMG, and the long-muscle-length literature transplanted from hamstrings, calves and quads, mostly in untrained subjects. He says so in dedicated limitations sections, and his heuristic — EMG is more trustworthy comparing *similar* exercises than dissimilar ones — is applied consistently. The tier-list format still implies more certainty than the method supports.

**The prediction that failed is the most instructive item here.** The 2024 side delt ranking put cable lateral raises in S tier and dumbbells in A on resistance-profile logic, while disclosing he was running the trial that would test it. [We Have to Talk About Lateral Raises](have-talk-about-lateral-raises.md) reports it: 24 trained lifters averaging 7.1 years, one arm cable and one dumbbell, 5 sets to failure at 12–16 reps, 2×/week for 8 weeks — **similar growth proximally and distally**. He leads with the failure, notes growth in both arms sat near measurement error, and reorganises the length literature into three categories — resistance challenge, range of motion, biarticular position — with long-length advantages surviving best in the last.

The best-sourced section in the domain is the impingement defence of upright rows — the only place resting on **outcome data**, notably subacromial decompression failing to beat placebo surgery, backed by a Cochrane review. The weakest is the biceps guide's supination argument, built on fine-wire EMG in a video that spends three minutes rejecting EMG for exercise selection, and contradicted elsewhere in the library on the grounds that supination *shortens* the biceps. [Should You Overhead Press](should-overhead-press-muscle-growth.md) is likewise moment arms and EMG throughout, and [This Arm Muscle is Underrated](arm-muscle-underrated-don-t-ignore.md) ranks brachialis exercises on **one 1995 study** plus a pump measure. His forearm work does something rarer: the received wisdom that neutral or pronated grips favour the brachioradialis fails the EMG count (2 pronated, 3 no difference, **4 supinated**), and he reports that against his audience's expectations.
