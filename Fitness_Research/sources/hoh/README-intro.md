# House of Hypertrophy - video notes

Notes on all 169 videos from the House of Hypertrophy YouTube channel. Nothing was excluded:
unlike every other source in this library, this channel has no vlogs, no physique updates and
no personal content to filter out. Every video is hypertrophy mechanisms, a single training
variable examined in depth, or a new study read closely.

**This is the most densely cited source in the library** - 1,557 reference citations across the
channel, 977 unique links, 745 of them resolved to a full citation with a DOI or PubMed ID. One
video on rep ranges alone cites 48 papers. The full bibliography is
[RESEARCH-CITATIONS.md](RESEARCH-CITATIONS.md).

It is also the narrowest. The median video is 12 minutes on one question, and the notes are
sized to match - a video that spends eleven minutes on whether the pump predicts growth gets a
note about that and nothing else.

## What to know before reading

**The creator is self-taught and has no formal credentials.** He says so himself, on his own
About page. He is worth reading anyway, because in practice he is more careful than most people
who do have them: he routinely presents the study that contradicts his own conclusion, states
sample sizes and training status, separates statistical significance from practical relevance,
and publicly reverses positions when new data arrives. The notes point this out where it happens,
and they point out the opposite too - where a conclusion rests on EMG, acute measures or a
mechanism rather than on measured growth.

**Twenty-six videos have no retrievable reference list**, and they are disproportionately his
longest and best work - the 73-study biceps guide, the 87-study mechanisms video, the 63-study
triceps guide. Those cite on screen only, and the descriptions carry nothing. Their notes say so
plainly and flag every study named aloud as unverified rather than inventing a citation. Reading
one of those notes, you are reading a summary of his argument, not a traceable chain to the
literature.

**Twenty-five videos carry his own topic-grouped reference lists** - his sub-headings, mapping
his claims to his sources. Where that grouping exists the notes preserve it, because it is a far
better claim-to-source map than anything that could be inferred from a transcript.

Where he contradicts material held elsewhere in this library - and he does, on rep-range floors,
on set-volume ceilings, on whether lengthened partials beat full range of motion, and on how much
weight to put on EMG - the notes mark the clash rather than smoothing it.
