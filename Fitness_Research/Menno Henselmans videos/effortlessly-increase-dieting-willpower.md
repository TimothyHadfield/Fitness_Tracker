# Willpower Doesn't Run Out — Happiness Does

**Source:** Menno Henselmans, "How to effortlessly increase your dieting willpower" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e4whoagNKyk)) · 2023-03-08 · 5 min
**Topic:** Psychology & behaviour

## Bottom line
- The muscle model of willpower — a finite tank that depletes with use and needs recharging — is, in his view, **wrong**. Something does decline over a day, which is why the model survived so long, but what declines is **happiness**, not a fuel.
- His mechanism: the anterior cingulate cortex monitors affect and regulates attention. When an activity generates no positive feeling, unhappiness accumulates and the ACC progressively redirects attention elsewhere. That redirection is what we experience as willpower failure.
- Practical consequence: **the same activity costs different amounts depending on how you perceive it.** His example is his girlfriend returning from a conference exhausted after a day of lectures he had spent voluntarily and found much less draining.
- The lever is therefore not more discipline but **more scheduled enjoyment** — hobbies, positive social contact, anything you genuinely like. Keep affect high and self-control becomes, in his phrasing, effectively unlimited.
- If you know you will be depleted after work, **plan something you will enjoy** before the gym or the prepped meal — and make it an active choice (one specific episode) rather than open-ended scrolling.

## The study
The paper he builds on is, by its title, *Positive emotions and their upregulation increase willingness to consume healthy foods*. What he takes from it is the direct link between affect and food choice: raise positive emotion and adherence improves without any extra effort of will.

He extends this to two adjacent claims: that mental fatigue is unhappiness rather than depletion, and that **central nervous system fatigue is "largely a myth"** because the brain is software and software does not fatigue. Pushing hard in the gym is unpleasant in the short term, and on this account it is the displeasure — not a physiological drain — that registers as fatigue.

## Practical stack
He is explicit that habit engineering is not this video's point but flags it as the prior step: meal plans, food prepped in advance, so no decision has to be made while hungry. The contribution here sits on top of that — treat your week as needing a budget of enjoyment, placed where you know your affect will bottom out.

## Overreach
One study carries a very large theoretical load here. The ego-depletion literature is genuinely contested and his position is defensible, but he presents the affect-based replacement model as settled rather than as one live account. The CNS-fatigue dismissal is a bigger stretch — asserted in a sentence, uncited, and resting on an analogy rather than evidence.
