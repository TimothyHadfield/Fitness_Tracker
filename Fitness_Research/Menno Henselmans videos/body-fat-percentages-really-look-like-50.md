# What Female Body Fat Percentages Actually Look Like: 50 Lab-Grade Scans

**Source:** Menno Henselmans, "What body fat percentages REALLY look like: 50 actual women's DEXA scans" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4jVmJ5w3fF8)) · 2025-08-29 · 5 min
**Topic:** Physiology

## Bottom line
- The body-fat comparison charts circulating on social media are mostly **stock photos with numbers invented by whoever made the graphic**. This is his own decade-long collection of client and student photos, each with a real measurement attached — mostly standardised, calibrated DEXA scans, a few by hydrostatic weighing or InBody.
- **Calipers and bioelectrical impedance scales systematically underestimate** body fat in women who are leaner or more muscular than average. People like the flattering number, so the bad estimate propagates.
- **~13% is the practical floor for women** — the top of the essential fat range. Claims of 5% are not survivable. Below 13% there is almost no subcutaneous fat left; what remains is visceral, neural and breast tissue.
- Most readers will find the numbers **higher than expected at every point on the scale**: 25% is lean and healthy, 21% is bikini-model level, 17% is stage-lean.
- **33% is the usual overweight threshold and 39% the obese threshold** by most standards.

## The scale, as he presents it

| Body fat | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **13–14%** | Absolute floor; upper end of essential fat. Shredded, competition-lean, very little subcutaneous fat anywhere. |
| **16%** | Physique-show lean — competitive at this level given enough muscle mass. |
| **17%** | Common bikini-competition condition. Abdominal definition visible unflexed, no love handles left. Full six-packs are rarer in women than men unless the abs are trained directly. |
| **18–20%** | Very lean, close in appearance to 17%. |
| **21%** | Still very lean — no love handles, no gut. "Essentially bikini model level" for many women. |
| **22%** | Already very lean; the visible gap to 17% is smaller than most expect. |
| **23%** | No protruding fat stores, but a thicker overall look. |
| **25%** | Lean, healthy, aspirational for most women. |
| **26%** | The first hint of love handles. |
| **27%** | Not fat by any reasonable standard, but love handles and clothing indentation appear. |
| **32%** | Clearly carrying fat; approaching the overweight cutoff. |
| **37%** | Overweight by most standards (threshold ~33%). |
| **39%** | The usual obesity threshold. Even distribution can hide a lot at this level. |
| **44%** | Obese. |

His illustrative case is a client who later turned IFBB Pro, shown at 39%, 25% and 14% — the auto-captions garble her name, so it is not recorded here. He points out she carries 39% unusually well because her fat distribution is very even, and that most women would not.

## Why the same number looks different on different women
Two caveats he stresses, and they matter as much as the table:

- **DEXA is not exact.** Even fasted, standardised and calibrated, scans are commonly a few percentage points off.
- **Distribution varies enormously.** A pear-shaped storage pattern (hips, thighs, glutes) and a centralised pattern produce very different-looking bodies at identical percentages, and a photo only shows part of the physique.

Both of these argue for treating the table as a band rather than a lookup.

## Fit with the rest of the library
This is the women's counterpart to his men's video, already in this library as `body-fat-percentages-really-look-like-50-2.md`. The methodological complaints are identical — invented chart numbers, calipers and BIA underestimating in trained people — and the practical conclusion is the same: **most lifters guess several percentage points too low**. The absolute numbers are not transferable between the two: women's essential-fat floor of ~13% sits above the 2–5% he gives for men, so the whole scale shifts roughly 8–10 points.
