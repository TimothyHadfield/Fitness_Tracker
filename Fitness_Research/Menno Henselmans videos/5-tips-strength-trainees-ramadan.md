# Training Through Ramadan: Five Practical Tips

**Source:** Menno Henselmans, "5 Tips for strength trainees in Ramadan" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5wilNwOqLQ)) · 2023-03-28 · 5 min
**Topic:** Psychology & behaviour

## Bottom line
- **Wake up later if you can.** It's the single easiest way to cut fasting hours, since the fast runs sunrise to sunset regardless of when you get up.
- **Pick one of three schedules and hold it.** Consistency matters more than which one — the body adapts, and hunger reorganises around fixed meal times. Chaotic schedules produce random hunger, disrupted sleep, and sleep deprivation drives appetite up, making everything harder.
- **Break the fast with a light meal, train, then eat the big meal.** A large meal after a long fast makes most people crash.
- **The main difficulty is mental, not physiological.** A sub-24-hour fast is no threat to health; dehydration is the more real concern.
- **Avoid fasted training if you can, do it anyway if you can't.** Training beats not training.
- **Ramadan is not meant to be suffering.** He quotes the Quran — "Allah intends for you ease and does not intend for you hardship" — and notes fasting days can be postponed if fasting genuinely endangers your health.

## The three schedules

**Schedule 1 — regular intermittent fast.** Wake late, break the fast with a light meal, train, then eat the big meal. Repeat daily. He calls this **physiologically ideal**: you avoid fasted training and get carbohydrate and protein in before the session — **at least 15 g carbohydrate and at least 0.4 g/kg protein**, more if you want. The catch is discipline: right after breaking a long fast, most people don't feel like training.

**Schedule 2 — early riser, two feeding windows.** Wake very early and break the fast in the morning. Works best in tropical time zones with roughly 6-to-6 daylight — you'd wake around 4am and go to bed early. Meal, daytime fast, train, big meal, second meal before an early bed, then the night fast. Splitting the day into two fasts makes each one comparatively easy.

**Schedule 3 — the siesta variant.** For people who can't manage either of the above; he associates it with Mediterranean countries like Spain. Wake before the fast starts, eat, go back to sleep, fast through the day, then train and eat at the end. Because it fragments your sleep, an afternoon nap is usually necessary.

His warning on schedule 3: **be careful with sleep deprivation.** It raises appetite and compounds over the month. Keep sleep intact and Ramadan gets easier as it goes; let it degrade and it gets steadily worse.

## Mindset
The physical demand is modest; the psychological demand is the whole problem. Knowing you are physically capable of the fast removes most of the difficulty. The practical advice is behavioural: **stay busy**. Sitting around thinking about how hard it is, and about food, is the worst possible use of the fasting hours.

## Notes
This is coaching guidance, not a research review — no studies cited, no fasting or performance literature discussed. The pre-workout numbers (15 g carbohydrate, 0.4 g/kg protein) are the only quantitative claims and arrive unsourced. His aside that fasting "may have positive health benefits" is the one line that runs ahead of what he presents.
