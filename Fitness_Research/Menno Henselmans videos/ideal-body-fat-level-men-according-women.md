# The Most Attractive Male Body Fat Level Is About 12%

**Source:** Menno Henselmans, "This is the ideal body fat level for men according to women" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s_IHYfGb-GI)) · 2025-06-03 · 5 min
**Topic:** Psychology & behaviour

## Bottom line
- Peak rated attractiveness for male bodies sits at roughly **10–15% body fat, optimum around 12%**. Within the healthy range the differences are small; ratings fall off sharply at overweight/obese levels and, less steeply, at very low levels.
- BMI shows the same shape, with an **optimum around 23** — much worse above, somewhat worse below.
- **Shoulder-to-waist ratio: 1.6** is the target, the so-called golden ratio. Broader than that adds essentially nothing but costs almost nothing either, until it looks cartoonish. Narrower shoulders or a thicker waist is clearly penalised.
- **Men and women agreed**, and the results held across China, Lithuania and the UK.
- Why leanness can be penalised: not because low body fat is unhealthy at these levels, but because it usually signals **low fat-free mass**. With enough muscle behind it, leaner reads as stronger.

## How the study was run
Participants rated photographs of male bodies selected to vary independently in body fat percentage, shoulder-to-waist ratio and BMI. Images were **black-and-white and anonymised** to strip out height, facial attractiveness and skin colour. Body fat was measured by **DEXA**, not estimated. Three national samples gave cross-cultural validation and both sexes rated the same images. He concedes the images look abstract, and argues the results still line up with research using more naturalistic photos.

## The muscle interaction
His most useful point is that body fat percentage is not an independent variable. Below roughly 10%, attractiveness declines mainly in malnourished or simply very skinny people, where low body fat reads as emaciated. With sufficient fat-free mass, the same number reads as definition and strength — and it also holds arithmetically, since the same absolute fat mass yields a lower percentage as lean mass rises. The prediction: **the more muscle you carry, the leaner you can get and stay attractive.** The shoulder-to-waist data show the same dual signal of muscularity plus leanness.

## The evolutionary framing
He anchors everything to survival and fertility: attractiveness ratings track health outcomes closely, and the researchers noted the most attractive male BMI sat near the level predicted for maximum survival. High body fat reads as unattractive because it signals worse health and lower fertility; broad shoulders signal the provide-and-protect role, with limited downside to overshooting — hence a plateau above 1.6 rather than a decline. The male-female agreement he attributes to it being evolutionarily advantageous for both sexes to read attractiveness in their own population the same way.

## Overreach
**The study is never cited.** The description's reference block holds only his own body-fat photo guide and another of his videos, so no figure here — the 12% optimum, BMI 23, the 1.6 ratio — is traceable to a source.

The evolutionary account is presented as established rather than as interpretation. "Genetically hardwired" is a strong claim from photo-rating data, and agreement across three national samples is consistent with, but does not demonstrate, a genetic mechanism. The alignment between attractiveness and survival optima is a correlation the researchers noted, not a causal chain the study tested.
