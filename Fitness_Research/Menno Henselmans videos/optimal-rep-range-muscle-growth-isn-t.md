# High Reps Are Underrated — And May Bias Growth Toward Type I Fibres

**Source:** Menno Henselmans, "The optimal rep range for muscle growth isn’t what you think" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g_43DyqsP-8)) · 2026-04-14 · 8 min
**Topic:** Training

## Bottom line
- A new within-participant study trained one side of the body with **8-12 reps** and the other with **20-25 reps** (biceps and quads), both to volitional failure. After 10 weeks there was **no difference** in ultrasound-measured muscle size or myofibrillar protein synthesis.
- His read of the totality: roughly **4 to 30 reps — probably higher — builds equivalent muscle**, provided each set goes close to failure.
- A second study (an **8-study meta-analysis, still preprint**) suggested 20-30% 1RM favours **type I** fibre growth while heavier loads favour **type II**. He treats it as weak and partly self-contradictory.
- Practical recommendation regardless: **use a wide variety of rep ranges** — heavier for compounds, higher reps for isolation — via daily undulating periodization.
- The real limiter on high-rep training is that almost nobody reaches true momentary failure at very high reps.

## Why the load-doesn't-matter finding is strong here
Same person, same failure proximity, two loads, two independent outcome measures (muscle size and myofibrillar protein synthesis), across two muscle groups. That removes between-subject variability and makes the null on load hard to explain away.

## The fibre-type claim, and why he doesn't buy it
The intuition is appealing: endurance-style training grows endurance fibres. He gives four reasons for scepticism.

1. **Prior evidence is conflicted.** An earlier meta-analysis comparing rep ranges found no difference in which fibres grew. BFR work and some Russian constant-tension studies did find greater type I hypertrophy at very high reps; many other studies find nothing.
2. **The size principle argues against it.** Larger, type II-rich motor units are recruited later in a set as fatigue reduces force output. Go close to failure with 30 reps and you still recruit them, just as with an 8RM.
3. **The models are logically contradictory.** Type I and type II growth must sum to total growth. There was no effect on total growth, yet the analysis showed limited type I effect and a larger type II effect — the graphs should mirror each other and don't.
4. **The data were statistically weak**, and contrasts were only meaningful at 20-30% 1RM, which is also why most studies find nothing.

Where he does find a mechanism plausible is the *opposite* direction from the headline: **type I fibres get more time under tension in a high-rep set.** In a 30-rep set the first ~20 reps are essentially fatigue accumulation for the higher-threshold units, which only kick in for the last eight or so — but the type I fibres are active for all 30, versus eight in an 8RM set.

## The failure-proximity caveat
His strongest practical objection to high-rep work. People are demonstrably bad at judging proximity to failure at high reps: with a set of eight you feel it by rep six and misjudging costs one or two reps, whereas at a 30RM you think you're close around rep 20 and still have ten grinding reps left. Squats at a 30RM "basically turn into cardio." If you don't get close enough to failure, the highest-threshold motor units never accumulate meaningful time under tension.

## What he actually programs
A wide spread of rep ranges via **daily undulating periodization**, with **higher reps for isolation and lower reps for compounds** — partly the research, partly practicality: compounds degenerate into cardio at high reps, and isolation exercises progress more easily in reps than load (adding 5-10 kg to a cable stack for lateral raises is a large jump, especially for women). Claimed secondary benefits are joint comfort and fatigue management.

**Overreach flag:** the fibre-type material is a preprint meta-analysis he himself calls statistically weak and internally contradictory, yet it supplies half the video's framing. His programming advice does not actually depend on it.
