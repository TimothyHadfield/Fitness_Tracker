# Stress and Sleep: The Two Lifestyle Factors That Can Halve Your Gains

**Source:** Menno Henselmans, "These are the top 2 underrated factors killing your gains" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dezSfj-agGs)) · 2025-07-01 · 11 min
**Topic:** Psychology & behaviour

## Bottom line
- In a 16-week program, students under high academic stress got **less than half** the leg press gains of low-stress students — and lost leg size while gaining body fat, while the low-stress group did the reverse.
- Highly stressed people take roughly **twice as long** to recover from a given workout ([Stults-Kolehmainen 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24343323/)).
- **Problem-focused active coping** beats emotion-focused and passive coping. Deal with the actual stressor; don't manage the feeling about it.
- Sleeping **5 vs 7.5 hours** during a diet flipped nutrient partitioning badly: over 50% less of the weight lost was fat, over 50% more was fat-free mass ([Nedeltcheva 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20921542/)).
- A 10-week sleep-optimisation intervention produced near-perfect recomposition: **−1.8 kg fat, +1.7 kg lean** vs **+0.8 kg fat, +1.3 kg lean** in controls ([Jåbekk 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32141273/)).
- His framing: sleep and stress belong **alongside macros and programming**, not down with marginal supplements.

## Why mental stress is a physical problem
Stress flips the body into sympathetic dominance — fight-or-flight — which is by design an off switch for reproduction, digestion, repair and growth. Acutely that's useful. Chronically it means you are permanently suppressing the processes muscular recovery depends on.

The supporting evidence he gives is a mix of training outcomes and mechanism. Beyond the academic-stress training studies, he cites roughly **double the injury rate** in footballers during high-stress periods, and the doubled recovery time above.

Stress also attacks the diet side: it raises appetite, lowers energy expenditure, blunts the thermic effect of food and anabolic hormone production, and in some cases lowers BMR. It also makes self-medicating with comfort food more likely.

**Overreach flag:** the two academic-stress training studies measured body composition by **limb circumference and skinfolds** — he says so himself — and the second one found no significant change in thigh circumference at all. "Half the gains" is a real signal but rests on weak measurement.

He also anticipates the objection: there's stigma among male lifters that stress management is soft. His answer is that your body doesn't care whether you take it seriously.

## Four stress strategies he'll defend
**1. Problem-focused active coping.** The single biggest determinant of how much damage a stressor does is how you cope with it. Passive coping — drinking, TV, procrastinating, venting to friends — is the default because it's easy, and research consistently finds it worse. Active coping splits into emotion-focused (managing the feeling) and problem-focused (fixing the cause). He argues emotion-focused coping is over-emphasised and problem-focused is by far the most effective. His reasoning is that this is what stress is *for*: it energises you to solve an environmental problem. If you have a problem with someone, talk to them.

**2. Mindfulness/meditation.** The one emotion-focused strategy he endorses, and explicitly not a spiritual one — sit and think about chairs. The mechanism he offers is clearing the mind's cache: working memory is loaded with open windows and humans cannot voluntarily *not* think about something (the pink elephant problem), so the only route is to think about something neutral instead. Caveat he volunteers: many personality types can't sustain it.

**3. Work hard, play hard — make stress episodic.** Stress evolved to be acute and transient; severe episodic stress is far less damaging than low-grade chronic stress that never lets up. So build in genuine disconnection: not a two-week holiday after six months of grind, but a period every single day where you fully switch off. Ideally right before bed, since it improves sleep quality.

**4. Nature exposure.** He concedes this sounds off-brand for the channel and says he goes where the research leads. Exposure to natural environments reduces stress markers, down to something as small as an indoor plant.

## Sleep
Direct evidence, not inference. Sleep deprivation reduces muscle protein synthesis including the myofibrillar fraction. In dieting studies, short sleep wrecks nutrient partitioning: the Nedeltcheva result above, and a later study where sleeping 14 minutes less midweek shifted the fat-free share of weight lost from a normal ~20% to ~80%.

He handles that second one honestly: he contacted the authors about statistical problems with the paper and they could not explain to him how the numbers add up. He still calls the direction clear. Treat the 80% figure as unreliable.

The Jåbekk intervention is the cleanest result — actual lifters, actual recomposition — but he notes the muscle-growth difference did not reach statistical significance. The fat-loss difference did.

Everything else follows: sleep deprivation degrades anabolic hormones, energy expenditure, appetite regulation and stress sensitivity.
