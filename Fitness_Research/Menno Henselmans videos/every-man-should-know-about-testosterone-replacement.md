# TRT: What "Replacement" Actually Means, and Where It Stops Being TRT

**Source:** Menno Henselmans, "What every man should know about testosterone replacement therapy (TRT)" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RhEzTYFoksY)) · 2022-12-21 · 6 min
**Topic:** Physiology

## Bottom line
- The load-bearing word is **replacement**. Because of the negative feedback loop, you cannot top up a little — adding exogenous testosterone suppresses your own production proportionally, so TRT means **shutting down natural production entirely** and then setting your level where you want it.
- **Genuine TRT confers no meaningful physiological advantage over being natural at a good level.** Testosterone acts through the androgen receptor and doesn't care whether it came from your testicles or a needle. If your level is already healthy, going on TRT shuts down your own production for nothing.
- **Above the physiological range, it isn't TRT any more.** He is direct that "TRT" has become a euphemism for cruising on steroids, and calls that disingenuous. Blast-and-cruise is a legitimate choice; mislabelling it is not.
- He nonetheless thinks **every man should know what TRT is and consider it** once age-related decline sets in, and says he will likely go on it himself — while noting most age-related decline is largely preventable through body composition and lifestyle.
- **Bloodwork is the gate.** Cholesterol profile (HDL in particular), blood pressure, prostate-specific antigen, plus FSH and LH to confirm full suppression. Liver, kidney and diabetes markers get checked but rarely become the issue.

## The mechanism, and why "a little bit" doesn't work
Testosterone aromatises to oestrogen, so in men the two stay proportional and oestrogen is essentially regulated as a by-product. The body monitors both and adjusts production accordingly. That feedback loop is why partial replacement is self-defeating: give a small dose and endogenous output falls to compensate. To control your level, you have to replace the whole thing.

The upside of that is control. If your natural level sat at the low end of the reference range, replacement lets you place it higher, which he says he advises for clients on TRT provided all biomarkers stay normal. He is also open that he'd consider going to the top of the physiological range or above it himself — while being explicit that at that point the label no longer applies.

## Where the line sits
His test for whether it is still TRT: **is your level within what your own natural production would plausibly have given you?** Above the normal range, you are cruising, not replacing. He notes that if FSH and LH are not fully suppressed, you may actually be under-dosed and not fully replacing.

On the natural-athlete question, his position is that someone on medically supervised, in-range TRT is **essentially natural for practical purposes**, because the hormonal environment is the same one they'd have had. He adds a clarification in the description that this is not how federations see it: testosterone is normally a banned substance, so anyone wanting to compete in a natural federation must disclose bloodwork and prescriptions and get permission in advance — never just compete while on it.

## Where the claim outruns the evidence
No sources are given anywhere in this video, and several claims would benefit from them: that most age-related decline is preventable through lifestyle, and that high-normal versus low-normal testosterone meaningfully affects muscle gain (he refers to another of his own videos for this). He is also candid that he is "not super convinced" of the PSA-prostate cancer relationship — an off-consensus aside offered without support.
