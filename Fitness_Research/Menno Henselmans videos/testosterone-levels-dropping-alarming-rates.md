# Why Testosterone Is Falling Across Generations — And Which Explanation Holds Up

**Source:** Menno Henselmans, "Why testosterone levels are dropping at “alarming” rates" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MBzhPvqbwzY)) · 2023-12-27 · 8 min
**Topic:** Physiology

## Bottom line
- The decline is real and independent of ageing: men in their 20s and 30s now have levels comparable to what previous cohorts had in their 50s.
- Of the three usual explanations — endocrine-disrupting chemicals, "feminisation" of society, and lifestyle — **only lifestyle has hard data behind it**.
- Once studies control for **body fat, sleep and mineral status, the cross-generational decline is no longer significant**.
- If you lift, sleep, eat reasonably and carry healthy body fat, there is nothing further to do. No supplement meaningfully raises testosterone; the only real lever is injecting it.
- He expects natural muscular potential has **not** declined over the last 50 years.

## Endocrine-disrupting chemicals: plausible, unproven
Plastics, pesticides and personal-care chemicals do show hormonal effects, but overwhelmingly in animal and in-vitro work. Human data are associational — people with higher measured exposure have lower testosterone, which can't be separated from the lifestyle and pollution factors that travel with it. No experimental human evidence shows real-world exposure levels lowering testosterone. He quotes the field's own summary: effects are demonstrated in the lab, human exposure data are scarce and don't uniformly support toxicity at environmental concentrations, and "the final answer is not yet in sight."

## The "feminisation of society" argument: internally inconsistent
Social context genuinely moves testosterone — status challenges raise it in people high in a hierarchy and lower it in people low in one. He gives three objections:

1. **The mechanism isn't about gender roles.** These are deep status and power-dynamic effects appearing in men and women alike, independent of whether the situation is framed as masculine or feminine.
2. **The direction is wrong.** Modern behaviour has shifted *toward* short-term mating strategies — hookup culture, fewer people having children, smaller families. Nurturing behaviour lowers testosterone; mate-seeking raises it. On this theory, current culture should push testosterone *up*.
3. **All documented effects are transient.** Levels move for hours around a status challenge or a spell of childcare and then return. Nothing shows they shift long-term resting levels, let alone across generations.

## Lifestyle: the explanation that survives
Higher body fat, less sleep and mineral deficiencies each lower testosterone, and obesity has risen sharply. When studies control for lifestyle variables, no significant generational decline remains — lifestyle is sufficient to account for it. The Mendelian-randomisation evidence he cites supports obesity causing lower testosterone rather than the reverse.

Practical: get body fat to a healthy level, train, sleep, manage stress, cover magnesium and zinc (boron follows from decent vegetable intake). Normal bloods and no symptoms means stop worrying.

**Overreach flag:** "entirely under our own control" is stronger than the evidence supports. Controlling for lifestyle removing statistical significance is not the same as lifestyle being the sole cause — it also reflects limited power to detect a residual chemical effect, which he concedes earlier but not in the conclusion.
