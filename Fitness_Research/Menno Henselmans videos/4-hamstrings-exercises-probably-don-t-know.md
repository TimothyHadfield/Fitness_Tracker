# Four Bodyweight Leg Curl Variants

**Source:** Menno Henselmans, "4 Hamstrings Exercises You Probably Don't Know" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=37VEuZUkZa8)) · 2023-09-06 · 5 min
**Topic:** Training

## Bottom line
- Four knee-flexion exercises requiring no leg curl machine. All four hit **all heads of the hamstrings plus the gastrocnemius**.
- **Suspended leg curls (TRX)** are the one to actually program — near-unlimited progressive overload by loading plates on the abdomen.
- **Hanging leg curls** are the best for pairing with squats: you're already in the rack, and there is evidence that pre-fatiguing the hamstrings potentiates quad strength via **antagonist potentiation**.
- Every variant scales the same way: **hips low is easier, hips high (held in a bridge) is much harder**, and one leg is harder again.
- The bodyweight variants **have a ceiling** — most will max out and need to cycle them in and out.

## 1. Hanging (gliding) leg curls
Hook your ankles over a bench and curl your entire body up. High felt tension for most people.

Its real selling point is **compatibility with squats**. Doing a leg curl before squatting tends to make the squat feel more stable, doesn't interfere with squat strength, and may improve it — he cites unnamed research on antagonist potentiation from pre-fatiguing the hamstrings. It slots naturally into par sets, supersets or combo sets since the rack is already occupied.

**Progression ladder** when bodyweight reps get high (say 30):
1. **Hold the hips high** in a bridge throughout the set — the hamstrings now work as hip extensors as well as knee flexors, and shortening them increases functional excursion.
2. **Move the bench further away** (comfort permitting).
3. **One leg, hips low.**
4. **One leg, hips high** — advanced, but not Nordic-curl territory. Many intermediates can manage it for moderate reps.

## 2. Walkouts
Walk out on your heels in tiny steps, hips low or high. He's frank that nobody will enjoy this one and it doesn't belong in a normal program. Its virtue is that it **requires literally nothing** — a hotel room or a bare floor is enough.

## 3. Sliding leg curls
The same movement with furniture sliders or Valslides on a smooth floor. He notes it also works on a **rowing machine**, which makes it viable in CrossFit-style gyms and hotel gyms with no hamstring equipment. Same hips-low/hips-high scaling. "Works a lot better than it looks."

## 4. Suspended leg curls
His pick of the four, and the only one he'd expect someone to run for months at a time. TRX or suspension straps set as low as possible; only the upper back touches the floor; **hands stay off the floor** — it's a pure bodyweight knee flexion.

**Loading:** plates on the abdomen, which sits stably. Two plates is comfortable for most; beyond that it gets cumbersome, at which point switch to hips-high, then one leg, then one leg with hips high.

He explicitly **advises against** the other difficulty lever — moving further from the anchor or changing strap height. It works, but you can't track it precisely enough to progressively overload, so the position becomes a hidden variable in your logbook. Use it only to make things *easier* if the low-anchor version is beyond you.

## Caveats
This is a demonstration video, not an evidence review. The only empirical claim is the antagonist potentiation one, which is left unsourced. Everything about which variant is "best" rests on his coaching judgement — reasonable, but treat it as such.
