# Seven Fat Loss Myths, All Downstream Of Energy Balance

**Source:** Menno Henselmans, "These 7 Popular Diet Myths Keep People Fat" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Es_XvaPVnc)) · 2024-11-14 · 8 min
**Topic:** Nutrition

## Bottom line
- **Carbs don't make you fat; calories do.** Low-fat and low-carb diets match for fat loss once energy and protein are equated. Insulin is a storage hormone, but "insulin cannot store what isn't there" — it's only net fat-promoting in a surplus.
- **Hormones matter far less than people think.** Even thyroid hormone — the master metabolic regulator — accounts for only about a **5% difference in resting energy expenditure** across the normal range. Treating clinically hypothyroid patients produces mostly **water loss**, not fat loss, because hypothyroidism causes heavy water retention.
- **The 3,500 kcal-per-pound rule is wrong** because it assumes you're also losing lean mass. To lose a kilo of **pure adipose tissue** the net deficit is about **8,260 kcal**, not the conventional ~7,000.
- **Stable weight does not mean energy maintenance.** For lifters especially: you can lose fat while gaining muscle, hold water in a stair-step pattern, or retain water from creatine — all with a flat scale.
- **Nobody is metabolically incapable of losing fat**, there is **no metabolic damage**, and there is **no genetic body-fat set point**.

## The hormone section
[00:44] The useful specifics are the thyroid numbers. Comparing people at the low versus high end of the *normal* thyroid range gives roughly 5% difference in resting energy expenditure. And in genuine hypothyroidism, replacement therapy produces modest weight loss that is mostly myxoedema water clearing. Hormones move appetite meaningfully; they move expenditure marginally.

## Weight maintenance versus energy maintenance
[02:11] He notes an image conflating the two appeared in *Nutrition Reviews*, a respectable journal. For sedentary people with everything else controlled it's practically true; for strength trainees it often isn't. His clinical example: clients losing fat and gaining muscle at constant weight, misread as a plateau because body fat wasn't tracked separately. He also describes the **whoosh effect** — fat loss proceeding linearly underneath while weight moves in steps as water is held and then released.

## Metabolic damage
[04:26] He says his research team looked for it in anorexia nervosa, bodybuilders in contest prep, wrestlers cutting to make weight, and the Minnesota starvation experiment, and found none. In every case metabolism was predictable from body composition: it slowed as tissue was lost (real adaptive thermogenesis), and returned to baseline when body composition did. **Temporary adaptation, not permanent damage.** A substantive contrarian stand, though the supporting work is his own team's review.

## Set point versus settling point
[05:19] He rejects a genetic set point outright — no evidence of a number in the brain the body steers toward. What exists is a **settling point**: the cumulative product of energy intake, food choices and activity level. He dislikes even that term, because it still implies something beyond your control. His explanation for regain is behavioural — treat a diet as "six weeks of suffering for the summer", revert to the old lifestyle, revert to the old body fat.

One genuinely physiological addition: **fat overshooting.** Non-lifters who lose muscle while dieting return to the same weight with *more* fat and still-missing muscle, and the lost muscle suppresses expenditure, making even the previous body composition harder to hold. That is his argument for lifting even when the goal is purely fat loss.

Flag: myth five — "everyone can lose fat... 100% guaranteed, the laws of physics demand it" — is true as thermodynamics and unhelpful as advice, since the difficulty was always sustaining the deficit.
