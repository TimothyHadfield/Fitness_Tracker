# TRT After the FDA Label Change: What the New Reviews Say

**Source:** Menno Henselmans, "Is TRT safe? FDA revises official guidelines [New research]" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4oLNnTS0g18)) · 2026-01-27 · 7 min
**Topic:** Physiology

*Medical topic. This note records what he said, not advice. Anything here is a matter for a doctor.*

## Bottom line
- Two large 2025 reviews, and the TRAVERSE trial behind them, support the **safety of genuine replacement-dose testosterone**. On that evidence the FDA removed many TRT warning labels, and the reviews found **no increased prostate cancer risk**.
- Going from low to normal testosterone appears to give a **null effect on all-cause mortality**: small benefits and small risks that roughly cancel.
- The safety case does **not** extend above replacement. "Sports TRT" is his term for supraphysiological low-dose steroid use; there is **no way to get steroid-like effects without steroid-like side effects**.
- Taking any exogenous testosterone **suppresses your own production**, so this is not a small decision.

## Why the dose, not the source, is the issue
The "it's artificial" objection he rejects outright: testosterone is chemically testosterone whether it comes from a needle, a patch or your own testes. The "more total testosterone" objection he treats as real but **a question of dose**, since testosterone has a well-established dose-response relationship for both benefits and harms.

Why the low end is unhealthy: genuinely low testosterone is not a benign state — beyond low libido and vitality it is associated with insulin resistance and low muscle mass, both carrying cardiometabolic cost. Restoring low to normal is a small absolute change with offsetting effects.

Above the reference range the picture changes. He is explicit that even sitting at the **top of the natural range** can produce meaningful side effects, and that a large share of people describing themselves as "on TRT" are not on replacement at all.

## Suppression makes it a serious decision
Exogenous testosterone shuts down natural production proportionally, so "topping up a little" is impossible — a small dose just replaces a small amount of your own output, leaving total levels unchanged. Any benefit requires displacing natural production entirely. Coming off is usually recoverable over weeks to months, but he flags that **long-term permanent gonadal suppression is a real risk**, particularly without something like hCG to mitigate it. (The transcript's "8CG" is an auto-caption mangling of hCG.)

## What he says to monitor
Before starting: confirm you actually have low testosterone. **Two blood tests**, both fasted and in the morning to rule out measurement error, measuring **total and free testosterone**.

He puts symptoms over numbers in both directions: low numbers with **no symptoms** of hypogonadism (difficulty gaining muscle, low libido, low energy) is not a problem needing treatment; borderline-normal numbers **with** symptoms may still benefit, though a conservative doctor will be harder to convince.

On treatment, regular blood work covering three things:
- **Haematocrit** — testosterone stimulates red blood cell production; too high raises thrombosis and clot risk. Ceilings vary by organisation, roughly **48-52%**.
- **Blood pressure** — easily measured at home; repeat readings to rule out white-coat effect.
- **Lipids** — HDL not tanking, LDL not climbing, read against the lab's reference ranges.

His framing: this monitoring, not the population-level result, is what determines whether TRT is safe for a given individual.

## Note on the evidence
The video presents the FDA change and the two reviews as close to settling the safety question ("quite unanimously supports the safety"). These are narrative reviews plus one large trial, on **replacement dosing in men with genuine deficiency** — a narrower claim than the confident tone conveys, and none of it speaks to the supraphysiological use he separately warns about.
