# Mechanical Tension Is the Only Established Mechanism

**Source:** Menno Henselmans, "Cut out the BS. Do THIS to build muscle [3 New Studies]" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rmOBmTZARq8)) · 2025-12-30 · 6 min
**Topic:** Training

## Bottom line
- A within-subject drop-set study came out **against** the effective-reps and training-density models and **for** a tonnage model: after 10 weeks the drop-set arm gained **significantly less** elbow flexor hypertrophy despite matched total reps and more time near failure. The difference was small — as was the difference in total tonnage.
- A new review concludes **mechanical tension is the only truly established mechanism of hypertrophy**, active or passive, and that **cell swelling, the pump and metabolic stress do not affect long-term adaptation**.
- **Sarcoplasmic hypertrophy is real but not targetable.** It occurs alongside myofibrillar growth, in rough proportion, and no rep scheme or rest interval preferentially drives it.
- **Hyperplasia does not appear to occur in humans.** A meta-analysis found no average increase in fibre number, though he notes counting fibres in a living human is notoriously hard.
- Programming implication: worry about **tension multiplied by time under tension** — think of it as the integral — and stop worrying about everything else.

## The drop-set study
Trained lifters trained one arm conventionally and the other with drop sets, 8–12RM loads, three-minute rests:

- **Traditional arm:** 4 sets preacher curl + 2 sets lat pulldown, to failure.
- **Drop-set arm:** 2 sets preacher curl + 1 set lat pulldown — half the volume — but with repeated 8% load drops triggered at 20% velocity loss.

Pilot testing had established that this produced the **same total repetitions per session** while keeping the drop-set arm at 0–2 RIR throughout.

That design makes it a clean model discriminator, which is why he likes it. **Effective-reps** predicts the drop-set arm wins (more reps near failure). **Density** predicts the drop-set arm wins (harder work, less time). **His own tonnage-based model** predicts it loses, because equal reps at a falling average load means less average tension per rep with time under tension held constant. The drop-set arm lost.

Two things worth keeping in proportion: the study is **still a preprint**, and the effect was small — consistent with the small tonnage difference, but also consistent with noise in a single trial. He presents it as an illustration of his model rather than a test that settles anything, which is the right register.

## What the review rules out
The mechanisms review lands squarely against a large chunk of influencer programming. Cell swelling and the pump "may feel satisfying" but do not affect long-term adaptation, and metabolic stress never had strong support — he argues it was largely a post-hoc rationalisation of the pump.

His explanation for why the pump-and-soreness model is so universal is a behavioural one: **lifters without exercise science default to what they can feel**, and what they can feel is the pump and the soreness. He notes in passing that soreness isn't even a good indicator of muscle damage. The felt signals are, at best, proxies for tension.

**Library cross-connection:** the mechanisms review is co-authored by Jeff Nippard alongside Stuart Phillips' group — the other main source in this library — so the two sources are converging here rather than competing.
