# Is Organic Food Worth Paying For?

**Source:** Menno Henselmans, "Is organic food worth it?" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ReeBSO0bawc)) · 2025-04-21 · 8 min
**Topic:** Nutrition

## Bottom line
- A 2024 systematic review of **147 studies** found no generalisable nutritional superiority of organic over conventional food. Most comparisons showed no difference; in some cases conventional food contained *more* of a nutrient.
- The **only consistent difference favouring organic is vitamin C** — trivially easy to get from any half-decent diet or a multivitamin.
- Toxic residue content varies by residue and food type with no clear trend, so "organic is safer" is not supported.
- The environmental case cancels out: organic is better per unit *area* but less productive, so per unit of food bought the advantage is near zero.
- Organic animal-welfare standards are weak; Animal Welfare Approved or Certified Humane are likely better proxies if that's your reason.
- His verdict: closer to a status symbol than a sound investment for a budget-conscious buyer.

## What "organic" actually certifies
There is no tight universal definition. The most common criterion is production without synthetic or artificial hormones, pesticides, fertilisers or GMOs. Three consequences most buyers miss:

- **Organic food is still grown with pesticides** — organic ones.
- **It says almost nothing about what an animal ate.** It certifies the feed was organic, not that it suited the animal. "Organic crap is still crap."
- **Organic does not always mean 100% organic**, and definitions vary by country and certifying body.

The factors he considers more important — soil quality, actual animal diet — aren't covered by the label at all.

## The health evidence
The 147-study review examined only objective attributes, not long-term health outcomes, and he flags this as a real limitation on its conclusion. His counter-argument is mechanistic: if the food isn't objectively different in what it contains, it's hard to see why outcomes would differ.

The few longer-term outcome studies are short and typically substitute only *some* organic food rather than a full 0%-to-100% shift — and mostly find nothing.

**Observational research does favour organic**, clearly so — organic eaters are healthier on multiple measures. He dismisses this as confounded: organic buyers are wealthier and more health-conscious across many dimensions.

## Environment and animal welfare
Per unit area, organic methods do come out ahead. But a recent review found similar total environmental impacts on average, because organic production is less productive — which is also why it costs more. Without the best fertilisers and pesticides, yield drops and more land is needed per unit of output, offsetting the per-area gain.

On welfare, organic certification typically guarantees something like access to an outdoor area of some size, "potentially unbeknownst to the animal in question."

**Overreach flag:** he endorses the review's line that belief in organic superiority "can only be grounded in subjective knowledge" while noting the review never examined health outcomes. He marks it as premature, but the video still leans harder on that conclusion than the design supports.
