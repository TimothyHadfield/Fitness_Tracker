# Melatonin: What It Does, What It Doesn't, and How Much

**Source:** Menno Henselmans, "Perfect sleep without addiction from a natural supplement?" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RvJk2MdqqRs)) · 2023-10-10 · 5 min
**Topic:** Supplements

## Bottom line
- Melatonin **helps you fall asleep, not stay asleep**. It reliably reduces sleep latency and has a small positive effect on sleep quality. If your problem is waking at 3am, this is the wrong tool.
- It is worth taking only if you take **more than ~20 minutes** to fall asleep (the standard research threshold for abnormal latency), or if you are deliberately shifting your schedule earlier or crossing time zones.
- **3–5 mg**, not the micro-doses commonly recommended. He explicitly disagrees with the start-very-low advice.
- **Fast release, not extended release.** Slow release keeps levels elevated into the morning and produces a groggy melatonin hangover.
- Take it **30–60 minutes before bed**; he finds it hits within 30 minutes and can fade within an hour, so the timing is worth self-testing.

## Where he breaks with the usual advice
The consensus line is to start at 0.3–1 mg because supraphysiological doses are unnecessary. He rejects that: in his reading the research effect sizes land at 3 mg for most people, with jet-lagged individuals and people with sleep disorders (he includes himself) still gaining up to 5 mg. He puts endogenous production at only 0.1–0.9 mg/day, so 3 mg is already far above physiological — and treats that as fine, on the grounds that even very high doses appear free of side effects.

He also states flatly that supplementing does **not** suppress natural production and is **not** addictive. That is the central selling point of the video, and none of it is sourced.

## What it will and won't fix
Night waking he attributes to stress, low magnesium, or light exposure — not to a melatonin deficit, since melatonin should already be high at that hour. It may help you get back to sleep; it will not stop the waking.

For schedule shifting he uses a rule of thumb of **about one hour of circadian shift per day**, which also gives the jet-lag estimate of one day per time zone.

On benefits he is deliberately dismissive of the marketing angle: there is research on year-long use showing fat loss and lean-mass effects, but he treats every downstream benefit as **mediated by improved sleep** rather than by melatonin itself. The one direct property he mentions is that it is a potent antioxidant.

## Overreach
The video is entirely uncited while making strong safety claims — no side effects even at very high doses, not addictive, no long-term suppression of natural production — plus a specific figure for endogenous output. The 3–5 mg recommendation directly contradicts the low-dose consensus and would need the dose-response data attached to be checkable. Note also a verbal slip where he says "grams" and immediately corrects to milligrams.
