# What a 23-Week Study of Finnish Physique Competitors Shows

**Source:** Menno Henselmans, "4 Things we can learn from Finnish bodybuilders" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XBk1GKTO5no)) · 2023-06-26 · 7 min
**Topic:** Nutrition

## Bottom line
- **Metabolic damage is a myth; metabolic adaptation is real.** The competitors' metabolisms slowed beyond what body mass loss predicted — and returned to baseline after the recovery phase, along with hormones and suppressed immune markers.
- **Sub-5% body fat (men) and sub-12% (women) is close to the floor.** These national-level competitors reached **5.6%** and **12.6%** on stage.
- **You can build muscle in a deficit.** The women gained significant lean mass over the 23 weeks into competition; the men did not.
- **Women respond as well as men** to dieting and training, relative to their starting point.
- Most health markers *improve* while dieting — insulin sensitivity, resting heart rate, lipids, often blood pressure. The main costs are mental, chiefly fatigue.

## Metabolic adaptation, unpacked
Most of the drop in energy expenditure on a diet is **predictable**: you carry less mass (including some lost muscle), which lowers even resting expenditure, and you eat less food, which lowers diet-induced thermogenesis.

**Adaptive thermogenesis** is the leftover — the reduction beyond what body composition predicts. His own analysis with Anastasia Zinchenko (transcript renders this "sinchenko") pooled wrestlers, bodybuilders and starvation experiments including the **Minnesota starvation experiment**, measuring it as the ratio of measured to predicted expenditure. The Finnish study found the same thing and the same reversal: **regain the lost fat and muscle and expenditure returns to its previous level**.

He's blunt about the behavioural angle — dieters are often "preoccupied with finding excuses to eat more food", and metabolic damage is the popular one.

He concedes a boundary: approaching **essential body fat levels** brings real negatives, including the immune suppression seen here. For someone chasing visible abs rather than stage condition, that's not the relevant range.

## The body fat floor
Essential body fat is roughly **3-5% in men** and **10-12% in women**. Competitors landing at 5.6% and 12.6% matches a systematic review of bodybuilding studies he cites elsewhere: below 5%/12% is nearly impossible. Stated plainly — when someone claims 1% body fat, they weren't.

## Recomposition and sex differences
The women's significant DEXA lean mass increase into the show is his evidence for gaining muscle in a deficit. He's careful with the inference: DEXA is accurate as a **sample average**, so the reasonable read is that at least some of these women gained muscle, not all.

His scaling: recomposition should be **the norm for beginners**, common in intermediates (most of his clients manage it), and increasingly dependent on genetics and execution as you advance.

On sex differences, women and men gain the **same percentage** increase in strength and size from an equivalent starting body composition; women start with less muscle, so absolute gains are smaller. He notes he wrote one of the first popular arguments for this. He repeats the claim in later videos backed by a 27-study meta-analysis; here it rests mainly on this cohort and his own article.

## Note on the evidence
This is a **single observational cohort** followed 23 weeks in and 23 weeks out. It suits the metabolic adaptation question and the body fat floor. It is weak evidence for the recomposition and sex-difference claims, where a subgroup result in a small non-randomised sample carries broad conclusions — he leans on his own prior articles for both without flagging the gap.
