# The Male Physique Women Actually Rate Highest

**Source:** Menno Henselmans, "This is the ultimate men's physique according to women" ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1a1vrFdqEcs)) · 2024-05-13 · 8 min
**Topic:** Psychology & behaviour

## Bottom line
- Across multiple studies, women's ratings follow an **inverted U**: attractiveness rises with muscularity up to a "decently built" level, then **falls off sharply** at bodybuilder levels. One study's summary — women prefer "moderately developed musculature and a rather slim build."
- **Men systematically misjudge this.** In a three-country study, men chose an ideal body about **28 lb (13 kg) more muscular than themselves**, and guessed women wanted one about **30 lb (14 kg) more muscular** — while women in a pilot preferred an ordinary male body with no added muscle.
- In his own polls: among men, the shredded fitness-model look took **41%** of the vote; among (mostly lifting) women, that same physique got about **10%**, and the bodybuilder physique came **last with zero votes**. The two physiques women rated top got roughly 10% from men.
- **The one exception is glutes.** Men over-rate the importance of every muscle group except the buttocks — women care about a muscular backside as much as men think they do.
- Despite these stated preferences, **more muscular men consistently report more sex**: more lifetime partners, more short-term partners, and more affairs with women in relationships.

## Why the curve turns over
[03:39] His explanation is evolutionary-psychological and offered as reasoning, not as tested mechanism. Muscularity signals capacity to acquire and defend resources and good genes. But a partner also has to **share** those resources and stay. One study he cites rated muscular men as sexier, more physically dominant, more volatile and **less committed to their mates** — which is the trade-off that produces the inverted U, and the appeal of the "dad bod": less capacity, more reliability.

## The gap between what's rated and what happens
[05:08] He's honest about the tension in his own material. If women rate very muscular men lower, why do those men report more partners? His answer, quoting researchers, is that highly muscular men may be more dominant, volatile, persistent, confident, coercive and narcissistic — traits that produce more partners regardless of rated attractiveness. He adds two of his own: women fall for bad-boy types, and women run short-term versus long-term mating strategies just as men do. Being rated attractive for a hookup is not the same as being rated attractive as a husband.

## Individual variation
[06:27] He flags averages explicitly. One consistent moderator: **more traditionally feminine women prefer more muscular, V-tapered physiques**, while less traditional women show less stereotyped preferences. He then extends this anecdotally — men in fitness tend to prefer curvier women, who in turn tend to prefer more muscular men — which he offers as a possible resolution of the paradox above. That last step is his personal observation, not a research finding, and he presents it more confidently than it deserves.

## Note on the evidence
This is a video built on ratings of silhouettes and photos plus self-reported sexual history, alongside two informal social-media polls of his own audience. The polls are not a sample of women in general; he says so for the female poll. The inverted-U result itself is well replicated; the causal story about commitment and resources is not tested here.
